Description
How did experiments explain blood, germs and atoms? Six discovery stories follow the evidence across almost three hundred years, from a tied arm and a willow tree to a swan-neck flask, the periodic table, X-rays and gold foil.

This 49-minute compilation brings together six History of Science films in chronological order of proof (1628 to 1911), with a new cold-open introduction, chapter cards, five bridges and an ink-drawn timeline.

Sources (The Hidden Circle): William Harvey, De Motu Cordis (Frankfurt, 1628), ch. 9, 11, 13 and dedication (cardiac output sum and forearm ligature proof); Hieronymus Fabricius, De Venarum Ostiolis (1603, one-way vein valves); Andreas Vesalius, De humani corporis fabrica, 2nd ed. (1555, interventricular septum); Marcello Malpighi, De pulmonibus (1661, pulmonary capillaries); Geoffrey Keynes, The Life of William Harvey (1966); Vivian Nutton, Ancient Medicine (2004, Galenic system).

Sources (Built From Air): J. B. van Helmont, Ortus Medicinae (1648, five-year willow tree experiment); Stephen Hales, Vegetable Staticks (1727, leaf transpiration and air nourishment); Joseph Priestley, "Observations on Different Kinds of Air", Philosophical Transactions 62 (1772, mint restoring injured air); Jan Ingenhousz, Experiments upon Vegetables (1779, sunlight oxygen production by green leaves); Jean Senebier, Mémoires physico-chymiques (1782, fixed air / CO₂ requirement); Nicolas-Théodore de Saussure, Recherches chimiques sur la végétation (1804, carbon mass balance).

Sources (The Invisible Enemy): Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society (1674–1677, microscopic organisms in water); Ignaz Semmelweis, Die Ätiologie, der Begriff und die Prophylaxis des Kindbettfiebers (1861, handwashing and childbed fever); Louis Pasteur, "Mémoire sur les corpuscules organisés qui existent dans l'atmosphère", Annales des Sciences Naturelles 16 (1861, swan-neck flask experiments); Joseph Lister, "On the Antiseptic Principle in the Practice of Surgery", The Lancet 90 (1867, carbolic acid antisepsis).

Sources (The Empty Chairs): Antoine Lavoisier, Traité Élémentaire de Chimie (1789, simple substances); J. W. Döbereiner, Annalen der Physik 15 (1829, triads) & J. A. R. Newlands, Chemical News 12 (1865, octaves); Stanislao Cannizzaro, Sunto di un corso di filosofia chimica (1858 / Karlsruhe Congress 1860, atomic weights); Dmitri Mendeleev, Zeitschrift für Chemie 12 (1869, periodic table and predicted gaps); P.-É. Lecoq de Boisbaudran, Comptes Rendus 81 (1875, gallium), L. F. Nilson, Comptes Rendus 88 (1879, scandium) & C. Winkler, Berichte 19 (1886, germanium).

Sources (A Light Through Skin): William Crookes, Philosophical Transactions 170 (1879, cathode-ray tubes); Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen, "Über eine neue Art von Strahlen", Sitzungsberichte der Physikalisch-Medizinischen Gesellschaft zu Würzburg (Dec 1895 & Mar 1896, barium platinocyanide screen fluorescence and Bertha Röntgen's hand radiograph); W. C. Röntgen (trans. A. Stanton), "On a New Kind of Rays", Nature 53 (Jan 1896, early medical radiography); Nobel Foundation, Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen Biographical (1901 Nobel Prize in Physics).

Sources (Inside the Atom): John Dalton, A New System of Chemical Philosophy (1808, relative atomic weights); Dmitri Mendeleev, Zeitschrift für Chemie 12 (1869, tellurium–iodine weight inversion); J. J. Thomson, "Cathode Rays", Philosophical Magazine 44 (1897, electron charge-to-mass ratio); Hans Geiger & Ernest Marsden, "On a Diffuse Reflection of the α-Particles", Proceedings of the Royal Society A 82 (1909, gold-foil back-scattering); Ernest Rutherford, "The Scattering of α and β Particles by Matter and the Structure of the Atom", Philosophical Magazine 21 (1911, atomic nucleus); H. G. J. Moseley, "The High-Frequency Spectra of the Elements", Philosophical Magazine 26 (1913, atomic number).
