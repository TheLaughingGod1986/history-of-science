## 1. Trees are made of air: recommended

- **Lane:** the tree outside your window. Jan Baptist van Helmont, Brussels, 1640s: planted a 5 lb willow in 200 lb of dried soil, watered it for five years. The tree weighed 169 lb; the soil had lost 2 oz. He concluded the tree was made of water, which was wrong. The film follows the pot to the real answer: Priestley's mint revives a mouse's air (1771), Ingenhousz shows it needs light (1779). Before: plants eat soil. Proof: the scales. After: we know where food, wood and the air's oxygen come from.
- **Neighbours (on-topic):** CrashCourse *Photosynthesis* 9.2M, Kurzgesagt *Trees Are So Weird* 6.7M, Amoeba Sisters *Photosynthesis* 6.2M, Veritasium *Where Do Trees Get Their Mass?* 2.5M, TED-Ed *The simple story of photosynthesis and food* 2.2M, Khan Academy *Photosynthesis* 2.2M, TED-Ed *How tall can a tree grow?* 1.4M.
- **Search:** "where do trees get their mass" and "van helmont willow tree experiment" are both in autocomplete. Only Veritasium is big on the first; the second tops out at 67k (Homework Clinic).
- **Title:** *The Willow Tree That Was Made of Air* (36). Test & Compare: *Where Does a Tree's Weight Come From?*
- **Thumbnail:** a full-grown willow in a small clay pot on one pan of a brass balance, a pinch of soil on the other pan, still level; words MADE OF AIR.
- **Risk:** van Helmont's own conclusion was wrong. That's the twist, but the script has to say so plainly. No single room for the whole story: the willow pot is the anchor.

## 2. The first vaccine

- **Lane:** the jab in your arm. Edward Jenner, Berkeley, Gloucestershire, 14 May 1796: cowpox from milkmaid Sarah Nelmes's hand into James Phipps's arm; six weeks later smallpox matter, and nothing happened. *Vacca* is Latin for cow.
- **Neighbours (on-topic):** TED-Ed *How we conquered the deadly smallpox virus* 9.1M, TED-Ed *How do vaccines work?* 3.5M, TED-Ed *How the COVID-19 vaccines were created so quickly* 1.3M, Kurzgesagt *The Side Effects of Vaccines* 17.9M.
- **Search:** "how was the first vaccine made" is in autocomplete; top results are SciShow 221k, WHO Europe 206k, BBC Bitesize 306k. A small channel called "The History Of Science" (not ours, 1,116 views) ranks for the Jenner phrase.
- **Title:** *Why Vaccines Are Named After a Cow* (34). Test & Compare: *The Milkmaid's Hands That Made the First Vaccine*.
- **Thumbnail:** a boy's bare arm with one small blister, a cow's big eye close behind; words FROM A COW.
- **Risk:** testing on an eight-year-old needs a calm, factual line. Smallpox pictures must stay non-gory (the blood film's rules on red apply). Vaccine topics attract comment-section heat.

## 3. Lightning is electricity

- **Lane:** lightning, and the rod on every church spire. Franklin's plan (1750); Dalibard proved it first at Marly-la-Ville, 10 May 1752; Franklin's kite and key in Philadelphia that June. Proof: a spark jumps from a key on a wet string.
- **Neighbours (on-topic):** SciShow Kids *What Causes Thunder and Lightning?* 5.6M, MinuteEarth *How to Survive a Lightning Strike* 4.0M, TED-Ed *The science of static electricity* 3.6M, Kurzgesagt *Lightning: Nature's Plasma Highway* 2.1M.
- **Search:** "franklin kite experiment" is in autocomplete. Peekaboo Kidz 1.6M (kids), Be Smart 690k (*The TRUE Story of Ben Franklin & His Kite*), HISTORY 164k.
- **Title:** *The Kite That Proved Lightning Is Electricity* (45).
- **Thumbnail:** a spark leaping from a brass key on a kite string to a knuckle, storm cloud above; words THE KEY SPARKED.
- **Risk:** the kite story is partly disputed (one second-hand account); Be Smart already covers the "true story" angle. The lightning-rod payoff is strong.

## 4. What killed the dinosaurs

- **Lane:** dinosaurs. Walter Alvarez, the Bottaccione gorge at Gubbio, and his father Luis's lab at Berkeley, 1980: a 1 cm clay layer between the Cretaceous and Tertiary rock holds about 30× normal iridium, everywhere on Earth. The Chicxulub crater is found in 1991.
- **Neighbours (on-topic):** Kurzgesagt *The Day the Dinosaurs Died* 23.0M and *How The Dinosaurs Actually Died* 8.8M, HHMI BioInteractive *The Day the Mesozoic Died* 14.7M (found by the title search), TED-Ed *When will the next mass extinction occur?* 1.8M. TED-Ed's other hits (jellyfish, Sahara predators) are off-story.
- **Search:** both phrases in autocomplete and crowded: BBC Earth Science 2.0M, Astrum Earth 3.1M, PBS Eons 855k, plus Kurzgesagt.
- **Title:** *The Thin Clay Line That Killed the Dinosaurs* (44). Needs work: the clay didn't kill them; *The Thin Clay Line That Proved What Killed the Dinosaurs* (56) is accurate.
- **Thumbnail:** a finger on a thin dark stripe in a pale cliff, a T. rex shadow falling across the rock; words ONE THIN LINE.
- **Risk:** the biggest audience on the list but the most crowded search. HHMI already tells exactly this story.

## 5. Gravity and the falling Moon

From the 005 topic pack (`topic_packs_2026-10-01/gravity/PITCH.md`), re-run today.

- **Lane:** a falling apple and the Moon; Newton at Woolsthorpe, 1666; the Moon test (it falls 1/3600 as far each second as an apple: 60² for 60 Earth radii).
- **Neighbours:** Veritasium *What Everyone Gets Wrong About Gravity* 15.5M, TED-Ed *Newton's three-body problem explained* 9.9M, TED-Ed *Newton's 3 Laws, with a bicycle* 2.9M (Newton, not gravity), TED-Ed *How do geckos defy gravity?* 1.7M (off-story).
- **Search:** "how did newton discover gravity" in autocomplete; BBC Studios 158k, BBC Bitesize 136k.
- **Title:** *How Gravity Keeps the Moon Falling* (34).
- **Thumbnail:** an apple and the Moon falling side by side towards a small Earth; words BOTH ARE FALLING.
- **Risk:** the proof is a sum; the apple is the most retold myth in science.

## 6. Measuring the Earth with a stick

- **Lane:** the size of the Earth. Eratosthenes, librarian at Alexandria, about 240 BC: no shadow down a well at Syene at noon on the solstice, a 7.2° shadow at Alexandria; 1/50 of a circle, so about 250,000 stadia round.
- **Neighbours:** on-topic only Vsauce *Is Earth Actually Flat?* 38.6M and minutephysics *TOP 10 REASONS Why We Know the Earth is Round* 2.7M (plus Carl Sagan's *Cosmos* clip, 3.0M, not in the tool's education list). The tool's TED-Ed hits are off-topic (tardigrades, climate), on both phrase sets (`neighbours.*`, `neighbours_round.*`).
- **Search:** Business Insider 788k, Carl Sagan 3.0M, kidomind 1.2M.
- **Title:** *The Stick That Measured the Whole Earth* (39).
- **Thumbnail:** a stick casting a short shadow beside a deep well with sun straight down it; words NO SHADOW.
- **Risk:** weakest on-topic pool, no TED-Ed on the subject; the well detail is partly legend and the stadion's length is uncertain.
