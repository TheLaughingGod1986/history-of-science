For two hundred years, "preformationists" drew a microscopic, fully formed little human curled up inside every sperm cell! Even in **eighteen eighty-eight**, when **Wilhelm Roux** killed one cell of a two-cell frog embryo with a hot needle and got a half-embryo, he claimed each cell division threw away half the body's blueprint.

Then in the **spring of eighteen ninety-one** in Naples, twenty-three-year-old **Hans Driesch** shook two-cell **sea-urchin embryos** in a glass tube of seawater until the two cells separated completely. Expecting two half-urchins, Driesch looked in his microscope the next morning and saw **two complete, miniature sea-urchin larvae**! Every early embryo cell kept the **entire blueprint**, which is how a split embryo becomes **identical twins**!

If every cell holds the same blueprint, how does a cell know *where* it is? In **May nineteen twenty-one** in Freiburg, **Hans Spemann** and twenty-two-year-old graduate student **Hilde Mangold** used a loop of **fine blond hair from Spemann's infant daughter** to perform a microscopic miracle. Mangold cut a pinhead speck of tissue — the **dorsal blastopore lip** — from a **pale newt embryo** and grafted it onto the belly of a **dark newt embryo**!

A **complete second spine and head** grew out of the host's belly! And because the host cells were dark, Mangold's microscope slices proved that the new brain and spinal cord were built out of **dark host belly cells** that the tiny graft — **`The Organizer`** — had commanded to change their fate (`tragically, Hilde Mangold died in a stove explosion in September nineteen twenty-four just as their paper appeared; Spemann won the nineteen thirty-five Nobel Prize`)!

For fifty years after Mangold, biochemists crushed embryos trying to find which **genes inside the nucleus** actually drew the body's map. Who finally cracked the genetic code — by breeding **mutant fruit flies with four wings and legs growing out of their heads**?

`Hox` genes explain how one fertilised egg builds a head, a heart, and ten fingers.
