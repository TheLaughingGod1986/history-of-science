### PART 02: 1891 & May 1921 — Hans Driesch's Shaken Twins & Hilde Mangold's Blond Hair Loop (`The Spemann-Mangold Organizer`) (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why for two hundred years "preformationists" drew a microscopic, fully formed little person (`a homunculus`) curled up inside every sperm cell, and why even in 1888 Wilhelm Roux thought each cell division threw away half the body's blueprint):** **Stazione Zoologica, Naples, Spring 1891 & Zoological Institute, Freiburg, Germany, May 1921**.
- **1888 vs Spring 1891 — Wilhelm Roux's hot needle vs Hans Driesch's shaken sea urchin (`Twinning & Embryonic Regulation`):**
  - In **1888**, German embryologist **Wilhelm Roux** killed one cell of a 2-cell frog embryo with a hot needle and watched the surviving cell grow into a **half-embryo** — claiming that as an egg divides, each cell only gets the instructions for its own piece of the body (`mosaic development`).
  - In the **spring of 1891** in Naples, 23-year-old **Hans Driesch** (1867–1941) tried to repeat Roux's test on **sea-urchin eggs** by vigorously **shaking 2-cell embryos in a glass tube** until the two cells completely separated (`instead of leaving a dead cell stuck to the living one`).
  - The next day, Driesch looked in his microscope expecting two half-urchins — and saw **two complete, perfectly formed miniature sea-urchin larvae**! Every early embryo cell still kept the **entire blueprint (`totipotency`)**, which is why a split human embryo becomes **identical twins**!
- **May 1921 (*Arch. Mikr. Anat. Entw.* 1924) — Hilde Mangold & Hans Spemann discover `The Organizer`:**
  - If every cell has the same blueprint, how does a cell know *where* it is? Using loops of **fine blond hair from his infant daughter**, **Hans Spemann** (1869–1941) and 22-year-old graduate student **Hilde Mangold** (née Pröscholdt, 1898–1924) performed the most famous experiment in embryology in **May 1921**.
  - Mangold took a tiny patch of tissue — the **dorsal blastopore lip** — from a **pale, unpigmented newt embryo (*Triton cristatus*)** and grafted it onto the belly flank of a **dark-pigmented newt embryo (*Triton taeniatus*)**.
  - In **embryo `1921 Um 132`**, a **complete second body axis** — neural tube, spine, and head — grew out of the host's belly! And because the donor cells were pale and the host cells were dark, Mangold's microscope slices proved that the new second brain and spinal cord were built out of **dark host belly cells** that the tiny graft (**`The Organizer`**) had chemically commanded to change their fate (`by secreting BMP-blocking morphogen signals like Noggin and Chordin, uncovered by Richard Harland and Eddy De Robertis in 1992–1994`)!
  - Tragically, in **September 1924**, just as the paper was published, 25-year-old Hilde Mangold died when a kerosene stove exploded in her kitchen; Spemann received the **1935 Nobel Prize**.
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Wilhelm Roux (1850–1924) — German anatomist and embryologist · WHEN: 1888 · WHEN: 1891 · WHEN: May 1921 · WHEN: September 1924 · WHEN: 1935 · FACT: Belly skin becomes a brain · DATA: inked Roux (1888 hot-needle half-embryo) vs Driesch (1891 shaken 2-cell and 4-cell sea-urchin blastomere separation yielding 100% complete miniature pluteus larvae) and Spemann & Mangold (May 1921 / 1924 embryo Um 132) chimeric neural tube cell-origin chart on period paper · SCALE: Hilde Mangold's two-colour newt transplant worked like building a house out of white and black bricks — when the new twin spine appeared, over four-fifths of its bricks were black belly bricks that the tiny white organizer patch had recruited on the spot`
- **Ends on:** For fifty years after Hilde Mangold, biochemists boiled, crushed, and pickled embryos trying to find the secret of how cells read their position — and hit a brick wall, because changing an embryo's chemical soup couldn't explain **which genes inside the nucleus** actually drew the body's map! Who finally cracked the genetic code of the body plan — by breeding **mutant fruit flies with legs growing out of their heads**?

### PART 03: 7 December 1978 & 30 October 1980 — Edward Lewis's Four-Winged Fruit Fly & Nüsslein-Volhard and Wieschaus's Striped Embryo Screen — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — building a body is so complicated that thousands of random genes must be scattered haphazardly across every chromosome! Wrong! A tiny team of master architect genes sits lined up along the chromosome in the EXACT head-to-tail order of the body itself!"):** **Caltech, Pasadena, 7 December 1978 & European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL), Heidelberg, 30 October 1980**.
- **7 December 1978 (*Nature*) — Edward B. Lewis, `Ultrabithorax` & the `Colinearity` miracle:**
  - Since **William Bateson (1894)** coined **`homeosis`** (when one body part transforms into another) and **Calvin Bridges (1915)** spotted a mutant fruit fly (*Drosophila*) whose tiny third-segment balancing knobs (`halteres`) had begun turning into wings, American geneticist **Edward B. Lewis** (1918–2004) spent 30 years at Caltech mapping the **`Bithorax` gene complex**.
  - On **7 December 1978** (*Nature*), Lewis unveiled the iconic **four-winged fruit fly (`Ultrabithorax`, `bithorax + postbithorax`)**: knocking out the gene that tells the third thoracic segment *"You are segment T3"* caused that segment to default to the blueprint of segment T2 and grow a **full second pair of wings** (`while another mutation, Antennapedia, tells the head to run the thorax program and sprouts a pair of walking legs right out of the fly's head where its antennae should be!`)!
  - Even more astonishing was Lewis's **Law of Colinearity**: the master genes along the chromosome sit in the **exact same left-to-right physical order as the head-to-tail body segments they control**!
- **30 October 1980 (*Nature*) — Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard & Eric Wieschaus's Heidelberg screen (`How 15 genes slice a 1-cell egg into stripes`):**
  - How do those segment genes know where to turn on in a 1-cell egg? At EMBL in Heidelberg in **1979–1980**, German biologist **Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard** (born 1942) and American biologist **Eric F. Wieschaus** (born 1947) sat face-to-face across a single dual microscope for a year examining **27,000 mutant fruit-fly lines**!
  - On **30 October 1980** (*Nature*, sharing the **1995 Nobel Prize** with Lewis), they showed that a 3-tier cascade of just **15 segmentation genes** slices a round egg into a body map: first **maternal morphogen gradients** (like **`Bicoid`** at the front tip, telling the embryo *"This end is the head!"*), then **`gap` genes** (broad regions), **`pair-rule` genes** (7 alternating stripes), and **`segment-polarity` genes** (like **`hedgehog`** and **`wingless`**, locking in the front and back of all **14 segments**)!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1894 · WHEN: 1915 · WHEN: 7 December 1978 · WHEN: 30 October 1980 · WHEN: 1995 · FACT: Legs growing on a fly's head · DATA: inked Nüsslein-Volhard & Wieschaus (30 October 1980, Nature 287: 795–801) and Lewis (7 December 1978, Nature 276: 565–570) Drosophila segmentation hierarchy chart on period paper showing Maternal gradient (Bicoid anterior-to-posterior concentration curve) -> Gap genes -> Pair-rule 7-stripe genes -> 14 Segment-polarity stripes -> 8 Colinear Homeotic (Hox) selector genes · SCALE: building a body from a single round egg is like painting a map on a blank globe — first a smooth gradient of maternal ink marks the North Pole as the head, then three waves of stencil genes divide the globe into fourteen numbered stripes, and finally each stripe reads its own Hox address code`
- **Ends on:** Every biologist assumed those fruit-fly segment and `Hox` genes were just a quirk of insects (`after all, humans don't have fourteen bug segments, antennae, or wings!`). What happened in **1984** when scientists sequenced the DNA of those fly genes and searched for that sequence inside **frogs, mice, and humans**?

### PART 04: March–July 1984 & 24 March 1995 — The `180-Letter Homeobox` & Why a Mouse Gene Builds an Eye on a Fruit Fly's Leg (4:55–6:45)
- **Cold open (the jaw-dropping discovery that an earthworm, a fruit fly, a mouse, and a human being all use the exact same 180-letter DNA password to build their bodies from head to tail):** **Indiana University, Bloomington & Biozentrum, University of Basel, Switzerland, March–July 1984 & 24 March 1995**.
- **March–July 1984 (*Nature* & *PNAS*) — Scott, Weiner, McGinnis, Levine & Gehring discover the `Homeobox`:**
  - Working independently in **1983–1984**, **Matthew P. Scott & Amy J. Weiner** at Indiana University (*PNAS* July 1984) and **William McGinnis, Michael S. Levine, Ernst Hafen, Atsushi Kuroiwa & Walter J. Gehring** in Basel (*Nature* **29 March 1984** & *Cell* **July 1984**) sequenced the fruit fly's `Antennapedia`, `Ultrabithorax`, and `fushi tarazu` genes.
  - Inside every single one of those master architect genes sat the **exact same 180-base-pair DNA sequence (`the Homeobox`)**, encoding a **60-amino-acid protein key (`the Homeodomain`)** that binds to DNA and switches hundreds of downstream builder genes on or off!
  - Then McGinnis and Gehring took a radioactive `Homeobox` probe from a **fruit fly** and tested it against the DNA of **earthworms, frogs (*Xenopus*), chickens, mice, and humans** (*Cell*, July 1984) — and the probe lit up across every single animal! Humans carry **39 `HOX` genes** (arranged in four duplicated clusters, `HOXA–HOXD`), lined up in the **exact same head-to-tail colinear order** as the fruit fly's 8 `Hox` genes!
- **24 March 1995 (*Science*) — Walter Gehring's lab puts a mouse `Pax6` eye gene into a fruit fly's leg:**
  - How deep does this shared genetic toolkit go? For a century, evolutionary biologists taught that the **camera-lens eye of a human or mouse** and the **faceted compound eye of a fruit fly** evolved completely independently (`with zero shared genetic blueprint`).
  - On **24 March 1995** (*Science*), **Georg Halder, Patrick Callaerts, and Walter Gehring** in Basel took the **mouse master eye-control gene (`Small eye / Pax6`)** — which is mutated in humans born without irises (`aniridia`) and in **`eyeless`** fruit flies — and switched that **mouse gene** on inside the developing **leg, wing, and antenna** of a fruit fly!
  - Out of the fly's leg grew a complete, gleaming red **fruit-fly compound eye**! Why didn't it grow a furry mouse eye? Because **`Pax6` is the ancient master switch** that says *"Build an eye right here!"*, and the fly's leg cells obeyed that mouse command using their own fly eye-building subroutines — proving that **worms, flies, mice, and humans inherited the same master body-plan switches from a common ancestor over `500 million years ago`**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: William McGinnis (born 1952) — American molecular biologist · WHEN: 29 March 1984 · WHEN: July 1984 · WHEN: 24 March 1995 · FACT: Mouse gene builds a fly eye · DATA: inked McGinnis, Garber, Wirz, Kuroiwa & Gehring (July 1984, Cell 38: 675–680) cross-species Southern blot & Halder, Callaerts & Gehring (24 March 1995, Science 267: 1788–1792) Pax6/eyeless ectopic eye-induction chart on period paper showing the 180-bp homeobox (60-aa homeodomain with >75–90% sequence identity between Drosophila and human HOX) and mouse Pax6 cDNA inducing compound eyes on Drosophila legs, wings, and antennae · SCALE: swapping the mouse Pax6 gene into a fruit fly is like unplugging the "Turn on the headlights" dashboard switch from a lorry and plugging it into a motorbike — the switch itself is identical, and the motorbike turns on its own headlight`
- **Ends on:** If **Hans Driesch's identical twins (`Part 02`)** start from the **exact same fertilised egg** with **100% identical DNA letters** and the **exact same `Hox` body map**, why do identical twins grow up with **different fingerprints, different health, and different lifespans** — and why is **almost every calico cat on Earth female**?

### PART 05: Why Your Ten Fingers Aren't Ten Identical Thumbs — & Why Identical Twins Grow Up Different (6:45–8:15)
- **Cold open (the everyday question of why your five fingers on each hand aren't five identical copies when every finger bud contains the exact same genes — because your developing limb bud releases a chemical gradient of a morphogen called `Sonic hedgehog` (`discovered by Clifford Tabin, Philip Ingham & Andrew McMahon in December 1993`, named after the video-game character!) from the pinky-finger edge (`the ZPA`), so cells bathed in strong `Sonic hedgehog` become a pinky, cells in the middle become ring and middle fingers, and cells with zero `Sonic hedgehog` become a thumb!):** Every time you wave your hand, you are looking at **Hilde Mangold's Organizer** and **Walter Gehring's `Hox` genes** in action: in **December 1993** (*Cell*), **Clifford Tabin, Philip Ingham, and Andrew McMahon** proved that a tiny patch at the pinky-finger edge of your embryonic hand releases a chemical gradient called **`Sonic hedgehog` (`SHH`)** that switches on nested **`HOXD9–HOXD13`** genes — high concentration builds a pinky finger, medium builds middle fingers, and zero builds a thumb (`and if a mutation turns Sonic hedgehog on at the thumb side too, a person or Ernest Hemingway's famous Key West cats are born with extra polydactyl toes!`)!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - `Hox` genes explain how one fertilised egg builds a head, a heart, and ten fingers. But once that body is built, how do your cells **remember** which genes to keep switched on and which genes to lock away for the rest of your life — without changing a single letter of DNA (**`epigenetics`**)?
  - Why did British geneticist **Mary Lyon on 22 April 1961** look at the orange-and-black patches on a **female calico cat** and realise that every cell in a female mammal embryo randomly **shuts down and crumples one of its two X chromosomes (`X-inactivation`)**?

  - And how did **Randy Jirtle's 2003 yellow-to-brown Agouti mouse diet experiment** and **Manel Esteller's 2005 study of 80 pairs of identical twins** prove that what we eat and how we live physically rewrites the chemical switches on our DNA (`068` candidate *Why Do Identical Twins With 100% Identical DNA Grow Up Different (`Epigenetics, X-Chromosome Inactivation & DNA Methylation`)?*)?
