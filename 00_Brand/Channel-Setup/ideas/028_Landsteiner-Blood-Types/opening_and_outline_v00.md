### PART 02: Sheep's Blood, the 150-Year Ban, and the Clumping Mystery (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why Harvey's circulation sparked a lethal experiment):** **London & Paris, 15 June 1667 – 1829**. As soon as **William Harvey** (`005`) proved in **1628** that blood circulates around the body like water in a closed plumbing system, physicians in London (**Richard Lower**, **1665–1667**) and Paris (**Jean-Baptiste Denys**, **15 June 1667**) asked an obvious question: if a patient bleeds nearly to death, why not top up their veins with blood from a gentle lamb or calf?
- On **15 June 1667**, Denys transfused about **9 ounces (`~250 mL`) of sheep's blood** into a teenage boy who had been bled repeatedly for a fever; because the dose was small, the boy survived!
- Yet in **December 1667**, after a second and third transfusion of calf's blood into **Antoine Mauroy**, the patient's pulse raced, his kidneys passed black urine (haemoglobin from burst red blood cells!), and he died. Within two years (**1668–1670**), the Paris Faculty of Medicine, the British Parliament/Royal Society, and the Vatican **banned blood transfusions outright**.
- **James Blundell's human-only revival (1818–1829):** Over a century later, appalled by watching new mothers bleed to death after childbirth, London obstetrician **James Blundell** (1790–1878) proved with dogs that blood **must never cross species**: only *human* blood can save a human (**first human-to-human transfusion 22 December 1818**, first successful survival **August 1825 / 1829**).
- Yet even using healthy human donors, **nearly 1 in 2 transfusions still ended in sudden, terrifying shock, clumped blood, and death**! By **1875**, German physiologist **Leonard Landois** showed that mixing animal and human blood causes red cells to clump (**agglutinate**) and burst — and when bacteriologists in the **1890s** saw one human's blood clump another human's blood, everyone assumed the clumping was caused by **germs or disease** (`001`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: James Blundell (1790–1878) — English obstetrician and physiologist · WHEN: 15 June 1667 · WHEN: 22 December 1818 · WHEN: 1875 · FACT: Animal blood banned in 1670 · DATA: inked timeline & survival chart on period paper comparing 1667 animal-to-human transfusions (banned 1668–1670), Blundell's 1818–1829 un-typed human-to-human transfusions (~50% random incompatibility / high mortality), and 1901 ABO-matched transfusions · SCALE: flipping a coin with a patient's life — before blood typing, nearly half of all human blood transfusions triggered a dangerous or fatal immune reaction`
- **Ends on:** Every doctor in Europe believed healthy human blood was identical from person to person, and that clumping only happened in sick patients. How did a thirty-one-year-old researcher in Vienna prove them wrong without touching a single patient?

### PART 03: Six Scientists' Blood in a 6 × 6 Grid (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open:** **University of Vienna, February 1900 – 14 November 1901**. Thirty-one-year-old Austrian pathologist **Karl Landsteiner** (1868–1943) doubted that blood clumping was a symptom of disease.
- Instead of testing hospital patients, Landsteiner drew blood from **six completely healthy people** in his own laboratory — five fellow scientists (**Dr St., Dr Plecnik, Dr Sturl, Dr Erdheim, and Zaritsch**) plus **himself**!
- Spinning each sample in a centrifuge, he separated the **red blood cells** at the bottom from the clear golden liquid (**blood serum / plasma**) at the top, then mixed every person's serum with every person's red cells in a **6 × 6 grid of 36 drops** (first noted in a **seven-line footnote in February 1900**, published in full on **14 November 1901** in *Wiener klinische Wochenschrift*).
- **The pattern that changed medicine:**
  - Nobody's serum ever clumped their *own* red cells;
  - Yet across the 36 squares, healthy blood fell into **three unmistakable groups**, which Landsteiner named **Group A**, **Group B**, and **Group C** (later renamed **Group O**, from German *Ohne*, meaning "without" / zero — and **Landsteiner's own blood was Group O**!). The fourth rarer group, **AB**, was found the next year (**1902**) by his students **Alfred von Decastello** and **Adriano Sturli**.
- **The mechanism (antigens and antibodies):**
  - **Type A** red cells carry **A antigens** on their surface, and their plasma carries **anti-B antibodies**;
  - **Type B** red cells carry **B antigens**, and their plasma carries **anti-A antibodies**;
  - **Type AB** carries both A and B antigens and **neither antibody** (making AB the **universal recipient** for red cells);
  - **Type O** red cells carry **neither A nor B antigens** on their surface (so neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies can grab them — making Type O red cells the **universal donor**!).
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: February 1900 · WHEN: 14 November 1901 · WHEN: 1902 · FACT: First printed in a footnote · DATA: inked molecular diagram on period paper showing red blood cell surface antigens (A pegs, B spheres, AB both, O smooth) and Y-shaped plasma antibodies (anti-B, anti-A, none, both) demonstrating why Type O red cells pass unattacked as universal donor and Type AB accepts all red cells · SCALE: each red blood cell carries around 2 million microscopic A or B sugar markers on its skin — if the wrong antibody locks on, millions of cells glue together like Velcro`
- **Ends on:** Landsteiner had solved a 230-year medical mystery in a single 6 × 6 table! Case closed? **Wrong.** Why did almost no surgeon use Landsteiner's blood groups for another twelve years — and why did doctors still have to sew a living donor's wrist artery directly to a patient's vein?

### PART 04: The 6-Minute Clot Trap & the 0.2% Pinch of Salt — Midpoint Twist (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **New York, 8 March 1908 & January 1915, and the Western Front, November 1917**. Even after Landsteiner published his blood groups in **1901** (and **Reuben Ottenberg** at Mount Sinai Hospital in New York began cross-matching donor and patient blood in **1907–1913**, proving in **1913** that blood groups are inherited by **Gregor Mendel's laws**, `012`!), hospitals still could **not** put blood in a bottle!
- Why? Because healthy blood has a second defence mechanism: the moment blood leaves a blood vessel and touches glass or air, **calcium ions** trigger a cascade of clotting proteins (fibrin) that turns liquid blood into a **solid jelly clot in 4 to 6 minutes**!
- **Sewing artery to vein (1906–1908):** Because blood clotted in tubes almost instantly, surgeons like **George Crile** (`1906`) and **Alexis Carrel** (1873–1944; on **8 March 1908** in New York, saving a dying five-day-old infant by stitching the baby's father's radial wrist artery directly to the baby's popliteal leg vein with fine silk thread — winning the **1912 Nobel Prize**!) had to perform dangerous live artery-to-vein surgery with donor and patient lying side by side, unable even to measure how much blood flowed across!
- **The 1914–1915 sodium citrate breakthrough:** Scientists knew **sodium citrate** (the harmless salt of citric acid found in lemons) binds calcium ions and stops blood clotting in a test tube, but everyone assumed sodium citrate was too toxic to inject into human veins (~1% concentrations seemed dangerous).
- In **1914–January 1915** — worked out nearly simultaneously by **Albert Hustin** in Brussels (`March 1914`), **Luis Agote** in Buenos Aires (`9 November 1914`), and German-born surgeon **Richard Lewisohn** (1875–1961) at Mount Sinai Hospital in New York (**published 23 January 1915**) — Lewisohn measured the exact minimum dose: just **0.2% sodium citrate** (`2 grammes per litre` of blood) completely prevented clotting while remaining **100% safe for the patient**!
- **The first blood bank in ammunition boxes (November 1917):** Two years later, after **Peyton Rous** and **J. R. Turner** (`1916`) added dextrose sugar to feed the red cells, US Army Captain **Oswald Hope Robertson** (1886–1966) arrived on the **Western Front in November 1917** before the Battle of Cambrai, collected **Universal Donor (Group O)** blood in citrated glass bottles packed in sawdust and ice inside old ammunition boxes, and created the **world's first blood bank**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Reuben Ottenberg (1882–1959) — American physician and haematologist · WHEN: 8 March 1908 · WHO: Alexis Carrel (1873–1944) — French surgeon and biologist · WHEN: 9 November 1914 · WHEN: 23 January 1915 · WHO: Oswald Hope Robertson (1886–1966) — American physician and medical researcher · WHEN: November 1917 · FACT: First blood bank in ammo boxes · DATA: inked diagram on period paper showing calcium-driven fibrin clotting in untreated blood (4–6 min) vs 0.2% sodium citrate chelating Ca²⁺ ions + Rous-Turner dextrose solution allowing 14–26 days of cold storage in Robertson's 1917 Western Front icebox · SCALE: storing Type O blood on ice in 1917 turned transfusion from a two-surgeon emergency operation into a bottle waiting at the front line`
- **Ends on:** Landsteiner finally won the **Nobel Prize in 1930** — thirty years after his Vienna footnote! Yet why did some patients given perfectly matched `A`, `B`, or `O` blood *still* suffer mysterious reactions until Landsteiner made one last discovery at age seventy-one?

### PART 05: The Plus and Minus (`Rh`) & 118 Million Donations a Year (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** What do the **`+` and `−`** signs after your blood type (`O−`, `A+`) actually mean — and why do people think human blood types are unique to humans?
- In **1937–1940** at the Rockefeller Institute in New York, the 71-year-old **Karl Landsteiner** and **Alexander S. Wiener** (1907–1976) — alongside **Philip Levine** and **Rufus Stetson** (`1939`) — discovered a second critical antigen on red blood cells using antiserum raised against the blood of **Rhesus monkeys**: the **Rhesus (`Rh`) factor** (`Rh+` has the D antigen, found in ~85% of people; `Rh−` lacks it). Solving `Rh` incompatibility explained both the remaining transfusion reactions and **haemolytic disease of the newborn** (subsequently prevented in the 1960s by **Rho(D) immune globulin**, saving millions of babies)!
- Today, every year **118.5 million blood donations** are collected worldwide (WHO), and **Type O-negative (`O−`)** — lacking A, B, *and* Rh antigens — stands in every emergency room and ambulance as the true universal red-cell donor for trauma patients when there isn't even a minute to test their blood!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - From William Harvey's circulating blood (`005`) and Karl Landsteiner's blood groups (`028`) to Alan Turing's universal computer (`027`), twentieth-century science was ready for the digital age — except for one giant bottleneck: early computers ran on **thousands of hot, fragile glass vacuum tubes** that burned out like lightbulbs every few hours.
  - How did two physicists at Bell Labs on **16 December 1947** press a tiny wedge of **gold foil** onto a crystal of germanium and invent the **transistor** — only for their own boss to lock himself in a Chicago hotel room out of jealousy and launch Silicon Valley (`029` candidate *The Silicon Transistor & Microchip: Bardeen, Brattain, Shockley, Kilby & Noyce*)?
