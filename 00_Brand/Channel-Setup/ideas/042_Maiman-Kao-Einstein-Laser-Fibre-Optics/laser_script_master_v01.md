Why does ordinary light from a candle or a torch spread out and blur?

Because inside an ordinary lamp, trillions of atoms get hot, jump to a higher energy level, and drop back down at completely random moments — spitting out photons of different colours in every direction (**spontaneous emission**). It's like a crowd of people all shouting different notes at once.

Then, in **nineteen sixteen and nineteen seventeen** in Berlin, **Albert Einstein** discovered a third quantum rule of light. Imagine an atom that is *already* sitting on an upper energy rung. If a passing photon of the exact right wavelength brushes past that excited atom, it tickles the atom into dropping immediately and releasing a **second photon of the exact same wavelength, direction, and phase**!

Einstein called this **stimulated emission**. Suddenly, one photon becomes two identical twins marching in lockstep. Two become four, four become eight — a chain reaction of pure, synchronised light!

For thirty-six years, physicists thought Einstein's trick could never amplify a beam, because most atoms sit on the bottom rung and swallow light instead of multiplying it. Then, in **nineteen fifty-four** at Columbia University, physicist **Charles Townes** pumped ammonia gas so full of energy that more molecules sat on the top rung than the bottom — **population inversion** — amplifying invisible microwaves inside a metal box he called the **MASER**.

In **November nineteen fifty-seven**, Columbia graduate student **Gordon Gould** had a midnight flash of insight: what if you placed **two parallel mirrors** at the ends of a tube to bounce visible light back and forth through excited atoms? Gould wrote down the acronym **LASER** — *Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation* — and raced to a **Bronx candy store on the thirteenth of November nineteen fifty-seven** to have the shopkeeper stamp his notebook with a notary seal, a year before Charles Townes and Arthur Schawlow published their December nineteen fifty-eight paper on optical masers!

With the Pentagon, Bell Labs, and Columbia pouring millions of dollars into a race to build the first laser, why did the world's top experts insist that **Theodore Maiman's pink ruby crystal could never work**?

While giant government-funded teams at Bell Labs and Columbia struggled with fragile tubes of glowing gas, thirty-two-year-old physicist **Theodore Maiman** at Hughes Research Laboratories in Malibu bet everything on a sturdy crystal of **synthetic pink ruby** — aluminium oxide tinted with a trace of chromium.

Case closed? Wrong! **Arthur Schawlow** and **Charles Townes** publicly declared that pink ruby could **never** work as a laser!

Why? First, ruby is a "three-level" crystal, meaning Maiman had to blast **more than half of all its trillions of chromium atoms** into their excited state at the exact same instant. Second, a respected textbook measurement claimed pink ruby wasted ninety-nine percent of its energy as heat, with a dismal **one percent** light efficiency!

Instead of trusting the textbook, Maiman measured pink ruby in his own lab — and discovered the textbook had tested an impure stone! Pure pink ruby actually had a dazzling **seventy percent efficiency**!

To pump more than half the atoms at once without melting the crystal, Maiman skipped continuous lamps and bought a twenty-five-dollar **coiled xenon photographer's strobe flashlamp**. He polished the ends of a **one-centimetre by two-centimetre pink ruby cylinder** flat, coated them with silver mirrors, and slipped the ruby inside the lamp coil.

On the afternoon of the **sixteenth of May nineteen sixty**, Maiman and his assistant Irnee D'Haenens raised the voltage past nine hundred and fifty volts and fired the flash. Out of the fingertip-sized ruby shot a pure, deep-red beam at **six hundred and ninety-four point three nanometres** — **the first laser in human history**!

Amazingly, when Maiman sent his paper to *Physical Review Letters*, the editor **rejected it without review** as "just another maser paper," forcing Maiman to publish in *Nature* on the **sixth of August nineteen sixty**! Reviewers joked that the laser was *"a solution looking for a problem"* — yet just nine years later, on the **twenty-first of July nineteen sixty-nine**, **Apollo eleven** astronauts placed a mirror on the Moon so a ruby laser could measure the Moon's distance to within a few centimetres!

Maiman had a beam of light that could carry millions of phone calls at once. Yet when engineers tried to shoot that laser down a **glass fibre**, **ninety-nine percent of the light vanished after just twenty metres**! How did an engineer in England solve that?

Light travels in straight lines — so how can you make a laser beam bend around corners and follow the curve of the Earth?

Back in **eighteen forty-two** in Geneva, Swiss physicist **Daniel Colladon** drilled a hole in a water tank and shone a beam of sunlight straight into the curving jet of pouring water. Whenever the light rays hit the inside edge of the water stream at a shallow angle, they couldn't escape into the air — they bounced back inside the curving water like a hall of mirrors! That phenomenon is **total internal reflection**.

By the nineteen sixties, doctors used short bundles of glass threads to peer inside the human body. Yet when telecom engineers tried to shoot a laser down a glass fibre to carry telephone calls, they hit a wall: the finest optical glass on Earth had a light loss of **one thousand decibels per kilometre** — meaning **ninety-nine percent of the laser light vanished after just twenty metres**!

Then, in **January and July nineteen sixty-six** at Standard Telecommunication Laboratories in Harlow, England, thirty-two-year-old Shanghai-born engineer **Charles Kao** and colleague George Hockham published the paper that changed the modern world — winning Kao the **two thousand and nine Nobel Prize**!

Kao proved that silica glass itself wasn't blocking the light. Instead, **parts-per-million traces of rust — iron and copper ions — and water molecules** trapped inside the melted glass were swallowing the photons! Kao calculated that if glassmakers could purify fused quartz until light loss dropped from one thousand down to just **twenty decibels per kilometre**, hair-thin glass fibres could carry laser signals across countries and oceans!

When Charles Kao travelled the world asking glass companies to build that ultra-pure fibre, almost everyone called it impossible. Who took Kao's dare — and how did three physicists in upstate New York build the glass backbone of our internet?

When Charles Kao claimed in nineteen sixty-six that glass could be made five thousand times clearer than a camera lens, critics scoffed that no furnace on Earth could remove every speck of iron from melted sand. How did three researchers prove the skeptics wrong?

At **Corning Glass Works** in New York, physicists Robert Maurer, Donald Keck, and Peter Schultz skipped melting raw sand altogether — instead spraying ultra-pure **vapourised silicon gas** inside a quartz tube to build soot-free glass atom by atom! In **August nineteen seventy**, Donald Keck shone a red laser down their newest hair-thin fibre, saw a bright red flash burst out the far end at **seventeen decibels per kilometre**, and wrote ***"Whoopee!"*** in his lab notebook! By **nineteen seventy-nine**, engineers pushed loss down to **zero point two decibels per kilometre**, and in **nineteen eighty-seven** added **erbium-doped fibre amplifiers** that use Einstein's stimulated emission to boost fading laser pulses right under the sea! Today, more than **one point four million kilometres** of undersea glass fibres carry **ninety-nine percent of all intercontinental internet traffic**!

Charles Kao transformed our world by proving that an invisible **contaminant** hiding inside seemingly clean glass was choking the signal. What about an invisible killer hiding inside seemingly crystal-clear **drinking water** in central London in **September eighteen fifty-four** — a plague that killed **six hundred and sixteen people inside ten days** on a single street while every medical authority blamed foul air? How did one quiet doctor named **John Snow** walk the streets of Soho with an ink pen and a map, solve the riddle of why the men in the local **brewery** never got sick, and convince officials to **unbolt a single iron pump handle on Broad Street**?

An equation in Berlin, a pink ruby in a camera flash, and a thread of pure glass under the sea. Together, they turned a single twin particle of light into the invisible web that connects our world.
