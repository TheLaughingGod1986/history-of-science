When cholera first invaded Britain in **eighteen thirty-one** and **eighteen forty-nine** — draining terrified patients of litres of fluid within hours — London's powerful Sanitary Commissioner, **Edwin Chadwick**, and the medical journal *The Lancet* were certain they knew the cause: **"miasma"**, foul-smelling air rising from rotting rubbish and cesspools.

Tragically, believing bad smells were the enemy, Chadwick ordered London's cesspools flushed straight into the **River Thames** — the very river from which water companies pumped London's drinking water!

One quiet Yorkshire-born doctor saw the flaw in the miasma myth. Forty-one-year-old **John Snow** — already famous for giving Queen Victoria chloroform during childbirth in **eighteen fifty-three** — pointed out a simple medical truth in **eighteen forty-nine**: if cholera were breathed in through the air, it would attack the **lungs** first. Instead, cholera always attacked the **gut** first — which meant victims were **swallowing** the poison!

In **eighteen fifty-three and eighteen fifty-four**, Snow discovered a natural experiment across **three hundred thousand people** in South London. Down the exact same streets ran the underground pipes of two rival water companies, supplying rich and poor houses side by side:
First, the **Southwark and Vauxhall Company** pumped filthy water straight from the sewage-choked Thames at Battersea.
Second, the **Lambeth Company** had moved its intake pipe upstream in **eighteen fifty-two** to clean water at Thames Ditton!

Walking from door to door and testing kitchen tap water with silver nitrate when tenants didn't know their supplier, Snow tallied the first four weeks of the eighteen fifty-four outbreak: out of **forty thousand and forty-six** homes on Southwark and Vauxhall water, **one thousand two hundred and sixty-three** people died — **three hundred and fifteen per ten thousand houses** — while next-door neighbours on clean Lambeth water suffered just **thirty-seven deaths per ten thousand houses**, more than **eight times lower**!

While Snow was still going door to door in South London, on the night of the **thirty-first of August eighteen fifty-four**, the deadliest single-street outbreak in British history erupted in **Soho**. What did Snow find when he walked to the **Broad Street pump**?

When cholera exploded around Broad Street in Soho on the **thirty-first of August eighteen fifty-four** — eventually killing **six hundred and sixteen people** inside a few hundred yards — John Snow rushed to the **Broad Street water pump** on the **third of September** and drew a glass of water.

Case closed? Wrong! To Snow's astonishment, the water from the Broad Street pump looked **sparkling clear**!

Refusing to be fooled by appearances, Snow got the addresses of the first **eighty-three Soho victims** from Registrar-General **William Farr** and paced the streets on foot. He discovered that **seventy-three of the eighty-three dead** lived closer to the Broad Street pump than to any other well in London!

Even better, Snow solved three baffling riddles that miasma believers could never explain:
First, right next to the pump stood the crowded **Poland Street Workhouse** with **five hundred and thirty-five inmates** — yet only **five** died! Why? Because the workhouse had its **own private well**!
Second, yards from the pump stood the **Lion Brewery** with **seventy workers** — and **zero died**! Why? Because the brewers were given free **malt beer** and never touched the pump's water!
Third, four miles away in high, clean **Hampstead**, fifty-nine-year-old widow **Susannah Eley** and her niece suddenly died of cholera — because Mrs Eley loved the fizzy taste of Broad Street water so much that her sons had a **large bottle of it delivered by cart on the thirty-first of August**!

On the evening of the **seventh of September eighteen fifty-four**, Snow presented his evidence to the St James's Parish Board of Guardians. The next morning, **the eighth of September**, workmen **unbolted the iron handle from the Broad Street pump**!

Yet even after the outbreak stopped, London's Board of Health *still* rejected Snow's theory! Why? Because nobody knew *how* cholera had got into a deep brick well — until a skeptical Soho priest set out to prove John Snow wrong!

Twenty-nine-year-old Soho curate **Reverend Henry Whitehead** knew every family on Broad Street — and at first, he was sure John Snow was wrong! Determined to find the truth, Whitehead spent six months combing through the parish death register.

In **April eighteen fifty-five**, Whitehead found the missing piece of the puzzle right behind the pump at **number forty Broad Street**!

Three days *before* the main outbreak began, on the **twenty-eighth of August eighteen fifty-four**, five-month-old baby **Frances Lewis** at number forty had fallen ill with severe diarrhoea before dying on the second of September. Whitehead interviewed the baby's mother, Sarah Lewis: for four days, she had washed the baby's soiled nappies in pails of water and poured them into the **brick cesspool** in the front cellar of number forty!

At Whitehead's urging, parish surveyor Jehoshaphat York dug up the street in **April eighteen fifty-five** — and discovered that the crumbling brick cesspool of number forty Broad Street sat just **two feet eight inches — eighty-one centimetres — from the brick wall of the Broad Street well**, leaking waste straight into the neighbourhood's drinking water! Whitehead immediately stood up and declared that John Snow was right!

And what was the microscopic killer hiding in that water? That exact same summer of **eighteen fifty-four** in Florence, Italian anatomist **Filippo Pacini** peered through a microscope at cholera tissue and spotted millions of curved, comma-shaped bacteria he named ***Vibrio cholerae*** — before German bacteriologist **Robert Koch** isolated and grew the **comma bacillus** in **August eighteen eighty-three** in Egypt and **January eighteen eighty-four** in India!

John Snow died of a stroke in **June eighteen fifty-eight** at just forty-five — the very week the "Great Stink" of the Thames hit Parliament. How did Snow's map and London's new sewers save millions of lives around the world?

For years, miasma believers insisted that bad smells alone spread cholera. How was that myth buried forever?

After the **June eighteen fifty-eight "Great Stink"** forced Parliament to act, engineer Joseph Bazalgette built **eighty-two miles** of giant brick intercepting sewers across London. Wherever Bazalgette's sewers and clean water pipes reached, cholera vanished — until **July eighteen sixty-six**, when cholera suddenly killed four thousand people in **East London**! Using John Snow's exact method, former skeptic **William Farr** traced the outbreak within days to a single water company that had illegally pumped uncleaned water from the River Lea! Together with clean water treatment and simple salt-and-sugar rehydration — which cut treated cholera deaths from **over fifty percent to under one percent** — John Snow's street map gave birth to **modern epidemiology**!

John Snow and Henry Whitehead dug three feet beneath a London street to uncover a hidden truth under our feet. How did Croatian meteorologist **Andrija Mohorovičić on the eighth of October nineteen oh nine**, German seismologist **Beno Gutenberg in nineteen fourteen**, and Danish mathematician **Inge Lehmann in nineteen thirty-six** use the shockwaves of distant **earthquakes** like a planet-sized X-ray machine to discover a churning ocean of liquid iron — and a **solid metal ball** — sitting at the centre of the Earth?

Two competing water pipes, seventy healthy brewery men, and an iron pump handle on Broad Street. Together, they proved how invisible germs travel — and made every glass of clean tap water possible.
