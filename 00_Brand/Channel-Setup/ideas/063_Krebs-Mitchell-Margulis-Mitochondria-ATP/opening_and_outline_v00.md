### PART 02: June 1937 — Pigeon Breast Muscle, the *Nature* Rejection Letter & the Spinning Krebs Cycle (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why when you lose weight, 84% of the fat you burn is breathed out of your nose and mouth as invisible carbon dioxide gas — and why *Nature* rejected the paper that explained how your cells do it):** **Department of Pharmacology, University of Sheffield, England, 10 June 1937**.
- **From Nazi Germany to Sheffield, and Albert Szent-Györgyi's 4-carbon clue:**
  - Dismissed from his hospital post in Freiburg by the Nazi regime in April 1933 (`packing his Warburg manometers into crates to start over in England`), **Hans Adolf Krebs** (1900–1981) had already co-discovered the **Urea Cycle** (`1932` with Kurt Henseleit) — the very first metabolic cycle in biology.
  - Now at Sheffield with graduate student **William Arthur Johnson** (1913–1994), Krebs tackled a bigger mystery: after **glycolysis** splits a 6-carbon glucose sugar in half into 3-carbon pyruvate (`and a 2-carbon acetyl group`), how do cells burn that carbon with oxygen (`017`) at **`37 °C`** without bursting into flame?
  - Using finely minced **pigeon breast muscle** (`because a flying pigeon's chest muscle is packed solid with mitochondria that burn oxygen at a furious rate`), **Albert Szent-Györgyi (`036`, `062`)** had shown that four-carbon acids (`succinate, fumarate, malate, oxaloacetate`) mysteriously speed up oxygen consumption, while **Carl Martius** and **Franz Knoop** (`March 1937`) traced how 6-carbon **citric acid** breaks down into 4-carbon oxaloacetate.
- **June 1937 — Closing the circle & *Nature*'s famous mistake:**
  - In **June 1937**, Krebs and Johnson found the missing link: when they added **oxaloacetate (`4 carbons`)** and **pyruvate (`2 carbons`)** to pigeon breast muscle, the tissue synthesized **citric acid (`6 carbons`)**!
  - Instead of burning food in a straight line, the cell joins the 2-carbon fuel onto the 4-carbon carrier to make 6-carbon citric acid, then spins it around an **8-step wheel (`The Citric Acid Cycle` or `Krebs Cycle`)**, snipping off **two molecules of `CO₂` (`which you exhale!`)**, regenerating the 4-carbon oxaloacetate for the next turn, and loading high-energy electrons onto **`NADH` and `FADH₂`**!
  - On **10 June 1937**, Krebs mailed his breakthrough to *Nature*. Five days later, the editor sent it back **rejected** (`complaining that Nature already had a backlog of letters`)! Krebs published it two months later in the Dutch journal *Enzymologia* (**August 1937**) — and when he won the **1953 Nobel Prize**, he kept *Nature*'s rejection letter as his favourite lesson for young scientists!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: April 1933 · WHEN: 10 June 1937 · WHEN: August 1937 · WHEN: 1953 · FACT: Rejected by Nature in June 1937 · DATA: inked 8-step Krebs Citric Acid Cycle diagram on period paper from Krebs & Johnson (August 1937, Enzymologia 4: 148–156) showing 2-carbon acetyl-CoA condensing with 4-carbon oxaloacetate to form 6-carbon citrate, then stepping through isocitrate, α-ketoglutarate, succinyl-CoA, succinate, fumarate, and malate to release 2 CO2 molecules and load 3 NADH, 1 FADH2, and 1 GTP/ATP per turn · SCALE: the Krebs cycle works like a waterwheel that never gets used up — a four-carbon bucket picks up a two-carbon piece of food, drops off two carbon dioxide molecules that you breathe out of your lungs, and comes right back around for the next load`
- **Ends on:** The **Krebs Cycle** itself makes only **two molecules of `ATP`** per glucose molecule — yet a whole cell makes **over thirty `ATP`** from that same glucose! How do the electrons loaded onto **`NADH`** inside the **mitochondrion** (`proven to house the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation by Eugene Kennedy and Albert Lehninger in 1948–1949`) manufacture the other **ninety percent of your body's energy**?

### PART 03: 8 July 1961 — The "Ox-Phos Wars" & Peter Mitchell's Country-Mansion Proton Dam (`Chemiosmosis`) — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)

- **The 20-year hunt for a ghost ("Compound X"):**
  - In **1948–1949**, **Eugene Kennedy** and **Albert Lehninger** proved that all the enzymes of the Krebs Cycle and **`oxidative phosphorylation`** live inside the bean-shaped organelles called **mitochondria** (`which Philip Siekevitz in July 1957 famously dubbed the "powerhouse of the cell"`).
  - For two decades, the biggest labs in biochemistry fought the **"Ox-Phos Wars"**, hunting for a high-energy phosphorylated chemical intermediate (`"Compound X"`) linking electron transport to `ATP`. None of them ever found it — because it didn't exist!
- **8 July 1961 (*Nature*) — Peter Mitchell's heresy & the Glynn House laboratory:**
  - On **8 July 1961** (*Nature*), iconoclastic British biochemist **Peter D. Mitchell** (1920–1992) proposed a hypothesis so radical that peers treated it as a joke: **Chemiosmosis**!
  - Driven out of Edinburgh by stomach ulcers (`032`) in 1963, Mitchell bought a derelict Regency manor house in Cornwall (**Glynn House**), restored it, and set up an independent two-person lab with his lifelong experimental partner **Jennifer Moyle** (1921–2016).
  - Together, Mitchell and Moyle proved how mitochondria actually work: as high-energy electrons from `NADH` cascade down four protein complexes in the **inner mitochondrial membrane (`the Electron Transport Chain`)** to oxygen (`forming water`), they pump **hydrogen ions (`protons, H⁺`)** across the membrane into the narrow intermembrane space!
  - That builds a **`150 to 180 millivolt` voltage and pH gradient (`proton-motive force`)** across a membrane just **`5 nanometres` thick** (`an electric field of ~30 million volts per metre!`). And in **1966** (**André Jagendorf's "acid-bath" chloroplast experiment**) and **1974** (**Efraim Racker and Walther Stoeckenius's artificial purple-membrane vesicles**), sceptics were forced to concede: a pure proton gradient makes `ATP` in pitch darkness with zero electron transport (`winning Mitchell the sole 1978 Nobel Prize in Chemistry`)!
