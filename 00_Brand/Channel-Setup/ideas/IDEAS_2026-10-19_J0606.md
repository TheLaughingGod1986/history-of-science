### 3. Why Do Our Cells Have a Built-In Countdown Clock That Makes Us Age (`Telomeres, the Hayflick Limit & Telomerase`)? Alexis Carrel's 1912 "Immortal" Chicken Heart Myth, Leonard Hayflick's December 1961 `50-Division` Limit, Alexey Olovnikov's 1971 Subway-Track Hypothesis, and Elizabeth Blackburn, Jack Szostak & Carol Greider's 1978–Christmas Day 1984 Discovery of Telomeres & Telomerase (recommended for 059)
- **Familiar thing:** the **plastic tips (`aglets`) at the ends of your shoelaces** — and the astonishing discovery that the tips of your 46 chromosomes carry repeating **6-letter DNA caps (`TTAGGG`, called `telomeres`)** that snip shorter every single time a cell divides, acting as a **molecular countdown clock (`40 to 60 divisions`)** that explains why our bodies age!
- **Person & room:** French surgeon and Nobel laureate **Alexis Carrel** (1873–1944) at the **Rockefeller Institute in New York on 17 January 1912**, keeping a dish of embryonic **chicken heart cells** alive in nutrient broth for **34 years (`until 1946`, celebrating its "birthday" in newspapers every year!)** and convincing every biologist that vertebrate cells are naturally immortal; young microbiologist **Leonard Hayflick** (1928–2024) and **Paul Moorhead** at the **Wistar Institute in Philadelphia in December 1961**, watching normal human cells suddenly stop dividing after **`~50` doublings (`the Hayflick Limit`)**; Russian theoretical biologist **Alexey Olovnikov** (1936–2022) standing in a **Moscow subway station in 1971**, realising that DNA polymerase is like a train engine that cannot copy the track directly beneath its own cab (`the end-replication problem`); and Australian-American biochemist **Elizabeth Blackburn** (born 1948), **Jack Szostak** (born 1952), and graduate student **Carol Greider** (born 1961) at **UC Berkeley on Christmas Day, 25 December 1984**, developing an X-ray film that revealed the immortal enzyme **`telomerase`** (`2009 Nobel Prize`)!
- **Date & twist:** After **Alexis Carrel's** famous **1912–1946** chicken-heart experiment convinced the world that isolated cells never grow old and that ageing must come from outside the cell, **Leonard Hayflick's** landmark paper in **1961** was initially **rejected by the *Journal of Experimental Medicine*** (`whose editor, Peyton Rous, wrote back that anyone claiming cells have a built-in division limit was simply bad at keeping cells clean!`). **Three-part twist:** (1) **December 1961 (*Experimental Cell Research*) — Leonard Hayflick's `50-division` countdown & the freeze-thaw memory experiment:** Mixing male cells (`40 divisions old`) with female cells (`10 divisions old`), Hayflick proved that the older male cells stopped dividing right on schedule while the younger female cells in the exact same dish kept thriving — and when he **froze cells in liquid nitrogen at division #20** and thawed them years later, they **"remembered" they were at #20** and divided only **30 more times** (`while Carrel's lab had unknowingly been adding fresh living cells every time they fed the dish unstrained chick-embryo juice!`)! (2) **1971 & 1978–1982 — Alexey Olovnikov's Moscow subway train & Elizabeth Blackburn and Jack Szostak's `TTGGGG` pond-scum shoelace caps (*Cell*):** Hearing a lecture on Hayflick's limit in **1971**, **Alexey Olovnikov** stepped into a Moscow metro station and realised why: the enzyme that copies DNA (`DNA polymerase`) needs an RNA primer to start, so it **cannot copy the very tip of a linear chromosome** — meaning every division shaves **`50 to 200 letters`** off the end! In **March 1978** (*J. Mol. Biol.*) and **1982** (*Cell*), **Elizabeth Blackburn** and **Jack Szostak** sequenced the chromosome tips (`telomeres`, named by Hermann Muller in 1938) of pond scum (*Tetrahymena thermophila*) and proved that stitching pond-scum **`TTGGGG` repeats** onto yeast chromosomes protected them across evolutionary kingdoms (`with human telomeres found in 1988 by Robert Moyzis to be thousands of repeats of TTAGGG — ~10,000 to 15,000 base pairs at birth, shrinking to ~4,000–5,000 in old age`)!
