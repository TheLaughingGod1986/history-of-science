### PART 02: August–October 1929 & 1941–1942 — The `ATP` Battery & Albert Szent-Györgyi's Living Muscle Threads (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why for over a century scientists thought muscles ran like little steam engines burning lactic acid, until two laboratories in 1929 found the universal rechargeable battery of every living cell on Earth):** **Kaiser Wilhelm Institute, Heidelberg, August 1929; Harvard Medical School, October 1929; & University of Szeged, Hungary, 1941–1942**.
- **August–October 1929 & 1939–1941 — Karl Lohmann, Cyrus Fiske & Yellapragada SubbaRow discover `ATP`:**
  - In **August 1929** (*Die Naturwissenschaften*), German biochemist **Karl Lohmann** (1898–1978) — and independently in **October 1929** (*Science*), Indian biochemist **Yellapragada SubbaRow** (1895–1948) and **Cyrus Fiske** (1890–1978) at Harvard Medical School — isolated a phosphorus-rich molecule from muscle tissue: **adenosine triphosphate (`ATP`)**, carrying a chain of **three negatively charged phosphate groups** coiled together like a compressed spring (`which Fritz Lipmann in 1941 named the universal "high-energy phosphate bond ~P" of life`).
  - Then, in **1939** (*Nature*), married Soviet biochemists **Vladimir Engelhardt** (1894–1984) and **Militsa Lyubimova** (1898–1975) in Moscow discovered a shock: the structural protein of muscle — **`myosin`** — is **itself the enzyme (`ATPase`)** that snaps the third phosphate off `ATP`!
- **1941–1942 — Albert Szent-Györgyi & Brunó Straub bring lifeless muscle threads to life in Szeged:**
  - At the University of Szeged in Hungary during World War II (`1941–1942`), Nobel laureate **Albert Szent-Györgyi** (1893–1986, who had discovered Vitamin C in paprika, `036`) and his young colleague **Brunó Ferenc Straub** (1914–1996) discovered that muscle contains **two** partner proteins: thick **`myosin`** and a brand-new thin protein Straub purified and named **`actin`**!
  - When Szent-Györgyi mixed `actin` and `myosin` into artificial threads (`actomyosin`) in a dish and added a drop of boiled muscle juice (**`ATP`**), the lifeless protein threads **violently contracted before his eyes**! Szent-Györgyi wrote: *"To see them contract for the first time, was the most exciting experience of my scientific career."*
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Brunó F. Straub (1914–1996) — Hungarian biochemist · WHEN: August 1929 · WHEN: October 1929 · WHEN: 1939 · WHEN: 1941 · WHEN: 1942 · FACT: Lifeless threads contracted in a dish · DATA: inked ATP hydrolysis energy & Szent-Györgyi actomyosin contraction chart on period paper from Lohmann (August 1929, Naturwissenschaften 17: 624–625), Fiske & SubbaRow (October 1929, Science 70: 381–382), Engelhardt & Lyubimova (1939, Nature 144: 668–669), and Szent-Györgyi (1942) showing ATP splitting into ADP + Pi (~30.5 to 50 kJ/mol released) to drive actomyosin thread superprecipitation and contraction, with the human body holding ~50–100 g of ATP and recycling ~50–75 kg/day · SCALE: your body holds only about fifty grams of ATP at any one moment, yet because every molecule is discharged and recharged nearly a thousand times a day, you burn through your own body weight in ATP every twenty-four hours`
- **Ends on:** Szent-Györgyi proved that **`actin`, `myosin`, and `ATP`** are the only three ingredients needed to make a muscle move. Yet everyone — including Szent-Györgyi and Linus Pauling — still assumed that `ATP` made the protein chains **coil up and shrink like rubber bands**. How did **two unrelated Englishmen both named Huxley** prove on the **exact same day in 1954** that the proteins **never shrink at all**?

### PART 03: 22 May 1954 — Two Unrelated Huxleys Publish Back-to-Back in *Nature* & Prove the Sliding Filament Theory — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — when a muscle contracts, its long protein molecules must fold up and shrink like tiny coil springs! Wrong! Neither the thick fibres nor the thin fibres get a single nanometre shorter — they slide past each other like interlocking fingers!"):** **MIT, Cambridge, Massachusetts & University of Cambridge, England, Saturday, 22 May 1954**.
- **Two Huxleys, two microscopes, one breakthrough:**
  - Under a microscope, skeletal and heart muscles are striped (`striated`), made of repeating units called **sarcomeres** (`each ~2.2 micrometres long`), with **dark `A-bands`** in the middle and **light `I-bands`** on the sides.
  - At **MIT** in **1953–1954**, British biophysicist **Hugh E. Huxley** (1924–2013) and zoologist **Jean Hanson** (1919–1973) used **X-ray diffraction and the new electron microscope** (`and selectively dissolved myosin out of muscle fibrils`) to prove that the **dark `A-band`** is a bundle of **thick `myosin` filaments (`1.6 micrometres long`)**, while the **light `I-band`** is an overlapping array of **thin `actin` filaments (`1.0 micrometre long`)**!
  - Meanwhile, across the Atlantic at **Cambridge University**, **Andrew F. Huxley** (1917–2012 — grandson of Thomas Henry Huxley and winner of the 1963 Nobel Prize for nerve impulses (`033`), though completely unrelated to Hugh Huxley!) and German physician **Rolf Niedergerke** (1921–2011) built a custom **interference microscope** to watch **living single muscle fibres** contract in real time!
- **Saturday, 22 May 1954 — Back-to-back in *Nature*:**
  - Both teams discovered the exact same shock: as a muscle contracts, **the dark `A-band` (`the thick myosin filaments`) stays 100% constant in length**!
  - Instead of coiling or shrinking, the **thin `actin` filaments slide into the spaces between the thick `myosin` filaments**, narrowing the light `I-band` and central `H-zone`!
  - Hearing of each other's findings, the two Huxleys agreed to publish **side-by-side in the same issue of *Nature* on 22 May 1954** — establishing the **Sliding Filament Theory**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1953 · WHEN: 22 May 1954 · FACT: 22 May 1954: two Huxleys, one issue · DATA: inked Andrew Huxley & Niedergerke (22 May 1954, Nature 173: 971–973) and Hugh Huxley & Hanson (22 May 1954, Nature 173: 973–976) sarcomere striation measurement chart on period paper plotting A-band width (constant at ~1.6 micrometres across all sarcomere lengths from 3.0 down to 1.6 micrometres) vs I-band and H-zone widths decreasing linearly as thin actin filaments slide past thick myosin filaments · SCALE: if you interlace the fingers of your left and right hands and slide your palms together, your overall span gets shorter even though not a single finger changed length — and that is how every muscle in your body contracts`
- **Ends on:** If the filaments don't shrink like springs, **what invisible force pulls the `actin` ropes past the `myosin` rods** — and why do muscles lock stiff in **rigor mortis** when **`ATP` runs out**?

### PART 04: 1957–1971 — Walking Molecular Hands (`Cross-Bridges`), the Rigor Mortis Paradox & How Calcium Pulls the Trigger (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (why `ATP` isn't what makes a muscle hand grab the rope — `ATP` is what lets the hand LET GO!):** **University College London & MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, Cambridge, 1957–1969 & University of Chicago, 1971**.
- **1957–1971 — Hugh Huxley's electron micrographs of walking cross-bridges & the Lymn-Taylor cycle:**
  - In **1957** (*J. Biophys. Biochem. Cytol.*) and **20 June 1969** (*Science*), **Hugh Huxley** captured ultra-thin electron micrographs showing **tiny side-arms (`cross-bridges` — the globular heads of `myosin`)** reaching out from the thick filaments to grab the thin `actin` ropes!
  - In **December 1971** (*Biochemistry*), **Richard W. Lymn** and **Edwin W. Taylor** (1929–2021) solved the four-step chemical cycle that couples `ATP` to those swinging heads:
    1. **The Rigor Paradox:** When a `myosin` head has **no `ATP`**, it locks **tightly** onto the `actin` rope (`which is why after death, when cells stop making ATP, every muscle locks stiff in rigor mortis!`).
    2. **Release:** A fresh **`ATP` molecule** binds to the `myosin` head, causing the hand to **let go** of the `actin` rope!
    3. **Cocking the spring:** The `myosin` head splits `ATP` into **`ADP` and phosphate (`Pi`)**, cocking its neck back by ~10 nanometres.
    4. **The Power Stroke:** The head grabs a new spot on the `actin` rope, kicks out the phosphate (`Pi`), and snaps forward by **`~5 to 10 nanometres`** (`pulling with ~3 to 5 piconewtons of force`)!
- **Setsuro Ebashi's 1963–1965 `Troponin` & `Calcium` safety lock:**
  - And why aren't your muscles contracting non-stop as long as you have `ATP`? Japanese pharmacologist **Setsuro Ebashi** (1922–2006) in **1963–1965** (*Nature*) discovered that the `actin` rope is covered by a safety cable (**`tropomyosin` and `troponin`**) that blocks the `myosin` hands until an electrical nerve impulse (`033`) floods the muscle cell with **calcium ions (`Ca²⁺`)**, which unlock the rope in milliseconds!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Setsuro Ebashi (1922–2006) — Japanese pharmacologist · WHEN: 1957 · WHEN: 1963 · WHEN: 20 June 1969 · WHEN: December 1971 · FACT: No ATP locks muscles stiff · DATA: inked Hugh Huxley (1969, Science 164: 1356–1365), Lymn & Taylor (December 1971, Biochemistry 10: 4617–4624), and Ebashi (1963–1965, Nature) calcium-troponin switch & actomyosin cross-bridge cycle chart on period paper showing Ca2+ binding troponin to shift tropomyosin off actin binding sites, followed by ATP detachment, hydrolysis cocking, and Pi-release 10-nm power stroke · SCALE: counterintuitively, a muscle doesn't need fresh ATP to grab the rope — it needs fresh ATP to let go of the rope for the next pull, which is why running out of ATP after death locks muscles rigid in rigor mortis`
- **Ends on:** If your muscles burn through **sixty kilograms of `ATP` every single day**, **what microscopic power station inside your cells recharges all that `ATP` without setting your body on fire** — and why did *Nature* **reject Hans Krebs's 1937 paper** on how it works?
