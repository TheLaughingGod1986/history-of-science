### PART 02: December 1987 – February 2005 — Yoshizumi Ishino's Mystery Palindromes & Francisco Mojica's Viral Mugshot Discovery (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why would a simple bacterium waste precious space in its tiny DNA book repeating the exact same 29 letters over and over?):** **Osaka University, Japan, December 1987 & University of Alicante, Spain, 1993–February 2005**.
- **December 1987 — Yoshizumi Ishino spots the first clustered repeats in *E. coli*:**
  - While sequencing the `iap` gene in *Escherichia coli*, 30-year-old Japanese molecular biologist **Yoshizumi Ishino** (born 1957) noticed something bizarre at the end of the gene (**December 1987**, *Journal of Bacteriology*): **five identical 29-base-pair DNA sequences** that read like palindromes, separated by **32-base-pair "spacer" sequences** that looked completely different from one another.
  - Ishino wrote in his paper that *"the biological significance of these sequences is not known."*
- **1993–February 2005 — Francisco Mojica names `CRISPR` (`2002`) & discovers the spacers are stolen virus DNA (`2005`):**
  - Across the 1990s in **Alicante, Spain**, microbiologist **Francisco J. M. Mojica** (born 1963) found the exact same kind of repeating palindromes in salt-Loving archaea (*Haloferax mediterranei*) from the Santa Pola salt marshes and in 20 different microbe species. In **March 2002**, together with Dutch researcher **Ruud Jansen**, Mojica coined the name **`CRISPR`** (*Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats*), alongside nearby **`cas` (*CRISPR-associated*) genes** that looked like DNA-cutting enzymes.
  - Working with almost no grant money, Mojica manually typed each 32-letter spacer into global DNA databases using **BLAST**. Suddenly, in **1993–February 2005** (*Journal of Molecular Evolution*, published **February 2005** alongside independent papers by **Christine Pourcel** and **Alexander Bolotin**), Mojica got a hit: spacer after spacer was an **exact 100% match to deadly bacteriophage viruses** — and any bacterium carrying a virus's spacer was **immune to that virus**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Ruud Jansen (born 1962) — Dutch molecular biologist · WHEN: December 1987 · WHEN: 1993 · WHEN: March 2002 · WHEN: February 2005 · FACT: Viral mugshots in DNA · DATA: inked CRISPR locus architecture & viral-spacer match chart on period paper from Ishino et al. (Dec 1987, J. Bacteriol. 169: 5429–5433), Jansen et al. (Mar 2002, Mol. Microbiol. 43: 1565–1575), and Mojica et al. (Feb 2005, J. Mol. Evol. 60: 174–182) showing 29-bp palindromic repeats interspaced with 32-bp spacers matching bacteriophage and plasmid genomes across >40% of bacteria and ~90% of archaea · SCALE: for every single bacterium on Earth, there are ten bacteriophage viruses hunting it — so bacteria evolved a genetic mugshot book to remember every virus their ancestors survived`
- **Ends on:** Most biologists still doubted that humble single-celled bacteria could possess a smart, adaptive immune system. How did two food scientists trying to protect **vats of dairy yogurt** in **March 2007** prove Mojica right beyond a shadow of a doubt?

### PART 03: 23 March 2007 — How Yogurt Bacteria Proved `CRISPR` Is a Heritable Immune System — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Not until you watch a bacterium get attacked by a virus, snip out a piece of the invader's DNA, and paste it into its own genome!"):** **Danisco Dairy Starter Laboratories, Dangé-Saint-Romain, France & Madison, Wisconsin, 23 March 2007**.
- **The dairy industry's billion-dollar phage problem:**
  - Every carton of **yogurt** and wheel of **mozzarella or cheddar cheese** relies on starter bacteria like ***Streptococcus thermophilus*** to ferment milk sugar (`lactose`) into tangy lactic acid.
  - If a single **bacteriophage virus** sneaks into a factory vat of warm milk, it hijacks the bacteria, bursts them open, and ruins thousands of litres of yogurt in hours!
- **23 March 2007 — Rodolphe Barrangou & Philippe Horvath's yogurt experiment (*Science*):**
  - Studying Danisco's collection of dairy cultures (`which stretched back decades in company freezers!`), food scientists **Rodolphe Barrangou** (born 1974) and **Philippe Horvath** (born 1970), together with **Sylvain Moineau**, unleashed two deadly dairy phages (`phage 858` and `phage 2972`) onto yogurt bacteria (**23 March 2007**, *Science*).
  - Almost all the bacteria died — except a tiny handful of survivors. When Barrangou and Horvath sequenced the survivors' **`CRISPR` locus**, they saw the smoking gun:
    - Each survivor had **captured a brand-new 30-base-pair snippet (`spacer`) of the attacking virus's DNA** and **spliced it into the front end of its `CRISPR` array**!
    - Adding **1 viral spacer** reduced phage attack **100,000-fold**; adding **2 spacers** reduced it **100-million-fold**; and when they artificially deleted the new spacer (or disabled the **`cas9` gene**, then called *`cas5`*), the bacterium instantly **lost its immunity** (`and because the mugshot is written into the bacterium's own DNA, every daughter cell inherits immunity from birth — true Lamarckian inheritance!`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 23 March 2007 · FACT: Yogurt bacteria snip viruses · DATA: inked Streptococcus thermophilus phage-resistance & spacer-acquisition chart on period paper from Barrangou, Fremaux, Deveau, Richards, Boyaval, Moineau, Romero & Horvath (23 March 2007, Science 315: 1709–1712) showing phage sensitivity dropping 10^5-fold with 1 acquired 30-bp viral spacer and >10^7-fold with 2–4 spacers while cas9 (cas5) inactivation abolishes immunity · SCALE: adding just one thirty-letter viral mugshot to a yogurt bacterium's CRISPR archive cuts virus infection a hundred thousand times — and every daughter cell inherits that shield the moment it divides`
- **Ends on:** Yogurt bacteria proved how `CRISPR` works in nature. But how did **Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna** take a bacterial defence system and turn it into **programmable molecular scissors** that can edit **any gene in a plant, animal, or human**?

### PART 04: March 2011 – 28 June 2012 — Emmanuelle Charpentier, Jennifer Doudna & the Programmable `CRISPR-Cas9` Scissors (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (how a walk through the cobblestone streets of Old San Juan, Puerto Rico, united the two missing pieces of the `Cas9` puzzle):** **Umeå University, Sweden; San Juan, Puerto Rico; University of California, Berkeley; & University of Vienna, March 2011 – 28 June 2012**.
- **31 March 2011 — Emmanuelle Charpentier discovers the missing `tracrRNA` key (*Nature*):**
  - How does the **`Cas9`** enzyme know how to load its viral mugshot RNA (`crRNA`)? Studying *Streptococcus pyogenes*, French microbiologist **Emmanuelle Charpentier** (born 1968) and **Elitza Deltcheva** (**31 March 2011**, *Nature*) discovered a crucial second small RNA molecule — **`tracrRNA` (*trans-activating crRNA*)** — that base-pairs with `crRNA` to form a handle that `Cas9` can grip!
- **March 2011 – 28 June 2012 — The Puerto Rico meeting & the `sgRNA` breakthrough (*Science*):**
  - In **March 2011** at an ASM conference in **San Juan, Puerto Rico**, Charpentier met American RNA biochemist **Jennifer A. Doudna** (born 1964) at a café and walked through the old city, agreeing to team up to figure out how `Cas9` cuts DNA.
  - Working between Berkeley, Umeå, and Vienna, postdoc **Martin Jinek** (born 1979) and graduate student **Krzysztof Chylinski** (born 1984) proved that `Cas9` uses **two cutting blades (`HNH` and `RuvC` domains)** to slice both strands of target DNA right next to a 3-letter safety tag (**`PAM = NGG`**, discovered by **Horvath, Moineau & Alexander Bolotin in 2005–2008**, which stops the bacterium from cutting its own `CRISPR` archive!).
  - Then came the stroke of genius that changed biology forever (**published online 28 June 2012 in *Science***): Jinek, Chylinski, Doudna, and Charpentier **stitched `crRNA` and `tracrRNA` together with a 4-letter loop into a single synthetic molecule — the `single-guide RNA (sgRNA)`**!
  - Now, any scientist on Earth needed only **two ingredients**: the universal **`Cas9` protein** and a **custom 20-letter `sgRNA`** (`adapted to edit living human and mouse cells in January 2013 by Feng Zhang at MIT/Broad and George Church at Harvard, winning Charpentier and Doudna the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Martin Jinek (born 1979) — Czech biochemist · WHO: Feng Zhang (born 1981) — Chinese-American biochemist · WHO: George M. Church (born 1954) — American geneticist · WHEN: 31 March 2011 · WHEN: 28 June 2012 · WHEN: January 2013 · WHEN: 2020 · FACT: 2 blades + 1 guide RNA · DATA: inked dual-RNA vs chimeric single-guide RNA (sgRNA) Cas9 cleavage diagram on period paper from Deltcheva & Charpentier (31 Mar 2011 Nature 471: 602–607) and Jinek, Chylinski, Fonfara, Hauer, Doudna & Charpentier (28 Jun 2012 Science 337: 816–821) showing crRNA:tracrRNA fused into a single sgRNA with a customizable 20-nt 5' guide directing HNH and RuvC nuclease domains to cut both DNA strands 3 bp upstream of an NGG PAM · SCALE: the three-letter PAM sign acts like a safety catch on a pair of scissors — because the bacterium's own CRISPR mugshot album lacks that three-letter tag, Cas9 slices invading viruses to pieces without ever cutting its own DNA`
- **Ends on:** In **November and December 2023**, regulators in Britain and the United States approved the world's first **`CRISPR` medicine (`Casgevy`)** to cure **sickle-cell disease**, and in **2024** gene therapy began restoring hearing in children born deaf! That raises a marvellous question about our own senses: how does a **pea-sized snail shell (`cochlea`)** inside your skull turn invisible air vibrations into music and speech — and why do healthy living ears **emit their own sounds back out into the room**?

### PART 05: From Yogurt Vats to Curing Sickle-Cell Disease — & How Your Inner Ear Amplifies Sound (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (why basic curiosity about "useless" bacterial repeats in Osaka, Spanish salt marshes, and yogurt vats created a medical revolution):** When **Yoshizumi Ishino in 1987** and **Francisco Mojica in the 1990s** studied weird repeating letters in microbes, nobody imagined they were looking at a tool to cure human genetic disease. In **November–December 2023**, the first **`CRISPR-Cas9` therapy (`Casgevy / exagamglogene autotemcel`)** was approved to cure **sickle-cell disease and beta-thalassaemia** by editing a patient's own bone-marrow stem cells!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Today, genetic medicines are even being tested inside the human **inner ear** to restore congenital hearing.
  - Think about what is happening inside your head **at this very second** as you listen to my voice: invisible air ripples hit your eardrum, and inside the hardest bone in your skull sits a fluid-filled **snail shell (`the cochlea`)** the size of a garden pea.
  - How did **Hermann von Helmholtz in 1863** and Hungarian engineer **Georg von Békésy in 1928–1947** (sprinkling **glittering silver dust** onto cadaver inner ears under a strobe lamp!) discover a **travelling ocean wave** that detects vibrations **smaller than the width of a single hydrogen atom (`<0.1 nanometres`)** — and why did British physicist **David Kemp in July 1978** seal a microphone into a quiet human ear and discover that **healthy living ears literally ring back with their own sound echoes (`otoacoustic emissions`)** (`055` candidate *How Do Your Ears Turn Air Vibrations Into Music & Speech (and Why Do Living Ears Emit Their Own Sounds)?*)?
