Every gardener knew that the sensitive plant, *Mimosa pudica*, unfolds its leaves toward the Sun every morning and folds them shut at dusk. Everyone assumed the plant was simply reacting to daylight — until **seventeen twenty-nine**, when French astronomer **Jean-Jacques d'Ortous de Mairan** locked a *Mimosa* plant inside a **pitch-black cupboard** in Paris!

When he peeked inside at noon, the leaves were **wide open**; at midnight, they were **tightly shut**! Even after Swiss botanist **Augustin de Candolle** in **eighteen thirty-two** showed that under constant lamp light the plant's clock runs on a **twenty-two-to-twenty-three-hour cycle**, sceptics for two centuries insisted living things were merely sensing subtle daily changes in room warmth, air pressure, or cosmic rays.

How could you prove that **human beings** carry an internal clock (`which physician Franz Halberg in nineteen fifty-nine named "circadian", from the Latin circa diem, meaning "about a day"`)?

In **June nineteen sixty-two**, German physiologist **Jürgen Aschoff** sealed volunteers inside a soundproof, windowless **underground bunker** in Andechs, Bavaria, while in **July nineteen sixty-two** twenty-three-year-old French geologist **Michel Siffre** camped **one hundred and thirty metres underground** on a pitch-black alpine glacier for two months without a watch! Both experiments revealed a sensation: in total darkness, human sleep, body temperature, and hormones didn't fall into chaos or stay locked to twenty-four hours — they ticked like clockwork on a slightly longer internal day (`measured by Charles Czeisler at Harvard as an average human period of twenty-four hours and eleven minutes`)!

Yet in **nineteen seventy**, almost every biologist swore that a twenty-four-hour behaviour was far too complex to be controlled by a **single gene**. How did two scientists in California prove them wrong with **three mutant fruit flies**?

Case closed — a twenty-four-hour body clock must require hundreds of brain nerves wired together; no single gene could ever set the length of a day! Wrong! Mutate **one single gene** on the X chromosome, and you can speed the day up to **nineteen hours**, slow it down to **twenty-eight hours**, or stop the clock dead!

At the California Institute of Technology, physicist-turned-geneticist **Seymour Benzer** and his graduate student **Ronald Konopka** knew that normal fruit flies always hatch from their pupae at dawn — every twenty-four hours — even in complete darkness. What would happen if they mutated the flies' DNA one random gene at a time?

After screening two thousand fly families, in **September nineteen seventy-one** in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, Konopka and Benzer announced a biological bombshell! They had found **three bizarre mutant fly strains**:
- The first mutant woke up, rested, and hatched on a **fast nineteen-hour day**!
- The second mutant lived on a **slow twenty-eight-hour day**!
- And the third mutant had **no twenty-four-hour rhythm at all**!

When Konopka mapped where the three mutations sat on the chromosomes, every single one hit the **exact same gene on the X chromosome**, which they named **`Period` (`per`)**!

Konopka and Benzer had found the master clock gene. Yet in **nineteen seventy-one**, nobody had any idea what the **`Period`** gene actually did inside a cell! How can a tiny molecule of DNA and protein count out **twenty-four hours**?

How does a single gene build a twenty-four-hour timer? In **October nineteen eighty-four**, **Jeffrey Hall** and **Michael Rosbash** at Brandeis University and **Michael Young** at Rockefeller University cloned the **`Period`** gene — and between **February nineteen ninety** and **March nineteen ninety-four** (`winning the two thousand and seventeen Nobel Prize`), they cracked the secret!

Inside almost every cell in your body, the **`Period`** gene and its partner gene **`Timeless`** work like a **self-shutting molecular hourglass**:
- All day long, the genes are switched **ON**, and **`PER` and `TIM` proteins** trickle into the cell.
- Around midnight, when the pile of `PER` and `TIM` proteins gets deep enough, they lock arms, march into the cell nucleus, and **switch their own genes OFF**!
- By sunrise, the proteins have worn away, the genes switch back **ON**, and a brand-new twenty-four-hour day begins!

And how does morning sunlight reset your **`twenty-four-hour-and-eleven-minute`** clock every day? On the **eighth of February two thousand and two** in *Science*, neuroscientist **David Berson** and **Samer Hattar** discovered a hidden **third kind of light detector** in your eye: special nerve cells coated with a pigment called **`melanopsin`** that senses **four-hundred-and-eighty-nanometre sky-blue light** and wires straight to the **twenty thousand master clock neurons** at the base of your brain (`which is why staring at a blue phone screen at midnight tricks your brain into thinking the Sun just came up`)!

If every cell in your body has a twenty-four-hour clock that primes your organs and muscles each morning, **how do your muscles actually turn the food you eat into physical motion** — and how did **two scientists both named Huxley on the twenty-second of May nineteen fifty-four** prove that muscles contract via **billions of walking molecular hands**?

Many people still assume you have **only one body clock** inside your head. Why does flying across six time zones leave you wide awake at midnight and starving at three in the morning?

Because while morning blue light through your eyes quickly resets the master clock in your brain, the **billions of `PER` and `TIM` clocks inside your liver, stomach, and muscles** take a full week of daylight and mealtimes to catch up — leaving your brain in London while your liver is still in New York!

And when your body clock wakes you up each morning and you swing your legs out of bed, how do your **muscles** turn breakfast into physical force? For centuries, scientists assumed that contracting muscles coil up like tiny rubber springs — and nobody knew that your body manufactures and burns through **your entire body weight in a single rechargeable chemical battery called `ATP` every twenty-four hours**! How did **Karl Lohmann and Cyrus Fiske & Yellapragada SubbaRow in August nineteen twenty-nine** discover **`ATP`**?
