### PART 02: 1729 – 1962 — De Mairan's Dark Cupboard, Franz Halberg's "Circadian" Rhythm & Jürgen Aschoff's Underground Bunker (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why every gardener knew that certain flowers open their leaves at dawn and fold them up at dusk, assuming the plant was simply reacting to the warmth and light of the Sun):** **Paris, 1729; Scarasson Cavern, French Alps & Andechs Bunker, Bavaria, June–September 1962**.
- **1729 — Jean-Jacques d'Ortous de Mairan's cupboard experiment (*Histoire de l'Académie Royale des Sciences*):**
  - In **1729** in Paris, astronomer **Jean-Jacques d'Ortous de Mairan** (1678–1771) took a "sensitive plant" (*Mimosa pudica*, whose leaves fold up at night and open by day) and locked it inside a **pitch-black cupboard** where no sunlight could reach it.
  - When he peeked inside at noon, the leaves were **wide open**; at midnight, they were **tightly folded** — proving the plant "sensed the Sun without ever seeing it"!
  - Even after **Carl Linnaeus (1751)** designed a "floral clock" of flowers that open at each hour and **Augustin Pyramus de Candolle (1832)** showed that under constant light *Mimosa* runs on a **`22-to-23-hour` free-running cycle**, sceptics until the 1950s insisted organisms were merely picking up hidden daily clues ("Factor X") like barometric pressure (`039`), room warmth, or cosmic rays!
- **1959 & June–September 1962 — Franz Halberg coins "Circadian" & Jürgen Aschoff and Michel Siffre go underground (*Science*):**
  - In **1959**, physician **Franz Halberg** (1919–2013) coined the word **"circadian"** (`from the Latin circa diem, meaning "about a day"`).
  - To prove humans have an internal circadian clock, in **1962** German physiologist **Jürgen Aschoff** (1913–1998) and **Rütger Wever** built a windowless, soundproof, electromagnetically shielded **underground bunker in Andechs, Bavaria** (`published in Science, 15 June 1962`), while 23-year-old French geologist **Michel Siffre** (1939–2024) camped **130 metres underground on a glacier in the Scarasson cavern** for two months (**16 July – 14 September 1962**) with no watch and no daylight.
  - Both experiments proved the same astonishing fact: in total darkness and isolation, human sleep, wakefulness, body temperature, and hormone levels (`060`) **keep cycling with clockwork regularity on an internal day slightly longer than 24 hours (`~24.2 to 25.0 hours`, refined in June 1999 by Charles Czeisler at Harvard to an exact human average of `24 hours and 11 minutes ± 16 minutes`)**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Franz Halberg (1919–2013) — Romanian-American chronobiologist · WHO: Michel Siffre (1939–2024) — French geologist and speleologist · WHEN: 1729 · WHEN: 1832 · WHEN: 1959 · WHEN: 15 June 1962 · WHEN: 16 July 1962 · FACT: Human clock: 24 hours, 11 minutes · DATA: inked Aschoff (15 June 1962, Science 136: 963–966), Siffre (July–September 1962 Scarasson cave log), and Czeisler et al. (25 June 1999, Science 284: 2177–2181) human free-running actogram & body-temperature rhythm chart on period paper showing sleep-wake and core temperature cycles drifting slightly later each day in constant conditions with an intrinsic human period of 24.18 hours (24 h 11 min) · SCALE: when Michel Siffre climbed out of his pitch-black alpine cave on the fourteenth of September nineteen sixty-two after fifty-eight days without a watch, his internal body clock had drifted so steadily that he thought it was still the twentieth of August`
- **Ends on:** Aschoff and Siffre proved the human body has a built-in clock. Yet almost every biologist in **1970** swore that a 24-hour behaviour was far too complicated to be controlled by a **single gene**. How did a physicist-turned-geneticist and his graduate student at Caltech prove them wrong with **three mutant fruit flies**?

### PART 03: September 1971 — Seymour Benzer & Ronald Konopka's Mutant Fruit Flies (`The Period Gene`) — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — a 24-hour clock must take hundreds of brain nerves wired together; no single gene could ever set the length of a day! Wrong! Mutate one single gene on the X chromosome, and you can speed the clock up to 19 hours, slow it down to 28 hours, or stop it dead!"):** **California Institute of Technology (Caltech), Pasadena, September 1971**.
- **September 1971 (*PNAS*) — Ronald Konopka & Seymour Benzer find `Period` (`per`):**
  - At Caltech, physicist-turned-geneticist **Seymour Benzer** (1921–2007) and graduate student **Ronald J. Konopka** (1947–2015) fed fruit flies (*Drosophila melanogaster*) a chemical mutagen (`EMS`) and screened their offspring (`noting that normal fruit flies always hatch from their pupal cases at dawn — every 24 hours — even in complete darkness`).
  - After screening ~2,000 fly lines, in **September 1971** (*PNAS*) Konopka and Benzer announced a discovery that stunned biology: they had found **three different mutant fly strains**, and **all three mapped to the exact same single gene on the X chromosome**, which they named **`Period` (`per`)**:
    1. **`perS` (Short):** ticked on a fast **`19-hour` day**!
    2. **`perL` (Long):** ticked on a slow **`28-hour` day**!
    3. **`per0` (Arrhythmic):** had **no 24-hour rhythm at all** (`0 hours`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: September 1971 · FACT: One gene sets the 24-hour day · DATA: inked Konopka & Benzer (September 1971, PNAS 68: 2112–2116) Drosophila eclosion & locomotor activity rhythm chart on period paper comparing wild-type (24.0 h period), per^01 (arrhythmic), per^S (19.0 h period), and per^L1 (28.0 h period) all mapping to a single locus on the X chromosome · SCALE: changing a single genetic letter inside the Period gene was like turning the regulating screw on the back of a grandfather clock — speeding a fly's entire day up to nineteen hours or slowing it down to twenty-eight`
- **Ends on:** Konopka and Benzer found the clock gene (`Period`), but in **1971** nobody had any idea what the `Period` gene actually **did** inside a cell! How can a tiny molecule of DNA and protein count out **twenty-four hours**?

### PART 04: 1984–1994 & 2002 — Hall, Rosbash & Young Crack the `PER/TIM` Molecular Hourglass (`2017 Nobel Prize`) & How Blue Light Resets Your Brain (`Melanopsin`) (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (how three geneticists in Massachusetts and New York cloned the `Period` gene in October 1984 and discovered that every cell tells time using a self-shutting molecular hourglass):** **Brandeis University, Waltham & Rockefeller University, New York, October 1984 – 1994 & Brown University, February 2002**.
- **October 1984 – 1994 — Jeffrey Hall, Michael Rosbash & Michael Young solve the `PER/TIM` negative-feedback loop (`2017 Nobel Prize`):**
  - Working in friendly competition, **Jeffrey C. Hall** (born 1945) and **Michael Rosbash** (born 1944) at Brandeis University and **Michael W. Young** (born 1949) at Rockefeller University simultaneously **cloned and sequenced the `Period` (`per`) gene in October 1984** (*Cell* & *Nature*, and rescued rhythmicity in `per0` flies!).
  - In **1988–February 1990** (*Nature*, with **Paul Hardin**), Hall and Rosbash discovered that **`PER` protein** and its **`per` mRNA** rise and fall in a 24-hour wave — and proposed the **Transcription-Translation Negative Feedback Loop (`TTFL`)**:
    - By day, master switches (`CLOCK` and `CYCLE`/`BMAL1`, cloned in mice by **Joseph Takahashi** in **1994–1997**) switch the **`period`** gene **ON**.
    - All afternoon and evening, **`PER` protein** builds up in the cell's cytoplasm.
    - Why doesn't `PER` shut its gene off immediately in 5 minutes? In **March 1994** (*Science*) and **1998** (*Cell*), **Michael Young** discovered the two missing pieces of the hourglass: a partner protein called **`TIMELESS` (`TIM`)** that must bind `PER` to escort it into the cell nucleus around midnight, and an enzyme called **`DOUBLETIME` (`DBT` / `CK1ε`)** that delays `PER` accumulation so the whole cycle takes **exactly 24 hours** (`and in 2001 Ying-Hui Fu & Louis Ptáček proved that humans with familial advanced sleep phase syndrome — waking at 4:00 a.m. every day — carry a mutation in the human PER2 gene!`)!
- **February 2002 (*Science*) — Why blue light at night resets your brain (`Melanopsin` & the `SCN`):**
  - How does morning sunlight reset your **`24 h 11 min`** internal clock (`via the brain's 20,000-neuron master clock, the suprachiasmatic nucleus or SCN`) — and why could even some completely blind mice and humans without working rods or cones (`049`) still lock their clocks to daylight?
  - In **1998–8 February 2002** (*Science*, by **Ignacio Provencio**, **David Berson**, and **Samer Hattar**), scientists discovered a hidden **third type of photoreceptor cell** in the human retina (**intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells, `ipRGCs`**) coated with a blue-light pigment called **`melanopsin` (`peak sensitivity ~480 nanometres`, the exact colour of a clear blue morning sky — and of a smartphone screen at midnight!)** that wires straight to the brain's master clock and tells the pineal gland to shut off the sleep hormone **`melatonin`**!

Berson (born 1953) — American neuroscientist · WHEN: October 1984 · WHEN: February 1990 · WHEN: March 1994 · WHEN: 8 February 2002 · WHEN: 2017 · FACT: Blue light (480 nm) resets your clock · DATA: inked PER/TIM/DBT feedback loop & melanopsin 480-nm blue-light phase-resetting chart on period paper from Bargiello, Jackson & Young (October 1984, Nature 312: 752–754), Zehring, Wheeler, Reddy, Konopka, Kyriacou, Rosbash & Hall (December 1984, Cell 39: 369–376), Hardin, Hall & Rosbash (8 February 1990, Nature 343: 536–540), Vosshall, Price, Sehgal & Young (18 March 1994, Science 263: 1606–1609), and Berson, Dunn & Takao (8 February 2002, Science 295: 1070–1073) showing the 24-hour PER/TIM nuclear negative feedback loop and ~480-nm melanopsin ipRGC signaling to the 20,000-neuron SCN · SCALE: staring at a bright blue phone screen at midnight hits your retina with four-hundred-and-eighty-nanometre sky-blue light — fooling the twenty thousand clock neurons at the base of your brain into thinking dawn has just broken`
- **Ends on:** If every cell in your body has a 24-hour clock (`061`) that primes your organs and muscles for action each morning, **how do your muscles actually turn the food you eat into physical motion** — and how did **two scientists both named Huxley on 22 May 1954** prove that muscles contract via **billions of walking molecular hands**?

### PART 05: Why Jet Lag Happens When Your Organ Clocks Split Apart — & How Your Muscles Turn Food Into Motion (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (the misconception that you only have one clock in your brain — when jet lag feels so awful because your brain's eye-clock resets in a day or two while the molecular `PER` clocks inside your liver, gut, and muscles take a full week to catch up):** Why does flying across six time zones leave you hungry at 3:00 a.m. and exhausted at noon? Because while blue light through your eyes (`melanopsin`) rapidly resets the **20,000 neurons of your brain's master clock (`the SCN`)**, the **trillions of peripheral `PER/TIM` clocks inside your liver, pancreas, and digestive tract (`057`, `060`)** reset only by mealtime and hormone waves — shifting at roughly **one hour per day** until your organs are back in sync!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - When your circadian clock wakes your body up each morning, you swing your legs out of bed, stand up, and move.
  - For centuries after **Galvani's frog legs (`019`)**, almost every scientist assumed that when a muscle contracts, its protein fibres must coil up like tiny rubber springs.
  - How did **Karl Lohmann and Cyrus Fiske & Yellapragada SubbaRow in 1929** discover the universal chemical battery **`ATP`** (`which your body recycles your entire body weight of every single day!`)?
  - How did **Albert Szent-Györgyi in war-torn Hungary in 1942** make threads of rabbit muscle violently contract on a table with a single drop of `ATP`?
  - And how did two unrelated Englishmen both named **Huxley** — **Hugh Huxley & Jean Hanson** and **Andrew Huxley & Rolf Niedergerke** — publish **back-to-back papers in *Nature* on 22 May 1954** proving that muscles work by **billions of microscopic `myosin` hands hauling `actin` ropes (`The Sliding Filament Theory`)** (`062` candidate *How Do Your Muscles Turn Food Into Motion (`Muscles, ATP & the Sliding Filament Motor`)?*)?
