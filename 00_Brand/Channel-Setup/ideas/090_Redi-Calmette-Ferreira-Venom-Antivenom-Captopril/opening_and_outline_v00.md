### PART 02: 1664 — Francesco Redi's Medici Viper Experiments, the Wineglass of Venom & Why Poison Isn't Venom (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open ("For two thousand years, Europe's greatest authorities taught that a viper has no venom in its fangs at all — claiming the snake kills you because its angry spirit heats up the yellow bile in its gall bladder! In 1664 in Florence, Grand Duke Ferdinando II de' Medici ordered his court physician Francesco Redi to test every word of that ancient wisdom against living vipers — and what Redi saw changed toxicology forever."):** **Medici Court, Florence, 1664**.
- **1664 (*Osservazioni intorno alle vipere*) — How Francesco Redi dissected the hollow syringe fang and drank the venom:**
  - Studying dozens of Italian vipers (*Vipera aspis*), **Francesco Redi** (1626–1697) first tested the ancient gall-bladder myth: he smeared viper gall-bladder bile into open wounds on pigeons and dogs — and nothing happened!
  - Looking inside the viper's mouth, Redi traced the real culprit: two **hollow, foldable fangs** connected by ducts to **two almond-shaped glands behind the snake's eyes**, filled with a clear-yellowish liquid (`the first experimental proof of how a biological hypodermic needle works, two centuries before doctors invented metal syringes!`). Even when a viper was dead and cold, pricking a pigeon's leg with a broom straw dipped in that yellow fluid killed the bird in minutes!
  - **The Wineglass Twist (`Venom vs Poison`):** What if that deadly yellow liquid was **swallowed** instead of injected? To the gasps of the Medici courtiers, Redi fed bread soaked in viper venom to dogs and pigeons — and his bold viper-handler **Jacopo Sozzi** drank a spoonful of pure viper venom dissolved in a glass of wine! He felt **zero harm**. Why? Because snake venoms are complex **proteins and peptides** (`070`): injected into blood (`005`), they stay intact to attack nerves (`033`) or blood clotting (`082`), whereas swallowed into a healthy stomach (`with no cuts or ulcers`), hydrochloric acid and digestive enzymes (`pepsin and trypsin, 077`) chop the protein chains into harmless amino acids!

- **Ends on:** Redi proved how snake venom enters the blood — yet for another **230 years**, if a cobra in India or a viper in France drove its fangs into your leg, no medicine on Earth could neutralize the venom once it was inside your veins! How did a **monsoon flood of cobras in Saigon** and **two rival teams in Paris** invent **`antivenom`** on **10 February 1894**?

### PART 03: 10 February 1894 — The Saigon Cobra Flood, Horse Serum & the Same-Day Paris Discovery of `Antivenom` — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)

- **1890–1894 — From Behring's Diphtheria Antitoxin (`065`) to Cobra & Viper `Antivenom`:**
  - In **December 1890**, **Emil von Behring** and **Shibasaburo Kitasato** (`065`) proved that animals injected with weakened bacterial toxins (`diphtheria and tetanus`) make blood antibodies (`antitoxins`). In **1887**, Henry Sewall in Michigan had shown pigeons could slowly build tolerance to rattlesnake venom.
  - In **October 1891** at the new Pasteur Institute in Saigon, 28-year-old naval physician **Albert Calmette** (1863–1933) received a barrel of live **monocled cobras (*Naja kaouthia*)** caught after a village flood killed four people. Milking their fangs onto watch glasses, Calmette discovered that heating cobra venom to `70–80 °C` or mixing it with calcium hypochlorite (`bleach`) weakened its toxicity without destroying its ability to train the immune system!
  - **The Midpoint Twist — 10 February 1894 (*Comptes Rendus de la Société de Biologie*):** Unknown to each other, **two rival French teams** raced to the finish line and read their papers at the **exact same meeting of the Société de Biologie in Paris on 10 February 1894**:
    1. **Césaire Phisalix** (1852–1906) and **Gabriel Bertrand** (1867–1962) at the National Museum of Natural History proved that the blood serum of guinea pigs immunized with heat-treated European viper venom neutralized lethal viper venom in other animals (`pp. 111–113`);

### PART 04: 1949–1977 — Maurício Rocha e Silva, Sérgio Ferreira's Pocket Vial of Viper Peptide & the Design of `Captopril` (`ACE Inhibitors`) (4:50–6:45)
- **Cold open (how did a flask of dog blood mixed with Brazilian pit-viper venom in 1949, a tiny vial carried in a young Brazilian scientist's coat pocket to London in 1965, and a molecular trick at a New Jersey lab in 1975 create the blood-pressure pills that have saved millions of lives?):** **São Paulo & Ribeirão Preto, Brazil (1949–1965), London (1965–1968) & Princeton, New Jersey (1974–1977)**.
- **1949 (*Am. J. Physiol.*) & 1965 (*Br. J. Pharmacol.*) — `Bradykinin` & Sérgio Ferreira's `Bradykinin Potentiating Factor (BPF)`:**
  - In **1949** in São Paulo, **Maurício Rocha e Silva** (1910–1983), **Wilson Teixeira Beraldo**, and **Gastão Rosenfeld** mixed venom from the Brazilian arrowhead pit viper (*Bothrops jararaca*) with blood plasma and discovered it released a natural human hormone that strongly dilates blood vessels and lowers blood pressure, naming it **`bradykinin`**.
  - Normally, your body destroys bradykinin in seconds inside the lungs (`074`). Yet in **1965** at Ribeirão Preto, Rocha e Silva's young colleague **Sérgio Henrique Ferreira** (1934–2016) discovered that the *jararaca* viper's venom ALSO contains a tiny peptide cocktail he named **`Bradykinin Potentiating Factor (BPF)`** — which stops the body from breaking down bradykinin so the victim's blood pressure crashes!
  - Carrying a freeze-dried vial of `BPF` in his pocket to **John Vane's (`041`)** laboratory at the Royal College of Surgeons in London (`1965–1968`), Ferreira, Vane, and **akhil Bakhle** discovered the jackpot: the viper's peptide (`especially a 9-amino-acid peptide called teprotide, BPP₉ₐ`) blocks the exact enzyme in human lungs — **`Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE)`** — that both destroys vessel-relaxing **bradykinin** AND manufactures vessel-squeezing **`angiotensin II`**!
- **1974–1977 (*Science* 1977) — David Cushman & Miguel Ondetti turn a viper peptide into an oral pill (`Captopril`):**
  - Why couldn't doctors just give patients a pill of the viper's peptide (`teprotide`)? For the **exact reason Francesco Redi discovered in 1664**: if you swallow a peptide pill, your stomach digests it!
  - At the Squibb Institute in Princeton (`inspired by consultant John Vane`), biochemist **David Cushman** (1939–2000) and organic chemist **Miguel Ondetti** (1930–2004) in **March 1974 – 1977** (*Science* **1977**) modeled the `zinc` atom at the active site (`070`) of the `ACE` enzyme, kept the viper peptide's terminal **`proline`** ring, and attached a tiny sulfur-containing **`thiol (-SH)` hook** that grabbed the zinc atom **2,000 times tighter** — creating **`Captopril`**, the world's first oral **`ACE inhibitor`** (and the first medicine ever designed from the 3D blueprint of an enzyme's active site)!
