### PART 02: Two Kinds of Earthquake Waves & Andrija Mohorovičić's 8 October 1909 Zagreb Discovery (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (how to X-ray a planet without a drill):** **Zagreb, Croatia, 8 October 1909**. Humans have tried to drill into the Earth — reaching `12.2 kilometres` at the Kola Superdeep Borehole (`1970–1989`) before `180 °C` heat softened the drill bit — yet the centre of the Earth lies **`6,371 kilometres` down**!
- **Two kinds of earthquake waves (`Richard Dixon Oldham, 1900–1906`):** Whenever a fault snaps in an earthquake, it sends two different waves racing through the body of the planet (recorded on heavy pendulum **seismographs** invented by John Milne and Emil Wiechert):
  1. **P-waves (`Primary` or push-pull compressional waves):** Like sound waves squeezing and stretching a spring, P-waves travel fastest (`~6 km/s` in crust, `8–13 km/s` in mantle) and can pass through **both solids and liquids**.
  2. **S-waves (`Secondary` or side-to-side shear waves):** Like shaking a rope from side to side, S-waves travel slower (`~3.5–7 km/s`) and **can only travel through solids — because you cannot shear a liquid**!
- **8 October 1909 — Andrija Mohorovičić discovers the bottom of Earth's crust (`the Moho`):** On **8 October 1909**, a strong earthquake struck the **Pokupsko valley** 39 kilometres southeast of **Zagreb**. Fifty-two-year-old Croatian meteorologist **Andrija Mohorovičić** (1857–1936) collected smoked-paper seismograms from **29 stations across Europe** (`all synchronized by railway telegraph clocks`) and spotted a startling race:
  - Within `200 km` of the quake, waves travelling straight through the shallow surface rock arrived first (`at ~5.6 km/s`).
  - Beyond `200 km`, a **second set of P-waves overtook the first**, racing at **`7.9 kilometres per second`**!
  - Why did the deeper road run faster? Because at a sharp boundary about **`35 kilometres` (`22 miles`) underground** — now called the **Mohorovičić discontinuity**, or **the Moho** — Earth's light, brittle **crust** ends and gives way to the much denser, stiffer rock of the **mantle** (`where stiffer rock snaps back faster, making waves speed up!`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Richard Dixon Oldham (1858–1936) — British geologist and seismologist · WHEN: 1906 · WHEN: 8 October 1909 · WHEN: 1910 · FACT: Deeper waves overtake shallow · DATA: inked travel-time & depth diagram on period paper from Andrija Mohorovičić (1910, Potres od 8. X. 1909) showing direct crustal P-waves (~5.6 km/s) overtaken beyond 200 km epicentral distance by refracted mantle Pn-waves racing at ~7.9 km/s beneath the 35-kilometre Mohorovičić discontinuity (the Moho) · SCALE: just thirty-five kilometres beneath your shoes — the distance of a forty-minute motorway drive — the familiar granite and basalt of Earth's crust suddenly ends and the dense green rock of the mantle begins`
- **Ends on:** If seismic waves speed up as they dive deeper and deeper through the solid mantle, why did seismometers on the far side of the planet suddenly go **completely blind** in a giant ring around the globe?

### PART 03: Beno Gutenberg's 1914 Shadow Zone at 2,890 Kilometres — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong — at 2,890 kilometres down, the waves hit an underground ocean!"):** **Göttingen, Germany, 1912–1914 & Cambridge, 1926**. For centuries, physicists including **Lord Kelvin (`040`)** insisted the entire Earth from crust to centre had to be **as rigid and solid as steel** (otherwise the Moon's gravity (`009`) would pull giant tides in the ground twice a day!).
- **1906–1914 — Richard Oldham and Beno Gutenberg map the `104° to 140°` Shadow Zone:** Following **Richard Dixon Oldham's 1906** hint of a central core, 24-year-old German seismologist **Beno Gutenberg** (1889–1960) in **Göttingen in 1914** analysed earthquake recordings from around the world and locked the exact boundary at **`2,890 kilometres` (`1,800 miles`) beneath the surface** (`still accurate to within a few kilometres today!`):
  1. At **2,890 km depth** (`the Gutenberg discontinuity`), **S-waves (`shear waves`) stop dead** — casting a total **S-wave shadow** across the entire far hemisphere of the Earth beyond `104°`!
  2. At that exact same depth of **2,890 km**, **P-waves suddenly slow down by 40% (`from ~13.6 km/s to ~8.1 km/s`)** — bending sharply inward like light entering a glass fishbowl and creating a dark, silent ring between **`104° and 140°`** (`~11,500 to 15,500 kilometres` from the quake) where **no direct P-waves can reach**: the **P-wave shadow zone**!
- **1926 — Harold Jeffreys proves the core is a liquid ocean of molten iron:** In **1926**, British geophysicist **Harold Jeffreys** (1891–1989) combined Gutenberg's `2,890 km` boundary with tidal measurements to prove beyond doubt that beneath 2,890 kilometres of solid rocky mantle lies a churning **ocean of liquid molten iron and nickel**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Harold Jeffreys (1891–1989) — British mathematician and geophysicist · WHEN: 1914 · WHEN: 1926 · FACT: Liquid iron ocean 2,890 km down · DATA: inked global ray-path & shadow-zone diagram on period paper from Beno Gutenberg (1914) and Harold Jeffreys (1926) showing P-waves dropping from 13.6 km/s to 8.1 km/s at the 2,890 km core-mantle boundary (refracting inward to leave a 104°–140° P-wave shadow zone) while shear S-waves are blocked completely by the liquid iron outer core · SCALE: halfway to the centre of the Earth — two thousand eight hundred and ninety kilometres down — solid rock abruptly gives way to a roaring ocean of liquid iron wider than the planet Mars`
- **Ends on:** For twenty-two years after Gutenberg's 1914 paper, every geophysics textbook on Earth taught that our planet had just three parts: a solid crust, a solid mantle, and a single ball of liquid iron at the core. So why did earthquake needles in Russia and Europe after the **17 June 1929 New Zealand earthquake** start twitching **right inside Gutenberg's forbidden `104°–140°` shadow zone**?

### PART 04: 17 June 1929 to 1936 — Inge Lehmann's Oatmeal-Box Cards & the Paper Titled *"P'"* (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (the forbidden echoes from New Zealand):** **Buller Gorge, New Zealand, 17 June 1929 & Copenhagen, 1936**. On **17 June 1929**, a magnitude `7.3` earthquake struck **Murchison (Buller Gorge) on New Zealand's South Island**.
- **Inge Lehmann spots what shouldn't be there:** Half a world away in **Copenhagen** (`~160° away, with Russian stations at Sverdlovsk and Irkutsk sitting between 104° and 140°!`), 48-year-old Danish mathematician and chief of the Royal Danish Geodetic Institute's seismological department **Inge Lehmann** (1888–1993) examined the smoked-paper traces from stations sitting squarely inside Gutenberg's **"silent" `104°–140°` P-wave shadow zone** — and saw **clear, unmistakable P-wave arrivals**!
- **Why other seismologists missed it — and Lehmann's cardboard-card breakthrough (1936):** Other scientists blamed sloppy clock readings or faulty instruments. Lehmann knew the station operators personally and knew the arrivals were real!
  - Sitting in her Copenhagen garden with **hand-written cardboard cards cut from oatmeal boxes** (`working completely alone with no computer`), Lehmann calculated toy earth models by hand until she found the only physical geometry that could throw P-waves into the `104°–140°` shadow zone:
  - Deep inside Gutenberg's liquid outer core — at a radius of about **`1,220 kilometres` (`5,150 km` beneath our feet)** — sits a **second core where P-waves suddenly speed back up (`from ~10.3 to ~11.2 km/s`)**, bouncing and bending waves into the shadow zone!
  - In **1936**, Lehmann published her discovery under one of the shortest titles in the history of science: ***"P'"*** (*P-prime*)!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 17 June 1929 · WHEN: 1936 · FACT: Paper title was just two characters · DATA: inked inner-core reflection & refraction diagram on period paper from Inge Lehmann's 1936 paper "P'" showing PKiKP and PKIKP seismic rays from the 17 June 1929 New Zealand earthquake bouncing off and speeding through the 1,220 km-radius inner core (~11.2 km/s) to arrive inside the 104°–140° shadow zone at 19–20 minutes travel time · SCALE: working alone in her garden with cardboard cards cut from oatmeal boxes, Inge Lehmann discovered a metal sphere inside the Earth nearly three-quarters the size of the Moon`
- **Ends on:** Why would the very centre of the Earth — where temperatures reach **`5,400 °C`**, as hot as the surface of the Sun — be **solid metal**, when the outer core around it is **liquid**? And why would life on Earth die without it?

### PART 05: Why the Inner Core Is Solid, How It Powers Earth's Magnetic Shield & Why Coffee Keeps You Awake (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (how can metal at 5,400 °C be solid?):** If the centre of the Earth is **5,400 degrees Celsius** (`as hot as the surface of the Sun`) and the outer core is liquid iron, how can the inner core at the very centre be **solid**?
- **Pressure beats temperature (`Francis Birch 1940–1952 & Dziewonski and Gilbert 1971`):** As American geophysicist **Francis Birch** proved in **1940–1952** (and **Adam Dziewonski & Freeman Gilbert** confirmed using whole-Earth seismic ringing in **1971**), the crushing weight of the entire planet presses down on the centre of the Earth with **3.6 million atmospheres of pressure (`360 gigapascals`)** — squeezing iron and nickel atoms so tightly together that they **cannot melt**, locking them into a **solid crystalline metal ball**!
- **Why Lehmann's inner core keeps us alive:** As the Earth slowly cools, liquid iron at the boundary freezes onto the solid inner core (`growing it by ~1 millimetre a year`). That freezing releases heat and buoyant lighter elements that stir the liquid outer core into a giant **geodynamo** — generating **Earth's magnetic field**, the invisible shield that deflects deadly solar wind and keeps our atmosphere and oceans from being stripped into space! (And Inge Lehmann herself lived to the astonishing age of **104**, passing away in **1993**!)
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Seismographs let us read invisible electrical and mechanical waves pulsing through our planet.
  - What about the chemical and electrical waves pulsing inside your own **brain** every morning when you take a sip of **coffee or tea** — humanity's favourite drug, drunk more than **two billion times a day**?
  - Why did 70-year-old poet **Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** on **3 October 1819** hand a box of rare Arabian mocha beans to 25-year-old chemist **Friedlieb Ferdinand Runge** in Jena, Germany, how did Runge isolate sparkling white needles of **caffeine**, and how did **Solomon Snyder and John Daly** in **1981** prove that caffeine doesn't actually give you energy, but slips a molecular block under your brain's **adenosine sleep brake** (`045` candidate *How Does Caffeine Keep You Awake?*)?
