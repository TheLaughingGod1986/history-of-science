### PART 02: 19 September 1966 & 1976 — Thomas Brock's Boiling Yellowstone Spring (*Thermus aquaticus*) & the Heat-Proof `Taq` Enzyme (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why did a microbiologist go fishing with microscope slides in a scalding Yellowstone geyser basin?):** **Mushroom Spring, Lower Geyser Basin, Yellowstone National Park, 19 September 1966 & University of Cincinnati, 1976**. In the 1960s, textbooks stated as fact that bacteria could never live above **`55 to 60 °C`** because heat unravels proteins like a boiled egg.
- **19 September 1966 — Thomas Brock & Hudson Freeze isolate *Thermus aquaticus* at `70 °C`:**
  - Visiting **Yellowstone National Park**, Indiana University microbiologist **Thomas D. Brock** (1926–2021) noticed vibrant pink, yellow, and orange gelatinous mats thriving right in the scalding runoff channels of geothermal springs!
  - On **19 September 1966**, Brock and his 20-year-old undergraduate student **Hudson Freeze** (born 1946) sampled the **`70 °C` (`158 °F`, thriving up to `79 °C`)** waters of **Mushroom Spring**, cultured a rod-shaped extremophile bacterium that grew happily near the boiling point, and named it ***Thermus aquaticus*** (`published August 1969, Journal of Bacteriology`).
  - Crucially for all of humanity, Brock deposited a living tube of his Yellowstone microbe — **strain `YT-1`** — free of charge into the **American Type Culture Collection (`ATCC`)**, where any scientist in the world could order it!
- **1976 — Alice Chien, David Edgar & John Trela purify `Taq` DNA Polymerase:**
  - How did *Thermus aquaticus* copy its own DNA without cooking? In **1976** at the University of Cincinnati, graduate student **Alice Chien** (born 1952), **David Edgar**, and **John Trela** isolated the bacterium's DNA-copying enzyme — **`Taq` DNA polymerase** — and showed it works fastest at a scorching **`75 to 80 °C`**!

- **Ends on:** For seven years, `Taq` polymerase sat quietly in laboratory freezers as a curiosity of nature. How did a late-night drive through the California redwoods in **April 1983** turn that Yellowstone enzyme into the most powerful copying machine in biology?

### PART 03: April 1983 – January 1988 — Kary Mullis's Moonlight Drive (`PCR`) & Swapping in Yellowstone's `Taq` — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong — you don't need to hunt through three billion letters of DNA if you can trick your target gene into doubling itself a billion times!"):** **California Highway 128, Mendocino County, April 1983 & Cetus Corporation, Emeryville, California, December 1983 – January 1988**.
- **April 1983 — Kary Mullis's moonlight drive (`The Polymerase Chain Reaction`):**
  - Since Watson, Crick, and Franklin (`016`), scientists knew how DNA's two strands unzip and copy. Yet finding one single-copy human gene in a drop of blood was a nightmare because **3 billion base pairs** of background DNA drowned it out.
  - On a Friday night in **April 1983**, Cetus Corporation chemist **Kary B. Mullis** (1944–2019) was driving along **Highway 128** through the moonlit California redwoods when a simple mathematical trick hit him:
    - Instead of trying to fish one tiny sentence out of a three-billion-letter library, what if you bracket just that sentence with **two short synthetic DNA primers** (`left and right bookmarks`)?
    - **Step 1 (`95 °C`):** Heat the tube to **`95 °C`** to melt the double helix apart into two single strands.
    - **Step 2 (`~54 °C`):** Cool the tube so the two primers lock onto the start and end of your target gene.
    - **Step 3 (`72 °C`):** Let **DNA polymerase** copy both strands — turning `1` DNA molecule into `2`, then `4`, `8`, `16`, and in **30 cycles (`2^30`), `1,073,741,824` copies**!
- **1985–1988 — The boiling-enzyme bottleneck & Randall Saiki's `Taq` breakthrough (`Science`, 1985 & 1988):**
  - When Mullis (`16 December 1983`) and **Randall K. Saiki**, **Henry A. Erlich**, and colleagues (**20 December 1985**, *Science*) proved PCR worked, there was a maddening catch: every time they heated the tube to **`95 °C`** to unzip the DNA, the `95 °C` heat **destroyed the ordinary *E. coli* polymerase**! Technicians had to sit by water baths for hours, opening the tube after every single cycle to pipette in fresh enzyme 30 times!
  - Then Mullis and Saiki remembered **Thomas Brock's Yellowstone bacterium (`Thermus aquaticus`)** sitting in the public culture collection! When **Saiki, David Gelfand, Susanne Stoffel, Mullis, and Erlich** (**29 January 1988**, *Science*) put **`Taq` polymerase** into the tube, it survived **`95 °C`** cycle after cycle — letting a machine run all 30 cycles automatically in one sealed drop of water (`winning Mullis the 1993 Nobel Prize`)!

### PART 04: 9:05 a.m., 10 September 1984 & 1986 — Alec Jeffreys's "Eureka!" Darkroom X-Ray (`DNA Fingerprinting`) & the First Exoneration (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (why 99.9% of your DNA is identical to every other human on Earth — and where your personal barcode hides):** **Department of Genetics, University of Leicester, 9:05 a.m., Monday 10 September 1984 & Enderby, Leicestershire, 1986–1987**.
- **9:05 a.m., Monday 10 September 1984 — Sir Alec Jeffreys's darkroom "Eureka!" (`Nature`, 7 March 1985):**
  - Any two humans share **`~99.9%`** of the exact same DNA sequence. So how could you ever tell two people apart from a speck of blood?
  - At the **University of Leicester**, British geneticist **Alec J. Jeffreys** (born 1950) was studying stuttering, repetitive stretches of non-coding DNA called **minisatellites (`VNTRs`, Variable Number Tandem Repeats)** found inside the seal myoglobin gene and human chromosomes.
  - Everyone carries the same core repeat letters, but the **number of times the stutter repeats** varies wildly from person to person!
  - At **9:05 a.m. on Monday 10 September 1984**, Jeffreys pulled a freshly developed X-ray film out of his darkroom tank showing DNA from his technician Vicky Wilson, her parents, and non-human samples. Looking at the rows of dark horizontal bars — like a **supermarket barcode unique to each person, yet half-inherited from mother and half from father** — Jeffreys shouted *"Eureka!"* Within 30 minutes, he and his team realised they had invented **DNA fingerprinting** (`published 7 March 1985 in Nature`)!
- **1986–1987 — The first crime solved by DNA & the first innocent person exonerated (`Richard Buckland & Colin Pitchfork`):**
  - In **1986**, police in Leicestershire asked Jeffreys to test crime-scene samples from two murders against a 17-year-old suspect, **Richard Buckland**, who had given a false confession under questioning.
  - Jeffreys's DNA fingerprint proved two stunning facts on the spot: both crimes were committed by the same man — and **Richard Buckland was 100% innocent**, making him the **first person in history exonerated by DNA** (`while a community screening and a falsified sample swap caught the real killer, Colin Pitchfork, in September 1987`)!
  - When forensic scientists combined **Alec Jeffreys's repeating DNA markers (`STRs`)** with **Kary Mullis and Thomas Brock's `PCR` photocopier**, detectives no longer needed a large bloodstain — they could generate a full DNA profile from **a single human cell**, a licked postage stamp, or a **40,000-year-old Neanderthal bone** (`Svante Pääbo`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Svante Pääbo (born 1955) — Swedish evolutionary geneticist · WHEN: 10 September 1984 · WHEN: 7 March 1985 · WHEN: 1986–1987 · FACT: 9:05 a.m. DNA barcode · DATA: inked minisatellite VNTR/STR barcode & random-match probability chart on period paper from Jeffreys, Wilson & Thein (7 March 1985, Nature 314: 67–73) showing polymorphic tandem repeat lengths inherited 50% from each parent and reducing random match probability across multiple loci to <1 in 10^9–10^12 (excluding identical twins), paired with PCR amplification from a single pg of DNA · SCALE:although ninety-nine point nine per cent of your DNA is identical to the person sitting next to you, counting the stutters at just a dozen repeating spots makes your genetic barcode rarer than one in a billion`
