### PART 02: August 1793–1794 & 2 August 1880 — Lazzaro Spallanzani's Blinded Bats, Charles Jurine's Wax Earplugs & the Curie Brothers' Piezoelectric Quartz (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open ("In August 1793 in Pavia, Italian biologist Lazzaro Spallanzani watched an owl and a bat fly inside a candlelit room: the instant the candle blew out, the owl crashed helplessly into the wall — while the bat flew through the pitch-black room as if it were broad daylight!"): Pavia (August 1793), Geneva (February 1794) & Paris (2 August 1880)**.
- **August 1793–1794 (*Lettere sopra il sospetto di un nuovo senso nei pipistrelli*) — Spallanzani's Silk Threads & Jurine's Earplugs:**
  - Testing whether bats had super-sensitive eyes (`083`), **Lazzaro Spallanzani** (1729–1799, `077`) blinded bats or covered their heads with opaque hoods and hung **fine silk threads** from the ceiling: the blinded bats dodged every thread, avoided branches, and even returned from the wild with their stomachs full of freshly caught insects!
  - In **February 1794**, Geneva surgeon **Louis (Charles) Jurine** (1751–1819) plugged bats' **ears** with wax or starch paste — and the bats immediately blundered into walls! Spallanzani confirmed it by inserting tiny **brass tubes** into bats' ears: when the tubes were open, blinded bats flew normally; when he plugged the tubes with sealing wax, the bats crashed (`writing in 1794: "Can it then be said that here their ears rather than their eyes serve to direct them in flight?"`).
  - Yet because human observers heard **complete silence**, French anatomist **Georges Cuvier** (`014`) scoffed in **1795–1800** ("What could they hear when nothing makes a sound?"), asserting bats felt air pressure with their wing membranes — a mistake textbooks repeated until 1938!
- **2 August 1880 (*Comptes Rendus*) & 1917 — Jacques and Pierre Curie's Piezoelectric Quartz & Paul Langevin's Submarine Sonar:**
  - Before anyone could hear a bat, physicists needed a machine that could hear above the `20,000-Hz` ceiling of the human cochlea (`055`).
  - On **2 August 1880** in Paris, 21-year-old **Pierre Curie** (`023`) and his brother **Jacques Curie** discovered **`piezoelectricity`** (from Greek *piezein*, "to squeeze"): compressing a **quartz crystal** generates an electric voltage, and applying an alternating voltage makes the crystal vibrate tens of thousands or millions of times per second!
  - After the *Titanic* hit an iceberg in **1912** and German U-boats stalked Allied ships in WWI, Pierre Curie's former student **Paul Langevin** (1872–1946) sandwiched quartz crystals between steel plates in **1917** in Toulon to ping **`40,000-Hz` ultrasonic beams** through seawater and listen for submarine echoes (**`SONAR`**)!

### PART 03: 1938–29 December 1944 — Donald Griffin, Robert Galambos & the 50,000-Hz Bat Microphone (`Echolocation`) — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("For 144 years after Spallanzani, every textbook insisted bats fly in the dark by feeling air currents with their wings! - **Spring 1938 (*J. Acoust. Soc. Am.*) & 1941 (*J. Exp. Zool.*) — The Midpoint Twist: Hearing the "Silent" Bat:**
  - At Harvard, physicist **George Washington Pierce** (1872–1956) had invented a piezoelectric **sonic amplifier (`heterodyne detector`)** to record ultrasonic insect songs (`like katydids and crickets`).
  - In **Spring 1938**, senior undergraduate **Donald Redfield Griffin** (1915–2003, who had banded bats in New England caves) brought a cage of little brown bats (*Myotis lucifugus*) to Pierce's lab: the instant they aimed the parabolic horn at the "silent" bats, the loudspeaker erupted with **furious `50,000-Hz` clicks** (`2.5 times higher than the highest pitch a human ear can hear`)!
  - Teaming up with neurophysiologist **Robert Galambos** (1914–2010) in **1940–1941** (*J. Exp. Zool.* 86: 481–506), Griffin hung **`1-millimetre` metal wires** across a soundproof room and ran the decisive three-way test:
    1. **Blindfolded bats:** dodged `70%–76%` of the thin wires effortlessly (`0` change from normal vision!).
    2. **Ear-plugged bats** (`Confirming Spallanzani & Jurine 1794!`): crashed helplessly into `65%–67%` of the wires!
    3. **Gagged bats** (`mouths gently tied shut and sealed with collodion so their ears were wide open, but they couldn't squeak`): crashed into **`65%` of the wires**, flying just as blindly as deafened bats!
  - In **29 December 1944** (*Science* 100: 589–590), Griffin coined the word **`echolocation`** — and in **1953–1960**, **Winthrop Kellogg** and **Kenneth Norris** (using soft rubber suction-cup blindfolds on bottlenose dolphins *Tursiops truncatus*) proved that **dolphins and toothed whales** fire ultrasonic clicks through the fatty lens (`melon`) in their foreheads to "see" through pitch-black water (where sound travels at **`1,500 m/s`**, over four times faster than in air, `038`)!
