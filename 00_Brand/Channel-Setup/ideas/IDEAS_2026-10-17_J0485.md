### 1. Sparks & Synapses: How the Brain Sends Messages — Santiago Ramón y Cajal's 1887 Kitchen Silver Stain & Otto Loewi's 1921 Easter Night Dream of Two Frog Hearts (recommended for 033; drafted in `033_Cajal-Loewi-Neurons-Synapses/`)
- **Familiar thing:** the **86 billion neurons** firing inside your brain right now as you watch and hear this sentence — bridging microscopic gaps (`20 nanometres` wide) with tiny puffs of chemicals in thousandths of a second.
- **Person & room:** Thirty-five-year-old Spanish anatomist and aspiring painter **Santiago Ramón y Cajal** (1852–1934) bending over a brass microscope at his **kitchen table in Barcelona in 1887–1888**, staining slices of embryonic bird and mammal brains with **Camillo Golgi's** (1843–1926) **black silver chromate reaction (*la reazione nera*)** and drawing intricate pen-and-ink forests of separate nerve cells; cut to German-born pharmacologist **Otto Loewi** (1873–1961) waking at **3:00 a.m. on Easter Sunday in Graz, Austria, in March 1921**, rushing to his dark laboratory to connect **two beating frog hearts in glass saline chambers**!
- **Date & proof:** Even after **Robert Hooke** and **Robert Remak** (`015`) proved the body is built of discrete cells, nineteenth-century scientists led by Italian histologist **Camillo Golgi** insisted the brain was the one great exception: a single fused, continuous web of protoplasm (**the Reticular Theory**), like water pipes soldered into one continuous network! Why? Because ordinary histological dyes stained every fibre in brain tissue at once, turning the microscope field into an unreadable solid tangle. Then, in **1873**, working by candlelight in a hospital apartment kitchen in **Abbiategrasso, Italy**, Camillo Golgi discovered **"the black reaction" (*la reazione nera*)**: soaking brain tissue in potassium dichromate and **silver nitrate ($AgNO_3$)** mysteriously stained just **1% to 5% of nerve cells stark jet-black** from root to tip while leaving the other 95% completely transparent! Fourteen years later, in **1887** in Madrid, psychiatrist **Luis Simarro** showed a Golgi-stained slide to 35-year-old Spanish anatomist **Santiago Ramón y Cajal**, who had wanted to be a painter as a boy. Working at his kitchen table in **Barcelona in 1888–1889**, Cajal made two decisive breakthroughs:
  1. He used **"double impregnation"** (soaking tissue twice in silver nitrate) to make the finest nerve endings crisp;
  2. Instead of studying adult brains (where thick fatty **myelin sheaths** obscure the tangled wires), he stained the brains of **embryonic chicks and newborn mammals** — where the "forest" was still saplings!
  3. Across hundreds of breathtaking pen-and-ink drawings in **1888–1889**, Cajal proved that **every nerve cell ("neuron", named by Wilhelm Waldeyer in 1891) is a completely separate, independent cell** (`free endings`, climbing fibres, and dendritic spines) that approaches its neighbour closely **but never fuses with it** — separated by a microscopic gap that British physiologist **Charles Scott Sherrington** named **the "synapse"** in **1897**!
- **Twist (three-part):**
  1. **The fiercest Nobel Prize stage fight in history (10–12 December 1906):** When Camillo Golgi and Santiago Ramón y Cajal shared the **1906 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** in Stockholm — the first time the prize was ever split — Golgi stood up on **11 December 1906** and used his Nobel lecture to **attack Cajal's Neuron Doctrine to his face**, insisting that nerve endings fused into a continuous reticulum! The very next day (**12 December 1906**), Cajal took the same podium with giant charts of his silver-stained drawings to defend independent neurons ("True, it would be very convenient... if nerve cells formed a continuous network; unfortunately, nature seems unaware of our intellectual need for convenience"). Who was right? Nearly fifty years later, in **1954–1955**, **George Palade, Sanford Palay, and Eduardo De Robertis** aimed the **electron microscope** (`100,000×` magnification) at a synapse and photographed the **`20-nanometre` synaptic cleft** — proving Cajal 100% right!
  2. **The War of the Soups and the Sparks & Otto Loewi's two-night Easter dream (March 1921):** If neurons don't touch across that `20 nm` gap, how does an electrical nerve impulse (`019` Galvani) cross from one cell to the next? Neurophysiologists ("the Sparks") insisted the electric current jumped directly across the gap; pharmacologists ("the Soups", led by **Henry Hallett Dale** and **Thomas Renton Elliott**) suspected nerve tips released a tiny squirt of chemical. On **Easter Saturday night in March 1921** in Graz, Austria, 47-year-old **Otto Loewi** woke from a deep sleep with a brilliant experiment, scribbled it on a scrap of thin paper, and went back to sleep. At **6:00 a.m. on Easter Sunday**, he knew he had written something momentous — **but couldn't read a single word of his own midnight scrawl**! Agonising all day, he went to bed Sunday night, **dreamed the exact same experiment again at 3:00 a.m.**, and didn't trust paper this time: he got dressed and walked straight to his laboratory!
  3. **Two frog hearts in saline prove your thoughts are chemical puffs:** Before dawn in Graz, Loewi placed two beating frog hearts into glass chambers filled with Ringer's salt solution: **`Heart #1`** still had its **vagus nerve** attached, while **`Heart #2`** had all its nerves stripped away. He electrically stimulated the vagus nerve of `Heart #1` for a few minutes until its heartbeat slowed down. Then he sucked up the clear saline bath around `Heart #1` with a glass pipette and dripped it onto nerve-free **`Heart #2`** — and **`Heart #2`'s beat slowed down immediately**, just as if its own vagus nerve had been stimulated! When he stimulated `Heart #1`'s accelerator nerve and transferred the fluid, `Heart #2` sped up! Nerves don't jump the synaptic gap with raw sparks: an electrical action potential races down the neuron at up to **100 metres per second** and triggers a microscopic chemical puff (**acetylcholine**, which Loewi called *Vagusstoff*, winning the **1936 Nobel Prize** alongside **Henry Dale**)!
