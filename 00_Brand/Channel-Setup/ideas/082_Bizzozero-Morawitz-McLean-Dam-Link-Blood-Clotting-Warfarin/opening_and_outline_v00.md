### PART 02: 1882 & 1905 — Giulio Bizzozero's Living `Platelets` & Paul Morawitz's `Thrombin-Fibrin` Net (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (for centuries, people thought blood clotted simply because it cooled down when it hit the air, like hot candle wax — until an Italian doctor in 1882 peered inside a living blood vessel and watched microscopic discs rush to repair a microscopic scratch before a single drop could escape!):** **University of Turin, 1882 & Heidelberg / Strasbourg, 1905**.
- **1882 (*Virchows Archiv*) — Giulio Bizzozero discovers `Platelets` (`Piastrine`):**
  - Since Anton van Leeuwenhoek (`001`), microscopists knew blood carried red blood cells (`074`) and white blood cells (`065`). Whenever they saw tiny, colourless specks floating between them (`1/4 the size of a red blood cell`), they dismissed them as broken debris from dead cells.
  - In **1882** at the University of Turin (*Virchows Archiv*), 36-year-old histologist **Giulio Bizzozero** (1846–1901, `who also mentored Camillo Golgi 033!`) looked at blood flowing inside the **living, transparent mesenteric vessels** of anaesthetised guinea pigs and rabbits (`instead of dead blood on a glass slide`).
  - He proved those tiny discs (**`piastrine` / `blood platelets`**, `150,000 to 400,000 in every cubic millimetre of blood!`) were a **third living element of blood**: the instant he nicked the inner wall of a vessel with a microscopic needle, passing platelets changed shape, sprouted sticky arms, and piled onto the exposed collagen within **seconds** to build a **white platelet plug (`primary haemostasis`)**!
- **1872 & 1905 (*Ergebnisse der Physiologie*) — Alexander Schmidt & Paul Morawitz's Four-Factor `Coagulation Cascade`:**
  - A soft pile of platelets can stop a tiny capillary leak, but high arterial blood pressure (`005`) would blow it away without reinforcement!
  - Building on **Alexander Schmidt's** (**1872**) discovery of the enzyme **`thrombin`**, German physician **Paul Morawitz** (1879–1936) in **1905** united the puzzle into the classic **Four-Factor Coagulation Cascade**:
    1. Torn tissue and clumped platelets release **`thromboplastin` (`tissue factor`, Factor III)**.
    2. With **`calcium ions` (`Ca²⁺`, Factor IV, `076`)**, thromboplastin converts an inactive blood protein made by the liver (`077`), **`prothrombin` (Factor II)**, into the active molecular scissors **`thrombin`**.
    3. **`Thrombin`** snips soluble **`fibrinogen` (Factor I)** floating in the plasma into sticky **`fibrin`** strands that self-assemble into a tough, cross-linked **fishing net** over the platelets!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Alexander Schmidt (1831–1894) — Baltic German physiologist · WHEN: 1872 · WHEN: 1882 · WHEN: 1905 · FACT: 15 million platelets per drop · DATA: inked Giulio Bizzozero (1882 Virchows Archiv 90: 261–332) living-vessel platelet plug formation & Paul Morawitz (1905 Ergebnisse der Physiologie 4: 307–422) 4-factor enzymatic amplification cascade (Thromboplastin + Ca²⁺ → Prothrombin → Thrombin → Fibrinogen to insoluble Fibrin mesh) on period paper · SCALE: the clotting cascade works like an avalanche on a mountainside — one triggered molecule at the cut activates ten, ten activate a hundred, and within two minutes a single microscopic nick weaves millions of fibrin threads to lock the platelet dam solid`
- **Ends on:** If every drop of your blood is packed with **`platelets`**, **`prothrombin`**, and **`fibrinogen`** ready to turn into a solid net in two minutes, **why doesn't all the blood inside your healthy veins clot into solid stone right now**? And how did a 25-year-old medical student in Baltimore in **1916** find the answer by accident while looking for the exact opposite?

- **1915–1916 (*Am. J. Physiol.* 1916 & 1918) — The Midpoint Twist (`Jay McLean looks for a clotter and discovers Heparin`):**
  - In **September 1915**, 25-year-old second-year medical student **Jay McLean** (1890–1957, `who had worked for over a year as a labourer and gold miner to save enough money for one year of medical school at Johns Hopkins!`) asked physiology professor **William Henry Howell** (1860–1945) for a research problem to prove himself in a single academic year.
  - Howell believed a fat-linked substance (`cephalin`) caused blood to clot, and assigned McLean to extract similar pro-clotting phosphatides (`"cuorin"` from heart and `"heparphosphatide"` from liver).
  - Working early every morning in **winter 1915–spring 1916**, McLean kept his liver extract batches on the shelf and tested whether they still sped up clotting — **until one batch didn't just lose its clotting power, it completely STOPPED fresh cat blood from clotting**!
  - Howell was so sceptical that McLean walked up to Howell's laboratory bench, mixed his liver extract into a beaker of fresh blood, and told his professor to call him when it clotted — **and it never clotted**!
  - Published by McLean in **June 1916** (*Am. J. Physiol.*) and named **`heparin`** (`from Greek hepar = liver`) by Howell and Emmett Holt in **1918** (`before Charles Best 035, Arthur Charles, and David Scott in Toronto in 1933–1936 purified clinical heparin for human patients`), `heparin` supercharges our natural blood-brake (**`antithrombin`**) by **1,000-fold** (`making open-heart surgery, kidney dialysis 072, and organ transplants possible`)!
- **1929–1935 (*Biochem. Z.* 1929 & *Nature* 27 April 1935) — Henrik Dam's bleeding chicks & `Vitamin K`:**
  - Meanwhile, at the University of Copenhagen in **1929**, Danish biochemist **Henrik Dam** (1895–1976) fed young chicks a fat-free diet to study cholesterol — and noticed the chicks developed spontaneous bleeding under their skin and muscles (`because their blood lacked prothrombin!`).

### PART 04: February 1933–1955 — Karl Paul Link's Blizzard Milk Can, `Dicoumarol` & `Warfarin` (`From Rat Poison to President Eisenhower`) (4:55–6:45)
- **Cold open (on a bitter, sub-zero Saturday afternoon in February 1933, Wisconsin dairy farmer Ed Carlson loaded a dead cow, a bundle of mouldy sweet clover hay, and a metal milk can sloshing with unclotted crimson blood into his truck, drove 190 miles through a raging snowstorm to Madison, and accidentally launched the discovery of the world's most famous blood thinner!):** **University of Wisconsin–Madison, February 1933 – 28 June 1939 – 1948 – September 1955**.
- **February 1933 – 28 June 1939 (*J. Biol. Chem.* 1940–1941) — Ed Carlson's 190-mile blizzard drive & the 6-year hunt for `Dicoumarol`:**
  - Since the 1920s (`first described by veterinarian Frank Schofield in Ontario in 1922 and Lee Roderick in North Dakota in 1929`), cattle across the northern plains that ate damp, mouldy **sweet clover hay** (*Melilotus alba* / *officinalis*) during hard winters died of mysterious internal bleeding (**"sweet clover disease"**).
  - In **February 1933**, farmer **Ed Carlson** had lost cow after cow on his farm near Deer Park, Wisconsin. Driving **190 miles through a howling blizzard**, he walked into agricultural biochemist **Karl Paul Link's** (1901–1978) unlocked laboratory in Madison and spilled unclotted cow's blood onto the floor.
  - Link couldn't give Carlson an instant cure (`other than telling him to stop feeding mouldy hay and transfuse healthy blood`), but that night Link and his student **ugenetics/biochemistry team** (**Harold A. Campbell, Wilhelm Schoeffel, William L. Roberts, Mark A. Stahmann, Charles F. Huebner**) vowed to isolate the chemical culprit.
  - After six years of testing hay extracts with a rapid rabbit prothrombin assay, at dawn on **28 June 1939**, **Harold Campbell** saw microscopic crystalline needles on his microscope slide (`published 1940–1941 in J. Biol. Chem.`): **`dicoumarol`** (`3,3'-methylenebis(4-hydroxycoumarin)`).
  - The chemistry was a revelation: fresh sweet clover contains harmless **`coumarin`** (`the molecule that gives newly mown grass its sweet vanilla scent!`), which does *not* thin blood. Only when **mould fungi** spoil damp hay do they oxidize two coumarin molecules and link them together into **`dicoumarol`** — a molecular double of **Henrik Dam's `Vitamin K`** that blocks the liver's Vitamin K recycling enzyme (`VKORC1`)!
- **1945–1955 (*Circulation* 1959) — Compound #42 (`Warfarin`), the 1951 Navy recruit, and President Eisenhower:**

  - Reviewing their synthesized analogues, Link's team picked **compound #42**, patented it in **1948** through the **Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation (`WARF`)**, and named it **`Warfarin`** (`WARF` + coum`arin`)!
  - For three years, `Warfarin` was sold strictly as **rat poison** — until **April 1951**, when a young US Navy inductee attempted suicide by swallowing massive doses of Warfarin rodenticide over **5 days**, walked into a naval hospital with bleeding gums, **was given blood transfusions and `Vitamin K` (`Dam's vitamin!`), and recovered completely**!
  - Doctors realized that unlike rat poisons of the past, `Warfarin` was a remarkably safe, predictable oral blood thinner (`marketed as Coumadin in 1954`) with an instant antidote (`Vitamin K`)! When **US President Dwight D. Eisenhower** suffered a major heart attack in **September 1955**, his physicians treated him with **`Warfarin`** — and as Link famously said, *"What was good for a national hero was good for grandma and grandpa"*, saving millions of lives from strokes and clots!
