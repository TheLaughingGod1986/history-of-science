### PART 02: 1863 & 1928–1947 — Helmholtz's Piano Harp vs Georg von Békésy's Glittering Cochlear Wave (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why a telephone engineer asked whether the human ear was better engineered than any telephone receiver on Earth):** **Heidelberg, Germany, 1863 & Hungarian Post Office Research Laboratory, Budapest, 1928–1947**.
- **1863 — Hermann von Helmholtz's piano-string resonance theory (*On the Sensations of Tone*):**
  - When you press the damper pedal on a piano and sing a note into the strings, only the piano string tuned to that exact frequency vibrates back.
  - In **1863**, German physicist **Hermann von Helmholtz** (`024, 049`) looked at the snail-shaped **cochlea** and its **`35-millimetre` basilar membrane** (`which is narrow and stiff at the base near the entrance, and five times wider and more flexible at the tip!`) and proposed that it works like a miniature harp of independent taut piano strings.
- **1928–1947 — Georg von Békésy's dental drill, silver dust & the cochlear travelling wave (`1961 Nobel Prize`):**
  - In **1928**, Hungarian telephone engineer **Georg von Békésy** (1899–1972) wanted to know how much money his telephone company should spend improving phone earpieces — which required knowing how the human inner ear actually moves!
  - Because the cochlea is entombed inside the **petrous temporal bone** (`the hardest bone in the human body`), Békésy used high-speed dentist drills under water to carve a tiny window into donated cadaver skulls without tearing the delicate membrane inside.
  - Sprinkling microscopic **silver dust and coal crystals** onto the transparent membrane and illuminating them with a **stroboscopic flashlamp**, Békésy saw something magical: the fibres are attached to one another like a ribbon, so a sound doesn't pluck one isolated string — it launches a **continuous hydrodynamic travelling wave** (`like snapping a rope or watching an ocean swell roll onto a beach`)!
  - High-frequency sounds (**`up to 20,000 Hz`**) crest and die out right near the stiff **base**, while low bass notes (**`down to 20 Hz`**) travel all the way to the floppy **apex** (`establishing the tonotopic frequency map that won Békésy the 1961 Nobel Prize and later enabled Graeme Clark, Ingeborg Hochmair, and Blake Wilson's multi-channel cochlear implants in 1977–1991!`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1863 · WHEN: 1928–1947 · WHEN: 1961 · FACT: 35 mm snail-shell wave · DATA: inked basilar membrane width, stiffness & travelling-wave envelope chart on period paper from Helmholtz (1863) and Békésy (1928–1947 / 1960 Experiments in Hearing) showing membrane width widening from ~0.1 mm at the base (20,000 Hz) to ~0.5 mm at the apex (20 Hz) while stiffness drops ~10,000-fold, causing each frequency's travelling wave to crest at a unique distance · SCALE: unrolled from its snail shell, your basilar membrane is just thirty-five millimetres long — the length of a paperclip, yet it sorts a thousand musical pitches from twenty hertz to twenty thousand hertz along its ribbon`
- **Ends on:** There was a huge problem with Békésy's wave in dead cadaver ears: the wave was **far too broad and sluggish** to explain how a living human can tell two neighbouring violin notes apart or hear a pin drop! What was missing inside a dead ear that only a **living ear** possesses?

### PART 03: 1948, July 1978 & January 1985 — Thomas Gold's Rejected Amplifier, David Kemp's Echoing Ear (`Otoacoustic Emissions`) & Dancing Outer Hair Cells — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? - **1948 — Thomas Gold's radical prediction (`rejected for 30 years`):**
  - Fresh from working on wartime radar, 28-year-old astrophysicist **Thomas Gold** (1920–2004) pointed out in **1948** (*Proceedings of the Royal Society B*) that trying to vibrate a delicate membrane inside thick cochlear water is like trying to swing a pendulum inside a bucket of syrup!
  - To get razor-sharp tuning, Gold argued, the living ear must act like a **regenerative radio receiver**, using an **active mechanical motor** to pump energy *into* the wave on every cycle — and he predicted that if that motor ever hummed on its own, **a microphone placed in someone's ear would hear a sound coming OUT of the ear**! The physiology establishment dismissed him completely.
- **July 1978 & 11 January 1985 — David Kemp hears the ear echo back (`Otoacoustic Emissions`) & William Brownell films outer hair cells dancing (`Science`):**
  - Thirty years later, in **July 1978** (*Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*), British physicist **David T. Kemp** (born 1945) sealed a miniature microphone and speaker into a quiet human ear canal, played a soft click, and recorded what came back: **`5 to 15 milliseconds` after the click, the living ear rang back with its own clear acoustic echo — `otoacoustic emissions` (`which disappear completely if the inner ear is damaged, and are now used in hospitals worldwide to test every newborn baby's hearing in seconds!`)**!
  - Where was the motor hiding? On **11 January 1985** (*Science*), **William E. Brownell** (born 1945) applied tiny electrical pulses to isolated cylindrical **outer hair cells (`~12,000 per ear`)** under a microscope and watched them **physically lengthen and contract like microscopic pistons at up to `20,000 times a second`** (`powered by a voltage-sensitive motor protein packed in their walls that Jing Zheng and Peter Dallos identified on 11 May 2000 in Nature and named prestin — from "presto", because it dances at musical speeds to boost quiet sounds by 40 decibels, or 100-fold!`)!

### PART 04: 1977–1985 — Jim Hudspeth's Microsecond Tip-Link Trapdoors & Why Loud Noise Causes Ringing (`Tinnitus`) (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (why vision and smell use slow chemical cascades — while hearing uses a literal physical rope tied to a spring-loaded trapdoor):** **Caltech & UCSF, 1977–1985 & Birmingham, England, 1984**.
- **1977–1985 — A. James Hudspeth, David Corey & James Pickles discover the mechanical tip-link spring:**
  - In `049` (*Colour Vision*), we saw that when light hits your eye, it triggers a chain of chemical reactions that takes **`20 to 50 milliseconds` (`thousandths of a second`)**. If your ears were that slow, speech and music at thousands of cycles per second would blur into noise!
  - In **1977–1983** (*PNAS* & *Journal of Neuroscience*), American biophysicists **A. James Hudspeth** (born 1945) and **David P. Corey** (born 1951) used a microscopic glass probe to nudge the staircase bundle of **`30 to 300` bristles (`stereocilia`)** atop a single hair cell — and proved that potassium ions rush in within **`~10 microseconds` (`ten millionths of a second — 1,000× faster than vision!`)**, meaning the ion channel must be pulled open **mechanically by a physical spring**!
  - In **1984**, British researcher **James O. Pickles** took high-resolution electron micrographs and saw the spring with his own eyes: an ultra-fine molecular thread — a **tip link** (`made of cadherin-23 and protocadherin-15`, locking onto the **`TMC1`** channel pore) — stretching from the tip of each short bristle to the side of its taller neighbour! Tilting the staircase toward the tallest bristle pulls the rope taut and pops the trapdoor open; tilting it back lets the door snap shut!
- **Why loud noise causes permanent hearing loss & phantom ringing (`Tinnitus`):**
  - You are born with only **`~15,500` hair cells per ear** (`~12,000` outer + `~3,500` inner) — compared with **126 million photoreceptors** in each eye!
  - Unlike birds, reptiles, and fish (which can regrow damaged hair cells), **human hair cells never divide or regenerate once destroyed** (`above ~85–100+ decibels`, when acoustic shockwaves snap tip links and kill hair cells). And when a patch of hair cells dies and stops sending signals on its frequency channel, your brain's **auditory cortex** turns up its internal volume gain looking for the missing input — creating the phantom ringing of **tinnitus**!
