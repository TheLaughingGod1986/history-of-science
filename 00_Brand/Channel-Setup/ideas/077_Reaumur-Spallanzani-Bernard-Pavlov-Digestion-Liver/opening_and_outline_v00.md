### PART 02: 1752 & 1780 — Réaumur's Pet Buzzard, Spallanzani's Swallowed Linen Bags & the Chemical Stomach (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why for two thousand years doctors fought over whether the stomach cooked food by heat, rotted it like a compost heap, or ground it like a stone mill — until a French physicist in 1752 stuffed raw meat inside tiny iron pipes and fed them to his pet buzzard!):** **Paris, 1752 & University of Pavia, Italy, 1780**.
- **1752 (*Mémoires de l'Académie Royale des Sciences*) — René-Antoine de Réaumur & his pet kite/buzzard:**
  - In the early 1700s, the **iatromechanist** school (`led by Giovanni Alfonso Borelli, who saw birds swallow gravel for their muscular gizzards`) claimed that the stomach digested food purely by **mechanical grinding and crushing**, while rival **vitalists** claimed food simply **fermented or rotted (`putrefaction`)** in the warm belly.
  - In **1752** in Paris, French physicist and naturalist **René-Antoine Ferchault de Réaumur** (1683–1757) designed an ingenious test: since birds of prey (`kites, buzzards, owls`) naturally regurgitate indigestible pellets (`bones and feathers`), Réaumur placed pieces of raw meat and bone inside **rigid, open-ended or grated metal tubes (`tin, lead, and iron`)** that no stomach muscle could crush, and fed them to his **pet buzzard (`buse`)**!
  - When the buzzard coughed the metal tubes back up hours later, the iron tubes weren't even dented — yet the meat and bone inside had been **completely dissolved into liquid** (`with zero smell of rotting putrefaction`)!
  - Even better, when Réaumur stuffed **small sponges** inside the perforated metal tubes, squeezed out the liquid the bird's stomach had soaked into them, and warmed meat in that liquid in a glass dish, it dissolved meat **outside the body**!
- **1780 (*Dissertazioni di fisica animale, e vegetabile*) — Lazzaro Spallanzani swallows wooden tubes & linen bags:**
  - In **1780** in Pavia, Italian priest-biologist **Lazzaro Spallanzani** (1729–1799) extended Réaumur's experiments across frogs, snakes, sheep, eagles — and **himself**!
  - Spallanzani bravely swallowed **perforated wooden tubes** and **small linen bags** filled with chewed bread and meat (`recovering them after 23 to 47 hours`), and even made himself vomit on an empty stomach to collect his own **gastric juice** — proving that human digestion is a **pure chemical solvent process** (`later identified as hydrochloric acid by William Prout in 1823 and the enzyme pepsin by Theodor Schwann in 1836, observed directly through Alexis St. Martin's stomach window by William Beaumont in 1825–1833 in 032`)!

Sci. 266–307 & 461–495) perforated metal-tube & sponge gastric-juice experiment and Lazzaro Spallanzani (1780 Dissertazioni di fisica animale, e vegetabile) in-vitro vs in-vivo chemical digestion chart on period paper · SCALE: by locking meat inside an iron cage that no muscle could squeeze and watching it melt away anyway, Réaumur proved that your stomach isn't a rock crusher — it is an acid bath that unzips food molecule by molecule`
- **Ends on:** If the stomach's acid and pepsin (`Réaumur, Spallanzani, Beaumont`) dissolve meat, doctors assumed the **stomach was the whole story of digestion** — and that only **plants (`006`)** could build sugars, while animals only burned them! ### PART 03: 1848 – 24 March 1857 — Claude Bernard's Pancreatic Fat Proof, the Overnight Washed Liver (`Glycogen`) & the `Milieu Intérieur` — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — the stomach does all the real work of digestion, and animals can never manufacture their own sugar! Wrong! Between 1848 and March 1857 in Paris, Claude Bernard proved that the stomach is merely an entrance hall — and discovered by leaving a washed dead liver on his bench overnight that your liver manufactures its own animal starch (`glycogen`) to hold your internal ocean steady!"):** **Collège de France, Paris, 1848 – 24 March 1857 (& Harvard Medical School, 1926)**.
- **1848 (*Comptes Rendus*) — Claude Bernard discovers that the pancreas digests fat:**
  - When **Claude Bernard** (1813–1878) noticed that herbivorous rabbits normally pass alkaline, cloudy urine (`like plant-eaters`) yet pass clear, acidic urine (`like meat-eaters!`) when fasting or fed meat, he looked inside the small intestine and noticed that milky white fat droplets (`chyle`) only appeared in the lymph vessels **below the duct of the `pancreas`**!
  - In **1848**, Bernard proved that stomach acid leaves butter and fats untouched: it is **`pancreatic juice`** (together with liver **`bile`**) pouring into the small intestine that emulsifies fats and splits them into fatty acids and glycerol (while neutralising stomach acid and finishing protein and starch digestion across the small intestine's vast villous surface)!
- **1855 – 24 March 1857 (*Comptes Rendus*) — The midpoint twist (`The washed liver that made sugar overnight`):**
  - Next, Bernard hunted for where blood sugar gets burned. Dogma held that only **green plants (`006`)** could synthesize sugar, so any glucose in an animal's blood had to come straight from sugar or starch in its meals.
  - Yet when Bernard fed dogs a diet of **100% pure meat (`zero carbohydrates`)** and measured blood entering the **liver** (`via the portal vein`) and leaving the liver (`via the hepatic veins`), he found **zero sugar entering the liver — and abundant glucose pouring OUT of the liver**!
  - Sceptics claimed the liver was just slowly leaking trapped meal sugar. Then one day in **1855**, pressed for time, Bernard measured the sugar in a fresh liver immediately after death, attached a hose to its blood vessels, and **flushed the liver with cold running water for 40 minutes until the water ran completely clear and tested at ZERO sugar**!
  - Leaving the dead, bloodless, sugar-free liver on his laboratory table overnight, Bernard came back 24 hours later, tested it again — and found the washed liver **brimming with fresh glucose**!
  - On **24 March 1857** (*Comptes Rendus*), Bernard isolated the snow-white, starch-like precursor stored inside liver cells and named it **`glycogen`** ("sugar-former"): your `1.5-kg liver` acts as a chemical buffer that turns excess meal glucose into `glycogen` and breaks it back down between meals — inspiring Bernard's famous **1859–1865** law of the **`milieu intérieur`**: *"The constancy of the internal environment is the condition for a free and independent life"* (`coined as "homeostasis" by Harvard physiologist Walter Bradford Cannon in 1926–1929`, and completed when Banting and Best `035` discovered insulin in 1921)!

### PART 04: 1897–1904 — Ivan Pavlov's Digestive Pouches, Sham Feeding & How the Brain Primes the Gut (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open (everyone has heard of "Pavlov's dogs" — yet almost nobody knows that Ivan Pavlov hated psychology, never used a bell in his famous experiments, and won the 1904 Nobel Prize in Medicine for discovering how your brain operates your stomach and pancreas like a telegraph wire!):** **Institute of Experimental Medicine, St Petersburg, Russia, 1897–1904**.
- **1897 (*Lectures on the Work of the Principal Digestive Glands*, English trans. 1902) — Ivan Pavlov & the `Cephalic Phase` of digestion:**
  - Before Russian physiologist **Ivan Petrovich Pavlov** (1849–1936, `inspired as a youth by George Henry Lewes's Physiology of Common Life!`), scientists tried to study digestion by cutting open animals under acute surgery — which shocked the nervous system and shut digestion down.
  - At the Institute of Experimental Medicine in St Petersburg in the **1890s**, Pavlov perfected aseptic surgical techniques (`001`) to create a miniature side-pouch of the stomach (**`the Pavlov pouch`**) with its **`vagus nerve`** wiring completely intact: whenever the main stomach digested a meal, the clean side-pouch secreted pure, uncontaminated gastric juice in exact lockstep!
  - Using **"sham feeding"** (where swallowed food exited a harmless neck opening before ever touching the stomach), Pavlov proved in **1897** that within five minutes of a dog tasting, smelling, or merely **seeing** a bowl of meat, the brain fires electrical signals down the **`vagus nerve`** that trigger a flood of acid and pepsin in the stomach (**`psychic / cephalic secretion`**) — and cutting the vagus nerve abolished that response immediately!
- **1901–1904 — From digestive reflexes to `Conditioned Reflexes` (and the 1902 `Secretin` surprise):**
  - Furthermore, Pavlov showed that the pancreas tailors its enzyme recipe to the meal (pouring out more starch-digesting amylase for bread, more protein-digesting trypsinogen for meat, and more fat-splitting lipase for milk), while his students **W. N. Boldyreff** and **I. F. Tolochinov** noticed that the dogs began secreting saliva and gastric juice at the **footsteps or white coat of the laboratory assistant** bringing dinner — launching the science of **`conditioned reflexes`** and earning Pavlov the **1904 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** (`"in recognition of his work on the physiology of digestion"`, right alongside William Bayliss and Ernest Starling's 1902 discovery `060` that once acid enters the duodenum, the chemical hormone `secretin` takes over from the vagus nerve)!

- **Ends on:** Every single time **Ivan Pavlov's** `vagus nerve` fires at the smell of dinner, that signal starts inside the **`1.4-kilogram` brain** inside your skull — a brain split cleanly down the middle into **two halves (`the left and right hemispheres`)** wired together by **200 million nerve fibres (`the corpus callosum`)**!
