For two thousand years, doctors fought a fierce war over a central mystery: what actually happens to your dinner after you swallow? Following Italian physicist **Giovanni Alfonso Borelli in sixteen eighty**, the **mechanical school** insisted the stomach was a muscular **grinding mill** that pulverized meat by sheer crushing force — but what if a stomach had no grinding teeth at all, or what if food simply **rotted and fermented** in the warm belly like a compost heap?

In **seventeen fifty-two** in Paris, naturalist **René-Antoine de Réaumur** found a brilliant way to test this puzzle. Because birds of prey naturally cough up indigestible pellets, Réaumur sealed chunks of raw meat and bone inside **rigid, perforated iron and tin tubes** that no stomach muscle could bend, and fed them to his **pet buzzard** — what would come back up?

When the buzzard regurgitated the tubes hours later, the iron cages weren't even dented — yet the meat inside had **melted into a clean liquid with zero smell of rotting**! Next, Réaumur stuffed **tiny sponges** inside the metal tubes, squeezed out the clear **gastric juice** the bird's stomach had soaked into them, and showed it dissolved meat in a dish! In **seventeen eighty** in Pavia, Italian priest-biologist **Lazzaro Spallanzani** bravely swallowed **perforated wooden tubes and linen bags** of food himself — proving human digestion is a pure chemical solvent (`later identified as hydrochloric acid by William Prout in eighteen twenty-three and the enzyme pepsin by Theodor Schwann in eighteen thirty-six`)!

If the stomach's acid and pepsin dissolve meat, doctors assumed the **stomach did all the real work of digestion** — and that only **green plants** could build sugars! Why was Parisian physiologist **Claude Bernard stunned in eighteen forty-eight and eighteen fifty-five** when he tested a **washed, bloodless liver** the morning after a dog died?

Case closed — the stomach does all the real work of digestion, and animals can never manufacture their own sugar! Wrong! Between **eighteen forty-eight and March eighteen fifty-seven** in Paris, **Claude Bernard** proved that the stomach is merely an entrance hall — and discovered by leaving a **washed dead liver** on his bench overnight that your liver manufactures its own animal starch (**`glycogen`**)!

First, in **eighteen forty-eight**, Bernard noticed that fat in food passed through the acid stomach completely untouched, turning into milky droplets only below the duct of the **`pancreas`** — proving that **`pancreatic juice`** (`with liver bile`) is the powerhouse that splits fats and finishes digestion in the small intestine!

Next, Bernard tackled a sacred scientific dogma: chemists insisted only **plants** could build sugar, so any glucose in your blood had to come straight from carbs in your meal. Yet when Bernard fed dogs **pure meat with zero sugar**, blood entering the liver had no sugar while blood leaving the liver poured out **fresh glucose**! In **September eighteen fifty-five**, Bernard attached a hose to a freshly removed liver and **flushed it with cold running water for forty minutes until every trace of sugar was zero**. Leaving that dead, washed-out liver on his table overnight, he tested it the next day — and found it **brimming with fresh sugar**! On the **twenty-fourth of March eighteen fifty-seven**, Bernard isolated the white starch-like reserve inside liver cells and named it **`glycogen`** ("sugar-former") — inspiring his **eighteen sixty-five** law that life depends on holding our **`internal environment` (`milieu intérieur`)** constant (`named "homeostasis" by Walter Cannon in nineteen twenty-six`)!

If the stomach, pancreas, and liver form a precision chemical refinery, how do they know **when** to pour out their juices? Why did **Ivan Pavlov in eighteen ninety-seven** win the **nineteen oh-four Nobel Prize** long before psychology textbooks talked about a bell?

Before Pavlov, scientists tried to study digestion by cutting open animals in acute operations — which terrified the animal and shut the stomach down. At the Institute of Experimental Medicine in **St Petersburg in eighteen ninety-seven**, Pavlov used gentle aseptic surgery to fashion a tiny, sealed side-pocket of the stomach — the **`Pavlov pouch`** — with its **`vagus nerve`** fibres completely intact. Whenever the main stomach worked, the clean little pouch mirrored it drop for drop!

Using **sham feeding**, where swallowed food slipped out of a harmless neck opening before ever touching the stomach, Pavlov made a stunning discovery: within **five minutes** of a dog merely **seeing, smelling, or tasting** a bowl of meat, the brain fired signals down the **`vagus nerve`** that made the empty stomach pour out tens of millilitres of pure acid and pepsin (**`the cephalic phase of digestion`**) — and cutting the vagus nerve stopped that psychic flow cold! When Pavlov noticed that the dogs began secreting juice at the mere **footsteps or white coat** of the assistant bringing dinner (`while William Bayliss and Ernest Starling in nineteen oh-two proved that the hormone secretin takes over in the intestine`), he uncovered **`conditioned reflexes`** and won the **nineteen oh-four Nobel Prize**!
