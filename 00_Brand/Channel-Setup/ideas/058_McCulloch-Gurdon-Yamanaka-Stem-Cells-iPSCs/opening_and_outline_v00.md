### PART 02: February 1961 & February 1963 — Ernest McCulloch, James Till & the Spleen Bumps That Proved Adult Stem Cells Exist (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (how your body replaces two million red blood cells every second without running out):** **Ontario Cancer Institute, Toronto, February 1961 – February 1963**.
- **The post-Hiroshima radiation puzzle:**
  - Every red blood cell in your bloodstream has no nucleus (`it ejects its nucleus to pack in more oxygen-carrying haemoglobin!`) and wears out after **`~120 days`**. That means your bone marrow must manufacture **`~2 million` new red blood cells every single second** (`~200 billion every day`), plus billions of white blood cells and platelets!
  - After the atomic bombings of **1945**, doctors knew that a lethal dose of radiation destroys the bone marrow and kills by blood failure — yet injecting healthy bone-marrow cells could mysteriously save an irradiated mouse's life. Nobody knew whether red cells, white cells, and platelets came from separate parent cells or one master cell.
- **February 1961 & 2 February 1963 — McCulloch & Till count bumps on a mouse spleen (*Radiation Research* & *Nature*):**
  - In **February 1961** (*Radiation Research*), Toronto physician **Ernest McCulloch** (1926–2011) and biophysicist **James Till** (born 1931) injected measured doses of healthy bone-marrow cells into irradiated mice. Ten days later, they noticed tiny round nodules (`colonies`) growing on the mice's spleens — and the number of nodules matched the exact number of injected marrow cells!
  - Using harmless radiation-induced chromosome markers (`with graduate student Andrew Becker and Lou Siminovitch`, **2 February 1963** in *Nature*), they proved that **every single nodule containing millions of red cells, white cells, and platelets had grown from ONE single cell** — and that cell had also made brand-new copies of itself!
  - That was the first rigorous quantitative proof of **stem cells** (`specifically hematopoietic stem cells, the two defining powers of every stem cell: self-renewal + differentiation, which now save tens of thousands of leukaemia and blood-disease patients every year via bone-marrow transplants`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Conrad H. Waddington (1905–1975) — British developmental biologist · WHO: Andrew J. Becker (born 1936) — Canadian medical researcher · WHEN: February 1961 · WHEN: 2 February 1963 · FACT: 1 cell builds all blood types · DATA: inked Till & McCulloch spleen-colony linear dose-response & clonal stem-cell chart on period paper from Till & McCulloch (February 1961, Radiation Research 14: 213–222) and Becker, McCulloch & Till (2 February 1963, Nature 197: 452–454) showing 1 bone-marrow hematopoietic stem cell (CFU-S, ~1 in 10,000 marrow cells) both self-renewing and differentiating into erythrocytes (2 million/sec), leukocytes, and megakaryocytes/platelets · SCALE: only about one cell in every ten thousand inside your bone marrow is a true blood stem cell — yet that tiny handful of master cells manufactures two million fresh red blood cells every single second of your life`
- **Ends on:** Adult bone-marrow stem cells could build blood — but they couldn't build a brain, a heart, or an eye. For decades, biologists believed that as an embryo grows into an adult, specialized cells **throw away or permanently lock** the genes they don't need. Could a fully grown adult cell still hold the recipe for an **entire animal**?

### PART 03: 1949 & December 1962 — The Boy Ranked 250th Out of 250 Who Cloned a Frog From a Gut Cell — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — once a cell becomes an intestinal cell or a skin cell, its unused genes are lost forever! Until a student whose teacher called him 'a total waste of time' proved the textbooks wrong."):** **Eton College, 1949 & University of Oxford, December 1962**.
- **The worst biology report card in history:**
  - In **1949**, 15-year-old **John B. Gurdon** (born 1933) finished **250th out of 250** in his year group in biology at Eton (`and stood in the bottom group in every other science subject`). His science master wrote a blistering report that Gurdon later framed above his desk for sixty years: *"I believe he has ideas about becoming a Scientist; on his present showing this is quite ridiculous… it would be a sheer waste of time, both on his part, and of those who have to teach him."*
- **1952 vs December 1962 — Why Briggs & King thought adult nuclei were locked, and how Gurdon's tadpole broke the dogma (*J. Embryol. Exp. Morphol.*):**
  - In **1952**, American embryologists **Robert Briggs** and **Thomas King** transplanted nuclei from very early frog embryos (`blastulae`) into frog eggs, hatching tadpoles — but when they tried nuclei from older cells, the eggs failed to develop. Almost every biologist concluded that **differentiated cells permanently lose or inactivate their genes**.
  - At Oxford in **1958–December 1962** (*Journal of Embryology and Experimental Morphology*), graduate student **John Gurdon** tested that dogma on the South African clawed frog (***Xenopus laevis***). Using an ultraviolet lamp to destroy the egg cell's own nucleus, he took a **fully differentiated epithelial cell from the lining of a feeding tadpole's intestine** (`a cell already specialized with a brush border to absorb food!`) and injected its nucleus into the empty egg.
  - When the first transplants stalled, Gurdon tried **serial nuclear transplantation** — and healthy, swimming **cloned tadpoles and fertile adult frogs** hatched right out of the dish!
  - **The twist:** Every specialized cell in your body — skin, gut, muscle, or nerve — **keeps the complete, intact 3-billion-letter DNA blueprint for the entire organism**, and the fluid inside an egg cell contains molecules that can **rewind** an adult nucleus back to the beginning (`the exact principle Ian Wilmut and Keith Campbell used on 5 July 1996 at the Roslin Institute in Scotland to clone Dolly the sheep — the first mammal cloned from an adult mammary cell!`)!

### PART 04: 25 August 2006 & November 2007 — Shinya Yamanaka's 24-Gene Hunt & the 4-Gene Time Machine (`iPSCs`) (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (how two researchers in Kyoto tested 24 genes at once — and discovered that just 4 switches turn adult skin back into an embryo):** **Kyoto University, Japan, 2000–25 August 2006 & November 2007**.
- **From orthopaedic surgeon to stem-cell detective:**
  - Japanese physician **Shinya Yamanaka** (born 1962) began his career as an **orthopaedic surgeon**, moved by patients with spinal-cord injuries and crippling diseases that surgery couldn't cure. Starting his own small lab in Nara and then **Kyoto University**, he set out on a mission most scientists thought would take decades: find the exact genes that keep embryonic stem cells **pluripotent** (`able to become any cell in the body`) and see if adding them to an adult skin cell could rewind it.
- **25 August 2006 (*Cell*) — Kazutoshi Takahashi & Shinya Yamanaka's brilliant subtraction experiment:**
  - Yamanaka and postdoctoral fellow **Kazutoshi Takahashi** (born 1977) drew up a list of **24 candidate genes** active in mouse embryonic stem cells.
  - When putting any **single** gene into adult mouse skin cells (`fibroblasts`) did **nothing**, Takahashi tried a daring gamble: he put **all 24 genes in at the exact same time**! Two weeks later, round **embryonic-like stem-cell colonies** appeared in the dish!
  - Then came the detective work: they ran 24 new dishes, **leaving out one gene at a time** (`24 minus 1`) to see which genes were actually required!
  - By **25 August 2006** (*Cell*, and in **human adult skin cells in November 2007** in *Cell*, alongside **James Thomson's** independent team in *Science*), they proved that just **FOUR transcription factor genes — `Oct3/4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc` (now famous worldwide as the "Yamanaka Factors")** — wipe clean an adult skin cell's epigenetic bookmarks and rewind it into an **Induced Pluripotent Stem Cell (`iPSC`)**!
  - From a tiny, painless pinch of a patient's own skin (`or even a drop of blood!`), doctors and scientists can now grow **that patient's own beating heart cells, insulin-making beta cells (`035`), retinal eye cells (`049`), or dopamine brain neurons** in a dish to test drugs and repair damaged tissue (`winning John Gurdon and Shinya Yamanaka the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: James A. - **Ends on:** If **stem cells** and **Yamanaka factors** can rewind a cell's clock and build fresh tissue, why don't our normal body cells simply keep dividing forever? Why do normal human cells stop dividing after **fifty divisions** — and what is the **molecular countdown clock** ticking away at the tips of your chromosomes?

### PART 05: Why Your DNA Never Deletes Its Chapters — & the Countdown Clock That Makes Us Age (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (the misconception that brain cells, heart cells, and skin cells contain different genes — when every nucleated cell in your body carries the exact same 20,000-gene encyclopedia, controlled by epigenetic bookmarks):** Many people still assume a heart cell has only "heart genes" and a skin cell has only "skin genes". In reality, as **John Gurdon** and **Shinya Yamanaka** proved, almost every cell in your body carries the **entire 3-billion-letter DNA manual**! What makes a skin cell different from a neuron is **epigenetics** — molecular bookmarks (`methyl tags and histone spools`) that prop open the chapters a cell needs and clip shut the rest, until the **four Yamanaka Factors** remove the clips and open the whole book again!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - If your cells carry the full blueprint for youth, why do our bodies **grow old**?
  - For thirty-four years (`1912 to 1946`), Nobel laureate **Alexis Carrel** kept a dish of **chicken heart cells** beating in New York, convincing the entire world that normal body cells are **naturally immortal**!
  - How did **Leonard Hayflick in December 1961** prove that Carrel's experiment was a mistake — and that normal human cells can divide **only about fifty times (`the Hayflick Limit`)** before hitting a wall?
  - And how did Russian theorist **Alexey Olovnikov** (`watching a subway train in a Moscow station in 1971`) and **Elizabeth Blackburn, Jack Szostak, and Carol Greider on Christmas Day, 25 December 1984**, discover the repeating **shoelace caps (`telomeres`)** at the tips of your chromosomes — and the immortal enzyme **`telomerase`** that rewinds the countdown clock (`059` candidate *Why Do Our Cells Have a Built-In Countdown Clock That Makes Us Age (`Telomeres, the Hayflick Limit & Telomerase`)?*)?
