Every red blood cell in your body wears out after **one hundred and twenty days** — which means your bone marrow has to manufacture **two million brand-new red blood cells every single second**, plus billions of white blood cells and platelets!

After **nineteen forty-five**, doctors knew that heavy radiation destroys the bone marrow and causes fatal blood failure, yet injecting healthy bone marrow could save a mouse's life. Nobody knew whether red cells, white cells, and platelets came from separate parent cells or one master cell — and in **nineteen fifty-seven**, biologist **Conrad Waddington** pictured cell development as a marble rolling down a hill into deep valleys from which it could never climb back up.

Then, in **February nineteen sixty-one** at the Ontario Cancer Institute in Toronto, physician **Ernest McCulloch** and biophysicist **James Till** injected measured doses of healthy bone-marrow cells into irradiated mice. Ten days later, they noticed tiny round bumps growing on the mice's spleens — in exact proportion to the number of injected cells!

On the **second of February nineteen sixty-three** in *Nature*, with graduate student **Andrew Becker**, they used unique chromosome tags to prove a sensation: **every single bump containing a million red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets had grown from ONE single cell** — and that cell had also made brand-new copies of itself! They had proved the existence of **adult stem cells**!

Yet bone-marrow stem cells could only build blood — not a brain or a heart. Biologists still insisted that once a cell specializes, it **throws away or permanently locks** the rest of its DNA. Could an adult cell still hold the blueprint for an **entire animal**?

In **nineteen fifty-two**, senior embryologists **Robert Briggs and Thomas King** had failed to clone frogs from older cell nuclei, convincing everyone that mature cells lose their genes. Yamanaka and postdoctoral fellow **Kazutoshi Takahashi** drew up a list of **twenty-four candidate genes**. When adding any single gene to adult mouse skin cells did nothing, Takahashi tried a bold gamble: he put **all twenty-four genes into the skin cells at the exact same time**! Two weeks later, round **embryonic stem-cell colonies** bloomed in the dish!

How many of the twenty-four genes were doing the magic? By running twenty-four new dishes and **removing one gene at a time** (`twenty-four minus one`), they watched which dishes failed — and on the **twenty-fifth of August two thousand and six** in *Cell* (`and in adult human skin cells in November two thousand and seven`), they stunned the world!

Just **four master switch genes — `Oct3/4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc`, the "Yamanaka Factors"** — are enough to wipe clean an adult skin cell's epigenetic bookmarks and rewind it into an **Induced Pluripotent Stem Cell (`iPSC`)**! From a painless pinch of a patient's skin, scientists can now grow that patient's own **beating heart cells, retinal eye cells, or brain neurons** in a dish — winning **John Gurdon and Shinya Yamanaka** the **two thousand and twelve** Nobel Prize!

Many people still assume that a heart cell contains only "heart genes" and a skin cell contains only "skin genes". Why did **John Gurdon** and **Shinya Yamanaka** prove that belief wrong?

Because almost every cell in your body carries the **exact same three-billion-letter DNA encyclopedia**! What makes a skin cell different from a brain neuron is **epigenetics** — chemical sticky notes and bookmarks that prop open the chapters a skin cell needs and clip the other chapters shut, until the **four Yamanaka Factors** sweep the bookmarks clean and open the entire book again!

That raises one last question: if every cell in your body holds the complete blueprint for youth, why do our bodies **grow old**? For thirty-four years (`from nineteen twelve to nineteen forty-six`), Nobel laureate **Alexis Carrel** kept a dish of **chicken heart cells** alive in New York, convincing the entire world that normal cells are **naturally immortal**! How did **Leonard Hayflick in December nineteen sixty-one** prove that Carrel's dish was a mistake — and that normal human cells can divide **only about fifty times (`the Hayflick Limit`)** before hitting a wall? And how did Russian theorist **Alexey Olovnikov** in a Moscow subway station and **Elizabeth Blackburn, Jack Szostak, and Carol Greider on Christmas Day, the twenty-fifth of December nineteen eighty-four**, discover the repeating **shoelace caps (`telomeres`)** at the tips of your DNA — and the immortal enzyme **`telomerase`** that rewinds the countdown clock?

A mouse spleen in Toronto, a framed report card at Oxford, and four genetic switches in Kyoto. Together, they proved your cells never forget how they began.
