### PART 02: 6 July 1924 & June 1938 — Hans Berger's Brainwaves & Nathaniel Kleitman's 32 Days in Mammoth Cave (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why evolution never got rid of sleep):** **Jena, Germany, 6 July 1924 & Mammoth Cave, Kentucky, 4 June – 6 July 1938**. From an evolutionary point of view, sleep looks like madness: while an animal sleeps, it cannot find food, cannot mate, and can be eaten by predators! Yet as Russian physician **Marie de Manacéïne** (`1894`) and Chicago researcher **Allan Rechtschaffen** (`1983`) proved, total sleep deprivation is **100% fatal** (`killing faster than starvation!`).
- **6 July 1924 — Hans Berger records the first human brainwaves (`EEG`):** Until the 1920s, most physiologists believed the sleeping brain simply went silent (`like turning off a motor`). On **6 July 1924** in **Jena, Germany**, psychiatrist **Hans Berger** (1873–1941) attached silver-foil electrodes to the scalp of a 17-year-old boy named Klaus (`keeping his discovery secret for five years until 1929 out of fear of being mocked!`) and invented the **electroencephalogram (`EEG`)** — proving that the living human brain hums with rhythmic electrical waves (`~10 Hz waking alpha waves` when eyes are closed, shifting to tall, slow **`0.5–4 Hz delta waves`** in deep sleep, mapped into sleep stages by **Alfred Loomis in 1935–1937**)!
- **4 June – 6 July 1938 — Living 32 days inside Mammoth Cave:** Was our daily sleep-wake cycle just a passive reaction to daylight and city noise, or did we have a clock wired inside our skulls? On **4 June 1938**, 43-year-old University of Chicago professor **Nathaniel Kleitman** (1895–1999) and his 23-year-old student **Bruce Richardson** carried two tall-legged beds (with legs standing in cans of rat poison to keep cave rats away!) **`120 feet` (`37 metres`) underground** into a pitch-black, `54 °F` (`12 °C`), utterly silent chamber of **Mammoth Cave, Kentucky**, for **32 days**.
  - They tried to force their bodies onto an artificial **28-hour day** (`19 hours awake, 9 hours asleep`, so 6 cave days equalled 7 real days).
  - While young Richardson slowly adapted, Kleitman's body temperature stubbornly rose and fell on its own **`~24-hour` cycle** — making him wide awake when the cave schedule said "sleep" and exhausted when it said "work," proving humans carry an endogenous **circadian clock**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Bruce Richardson (born 1915) — Chicago physiology researcher · WHEN: 6 July 1924 · WHEN: 1929 · WHEN: 4 June 1938 · WHEN: 6 July 1938 · FACT: Hid EEG five years · DATA: inked Mammoth Cave temperature & circadian rhythm chart on period paper from Nathaniel Kleitman (1939, Sleep and Wakefulness) comparing the imposed 28-hour cave schedule (6 cycles per 7-day week at 54 °F / 12 °C in total darkness for 32 days) against Kleitman's persistent ~24-hour body-temperature oscillation (36.1–37.1 °C, peaking every 24 hours regardless of the cave clock) · SCALE: locked a hundred and twenty feet underground in pitch darkness for thirty-two days, Nathaniel Kleitman's body temperature kept ticking on a twenty-four-hour rhythm like a wristwatch sealed inside a vault`
- **Ends on:** Kleitman had proved that an internal clock tells us *when* to fall asleep. Yet even Kleitman still assumed that once you fell asleep, your eyes and brain stayed quiet all night long — until he handed a dusty, broken-down polygraph machine to a penniless graduate student named **Eugene Aserinsky**.

### PART 03: 1952 to 4 September 1953 — Eugene Aserinsky Wires Up His 8-Year-Old Son Armond & Discovers REM Dreaming Sleep — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong — Aserinsky thought his rusty polygraph machine was broken!"):** **University of Chicago, 1952 – 4 September 1953**. In **1951**, 30-year-old graduate student **Eugene Aserinsky** (1921–1998) — trying to support his wife and young son on a tiny GI Bill stipend — was given the most boring assignment in Kleitman's lab: sit in the dark and watch the closed eyelids of sleeping babies.
- **1952 — Wiring 8-year-old Armond Aserinsky to a discarded Offner Dynograph:** Watching eyelids in the dark gave Aserinsky headaches. So he dug an early, temperamental **Offner Dynograph** polygraph recorder out of a basement storage room (`a vacuum-tube machine whose ink pens constantly clogged and drifted`) and decided to test it overnight on a convenient guinea pig: his own **8-year-old son, Armond Aserinsky**!
  - He taped electrodes beside Armond's eyes (`to measure the eyeball's natural electrical dipole, the electrooculogram or EOG`) and onto his scalp (`for EEG brainwaves`).
  - Around **two hours after Armond fell into deep, slow-wave delta sleep**, the ink pens suddenly went **completely wild** — scratching violent, rapid eye-movement spikes while the brainwave pen switched from slow sleep waves to **fast, low-voltage waves that looked as if Armond had woken right up**!
- **The twist — "The machine isn't broken — he's dreaming!":**
  - At first, both Aserinsky and Kleitman were convinced **the cheap, rusty Offner Dynograph was malfunctioning** (`an electrical leak or muscle twitch`)!
  - To prove it wasn't a machine glitch, Kleitman insisted on seeing it on his own teenage daughter ** Esther**, and they crept into the dark bedroom with a **flashlight (`torch`)** while the pens were going wild — watching the sleeper's eyeballs **darting rapidly back and forth beneath closed lids** while the body's skeletal muscles lay **completely paralysed** (`REM atonia`, later explained by **Michel Jouvet in 1959** so we don't act out our dreams!)!
  - When Aserinsky and Kleitman (`published in Science on 4 September 1953`) woke adult volunteers in the middle of **Rapid Eye Movement (`REM`) sleep**, **20 out of 27 awakenings (`74%`) produced vivid, detailed, story-like dreams** — compared with only **4 out of 23 (`17%`, and mostly vague thoughts)** during non-REM sleep! Together with **William Dement (`1957`)**, they proved the sleeping brain cycles every **`~90 minutes`** through a repeating staircase of deep slow-wave sleep and fiery REM dreaming!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Armond Aserinsky (1943–2012) — eight-year-old test subject · WHO: William C. Dement (1928–2020) — American sleep researcher · WHEN: 1952 · WHEN: 4 September 1953 · WHEN: 1957 · FACT: Thought machine was broken · DATA: inked polygraph & dream-recall bar chart on period paper from Eugene Aserinsky & Nathaniel Kleitman (4 September 1953, Science 118: 273–274) showing 20 of 27 awakenings during Rapid Eye Movement (REM) periods yielding vivid dream recall (74.1%) vs 4 of 23 awakenings during non-REM ocular quiescence (17.4%), recurring in ~20-minute bursts every ~90 minutes · SCALE: four or five times every single night, your brain switches into a dreaming state where it burns nearly as much oxygen and electricity as it does when you are wide awake solving a maths puzzle`
- **Ends on:** After 1953, scientists knew that **REM sleep** is when we dream, consolidate emotional memories, and rewire creative connections (`like Otto Loewi's 1921 Easter dream in 033 and Mendeleev's 1869 periodic table dream in 002!`). But what about the *other* half of the night — the deep, dreamless **slow-wave sleep**? Why do you die if you lose it?

### PART 04: 18 October 2013 — Maiken Nedergaard Discovers the Brain's Nighttime Dishwasher (`The Glymphatic System`) (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (the puzzle of how a sealed skull takes out its rubbish):** **University of Rochester Medical Center, New York, 2012 – 18 October 2013**. Every organ in your body burns fuel and produces toxic chemical rubbish. Below your neck, a network of tubes called the **lymphatic system** collects that cellular waste and dumps it into the bloodstream to be cleaned by your liver and kidneys.
- **The paradox — the brain has no lymphatic pipes inside its tissue!**
  - Your brain weighs only **2% of your body weight (`~1.4 kilograms`)**, yet it burns **20% of all your body's energy** — generating grams of toxic protein waste every month, including **adenosine (`045`)** and sticky **beta-amyloid** and **tau** proteins (`the plaques that build up in Alzheimer's disease`).
  - Yet when anatomists looked inside brain tissue, there were **no lymphatic vessels at all**! How could the busiest organ in the body clean out its own trash?
- **18 October 2013 — Looking into a living sleeping brain (`Science`):**
  - Danish neuroscientist **Maiken Nedergaard** (born 1957), working with **Jeff Iliff** (`TED 1.74M views`) and **Lulu Xie** at the University of Rochester, trained a **two-photon laser microscope (`042`)** into the brains of living mice while they were **awake** and while they were in **deep slow-wave sleep** (`published in Science on 18 October 2013`).
  - What they saw stunned neuroscience: as soon as the brain switched from waking into **deep slow-wave delta sleep** (`when noradrenaline drops`), the brain's support cells (**glial cells / astrocytes**) literally **shrank in volume**, causing the fluid channels between brain cells (`the interstitial space`) to **expand by more than 60% (`from 14% to 23% of brain volume`)**!
  - Through those newly opened floodgates — which Nedergaard named the **glymphatic system** (`glial + lymphatic`) — clear **cerebrospinal fluid (`CSF`)** pumped in along the outsides of pulsing arteries (`driven by your heartbeat and slow delta waves!`), swept through the brain tissue at **10 to 20 times the waking rate**, and washed **beta-amyloid plaques out of the brain twice as fast as when awake**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Jeff Iliff (born 1976) — American neuroscientist · WHEN: 2012 · WHEN: 18 October 2013 · FACT: 2% weight, 20% energy · DATA: inked two-photon tracer & beta-amyloid clearance chart on period paper from Xie, Kang, Xu, Iliff & Nedergaard (18 October 2013, Science 342: 373–377) showing interstitial fluid space rising >60% (from 14% awake to 23% in slow-wave sleep), CSF tracer influx increasing ~10–20×, and radiolabelled 125I-beta-amyloid clearance rate doubling during sleep vs wakefulness · SCALE: your brain cannot think at full speed and power-wash its own hallways at the same time — so every night it shrinks its support cells by sixty percent and turns on a high-pressure dishwasher while you sleep`
- **Ends on:** If your brain has to run both a **nighttime dishwasher (`deep slow-wave sleep`)** and a **memory-and-dream theatre (`REM sleep`)** every ninety minutes, what happens when modern electric light, late-night caffeine (`045`), and glowing screens cut your sleep short?

### PART 05: Why Every Hour of Sleep Counts — & How a Frozen Gas Cylinder Created Teflon and Bulletproof Kevlar (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (why cutting from 8 hours to 6 hours cuts out your final REM cycle and your brain wash):** Because deep slow-wave **"brain-washing" sleep** is front-loaded in the **first half of the night** and long **REM dreaming and memory-stitching bursts** are stacked in the **last two hours before morning**, sleeping only five or six hours doesn't just trim 25% of your rest — it can rob you of **up to 50–70% of your morning REM sleep** while leaving yesterday's metabolic waste unwashed!
- **From Jena (`1924`), Mammoth Cave (`1938`), and Armond Aserinsky's cot (`1952`) to Rochester (`2013`):** Four experiments proved that the third of your life spent in bed is not wasted time — it is the nightly maintenance crew that keeps every memory, synapse (`033`), and thought alive.
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Eugene Aserinsky discovered REM sleep because he refused to ignore a strange reading on a piece of laboratory equipment everyone else thought was broken.
  - Fourteen years earlier, on the morning of **6 April 1938** in **Deepwater, New Jersey**, 27-year-old DuPont chemist **Roy Plunkett** opened the valve on a steel cylinder of compressed refrigerant gas — and **not a single hiss of gas came out**, even though the "empty" cylinder still weighed the exact same on the scales!
  - When Plunkett sawed the steel cylinder in half, what slippery white powder did he find coated inside (`Teflon`), why did 41-year-old chemist **Stephanie Kwolek** in **1965** insist on spinning a cloudy, watery polymer batch that spun into a golden fibre **five times stronger than steel by weight (`bulletproof Kevlar`)**, and how did synthetic polymers transform the modern world (`047` candidate *Accidental Plastics & Bulletproof Kevlar*)?
