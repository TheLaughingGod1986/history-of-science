At first glance, sleep looks like an evolutionary blunder: while an animal sleeps, it cannot gather food, cannot guard its young, and lies helpless before predators. Yet total sleep deprivation is **one hundred percent fatal**!

Until the nineteen twenties, most doctors believed the sleeping brain simply went silent like a switched-off motor. Then, on the **sixth of July nineteen twenty-four** in Jena, Germany, psychiatrist **Hans Berger** placed two silver-foil electrodes on the scalp of a seventeen-year-old boy and recorded the **first human electroencephalogram — the EEG**!

Fearing colleagues would laugh at him, Berger kept his notebooks secret for **five years** until **nineteen twenty-nine**. His wavy ink lines proved the living brain never goes dead: when you close your eyes awake, it hums at **ten cycles per second** in **alpha waves**, and when you sink into deep sleep, it shifts into tall, rolling **one-to-four-cycle-per-second delta waves**!

Was our daily sleep cycle just a habit caused by sunrise and sunset, or did we have a clock wired inside us? On the **fourth of June nineteen thirty-eight**, forty-three-year-old University of Chicago professor **Nathaniel Kleitman** and his student **Bruce Richardson** carried two beds — with their legs set in tin cans to keep cave rats from climbing up — **one hundred and twenty feet underground** into a pitch-black, **fifty-four degree Fahrenheit** chamber of **Mammoth Cave, Kentucky**, for **thirty-two days** until the **sixth of July nineteen thirty-eight**.

In total darkness and silence, they tried to force their bodies onto an artificial **twenty-eight-hour day** — nineteen hours awake and nine hours asleep. Yet Kleitman's body temperature stubbornly rose and fell on its own **twenty-four-hour rhythm**, proving that every human carries a built-in **circadian clock**!

Even Kleitman still assumed the sleeping brain stayed quiet all night long — until he handed a dusty polygraph machine to graduate student **Eugene Aserinsky**. What happened when Aserinsky wired up his eight-year-old son?

In **nineteen fifty-one**, thirty-year-old Chicago graduate student **Eugene Aserinsky** was given the dullest job in Kleitman's lab: sit in the dark and stare at the eyelids of sleeping babies.

Tired of straining his eyes in the dark, Aserinsky hauled a discarded, vacuum-tube polygraph recorder called an **Offner Dynograph** out of a basement storage room in **nineteen fifty-two** and tested it overnight on his own **eight-year-old son, Armond** — taping electrodes beside Armond's eyes and onto his scalp.

Two hours after Armond fell into deep, slow-wave sleep, the ink pens suddenly went **wild** — scribbling rapid eye jerks while the brainwave pen switched to **fast, low-voltage waves that looked as if Armond were wide awake**!

Case closed? Wrong! At first, both Aserinsky and Kleitman were convinced **the rusty old Offner machine was broken**!

Only when they crept into the dark room with a hand torch and watched a sleeper's closed eyelids **darting rapidly back and forth** while the body lay completely still did they realise the truth!

When Aserinsky and Kleitman woke twenty adult volunteers during these **Rapid Eye Movement — or REM — bursts** and published their results in *Science* on the **fourth of September nineteen fifty-three**, **twenty out of twenty-seven awakenings — seventy-four percent — produced vivid, story-like dreams**, compared with just **four out of twenty-three — seventeen percent** — during quiet non-REM sleep! And with **William Dement** in **nineteen fifty-seven**, they proved the brain cycles every **ninety minutes** between deep slow-wave sleep and fiery **REM dreaming**, while temporarily paralysing your muscles so you don't act out your dreams!

REM sleep explained when we dream and stitch memories together. But what about the *other* half of the night — deep, dreamless **slow-wave sleep**? Why do you die without it?

For decades, anatomists faced a baffling riddle. Below your neck, a network of pipes called the **lymphatic system** collects cellular rubbish and flushes it out of your tissues.

Your brain weighs only **two percent of your body weight**, yet it burns a staggering **twenty percent of all your body's energy** — churning out toxic protein waste every single day, including **adenosine** and sticky **beta-amyloid plaques**, the hallmark of Alzheimer's disease. Yet inside brain tissue, there are **no lymphatic pipes at all**! How does a sealed skull take out its rubbish?

In **twenty twelve** and on the **eighteenth of October twenty thirteen** in *Science*, Danish neuroscientist **Maiken Nedergaard**, working with **Jeff Iliff** and Lulu Xie in Rochester, New York, aimed a two-photon microscope into living brains while they were awake and while they were in **deep slow-wave sleep**.

What they saw took their breath away. While you are awake and thinking, your brain cells are packed so tightly together that the fluid channels between them — the **interstitial space** — make up only **fourteen percent** of the brain's volume, allowing barely a trickle of fluid through.

The moment the brain drops into **deep slow-wave delta sleep**, its support cells — called **glial cells** — literally **shrink**, causing the channels between your neurons to **expand by more than sixty percent**, up to **twenty-three percent of the brain's volume**!

Through those wide-open floodgates — which Nedergaard named the **glymphatic system** — clear **cerebrospinal fluid** rushes through brain tissue at **ten to twenty times the waking rate**, washing out **beta-amyloid plaques twice as fast** as when you are awake!

If your brain runs a **nighttime dishwasher** in deep sleep and a **dream-and-memory theatre** in REM sleep, what happens when you cut your sleep from eight hours to six?

Some people boast that they can get by on five or six hours of sleep — or think losing two hours of sleep at the end of the night only costs a quarter of their rest. Why is that a dangerous trap?

Because your ninety-minute sleep cycles aren't identical! Your brain front-loads its **deep slow-wave dishwasher cycles** in the first half of the night, and stacks its longest, richest **REM dreaming and memory-stitching cycles** in the **last two hours before morning**. So cutting your sleep from eight hours to six doesn't just lose twenty-five percent of the night — it chops off **up to two-thirds of your morning REM sleep** while leaving yesterday's toxic waste unwashed!

Eugene Aserinsky discovered REM sleep because he refused to ignore a strange reading on a machine everyone else thought was broken. Fourteen years earlier, on the morning of the **sixth of April nineteen thirty-eight**, twenty-seven-year-old chemist **Roy Plunkett** opened the valve on a frozen steel cylinder of refrigerant gas — and **not a single hiss of gas came out**, even though the "empty" cylinder still weighed the exact same on the scales! When Plunkett sawed the steel cylinder in half, what slippery white super-material did he find inside — and why did chemist **Stephanie Kwolek in nineteen sixty-five** insist on spinning a cloudy, buttermilk-like liquid that turned into **bulletproof Kevlar**, five times stronger than steel by weight?

Two beds in a Kentucky cave, an eight-year-old boy wired to a rusty polygraph, and brain cells shrinking by sixty percent in the dark. Together, they proved that going to sleep is how your brain stays alive.
