  - In **1612** in Padua, Italian physician **Santorio Santorio** (1561–1636, friend of **Galileo**, `031`) took the air thermoscope, curved its long glass tube so a patient could hold the bulb **inside their mouth** (`or grip it in their hand`), and added a **graduated scale** (`counting ten ticks of a pendulum`), inventing the **first clinical thermometer** to measure human body heat!

  - Over 16 years at the University of Leipzig clinic, Professor **Carl Reinhold August Wunderlich** (1815–1877) and his staff recorded **more than 1,000,000 armpit temperature readings** from **25,000 patients**, publishing his landmark book in **1868**:
    - He established **`37.0 °C` (`98.6 °F`)** as the famous mean temperature of a healthy adult, with an normal healthy range from **`36.2 °C` to `37.5 °C`** (`97.2–99.5 °F`, with modern oral studies showing averages closer to `36.6 °C / 97.9 °F`).
    - Crucially, Wunderlich proved that your temperature is **never flat**: it follows a daily **circadian wave (`061`)**, dropping to its lowest point around **4:00 a.m.** and peaking around **4:00–6:00 p.m.**!
    - And he proved that **fever is not a disease in itself** — it is a lawful, measurable response where typhus, malaria (`048`), and pneumonia each trace a signature temperature curve (`and any temperature above 38.0 °C / 100.4 °F marks a clinical fever`)!

### PART 04: 1948 & 1975 — Paul Beeson's Immune Pyrogens & Matthew Kluger's Fever-Seeking Iguanas (4:55–6:45)
