### PART 02: 17 February 1842 & 1 October 1924 — William Bowman's Microscopic Cup & Alfred Richards's Quartz Needle (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why for two centuries after Marcello Malpighi spotted tiny red spheres in the kidney in 1666, nobody knew how blood turned into urine — until a 25-year-old London surgeon injected coloured dye into a kidney in 1842 and triggered an 80-year battle between "vitalists" and "mechanists"!):** **King's College Hospital, London, 17 February 1842 & University of Pennsylvania, Philadelphia, 1 October 1924**.
- **17 February 1842 (*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society*) & 1844 — William Bowman's capsule & Carl Ludwig's mechanical sieve:**
  - On **17 February 1842**, 25-year-old English surgeon **William Bowman** (1816–1892) read a landmark paper before the Royal Society showing that each tiny capillary ball (`the glomerulus`, ~`200 micrometres` wide) sits tucked inside the blind, cup-like beginning of a winding urine tube (`now called Bowman's capsule`, together forming the **`nephron`** — **~1 million in each human kidney, whose tubules would stretch 80 kilometres end to end!**).
  - Bowman (`and later Rudolf Heidenhain in 1874`) believed living cells lining the tubule actively *secreted* urine using a vital force.
  - Two years later, in **1844** in Marburg, German physiologist **Carl Ludwig** (1816–1895) proposed a radical physical heresy: the glomerulus is a passive **hydrostatic pressure sieve (`ultrafiltration`)** where heart blood pressure squeezes protein-free plasma into Bowman's capsule, and the tubule simply concentrates it back down!
- **1 October 1924 (*American Journal of Physiology*) — Joseph Wearn & Alfred Newton Richards puncture a single living glomerulus:**
  - For 80 years, neither side could prove its case because no instrument on Earth was small enough to sample fluid from a single `200-micrometre` Bowman's capsule without tearing the kidney apart.
  - Using Robert Chambers's new micromanipulator at the University of Pennsylvania, **Joseph T. Wearn** (1893–1984) and **Alfred Newton Richards** (1876–1966) heated quartz rods, pulled them into microscopic needles **`10 micrometres` wide** (`sharpened on an Arkansas oilstone`), and guided the quartz tip into a single `Bowman's capsule` on the translucent edge of a living frog's kidney.
  - Published on **1 October 1924** (*Am. J. Physiol.*), their 1-microlitre droplets proved **both** sides half-right: Carl Ludwig was right that the glomerulus is a **purely physical pressure filter** (`the fluid inside Bowman's capsule was blood plasma stripped of large proteins, packed with sugar and salt`) — and the winding tubule then **actively reabsorbs `100%` of the glucose and `~99%` of the water and salt** back into the blood!

- **Ends on:** If the kidney only needs to get rid of **1.5 litres of waste** a day, how much fluid do those two million glomeruli actually squeeze out of your blood every 24 hours? Why did **Homer Smith's 1934** measurement with **Jerusalem artichoke sugar** shock every doctor in the world — and why on Earth would evolution design a kidney that dumps **a whole bathtub of water** every day just to take 99% of it back?

### PART 03: 1934–1953 — Homer Smith's `180-Litre` Shock & Why Fish Evolution Built Your Kidney Backwards — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Why would an engineer build a factory that throws away ninety-nine percent of its raw material every forty minutes and spends all day picking it back up off the floor? It wouldn't — unless that factory started out four hundred million years ago inside a prehistoric fish drowning in fresh river water!"):** **New York University School of Medicine, 1934–1953**.
- **1934–1935 (*Journal of Clinical Investigation*) — Homer Smith & James Shannon measure the human `GFR` (`125 mL/min = 180 L/day`) with `Inulin`:**
  - You couldn't stick quartz micropipettes into a living human patient's kidney. To measure how fast human glomeruli filter blood (**`the Glomerular Filtration Rate, or GFR`**), NYU physiologist **Homer W. Smith** (1895–1962) and **James A. Shannon** (1904–1994) needed a harmless tracer molecule small enough to pass freely through the glomerular sieve, yet **neither reabsorbed nor secreted** by the nephron tubules.
  - In **1934–1935** (*J. Clin. Invest.*, independently discovered with Alfred Richards), Smith found the perfect molecule in **Jerusalem artichoke tubers**: a starch-like plant sugar called **`inulin`**.
  - By infusing `inulin` into blood and measuring how fast it appeared in urine, Smith proved that two healthy human kidneys filter **`125 millilitres of plasma every single minute`** — **`7.5 litres an hour`, or `180 litres every 24 hours`** (`36 times your entire blood plasma volume, and more than 3 times your total body weight!`)!
- **Midpoint twist (1932–1953, *From Fish to Philosopher*, & Werner Kuhn's 1951 `Loop of Henle` countercurrent multiplier) — How freshwater fish & land vertebrates rigged the nephron:**
  - Why does your kidney filter `180 litres` a day only to reabsorb `178.5 litres`? In his classic evolutionary work (*From Fish to Philosopher*, 1953), **Homer Smith** solved the riddle!
  - Around **400 million years ago**, when our early vertebrate fish ancestors migrated from the salty ocean into **freshwater rivers**, their salty blood had a much higher solute concentration than the river water — so **fresh water flooded relentlessly into their bodies by osmosis (`071`)**! To stop from swelling up and bursting like Peter Agre's frog eggs (`071`), freshwater fish evolved **high-pressure glomerular sieves** to bail huge volumes of water *out* while clawing scarce salt back *in*.
  - Then, when vertebrates crawled onto **dry land** and evolved into reptiles, birds, and mammals, the problem flipped upside down: now water was precious! Evolution couldn't delete the glomerular sieve without killing the animal — so instead, mammals evolved a brilliant hairpin plumbing attachment (**`the Loop of Henle`**, mapped by **Friedrich Gustav Jakob Henle in 1862** and explained as a **`countercurrent salt multiplier`** by Swiss chemist **Werner Kuhn in 1951**) plus hormone-controlled **`aquaporin` water gates (`071`)** that use a hyper-salty kidney core to suck **99% of the `180 litres` of water right back into the blood**!

### PART 04: 17 March 1943 – 11 September 1945 — Willem Kolff's Sausage-Casing Artificial Kidney in Nazi-Occupied Holland (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open (fifteen patients in a row died on his machine — until on the eleventh of September 1945, a 67-year-old woman in a terminal uremic coma was hooked up to twenty metres of cellophane sausage casing spinning in a porcelain laundry tub, opened her eyes after eleven and a half hours, and asked where she was!):** **Groningen & Kampen Municipal Hospital, Netherlands, October 1938 – 11 September 1945**.
- **October 1938–1942 — Jan Bruning's death in Groningen & Willem Kolff's tin-can sausage experiment:**
  - In **October 1938** at the University of Groningen, 27-year-old physician **Willem Johan Kolff** (1911–2009) watched helplessly as a 22-year-old farmer's son, **Jan Bruning**, died slowly and agonizingly of kidney failure (`generating just 20 grams of toxic urea a day that his kidneys couldn't clear`).
  - Remembering Scottish chemist **Thomas Graham's 1861** discovery of **`dialysis`** (`that small molecules like urea diffuse through microscopic pores in parchment while large proteins stay behind`) and German physician **Georg Haas's 1924–1928** brief blood-washing experiments in Giessen, Kolff realized he now had two new weapons: **`Heparin`** (`to stop blood clotting outside the body`) and cheap commercial **`cellophane` sausage casings** (`regenerated cellulose tubing from the Visking Company in Chicago`)!
  - Kolff poured blood spiked with urea into a **45-centimetre strip of cellophane sausage skin**, tied both ends, and shook it up and down in a bath of salt water (`saline`): **within 5 minutes, practically all the poisonous urea had diffused out into the salt water while every single red blood cell stayed safely inside the sausage skin**!
- **17 March 1943 – 11 September 1945 (*Acta Medica Scandinavica* 1944) — The Kampen laundry-tub kidney & Patient #16 Sofia Schafstadt:**
  - When the Nazis invaded the Netherlands in **May 1940** and installed a Dutch Nazi chief at Groningen University, Kolff refused to serve under him and moved to a tiny hospital in the rural town of **Kampen** (where he also hid Jewish refugees and Dutch resistance fighters by faking uremia lab tests for them!).
  - Working covertly with local enamel-factory engineer **Hendrik Berk**, Kolff built a rotating horizontal drum out of **wooden slats** (after aluminium from a downed bomber ran short), wrapped **30 to 40 metres of cellophane sausage tubing** around it, spun it in a **100-litre porcelain enamel bath of warm Ringer's salt water** using a **sewing-machine motor** and a **Ford Model T water-pump coupling** (`so gravity moved the blood through the turning spiral without crushing red blood cells!`).
  - Starting on **17 March 1943** (published in **1944** in *Acta Medica Scandinavica*), Kolff treated **15 desperate, dying end-stage kidney patients** — and **all 15 died**. Critics called his contraption lethal folly.
  - Then, on **11 September 1945**, Kolff dialysed Patient #16 — 67-year-old **Sofia Maria Schafstadt**, who lay in a deep uremic coma with complete kidney shut-down (`acute renal failure`). After **11.5 hours** on the spinning sausage-casing drum, Kolff flushed **60 grams of toxic urea** out of her blood. Sofia woke up from her coma, recovered her kidney function, and **lived another 7 years** — proving the **artificial kidney (`hemodialysis`)** worked (`and after Kolff gifted his remaining machines to London, Montreal, and Mount Sinai New York, and Belding Scribner invented the permanent Teflon-Silastic blood shunt in 1960, chronic dialysis went on to save tens of millions of lives`)!
