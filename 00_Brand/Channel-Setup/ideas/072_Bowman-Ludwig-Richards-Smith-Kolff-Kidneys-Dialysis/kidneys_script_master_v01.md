For nearly two centuries after **Marcello Malpighi in sixteen sixty-six** spotted tiny red globes inside the kidney, nobody knew how blood turned into urine. Then on the **seventeenth of February eighteen forty-two**, twenty-five-year-old London surgeon **William Bowman** injected coloured dyes into kidney blood vessels and read a landmark paper at the Royal Society: each microscopic capillary ball (**`the glomerulus`**) sits tucked inside the blind, cup-like funnel of a winding urine tube — now called **`Bowman's capsule`**, forming the **`nephron`** (`one million in each kidney, whose hair-thin tubes would stretch eighty kilometres end to end!`).

Bowman (`and Rudolf Heidenhain in eighteen seventy-four`) believed living cells lining the tube actively secreted urine by a vital force. Two years later, in **eighteen forty-four**, German physiologist **Carl Ludwig** proposed a physical heresy: heart blood pressure mechanically **squeezes protein-free plasma through the glomerulus like a sieve**, and the tube simply concentrates it!

For eighty years, neither side could prove its case — until **Joseph Wearn** and **Alfred Newton Richards** in Philadelphia pulled molten quartz into needles **`ten micrometres` wide** and guided the tip into a single **`Bowman's capsule`** in a living frog's kidney! Published on the **first of October nineteen twenty-four**, their one-microlitre droplets proved **both** sides half-right: the glomerulus is Carl Ludwig's **physical pressure sieve**, and the winding tubule then **actively pumps one hundred percent of the sugar and ninety-nine percent of the water and salt** back into the blood!

In **October nineteen thirty-eight** in Groningen, twenty-seven-year-old Dutch doctor **Willem Kolff** watched helplessly as a young farmer's son died of kidney failure.
