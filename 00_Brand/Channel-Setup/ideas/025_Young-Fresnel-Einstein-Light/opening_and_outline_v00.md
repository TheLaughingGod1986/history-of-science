### PART 02: The Man Who Read the Rosetta Stone and Split a Sunbeam (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (quirky character & polymathy):** **London, 1801**. Known to his Cambridge classmates as **"Phenomenon Young"**, twenty-eight-year-old physician **Thomas Young** (1773–1829) could read fluently by age two, learned a dozen languages including Ancient Egyptian (later becoming the first person to decipher the phonetic royal name *Ptolemy* inside the cartouches on the **Rosetta Stone** in **1814–1819**!), discovered how the human eye focuses (astigmatism) and how three colour receptors in our retina see every colour (**Young-Helmholtz trichromatic vision**), and walked a tightrope for fun!
- Studying sound waves and human speech, Young notices how two organ pipes of slightly different pitch produce pulsing **beats** — moments where two sound waves reinforce each other into a loud roar, then cancel each other into silence.
- **The Bakerian Lecture (24 November 1801 & 24 November 1803):** What if **Christiaan Huygens** (1629–1695) had been right in **1690** that light is also a wave, and **Isaac Newton** (`009`) had been wrong?
- In a darkened room, Young drills a fine hole in a window shutter, covers it with a pinhole pricked in thick paper, uses a mirror to direct the narrow cone of sunlight across the room, and holds a thin **slip of card (`1/30th of an inch` thick)** edgewise in the middle of the sunbeam (and also tests two tiny pinholes or parallel slits cut close together in a screen).
- **The proof:**
  - Where the two halves of the split sunbeam overlap on the wall, the shadow of the card is filled with rainbow-fringed **bright and dark interference bands** — with a **bright white band right down the dead centre of the card's shadow**!
  - Then comes Young's masterstroke (**24 November 1803**): keeping the card in place, he brings a tiny opaque screen up to block the light passing on **just one side** of the card. Instantly, **all the bright fringes inside the shadow disappear**, leaving darkness!
  - If light were particles, letting *more* light through the second side could never make a bright spot go dark. Only **waves** can cancel crest-to-trough: **Light + Light = Darkness**!
  - Measuring the spacing of the fringes (`Δy = λL / d`), Young calculates the exact wavelengths of every colour of Newton's rainbow: from **`1/36,000th of an inch` (`~700 nm`) for extreme red** down to **`1/60,000th of an inch` (`~420 nm`) for extreme violet**!
- **The backlash (1802–1804):** Outraged that anyone would contradict Isaac Newton, young politician **Henry Brougham** (1778–1868) publishes scathing anonymous reviews in the *Edinburgh Review* (1803–1804), declaring Young's papers *"destitute of every species of merit"*! When Young prints a forty-page pamphlet answering Brougham in **1804**, the publisher reports that **only one single copy was ever bought**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Christiaan Huygens (1629–1695) — Dutch physicist and astronomer · WHEN: 1690 · WHEN: 24 November 1801 · WHEN: 24 November 1803 · WHO: Henry Brougham (1778–1868) — Scottish writer and politician · WHEN: 1804 · FACT: Also cracked Rosetta Stone · FACT: Pamphlet sold just 1 copy · DATA: inked interference path-difference diagram on period paper showing crest + crest = 2x amplitude (bright fringe at path difference 0, 1λ, 2λ) and crest + trough = 0 (dark fringe at path difference 0.5λ, 1.5λ), with Young's 1801 table from Red (1/36,000 in = ~700 nm) to Violet (1/60,000 in = ~420 nm) · SCALE: moving just one fifty-thousandth of an inch sideways on the wall turns bright sunlight into pitch darkness`
- **Ends on:** Ridiculed in Britain, Young gave up publishing on optics and went back to medicine and Egyptian hieroglyphs. How did a young French road engineer who couldn't even read English accidentally revive the wave theory fourteen years later — and walk straight into an ambush at the French Academy of Sciences?

### PART 03: The Trap That Backfired — Poisson's Bright Spot (3:05–4:55)
- **Cold open:** **Paris, March 1818 – 15 March 1819**. Unaware of Young's forgotten English papers, thirty-year-old French civil engineer **Augustin-Jean Fresnel** (1788–1827) — who had been suspended from his road-building job during Napoleon's Hundred Days in **1815** and began measuring shadows in his mother's village with a drop of honey in a hole in a metal sheet as a lens and a micrometer made by the village blacksmith! — works out the exact mathematics of wave diffraction.
- **The 1818 French Academy Grand Prix:** Hoping to bury the wave theory forever and prove Newton's particle theory right, the **French Academy of Sciences** sets its **1818 Grand Prix** competition on diffraction. The judging committee is stacked with powerful particle-theory ("emissionist") titans: **Pierre-Simon Laplace** (1749–1827), **Jean-Baptiste Biot** (1774–1862), and **Siméon Denis Poisson** (1781–1840), chaired by astronomer **François Arago** (1786–1853).
- **Poisson's "absurd" mathematical ambush:** Reading Fresnel's memoir, **Siméon Denis Poisson** spots a way to prove Fresnel's wave integrals ridiculous. Poisson calculates what Fresnel's equations predict if you shine a point of light onto a **solid, circular opaque disc**:
  - Because every point around the circular rim of the disc is the **exact same distance** from the centre of the shadow behind it, all the light waves bending around the edge must arrive at the dead centre of the shadow **in phase (crest-to-crest)**!
  - Therefore, Poisson announces in triumph, Fresnel's wave theory predicts that the **very centre of the dark shadow of a solid metal disc must contain a bright spot of light**, almost as bright as if the metal disc weren't there at all! *"Let M. Fresnel explain that absurdity!"*
- **Arago walks into the lab:** Instead of laughing Fresnel out of the room, committee chairman **François Arago** decides to actually run the experiment!
- Arago glues a **2-millimetre brass disc** onto a clean glass plate with a dab of wax and shines a point source of light onto it in a dark room.
- Looking into the dark circular shadow behind the metal disc with an eyepiece, Arago sees — **shining right in the dead centre of the dark shadow — a crisp, unmistakable bright star of light** (**Arago's spot / Poisson's spot**)!
- Poisson's own mathematical trap has produced the most spectacular proof of the wave theory in history! On **15 March 1819**, the Academy unanimously awards **Fresnel** the Grand Prix (and Fresnel soon invents the stepped **Fresnel lighthouse lens** that saves countless ships around the world!).
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: France, 1815 · WHO: Augustin-Jean Fresnel (1788–1827) — French civil engineer and physicist · WHEN: Paris, 1818 · WHO: Pierre-Simon Laplace (1749–1827) — French mathematician and astronomer · WHO: Jean-Baptiste Biot (1774–1862) — French physicist · WHO: Siméon Denis Poisson (1781–1840) — French mathematician and physicist · WHO: François Arago (1786–1853) — French physicist and astronomer · WHEN: 15 March 1819 · FACT: Used honey drop as a lens · DATA: inked circular-diffraction geometry diagram on period paper showing a 2 mm opaque metal disc where every ray from the circular rim travels the exact same path length $\sqrt{R^2 + L^2}$ to the centre of the shadow ($\Delta L = 0$), summing constructively to form Poisson's/Arago's bright spot · SCALE: behind a solid 2-millimetre metal coin-like disc, every wave around the rim meets in step to light up the dead centre of the dark shadow`
- **Ends on:** After Arago's bright spot in 1819 and James Clerk Maxwell's electromagnetic wave equations in 1865, every physicist on Earth agreed the debate was over forever: light is 100% a wave. Why did an experiment with ultraviolet light and a metal plate in 1902 blow the case wide open all over again?

### PART 04: The Ultraviolet Puzzle and Einstein's Heresy — Midpoint Twist (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Karlsruhe, Kiel & Bern, 1887–17 March 1905**. Ironically, in **1887**, while proving that **James Clerk Maxwell's** electromagnetic waves exist (`021`), German physicist **Heinrich Hertz** (1857–1894) notices something bizarre: his electric spark jumps across a gap more easily whenever **ultraviolet light** (`018`) shines on the metal electrodes!
- By **1902**, **Philipp Lenard** (1862–1947) measures the electrons knocked out of a clean metal plate by light (**the photoelectric effect**) and finds three rules that **violate wave physics completely**:
  1. If light is a wave, making a **red light** brighter (taller waves) should shake electrons harder and knock them out with more energy. Instead, even a **blindingly bright red light knocks out ZERO electrons** from sodium or zinc!
  2. Yet a **faint, dim violet or ultraviolet light** kicks electrons out **instantly** (with zero time delay)!
  3. Making the ultraviolet light brighter knocks out *more* electrons, but doesn't make each electron fly any faster — only **raising the frequency (colour)** of the light makes the electrons fly faster!
- **Einstein's 17 March 1905 "revolutionary" paper:** In **Bern, Switzerland**, twenty-six-year-old patent clerk **Albert Einstein** (1879–1955) writes to his friend Conrad Habicht in **May 1905** that of all the papers he is writing that year (including Special Relativity and $E = mc^2$!), only one is truly **"very revolutionary"**: his **17 March 1905** paper on **light quanta**!
- Building on **Max Planck's** (1858–1947) **December 1900** quantum constant $h$, Einstein proposes that light's energy isn't spread thinly across the wave front like butter on bread, but concentrated in tiny, indivisible bullets of energy (**light quanta**, later named **photons**) whose energy depends *only* on their frequency: **$E = h f$**!
- Why does bright red light fail while dim ultraviolet succeeds? Because knocking one electron out of a metal takes a fixed minimum toll (**the work function $\phi$**). Each red photon is like a child throwing ping-pong balls at a bowling pin: throwing a billion ping-pong balls a second never knocks the pin down! Each ultraviolet photon is a bowling ball: even a single ultraviolet photon has enough energy ($hf > \phi$) to knock an electron straight out of the metal (`$K_{\max} = hf - \phi$`)!
- **Even Planck thought Einstein had gone too far (1913–1921):** Physicists hated light quanta so much that when **Max Planck** recommended Einstein for the Prussian Academy in **1913**, he apologized for Einstein having *"missed the target in his speculations, as, for example, in his hypothesis of light quanta"*, and American experimentalist **Robert Millikan** (1868–1953) spent **ten years (1905–1915)** trying to prove Einstein's equation *wrong* — only to confirm Einstein's straight-line equation to the decimal place (`1916`), winning Einstein the **1921 Nobel Prize in Physics**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1887 · WHO: Heinrich Hertz (1857–1894) — German physicist · WHEN: 1902 · WHO: Philipp Lenard (1862–1947) — Hungarian-German physicist · WHEN: Bern, 17 March 1905 · WHO: Albert Einstein (1879–1955) — Swiss patent clerk and physicist · WHO: Max Planck (1858–1947) — German theoretical physicist · WHEN: December 1900 · WHEN: 1913 · WHO: Robert Millikan (1868–1953) — American experimental physicist · WHEN: 1916 · WHEN: 1921 · FACT: Nobel was for photoelectric, not E=mc2 · DATA: inked photoelectric threshold graph on period paper plotting maximum electron kinetic energy $K_{\max}$ against light frequency $f$ ($K_{\max} = hf - \phi$): below threshold frequency $f_0$ (red light), 0 electrons emitted regardless of brightness; above $f_0$ (blue/UV light), electron energy rises linearly with slope $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$ · SCALE: a billion red photons hit the metal like ping-pong balls and knock loose zero electrons — while a single ultraviolet photon hits like a cannonball and kicks an electron straight out`
- **Ends on:** So who was right after two hundred years — Isaac Newton, who said light is a particle, or Thomas Young, who proved light is a wave? What happens when you fire just one single photon at a time through Young's two slits?

### PART 05: Firing One Photon at a Time & How Your Phone Camera Sees (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** People often ask: *"Is light really a wave that sometimes looks like a particle, or particles bumping into each other to make a wave?"*
- **No:** as **Geoffrey Ingram Taylor** first tested in **1909** (exposing a photographic plate inside a light-tight box for three months with light so dim that at most one photon was in the box at a time) and modern single-photon detectors confirm, **each individual photon lands on the screen at a single tiny dot like a particle — yet over thousands of single photons, those individual dots build up Thomas Young's exact wave interference stripes**!
- Each photon doesn't bounce off other photons: **each single photon's quantum wave travels through both slits at once and interferes with itself**, before landing as a single particle at one pixel (`wave-particle duality`)! And in **1924–1927**, **Louis de Broglie** and **Clinton Davisson & Lester Germer** proved that even matter particles like **electrons** (`004`) make interference stripes too — the birth of **quantum mechanics** and the **electron microscope** (`015`)!
- Every time your smartphone camera takes a picture or a rooftop solar panel charges a battery, it uses **Einstein's photoelectric effect** (one photon kicking one electron loose in silicon) after the light has been focused through a lens as **Young and Fresnel's wave**!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - And if quantum mechanics showed that nature operates on mathematical rules of information and probability, how did humans build a physical machine that could follow any mathematical rule ever written?
  - Long before silicon chips existed, a twenty-seven-year-old woman in London named **Ada Lovelace** wrote the world's first computer algorithm for a steam-powered brass engine in **1843** — and ninety-three years later, **Alan Turing** imagined a strip of paper tape that broke the "unbreakable" **Enigma machine** (`026` candidate *The First Computer & Breaking Enigma: Ada Lovelace & Alan Turing*, or `026` *Fritz Haber & Carl Bosch: Turning Air Into Bread*).
