### PART 02: 31 October 1794 & 28 July 1844 — John Dalton's Pink Geranium, the Scarlet Gown & His Dissected Eyeballs (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why did a pink flower change colour when the sun went down?):** **Manchester, England, 31 October 1794**. Twenty-eight-year-old Quaker teacher and chemist **John Dalton** (`who would soon propose atomic weights in 002 & 004!`) had always wondered why other people used strange words for colours (`to Dalton, red sealing wax and green grass looked almost the exact same shade of muddy brown, and when he bought his pious Quaker mother a pair of "drab, modest dark-blue" silk stockings, she cried out that they were bright scarlet red!`).
- **31 October 1794 — The pink geranium by candlelight:** In **1792**, Dalton looked at a **pink crane's-bill flower (*Geranium zonale* / *Pelargonium*)**. By daylight, the petals looked **sky-blue** to him. Yet when he carried the flower near a flickering **candle at night**, the petals suddenly looked **reddish-yellow**!
  - Testing everyone around him, Dalton discovered that nobody else saw the flower turn blue — **except his own brother Jonathan**, and nearly **one in twelve men** he tested (`while almost no women had the condition`)!
  - On **31 October 1794**, in his very first paper read to the Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society (*"Extraordinary facts relating to the vision of colours"*), Dalton gave the world its first scientific study of **colour blindness (`Daltonism`)** — and proposed a bold theory: that the clear jelly filling his eyeballs (`the vitreous humour`) must be **tinted blue**, absorbing red light like blue glass!
- **28 July 1844 — The post-mortem eye experiment that proved Dalton wrong:**
  - Dalton was so determined to test his blue-jelly hypothesis that he ordered his physician, **Joseph Ransome**, to **remove and dissect his eyes after he died**!
  - On **27 July 1844**, Dalton died at age 77. The next morning, **28 July 1844**, Ransome removed both of Dalton's eyes.
  - He drained the vitreous humour from the first eye into a watch glass — and saw that the fluid was **completely colourless and normal**, not blue at all!
  - Then Ransome sliced a tiny window in the back of Dalton's second eyeball and **peered right through Dalton's lens and eye jelly** at scarlet and green objects in the room: both red and green shone through **bright and unchanged**! The defect wasn't a blue filter in the jelly — it lay hidden in the **retina or the brain** (`and Ransome preserved Dalton's eyes in a glass jar in Manchester!`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1792 · WHEN: 31 October 1794 · WHEN: 27 July 1844 · WHEN: 28 July 1844 · FACT: Dissected his own eyes · DATA: inked daylight vs candlelight spectral transmission diagram on period paper from John Dalton (31 October 1794 / 1798 Manchester Memoirs 5: 28–45) showing why a pink geranium (reflecting both short-wavelength blue ~450 nm and long-wavelength red ~650 nm) looked sky-blue to Dalton in blue-rich daylight but reddish in red-rich candlelight, alongside Joseph Ransome's 28 July 1844 autopsy finding of 100% clear vitreous humour · SCALE: John Dalton waited fifty years — from his seventeen ninety-four lecture to the day after his death in eighteen forty-four — so his doctor could look through the back of his own eyeball and prove his blue-jelly theory wrong`
- **Ends on:** If the liquid inside Dalton's eye was crystal clear, why couldn't his retina tell red from green — and how had another British polymath already guessed the secret **forty-two years before Dalton died**?

### PART 03: 1802, 1852 & 1995 — Young and Helmholtz's Three Cones & Sequencing Dalton's 150-Year-Old Eye — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong — your eye doesn't have a receptor for every colour of the rainbow; it only has three!"):** **Royal Institution, London, 1802; Königsberg, 1852; & Manchester/Cambridge, 17 February 1995**. Since **Isaac Newton (`009`)** split sunlight with a prism in **1666**, physicists knew the rainbow has an infinite spectrum of wavelengths (`400 to 700 nanometres`).
- **1802 & 1852 — Why three receptors are enough (`Young-Helmholtz Trichromacy`):**
  - On **24 November 1801 (`025`)** and **1802**, physician **Thomas Young** (1773–1829) realised it would be physically impossible to pack an infinite number of different wavelength sensors onto every tiny pinpoint of your retina!
  - Instead, Young proposed in **1802** — and German physicist **Hermann von Helmholtz** (1821–1894) proved with quantitative colour-matching lamps in **1852** — that the human retina needs only **three types of colour-sensing cone cells**: **Short-wavelength (`Blue`, peaking at `~420 nm`)**, **Medium-wavelength (`Green`, `~534 nm`)**, and **Long-wavelength (`Red`, `~564 nm`)** (`backed up by Ewald Hering's 1878 opponent-process wiring in retinal ganglion cells`)!
  - When pure yellow sunlight (`580 nm`) hits your eye, it stimulates your Red and Green cones simultaneously. That's why a phone or TV screen with **only Red, Green, and Blue LEDs** can light up a Red pixel and a Green pixel side by side — and your brain sees **pure yellow** even though zero yellow wavelengths exist on the screen!
- **17 February 1995 — Extracting DNA from John Dalton's 150-year-old shrivelled eye:**
  - For 150 years, textbooks assumed John Dalton was missing his **red-sensitive cone (`protanopia`)**.
  - Because Joseph Ransome had saved Dalton's eyes in a jar at the Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society (`surviving even the 1940 Blitz bombing of Manchester!`), molecular geneticist **David Hunt** and visual neuroscientist **John Mollon** in **1994–1995** took tiny microscopic crumbs from Dalton's **150-year-old retina** and amplified his **DNA** (`published in Science on 17 February 1995`, building on **Jeremy Nathans's 1986** cloning of the opsin genes on the **X chromosome**)!
  - The DNA twist: Dalton actually had a perfectly normal **red-cone gene** — what he was missing was the **middle-wavelength green-cone gene (`deuteranopia`)**! And because both the red and green cone genes sit side by side on the **X chromosome** (`where men have only one copy, XY, while women have a backup copy, XX`), **1 in 12 men (`8%`)** vs only **1 in 200 women (`0.5%`)** are red-green colour-blind!

- **Ends on:** Having three cones explains *which* wavelengths your eye detects. But how does a cone or rod cell physically catch a **single particle of light (`a photon`)** and turn it into an electrical spark in your brain — and what does that have to do with eating **carrots (`Vitamin A`)**?

### PART 04: 1933–1958 — Franz Boll's Bleaching Frog Retina, George Wald's Vitamin A Switch & the 200-Femtosecond Snap (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (why does a freshly dissected retina glow red in the dark and turn pale yellow in sunlight?):** **Rome, 1876 & Berlin / Harvard, 1933–1958**. In **1876**, German physiologist **Franz Boll** (1849–1879) opened a frog's eye in a darkroom and saw that the retina was a striking **reddish-purple (`visual purple / rhodopsin`)** — until he held it up to the window, where sunlight bleached it **pale yellow in seconds** (`and Willy Kühne in 1877–1878 even used a living rabbit's eye like a camera to capture an "optogram" of a barred laboratory window on its retina!`)!
- **1933–1958 — George Wald discovers Vitamin A inside the eye (`11-cis-retinal`):**
  - Why did sunlight bleach the retina yellow? In **1933**, 26-year-old American biochemist **George Wald** (1906–1997), working in Berlin, Heidelberg, Zurich, and Harvard, extracted the bleached yellow pigment from hundreds of animal retinas — and discovered it was **retinal (`retinaldehyde`), a derivative of Vitamin A (`036`)** (`explaining why people with Vitamin A deficiency suffer from night blindness!`)!
  - In **1952–1958**, Wald and **Ruth Hubbard** (1924–2016) discovered the exact mechanical trick at the heart of all animal vision (`1967 Nobel Prize`):
    - In the dark, the Vitamin A molecule sits inside a pocket of an **opsin protein**, bent at a sharp kink at its eleventh carbon atom (**`11-cis-retinal`**) like a cocked mousetrap.
    - The instant a **single photon of light** hits that bent molecule, the energy snaps the kink **completely straight (`all-trans-retinal`) in just `200 femtoseconds` (`0.0000000000002 seconds`)**!
    - As the straightening molecule elbows the surrounding opsin protein out of shape, it triggers a cascade that amplifies that **one photon into hundreds of thousands of ion-channel changes**, sending an electrical nerve impulse (`033`) down the optic nerve to your brain (`before enzymes bend the Vitamin A molecule back into its kinked shape so you can see the next photon`)!

- **Ends on:** Because your three cone opsins wrap that exact same Vitamin A switch in slightly different protein pockets, one cone tunes to blue, one to green, and one to red. ### PART 05: Seeing Beyond the Human Rainbow — & How a Man Who Forgot Every 30 Seconds Revealed How Memories Form (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (why dogs have 2 cones, humans have 3, and birds have 4):** Most mammals (including dogs, cats, and bulls waving at red capes!) have only **two cone types (`dichromats`)** — yet early primates evolved a **third cone (`splitting red from green`)** to spot ripe fruit against green jungle leaves (`and in 2009, Jay and Maureen Neitz used gene therapy to add the human red-cone gene into colour-blind squirrel monkeys, letting them see red-vs-green dots for the very first time!`).
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Your eyes turn photons into electrical sparks in two hundred femtoseconds and stream **millions of images** into your brain every day.
  - But how does your brain **store** what you see as a **memory** that lasts 80 years — and why can you remember how to ride a bicycle after 20 years without trying, yet forget a person's name 30 seconds after meeting them?
  - Why did German psychologist **Hermann Ebbinghaus** in **1885** memorise **2,300 nonsense syllables (`WID, ZOF, KAX`)** to discover the **Forgetting Curve**, and what happened in **1953–1957** when neuropsychologist **Brenda Milner** asked a 27-year-old man whose **hippocampus** had been removed (**Patient H.M., Henry Molaison**) to trace a **five-pointed star in a mirror** — discovering that his hand grew near-perfect over three days even though he swore every morning he had never seen the star in his life (`050` candidate *How Do Memories Form & Why Do We Forget?*)?
