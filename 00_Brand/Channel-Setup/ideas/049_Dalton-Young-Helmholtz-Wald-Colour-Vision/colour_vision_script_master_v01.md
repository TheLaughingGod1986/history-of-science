Growing up in England, Quaker chemist **John Dalton** always wondered why people used such puzzling names for colours. To Dalton, red sealing wax and green grass looked almost the exact same shade of muddy brown — and when he bought his strict Quaker mother a pair of what he thought were "modest, dark-blue" silk stockings, she gasped that they were **bright scarlet red**!

Then, in **seventeen ninety-two**, Dalton looked closely at a **pink crane's-bill geranium flower**. By daylight, its petals looked **sky-blue** to him. Yet when he carried the flower near a flickering **candle at night**, the petals suddenly turned **reddish-yellow**!

Testing everyone around him, Dalton found that nobody else saw the flower turn blue — **except his brother Jonathan**, and nearly **one in twelve men** he tested! On the **thirty-first of October seventeen ninety-four** in Manchester, Dalton read the world's first scientific paper on **colour blindness** — and proposed a bold theory: that the liquid jelly filling his eyeballs was **dyed blue**, blocking red light like a blue glass window!

To test his own hypothesis, Dalton ordered his physician **Joseph Ransome** to **cut open his eyes after he died**! Dalton died on the **twenty-seventh of July eighteen forty-four**, and the very next morning, the **twenty-eighth of July eighteen forty-four**, Ransome removed Dalton's eyes, drained the jelly from the first eye, and cut a tiny window in the back of the second. The jelly was **completely clear** — and when Ransome looked right through Dalton's eyeball at scarlet and green ribbons, both colours shone through bright and unchanged!

If the liquid inside Dalton's eye was crystal clear, what was missing inside his retina — and how did DNA from Dalton's preserved eyeball finally solve the mystery **one hundred and fifty years later**?

Ever since Isaac Newton split sunlight with a prism in **sixteen sixty-six**, physicists knew the rainbow holds thousands of different wavelengths.

Case closed — does your eye have thousands of different colour sensors? Wrong! In **eighteen oh two**, English physician **Thomas Young** realised you could never pack thousands of different wavelength detectors onto every tiny spot of your retina!

Instead, Young proposed in **eighteen oh two** — and German physicist **Hermann von Helmholtz** proved with colour-matching lamps in **eighteen fifty-two** — that your retina builds the entire rainbow out of just **three types of cone cells**: **Short-wavelength Blue**, **Medium-wavelength Green**, and **Long-wavelength Red**, wired into opposing pairs as **Ewald Hering** showed in **eighteen seventy-eight**!

When pure yellow sunlight hits your eye, it tickles your Red and Green cones at the same time. That is why your phone or television screen can shine a tiny **Red pixel** and a **Green pixel** side by side — with **zero yellow light in the room** — and trick your brain into seeing a bright yellow lemon!

And what about John Dalton? After **Jeremy Nathans** mapped the red and green cone genes on the **X chromosome** in **nineteen eighty-six**, British scientists **David Hunt and John Mollon** on the **seventeenth of February nineteen ninety-five** took a tiny crumb from Dalton's **hundred-and-fifty-year-old pickled eye**, amplified his **DNA**, and proved Dalton wasn't missing his red cone as textbooks had claimed — he was missing the **middle-wavelength green cone gene**!

Having three cones explains *which* wavelengths your eye catches. But how does a cone physically turn a **single photon of light** into an electrical spark — and what does that have to do with **Vitamin A**?

Why did light bleach the retina yellow? In **nineteen thirty-three**, twenty-six-year-old American biochemist **George Wald** extracted that bleached yellow chemical and discovered it was **retinal — made directly from Vitamin A**, which is why Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness!

Then, between **nineteen fifty-two and nineteen fifty-eight**, Wald and **Ruth Hubbard** uncovered the mechanical trigger behind all animal sight, winning the **nineteen sixty-seven** Nobel Prize!

By wrapping that exact same Vitamin A switch in three slightly different protein pockets, your three cones tune to Blue, Green, and Red. What does the world look like to animals with **two cones** — or **four**?

Many people still believe a bull charges in a bullring because the matador's **bright red cape** makes it furious. Why would John Dalton have laughed at that idea?

Because bulls — like dogs, cats, and most other mammals — only have **two types of colour cones**, meaning a bull is **red-green colour-blind** and charges the whipping motion of the cape, not its red colour! Humans and other primates evolved a **third cone** to spot ripe red and orange fruit against green forest leaves — and in **twenty oh nine**, researchers even used gene therapy to give adult colour-blind monkeys that missing third cone so they could see red from green for the very first time!

Every second, your three cones stream millions of electrical sparks into your brain.
