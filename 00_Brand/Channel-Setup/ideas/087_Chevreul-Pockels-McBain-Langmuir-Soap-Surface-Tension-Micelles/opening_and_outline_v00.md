### PART 02: 1823 & 12 March 1891 — Michel Eugène Chevreul's `Saponification` Proof & How 19-Year-Old Agnes Pockels Built the First Surface-Tension Trough at Her Kitchen Sink (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (how can boiling greasy animal fat with stinging wood-ash lye create a bar of soap that washes grease away — and how did a 19-year-old woman with no university laboratory and no degree measure the invisible skin of water at her kitchen sink while washing her family's greasy pots?):** **Paris, 1823 & Brunswick, Germany, 1881–12 March 1891**.
- **1813–1823 (*Recherches chimiques sur les corps gras d'origine animale*) — Michel Eugène Chevreul unlocks fats, fatty acids & `saponification`:**
  - Since ancient Babylon and Rome, soapmakers boiled animal tallow or olive oil with alkaline potash from wood ashes (`or soda ash made by Nicolas Leblanc's 1791 process`), yet nobody knew the chemical reaction taking place in the kettle.
  - Between **1813 and 1823** in Paris, French chemist **Michel Eugène Chevreul** (1786–1889, `who later studied colour contrast at Gobelins and lived to age 102, 049!`) isolated the first pure fatty acids (**`stearic acid`**, **`oleic acid`**, **`margaric acid`**) and proved that every animal fat or vegetable oil (`triglyceride`) is an ester of three long, greasy **`fatty-acid` chains** clamped onto one sweet **`glycerol` (`glycerin`)** backbone!
  - When you boil fat with strong alkali (`sodium hydroxide, NaOH`), the alkali snaps those three ester clamps (**`saponification`**, literally "soap-making"), freeing the glycerin (used by Chevreul to invent dripless **stearin candles** that replaced smelly tallow candles across Europe!) and capping each long oily hydrocarbon tail (`C₁₇H₃₅−`) with a charged, water-loving sodium head (`−COO⁻Na⁺`)!
- **1881–12 March 1891 (*Nature*) — Agnes Pockels's kitchen-sink trough & her letter to Lord Rayleigh:**
  - Why does pure water bead up on greasy skin in the first place? Because water molecules pull on each other with cohesive **hydrogen bonds**, creating an elastic surface skin (**`surface tension`**, `72.8 millinewtons per metre at 20 °C`) strong enough to float a steel needle or a water strider insect!
  - In **Brunswick, Germany in 1881**, 19-year-old **Agnes Pockels** (1862–1935) wanted desperately to study physics, but German universities barred women (`so she read her younger brother Friedrich's university physics textbooks at home while caring for her ailing parents and washing the dishes!`).
  - Standing at her **kitchen sink**, Pockels noticed how tiny traces of soap or cooking grease dramatically altered the behaviour of water's surface skin. With household items, she built a **rectangular tin trough (`70 cm long, 5 cm wide, 2 cm deep`)** with a **movable tin strip (`slide`)** across the top to sweep or compress invisible oil and soap films, and measured the exact force needed to pull a **`6-mm` porcelain coat button** off the water's surface using a simple balance scale!
  - When she read in 1890 that British physicist **Lord Rayleigh** (`John William Strutt`, 1842–1919) was beginning to study oil films on water, she wrote him a letter on **10 January 1891** (`"My Lord, Will you kindly excuse my venturing to trouble you with a German letter on ascientific subject... Having heard of the fruitful researches carried on by you last year on the hitherto little understood properties of water surfaces, I thought it might interest you to know of my own observations on the subject"`). Rayleigh was so astonished by her precision that he immediately translated her letter and had it published in ***Nature* on 12 March 1891**!

- **Ends on:** **Chevreul** showed what soap is made of, and **Agnes Pockels** proved how soap relaxes water's surface skin at the top of the dishpan — but what happens **deep inside the soapy water** to make insoluble cooking grease vanish down the drain? Why did Britain's top chemists in **1913** laugh at **James William McBain** and call his answer **"McBain's nonsense"**?

### PART 03: 1913 — "McBain's Nonsense!" & How Two-Faced Soap Molecules Snap Into Grease-Trapping `Micelles` — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — soap washes away grease simply because soap dissolves into individual molecules in water and makes the water slippery! Wrong! In 1913 at the University of Bristol, a young physical chemist proved that individual soap molecules in water secretly gang up by the dozens to build microscopic spherical cages with an oily core inside — and when he presented his discovery in London, the chairman of the meeting laughed and called it 'McBain's nonsense!'"):** **University of Bristol & Royal Society of London, 1913**.
- **1913 (*Transactions of the Faraday Society*) — The Midpoint Twist (`James William McBain discovers amphiphilic soap "Micelles"`):**
  - At the **University of Bristol in 1911–1913**, Canadian chemist **James William McBain** (1882–1953) was puzzling over a bizarre anomaly in soapy water: when you boil soapy water, its boiling point barely rises (`as if the soap molecules had clumped into giant heavy particles`), yet when you pass an electric current through that same soapy water, it **conducts electricity brilliantly** (`as if it were full of fast, highly charged ions`)!
  - How could soap act like giant slow colloidal clumps *and* fast charged ions at the exact same time?
  - In **1913** (*Trans. Faraday Soc.*), McBain realized the secret lies in the **two-faced (`amphiphilic`)** shape of every soap molecule:
    1. One end is a **polar, negatively charged carboxylate head (`−COO⁻`)** that loves water (**`hydrophilic`**);
    2. The other end is a **long, non-polar 16-to-18-carbon chain (`−C₁₇H₃₅`)** that hates water (**`hydrophobic`**) and loves oil (**`lipophilic`**)!
  - Once you add enough soap to pass a threshold (**`critical micelle concentration, CMC`**), roughly **50 to 100 soap molecules** spontaneously snap together into a microscopic sphere (`~5 nanometres across`) that McBain named a **`micelle`**: every oily tail hides together in the dry centre away from the water, while all the negatively charged heads point outward to form a water-soluble shell!
  - When a droplet of cooking grease or dirt is scrubbed on your hands, the hydrophobic tails plunge into the grease droplet, wrapping it inside a water-loving **micelle cage** that floats effortlessly away in the rinse water!
  - When McBain presented his `micelle` model to London's senior physical chemists, the chairman scoffed: **"Nonsense, McBain!"** — yet by the 1920s and 1930s (`with G. S. Hartley's 1936 spherical model and X-ray diffraction`), **"McBain's nonsense"** was proven to be the fundamental law of every soap, detergent, and biological membrane (`071`) on Earth!

Faraday Soc. 9: 99–101) soapy water electrical conductivity vs osmotic pressure anomaly & 50–100 amphiphilic soap molecules self-assembling above the Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC) into a ~5-nm grease-trapping spherical micelle on period paper · SCALE: each soap molecule is like a tiny magnet with two opposite personalities — an oil-loving tail and a water-loving head — so fifty to a hundred of them team up into a microscopic sphere that hides a drop of grease on the inside while wearing a water-friendly disguise on the outside`
- **Ends on:** If soap molecules can build microscopic spheres inside water (`087`), what happens when they line up on the surface of **Agnes Pockels's trough** — and why does rubbing soap on your hands for **20 seconds** literally **rip deadly viruses (`064`) to shreds**?

### PART 04: 1917–1935 — Irving Langmuir & Katharine Blodgett's One-Molecule-Thick Films & Why 20 Seconds of Soap Shatters Viruses (4:55–6:45)
- **Cold open (can you measure the exact length and width of a single invisible molecule using nothing more than a ruler and a tray of water — and why is a bar of ordinary kitchen soap a deadlier weapon against enveloped viruses than almost anything else in your house?):** **General Electric Research Laboratory, Schenectady, New York, 1917–1935**.
- **1917 (*J. Am. Chem. Soc.*) & 1935 — Irving Langmuir & Katharine Blodgett prove surface films are one molecule thick (`Monolayers`):**
  - At the **General Electric Research Laboratory** in **1917** (*J. Am. Chem. Soc.*), American chemist **Irving Langmuir** (1881–1957, `1932 Nobel Prize in Chemistry`) refined **Agnes Pockels's 1891 kitchen-sink trough** into the **`Langmuir trough`** (and in **1931** helped ensure Pockels was awarded the Laura Leonard Prize and an honorary doctorate from Brunswick University at age 70!).
  - Spreading a tiny, known volume of fatty acid (`like stearic acid or olive oil`) onto water and pushing the barrier until the film resisted compression, Langmuir proved that the film stops compressing when it is **exactly one molecule thick (`a monolayer, ~2.5 nanometres`)** — and that every single molecule is **standing straight up on its head**, with its hydrophilic `−COOH` head buried in the water and its hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail sticking up into the air!
  - By dividing the volume of the oil drop by the area of the compressed film, Langmuir calculated the exact height (`~2.5 nm`) and cross-sectional area (`~0.21 nm²`) of a single molecule (`before X-ray crystallography could even see organic molecules, and directly inspiring Gorter and Grendel's 1925 discovery of our cells' 2:1 phospholipid bilayer membrane, 071!`). And in **1935**, Langmuir's colleague **Katharine Burr Blodgett** (1898–1979) stacked dozens of those single-molecule soap films onto glass (`Langmuir-Blodgett films`) to invent the world's first **non-reflective "invisible glass"** for camera lenses and microscopes!
- **Why 20 seconds of soap rips enveloped viruses (`064`) apart:**
  - Now look at a deadly enveloped virus like **influenza** or a **coronavirus (`064`)**: its genetic code is wrapped inside a fragile **lipid bilayer membrane (`fatty envelope`, `071`)** studded with spike proteins.
  - When you wash your hands with soap and water for **20 seconds**, the hydrophobic tails of billions of soap molecules (`which look almost identical to the virus's own membrane lipids!`) wedge themselves like **molecular crowbars** straight into the virus's fatty shell — **rupturing the viral envelope**, spilling its insides, and trapping the harmless fragments inside **McBain's `micelles`** to be flushed down the drain!

Soc. 39: 1848–1906) Langmuir-Pockels trough pressure-area isotherm (cross-sectional area ~0.21 nm²/molecule, height ~2.5 nm) & 20-second amphiphilic soap tail disruption of viral lipid bilayer envelopes on period paper · SCALE: by spreading a single drop of oil across Agnes Pockels's water trough and measuring the rectangle with an ordinary ruler, Irving Langmuir measured the height of a single molecule to within a billionth of a metre — and proved why twenty seconds of soap wedges open the fatty armour of a virus like a crowbar`
