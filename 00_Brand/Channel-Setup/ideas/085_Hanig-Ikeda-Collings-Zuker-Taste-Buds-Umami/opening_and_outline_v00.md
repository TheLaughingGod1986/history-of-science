### PART 02: 1867, 1901, 1942 & 1974 — Taste Buds, David Hänig's Brush & How Edwin Boring Accidentally Created the 100-Year "Tongue Map" Myth (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (if any six-year-old child can put a grain of salt or a drop of honey on the tip, sides, or back of their tongue and taste it in one second, how did every biology textbook on Earth spend a hundred years teaching a "Tongue Map" that anyone can disprove at the breakfast table?):** **University of Leipzig, 1901, Harvard University, 1942 & University of Pittsburgh, 1974**.
- **1867 & 1901 (*Philosophische Studien*) — Discovery of `taste buds` & David Pauli Hänig's threshold brush:**
  - Look at your tongue in a mirror: those tiny pink bumps (**`papillae`**) are *not* taste buds! In **1867**, German anatomist **Gustav Schwalbe** (1844–1916) and Swedish physician **Christian Lovén** (1835–1904) discovered that tucked into the moist trenches around each bump sit microscopic onion-shaped clusters of 50 to 100 sensory cells — the **`taste buds`** (`each poking microscopic hair-like microvilli out through a tiny taste pore into your saliva`)!
  - In **1901** at the University of Leipzig (*Philosophische Studien*), German researcher **David Pauli Hänig** asked whether all parts of the tongue's perimeter were equally sensitive to the four classical tastes (`sweet, salty, sour, bitter`).
  - Dipping a fine camel-hair brush into dilute solutions of **sucrose (`sweet`)**, **quinine (`bitter`)**, **hydrochloric acid (`sour`)**, and **table salt (`salty`)**, Hänig found that **every region around the tongue tasted all four flavours** — with only a tiny threshold difference (`the tip was slightly more sensitive to sweet, and the back slightly more sensitive to bitter`)!
- **1942 (*Sensation and Perception*) & 1974 (*Perception & Psychophysics*) — Edwin Boring's unlabelled graph & Virginia Collings's debunking:**
  - Forty-one years later, in **1942**, Harvard psychologist **Edwin Garrigues Boring** (1886–1968) translated Hänig's data into a graph in his book *Sensation and Perception in the History of Experimental Psychology* — plotting the reciprocals of Hänig's numbers **without a clear vertical scale**, so the low points of each curve touched zero!
  - Textbook authors misread "zero on Boring's relative graph" as **"zero taste sensitivity"** — drawing cartoon **Tongue Maps** with four separate zones!
  - In **1974** (*Perception & Psychophysics*), psychologist **Virginia B. Collings** at the University of Pittsburgh re-tested all regions of the human tongue (and the **soft palate** on the roof of the mouth!) using tiny `4-mm` filter-paper discs: she proved that **all tastes are detected everywhere taste buds exist**, and that the tiny threshold variations are utterly meaningless in real eating!

### PART 03: 25 July 1908 — Kikunae Ikeda's 38 Kilograms of *Konbu* Kelp & the Discovery of `Umami` — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — since ancient Greece, Aristotle had declared there are only four basic tastes: sweet, salty, sour, and bitter, and everything else is just smell! Wrong! - **1907–25 July 1908 (*J. Chem. Soc. Tokyo*) — The Midpoint Twist (`Kikunae Ikeda isolates 30 grams of glutamate from 38 kilograms of kelp and coins "Umami"`):**
  - Between **1899 and 1901**, Japanese chemist **Kikunae Ikeda** (1864–1936) studied physical chemistry in **Leipzig** (`the exact same university where Hänig was testing tongues in 1901!`), where he tasted German **tomatoes, asparagus, meat, and cheese** for the first time and noticed an unmistakable savory richness that also existed in Japanese ***dashi* broth** made from dried ***konbu* kelp** (*Saccharina japonica*).
  - Back at **Tokyo Imperial University in 1907**, Ikeda realized this savory taste could **never** be blended from sweet, salty, sour, or bitter. What single molecule inside kelp was responsible?
  - Using massive porcelain evaporating dishes, Ikeda boiled **38 kilograms (`84 pounds`) of dried *konbu* seaweed**, precipitated out the salts and mannitol, and slowly crystallized just **30 grams (`1 ounce`) of pure `L-glutamic acid` (`C₅H₉NO₄`)** — one of the 20 amino-acid building blocks of every protein (`080`)!
  - When neutralized with sodium into water-soluble **`monosodium glutamate` (`MSG`, patented on 25 July 1908)**, a single pinch on the tongue burst with pure savory flavour — which Ikeda named **`umami`** (from the Japanese *umai*, "delicious/savory", + *mi*, "essence/taste")!
- **1913 & 1960 — Shintaro Kodama & Akira Kuninaka discover the `8×` Umami Synergy (`Why parmesan + tomato or bacon + mushrooms taste explosive together`):**
  - Even better, Ikeda's student **Shintaro Kodama** in **1913** (`isolating inosinate / IMP from dried bonito fish flakes`) and microbiologist **Akira Kuninaka** in **1960** (`isolating guanylate / GMP from shiitake mushrooms`) discovered a stunning biochemical secret: when you combine **`glutamate`** (from tomatoes, cheese, or kelp) with **`ribonucleotides` (`IMP` or `GMP` from meat, fish, or mushrooms)**, they lock onto the taste receptor together and **multiply the `umami` signal up to eight times stronger** than either ingredient alone — explaining why humans across every culture pair **cheese with tomato, beef with mushrooms, and fish with kelp**!

### PART 04: 1999–2002 — Nirupa Chaudhari, Charles Zuker & Nicholas Ryba Discover the Molecular Locks for All Five Tastes (`T1R`, `T2R`, `ENaC` & `OTOP1`) (4:55–6:45)
- **Cold open (how do 50 to 100 microscopic cells inside a single taste bud tell the difference between a spoonful of sugar, a pinch of salt, a slice of lemon, a deadly bitter poison, and a bowl of savory broth — in less than a tenth of a second?):** **University of Miami, UC San Diego & NIH Bethesda, 1999–2002**.
- **1999–2002 (*Cell*, *Nature* & *Nature Neuroscience*) — Cracking the `T1R` and `T2R` receptor code:**
  - Just as **Linda Buck and Richard Axel in 1991 (`056`)** had found the G-protein-coupled receptors for smell in the nose, neuroscientists **Nirupa Chaudhari** and **Stephen Roper** (**2000**, *Nature Neuroscience*, identifying truncated **`mGluR4`** glutamate receptors in taste buds) and molecular biologists **Charles Zuker** (at UC San Diego) and **Nicholas Ryba** (at the NIH) between **1999 and 2002** (*Cell* & *Nature*) hunted the human genome (`080`) for the molecular locks on taste-bud cells:
    1. **Bitter (`2000`, *Cell* — The Poison Alarm):** Zuker and Ryba discovered a family of **~25 `T2R` (`TAS2R`) receptors** — all wired into the **same bitter taste cells** (`so whether a plant toxin is strychnine, cyanide, or quinine, your brain gets one urgent message: "SPIT IT OUT!"`)!
    2. **Sweet (`2001`, *Cell* — The Carbohydrate Fuel Gauge):** They discovered that two proteins — **`T1R2` + `T1R3`** — snap together as a pair (`heterodimer`) to form the **sweet receptor**, recognizing every natural sugar and artificial sweetener!
    3. **Umami (`2002`, *Nature* — Ikeda's 94-Year Vindication):** Then in **March 2002** (*Nature*), Zuker and Ryba discovered what happens when that same **`T1R3`** protein pairs up with **`T1R1`** instead (**`T1R1 + T1R3`**): it forms a dedicated **amino-acid / `L-glutamate` receptor** (`with a binding pocket where IMP and GMP clamp the hinge shut to boost the signal 8-fold!`), proving once and for all that **Ikeda's `umami` is a true fifth basic taste** (`designed by evolution to detect protein — including the rich free glutamate in human breast milk!`)!
    4. **Salty & Sour (`Ion Channels`, `071`):** Meanwhile, **salty** (`Na⁺` ions needed for every nerve spark, `033`) flows directly through **`ENaC` sodium channels**, and **sour** (`H⁺` protons from acids) flows through **`OTOP1` proton channels** (`identified by Emily Liman's team in 2018–2019`)!
  - And when Zuker and Ryba mapped where these receptors sit on the tongue, they found **all five types of taste cells inside individual taste buds across the front, sides, and back of the tongue** — driving the final nail into the coffin of the 1942 Tongue Map!
