For fifteen hundred years after Galen, doctors believed the lungs were just radiator fans that cooled a literal fire burning inside the heart. Even after William Harvey proved blood circulates and **Marcello Malpighi in sixteen sixty-one** spotted microscopic air sacs at the ends of the lung pipes, it took **Robert Hooke** on the **twenty-fourth of October sixteen sixty-seven** (`pumping continuous fireplace bellows into a lung at the Royal Society`) and physician **Richard Lower** in **sixteen sixty-nine** (*Tractatus de Corde*) to prove that dark purple venous blood turns **bright scarlet right inside the lungs** simply by touching fresh air!

Two centuries later, in **nineteen oh-four** in Copenhagen, **Christian Bohr** (`father of atomic physicist Niels Bohr`) and his young student **August Krogh** discovered the **`Bohr Effect`**: when exercising muscles pour **carbon dioxide and acid** into your blood, they shift **`haemoglobin`'s** grip and force it to **dump extra oxygen right where the muscles are starving**! Yet Christian Bohr (`and Oxford's John Scott Haldane`) clung to one stubborn myth: they insisted that at high altitude or during hard exercise, living lung cells actively **pumped (`secreted`)** oxygen into the blood against pressure!

To test his beloved teacher's theory, **August Krogh** and his wife, physician **Marie Krogh**, invented a hair-thin glass bubble analyser — **`the microtonometer`** — that measured the exact oxygen pressure of a single microscopic air bubble equilibrated against flowing blood. In **nineteen ten**, the Kroghs published **seven back-to-back papers** — famous across physiology as **`"The Seven Little Devils"`** — proving that oxygen pressure inside the lung air sacs (`alveoli`) is **always higher** than in the blood (`winning August Krogh the nineteen twenty Nobel Prize`)!

If oxygen enters the blood by simple diffusion, plain water in your blood plasma could dissolve less than **two percent** of the oxygen you need to stay alive! How does **`haemoglobin`** pack **seventy times more oxygen** into your blood — and why did **Max Perutz in Cambridge** spend **twenty-two years shooting X-rays at horse blood crystals** to watch haemoglobin *breathe*?

Ever since **Felix Hoppe-Seyler** crystallised **`haemoglobin`** in **eighteen sixty-two** and **Gilbert Adair** proved in **nineteen twenty-five** that each haemoglobin molecule carries **four iron `haem` groups**, chemists were baffled by its S-shaped oxygen curve. Unlike single-chain muscle **`myoglobin`** (`whose structure John Kendrew solved in nineteen fifty-eight`), haemoglobin is reluctant to pick up its *first* oxygen molecule, yet the instant that first oxygen binds, the remaining three iron seats become **hundreds of times more eager** to grab oxygen (**`cooperative binding`**)! How could four iron atoms sitting far apart in the same protein talk to one another?

Starting in **nineteen thirty-seven** at the Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge, **Max Perutz** spent **twenty-two years** shooting X-rays at horse haemoglobin crystals (`a giant protein of ten thousand atoms`).
