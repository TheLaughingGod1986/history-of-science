### PART 02: Bitter Willow Bark, a Baker's Oven & the Acid That Burned Stomachs (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (chewing on a tree):** **Chipping Norton, Oxfordshire, 1758–25 April 1763 & Europe, 1828–1876**. For more than **3,500 years** — from the **Egyptian Ebers Papyrus (`~1550 BC`)** and **Hippocrates (`~400 BC`)** — healers knew that chewing the bark of a **willow tree** cooled fevers and dulled pain.
- **Reverend Edward Stone's 50-patient trial (`1758 → 25 April 1763`):** In **1758** near Chipping Norton in Oxfordshire, clergyman **Edward Stone** (1702–1768) accidentally tasted a piece of **white willow bark (*Salix alba*)** and was shocked by its intense bitterness — tastes just like expensive Peruvian cinchona bark (`quinine`) used for malaria ("ague")!
  - Gathering a pound of willow bark, Stone dried it in a **baker's oven** for three months, pounded it into powder, and over **five years** tested small doses (`20 grains` / `~1.3 grams` every four hours) on **50 feverish patients**. Every single patient recovered — reported in his famous letter read to the **Royal Society on 25 April 1763**!
- **Isolating the crystal — and its agonising flaw (1828–1876):** In **1828–1829**, **Johann Andreas Buchner** and **Henri Leroux** isolated the bitter yellow crystal from willow bark and named it **salicin** (from *Salix*, Latin for willow); in **1838**, Italian chemist **Raffaele Piria** converted it into stronger **salicylic acid** (also extracted from **meadowsweet flowers, *Spiraea ulmaria***), and in **1876**, Scottish physician **Thomas Maclagan** proved it dramatically reduced rheumatic fever and joint swelling.
  - **The catch:** Patients had to swallow **4 to 6 grams a day** of salicylic acid — and the raw acid was so harsh and corrosive that it irritated the mouth, triggered violent nausea and ringing in the ears, and **made patients' stomachs bleed**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Raffaele Piria (1814–1865) — Italian chemist · WHEN: 1550 BC · WHEN: 1758 · WHEN: 25 April 1763 · WHEN: 1828 · WHEN: 1838 · WHEN: 1876 · FACT: Baked bark in a bread oven · DATA: inked extraction & dose diagram on period paper showing 1 pound of dried white willow bark (Edward Stone 1763, 50 fever patients cured with 20 grains / 1.3 g) yielding salicin (1828) and salicylic acid (Piria 1838), where arthritis patients required 4–6 grams per day of corrosive acid · SCALE: to calm swollen joints in the eighteen seventies, arthritis patients had to swallow up to six grams of raw salicylic acid every single day — a dose so caustic it stripped the lining of their stomachs`
- **Ends on:** How did a 29-year-old chemist in a German dye factory — desperate to spare his own father from that stomach-burning acid — tame the molecule on **10 August 1897**?

### PART 03: Felix Hoffmann's 10 August 1897 Notebook & Why Bayer Almost Shelved Aspirin — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong — his boss called it poison for the heart!"):** **Friedrich Bayer & Co., Elberfeld, Germany, 10 August 1897 – 1899**. In the 1890s, the **Friedrich Bayer** dye factory in Elberfeld opened a pharmaceutical lab led by chemist **Arthur Eichengrün** (1867–1949) and pharmacology chief **Heinrich Dreser** (1860–1924).
- **10 August 1897 — Felix Hoffmann's chemical shield:** Working at the bench was 29-year-old chemist **Felix Hoffmann** (1868–1946), whose father suffered from crippling rheumatism and couldn't stomach raw salicylic acid.
  - Building on an impure earlier reaction by Charles Frédéric Gerhardt (`1853`), Hoffmann on **10 August 1897** heated salicylic acid with **acetic anhydride**, swapping one harsh hydrogen atom for a gentle **acetyl group** to produce **100% pure, stable crystals of acetylsalicylic acid (`ASA`)**!
  - Even the name **"Aspirin"** (registered **6 March 1899**) tells the chemical story: **"A"** for **acetyl**, **"-spir-"** from ***Spiraea*** (the meadowsweet flower), and **"-in"**!
- **The shocking twist — Bayer rejects Aspirin while promoting "Heroin"!** When Hoffmann and Eichengrün handed pure acetylsalicylic acid to pharmacology boss **Heinrich Dreser**, Dreser **refused to test or market it**!
  - Why? Because Dreser claimed salicylic compounds **"enfeebled and poisoned the heart"** — and Dreser was busy championing *another* compound Felix Hoffmann had acetylated just **eleven days later, on 21 August 1897**: diacetylmorphine, which Bayer proudly marketed as a "non-addictive" cough medicine named **Heroin**!
  - Refusing to let acetylsalicylic acid die on the shelf, **Arthur Eichengrün** secretly tested the powder on himself (feeling zero stomach burn!) and slipped samples to Berlin doctors — who reported such miraculous pain and fever relief that Bayer was forced to launch **Aspirin in 1899**, turning it into the best-selling medicine in human history!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Arthur Eichengrün (1867–1949) — German chemist · WHO: Heinrich Dreser (1860–1924) — German pharmacologist · WHEN: 1853 · WHEN: 10 August 1897 · WHEN: 21 August 1897 · WHEN: 6 March 1899 · FACT: Boss feared it hurt the heart · DATA: inked molecular & gastric-irritation comparison chart on period paper from Hoffmann's 10 August 1897 notebook showing salicylic acid (exposed phenolic −OH group irritating stomach mucosa) vs acetylsalicylic acid (acetyl group −COCH3 shielding the stomach before releasing active salicylate in the bloodstream) · SCALE: swapping a single hydrogen atom for an acetyl group turned a corrosive crystal that burned the throat into a gentle tablet that passed safely through the stomach`
- **Ends on:** By the 1960s, humanity was swallowing billions of aspirin tablets every month. Yet how is it possible that **for 74 years (`1897–1971`)**, nobody knew how an aspirin tablet actually stops pain — or how a pill in your stomach knows where you hurt?

### PART 04: How Does a Pill Know Where It Hurts? John Vane's 1971 Prostaglandin Breakthrough (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (the everyday question):** **Royal College of Surgeons, London, June 1971**. When you swallow an aspirin for a stubbed toe or a headache, how does the pill "know" where to go?
- **It doesn't!** Aspirin dissolves into your bloodstream and travels **everywhere** in your body at once! So why do you only feel relief in your injured toe or aching head?
- **23 June 1971 — Sir John Vane discovers the COX enzyme lock:** At the Royal College of Surgeons in London, 44-year-old British pharmacologist **John Vane** (1927–2004) solved the 74-year mystery (published in *Nature New Biology* on **23 June 1971**, winning the **1982 Nobel Prize** alongside Sune Bergström and Bengt Samuelsson):
  - Whenever cells in your toe or head are injured or infected, an enzyme inside those cells called **cyclooxygenase (`COX-1` and `COX-2`)** converts fatty acids into local chemical alarm molecules called **prostaglandins**.
  - Prostaglandins sensitise your nerve endings (making them shout *"PAIN!"* to your brain), dilate local blood vessels (causing **swelling and inflammation**), and signal your brain's hypothalamus thermostat to raise your body temperature (**fever**)!
  - **What aspirin does:** As **Philip Majerus** and **Gerald Roth** proved in **1975**, aspirin permanently transfers its **acetyl group** (`the exact group Felix Hoffmann added on 10 August 1897!`) onto a specific amino acid (`Serine-530`) inside the **COX enzyme tunnel** — physically plugging the enzyme like a key snapped off inside a lock! Because only your injured cells are frantically churning out pain prostaglandins, shutting down COX across the body silences the pain **right where it hurts**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 23 June 1971 · WHEN: 1975 · WHEN: 1982 · FACT: Plugs the pain enzyme tunnel · DATA: inked enzyme-lock diagram on period paper from John Vane (1971) and Roth & Majerus (1975) showing arachidonic acid entering the cyclooxygenase (COX) enzyme channel to produce pain/fever prostaglandins and clotting thromboxane A2 vs acetylsalicylic acid permanently acetylating Serine-530 to block the channel by >95% · SCALE: a single three-hundred-milligram aspirin tablet — weighing less than five grains of rice — floods your bloodstream with a quintillion molecules that silence pain factories from head to toe`
- **Ends on:** Remember how Bayer's pharmacology boss in 1897 refused to release Aspirin because he claimed it was *"bad for the heart"*? What did doctors discover when they saw what blocking that exact same enzyme does inside **human blood platelets**?

- **Cold open (irony of medical history):** In 1897, Heinrich Dreser tried to block Aspirin because he thought it would harm the heart. Was he right?
- **The exact opposite — low-dose aspirin saves millions of hearts!** Because blood **platelets** (`tiny cell fragments with no nucleus to make new enzymes`) use the `COX-1` enzyme to make a sticky clotting chemical called **thromboxane A2**, a single low-dose **`75-to-81-milligram` baby aspirin** permanently disables a platelet's clotting factory for its entire **8-to-10-day lifespan** (pioneered clinically by **Lawrence Craven** in `1948–1956`, proved mechanistically by **Vane, Samuelsson, and Majerus** in `1971–1975`, and confirmed in the landmark **1988 ISIS-2 trial** of `17,187` heart-attack patients, where one month of low-dose aspirin cut vascular deaths by **23%**)!
