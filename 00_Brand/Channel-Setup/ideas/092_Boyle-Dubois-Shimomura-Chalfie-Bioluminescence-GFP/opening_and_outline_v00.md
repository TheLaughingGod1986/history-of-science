### PART 02: October 1667 & 1885–1887 — Robert Boyle's Glowing Wood in a Vacuum & Raphaël Dubois's Hot-and-Cold Water Discovery of `Luciferin` and `Luciferase` (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open ("Since Aristotle, philosophers had been baffled by 'cold fire': rotting forest logs covered in fungal mycelium (`foxfire`, `091`), dead fish, and summer fireflies that glowed in pitch darkness without burning your fingers! On 29 October 1667 in London, Robert Boyle placed a piece of glowing wood inside his vacuum air-pump, blew out the candles, and pumped out the air — and what happened in the dark solved the first half of the mystery."):** **London (October–December 1667) & Lyon, France (1885–1887)**.
- **29 October 1667 (*Philosophical Transactions*) — Robert Boyle's Glowing Wood vs Red-Hot Iron in a Vacuum (`039`, `017`):**
  - When **Robert Boyle** (1627–1691) pumped the air out of his glass receiver containing a piece of glowing forest wood (`colonized by bioluminescent fungal mycelium, 091`), the eerie green light faded until it **vanished completely**!
  - Then Boyle turned the stopcock to let air rush back in — and **instantly, without a match or spark, the wood burst back into cold green light**! (When he repeated the test with a piece of **red-hot iron or glowing coal**, pumping out the air dimmed it, but letting cold air back in minutes later could never re-light the dead coal — proving that **bioluminescence** is not combustion by heat, but a slow, cold chemical oxidation requiring air/oxygen, `017`!)
- **1885–1887 (*Comptes Rendus de la Société de Biologie*) — Raphaël Dubois's Two-Beaker Trick (`Luciferin + Luciferase`):**
  - Two centuries later in Lyon, French physiologist **Raphaël Dubois** (1849–1929) studied glowing West Indian click beetles (*Pyrophorus*) and glowing marine clams (*Pholas dactylus*).
  - Crushing the glowing organs in **cold water**, Dubois watched the glow slowly fade until the beaker went **dark** (`because the chemical fuel had been used up`).
  - Next, he crushed a fresh batch of glowing organs in **near-boiling hot water**: the heat instantly extinguished the glow (`because heat unfolds proteins/enzymes, 070`, while leaving the small fuel molecule intact), and he cooled that dark hot-water extract down.
  - Then, in a pitch-black room, Dubois **poured the dark hot-water extract into the dark cold-water beaker** — and the mixture **erupted back into brilliant cold light**! He named the heat-stable chemical fuel **`luciferin`** (from *Lucifer*, "light-bearer") and the heat-fragile enzyme **`luciferase`** (`later shown by William McElroy at Johns Hopkins in 1947 to require the cell's energy currency ATP, 062, in fireflies!`).

- **Ends on:** For seventy-five years after Dubois, every biochemist on Earth believed **all** glowing creatures in nature used the **luciferin-plus-luciferase** recipe! Then in **Summer 1961**, a young Japanese chemist named **Osamu Shimomura** arrived at a rocky island in Washington State to study a crystal jellyfish — and every single luciferin test **failed**! How did throwing his ruined jellyfish extracts into a **wet laboratory sink** reveal a completely new law of living light?

### PART 03: Summer 1961–1962 — Osamu Shimomura's 10,000 Jellyfish, the Kitchen-Sink Flash & the Discovery of `Aequorin` and `GFP` — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — every glowing animal on Earth makes light by mixing a luciferin fuel with a luciferase enzyme! Wrong! In Summer 1961 at Friday Harbor in Washington State, 32-year-old Japanese chemist Osamu Shimomura netted ten thousand glowing crystal jellyfish, tested every enzyme trick in the textbook, and failed completely — until he dumped his failed extracts into a dirty laboratory sink!"):** **Nagasaki (1945), Nagoya (1955) & Friday Harbor Laboratories, Washington (Summer 1961–June 1962)**.
- **From the 1945 Nagasaki Flash to 10,000 Crystal Jellyfish (*Aequorea victoria*):**
  - As a 16-year-old boy in **August 1945**, **Osamu Shimomura** (1928–2018) survived the blinding flash of the Nagasaki atomic bomb 12 kilometres from ground zero (`and later worked in a makeshift pharmacy school lab where he crystallized stubborn sea-firefly *Cypridina* luciferin in 1955, catching the eye of Princeton's Frank Johnson`).
  - In **Summer 1961**, Johnson and Shimomura drove across America to **Friday Harbor** on San Juan Island, where millions of transparent saucer-sized **crystal jellyfish (*Aequorea victoria*)** drifted past the wooden lab dock every morning and evening, glowing with a ring of green dots around their umbrella margin.
  - Netting **10,000 jellyfish** that first summer and slicing off their margins (`"squeezates"`), Shimomura tried Dubois's hot-and-cold water luciferin-luciferase test, tried adding `ATP` (`062`), tried changing temperature — and **every extract went dead**.
- **The Midpoint Twist — The Seawater Sink Eureka (`Aequorin` + `Green Fluorescent Protein`):**
  - Late one evening in **Summer 1961**, frustrated after another failed test, Shimomura poured his leftover jellyfish extract into the **laboratory sink** and switched off the lights to walk home — and **the sink drain exploded with a dazzling blue flash**!
  - Staring into the sink, Shimomura realized the sink held **overflowing seawater from an aquarium**: and what is in seawater? **Calcium ions (`Ca²⁺`)**! Unlike fireflies (`which use luciferin, luciferase, oxygen, and ATP`), the jellyfish stores a pre-charged photoprotein Shimomura named **`aequorin`** that flashes **instantly whenever calcium touches it**!
  - Wait — in the sink, purified `aequorin` flashed **bright blue (`470 nm`)**, yet a living jellyfish in the sea glows **emerald green (`509 nm`)**! In a famous footnote to his **June 1962** paper (*J. Cell. Comp. Physiol.* 59: 223–239), Shimomura isolated a **second companion protein** from those 10,000 jellyfish: a tiny protein that absorbs `aequorin`'s blue flash and re-emits it as neon green — **`Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP)`**!

Physiol. 59: 223–239) calcium-triggered Aequorin blue luminescence (470 nm) & Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP) green emission (509 nm) spectrum + 1961–1979 jellyfish collection chart on period paper · SCALE: over nineteen summers at Friday Harbor, Osamu Shimomura, his wife Akemi, and their children netted more than eight hundred and fifty thousand jellyfish by hand — all to solve the chemical puzzle of one tiny glowing molecule`
- **Ends on:** In **1979**, Shimomura deciphered the glowing heart (`chromophore`) at the centre of `GFP` — yet for **thirty years (`1962–1992`)**, neither Shimomura nor any other biochemist imagined `GFP` could work inside a human or worm cell (`because everyone assumed a jellyfish needed a whole factory of special jellyfish enzymes to fold GFP into a glowing lantern!`). How did **Douglas Prasher** (`who lost his job and became a courtesy shuttle driver!`), **Martin Chalfie in February 1994**, and **Roger Tsien** prove that **the `GFP` gene glows all by itself inside any living creature on Earth**?

### PART 04: 1992–1995 — Douglas Prasher's Cloned Gene, Martin Chalfie's Glowing Worm Neurons & Roger Tsien's Neon Rainbow (4:50–6:45)
- **Cold open (how did a scientist at Woods Hole clone the jellyfish's glowing gene in 1992 just before his research money ran out, mail the DNA tube to two labs, and spark a revolution that won the 2008 Nobel Prize?):** **Woods Hole, Massachusetts (1992), Columbia University, New York (11 February 1994) & UC San Diego (1994–1995)**.
- **May 1992 (*Gene*) — Douglas Prasher Clones the `GFP` Gene & Shares the Tube:**
  - At Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, molecular biologist **Douglas Prasher** (born 1951) had a visionary idea: what if you cloned the **DNA gene** for `GFP` (`016`, `080`) and attached it to the gene for any invisible protein inside a living cell (`like welding a glowing lightbulb onto a protein so you can watch where it goes under a microscope!`)?
  - In **May 1992** (*Gene* 111: 229–233), Prasher sequenced and cloned the **238-amino-acid `GFP` gene** from *Aequorea victoria* — just as his grant funding ran out! Before leaving research (`eventually working for a time driving a car-dealership shuttle van for $8.50 an hour in Alabama`), Prasher generously mailed his frozen `GFP` DNA samples to **Martin Chalfie** at Columbia and **Roger Tsien** at UC San Diego.
- **11 February 1994 (*Science*) & 1994–1995 (*Nature*) — Martin Chalfie's Glowing Worm & Roger Tsien's Rainbow Palette:**
  - Everyone warned **Martin Chalfie** (born 1947) it would fail because bacteria and worms lacked the jellyfish enzymes thought necessary to build `GFP`'s glowing chromophore.
  - Yet when Chalfie's graduate student **Ghia Euskirchen** inserted **only the `GFP` gene** into *E. coli* bacteria in **September 1992** (`and into the six touch-receptor neurons of the transparent roundworm *Caenorhabditis elegans*`), they shone blue light into the microscope — and **the living bacteria and worm neurons lit up brilliant jellyfish green all by themselves**!
  - Why didn't `GFP` need any jellyfish enzymes? As **Roger Tsien** (1952–2016) proved in **1994–1995** (*PNAS* **1994** & *Nature* **1995**), the 238-amino-acid chain folds itself into an **11-stranded soda-can barrel (`beta-barrel`)**, pulls three of its own amino acids (`Serine-65, Tyrosine-66, Glycine-67`) tight inside the middle, and uses **ordinary oxygen (`O₂`, `017`)** to snap them into a glowing ring automatically! By mutating those central amino acids (`plus red coral proteins`), Tsien engineered a **neon rainbow of blue, cyan, yellow, and red fluorescent proteins** — winning **Shimomura, Chalfie, and Tsien the 2008 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** (where they invited **Douglas Prasher** to Stockholm as their honoured guest)!

- **Ends on:** `GFP` gave biologists a glowing lantern to see inside transparent cells and shallow tissues — yet what if a doctor needs to see **deep inside a living human body**, where light cannot penetrate (`like checking a beating heart, 076, or seeing an **unborn baby** safely inside a mother's womb, 067, without dangerous X-rays, 003`)? How did **Lazzaro Spallanzani and Charles Jurine's blinded bats in 1793–1794**, **Pierre Curie's vibrating quartz crystal in 1880**, **Donald Griffin's ultrasonic bat microphone in 1938**, and **Scottish doctor Ian Donald's shipyard metal-flaw detector in 1958** give the world **medical ultrasound**?
