Since ancient Greece, naturalists had been baffled by "cold fire": rotting woodland logs (`foxfire`), glowing sea creatures, and summer fireflies that shone in pitch darkness without burning your fingers! Was living light trapped sunlight, or a slow chemical fire?

On **twenty-nine October sixteen sixty-seven** in London, **Robert Boyle** placed a piece of **glowing wood** inside his glass **vacuum air-pump**, blew out the candles, and pumped out the air: as the receiver emptied, why did the eerie green glow fade until it **vanished completely**? And when Boyle turned the brass valve to let air rush back in, **instantly, without a match or spark, the wood burst back into cold green light** (`proving that living light is not heat, but a chemical reaction that requires air`)!

Two centuries later in Lyon, French physiologist **Raphaël Dubois** in **eighteen eighty-five** and **eighteen eighty-seven** crushed the glowing organs of click beetles and sea clams in **two beakers of water**. Why did a **cold-water** extract glow for a while and then go **dark**, while a **near-boiling hot-water** extract went dark **immediately**? Because the cold beaker ran out of fuel, while boiling heat destroyed the delicate enzyme! When Dubois cooled the boiled beaker down and **poured the two dark liquids together**, the mixture **erupted back into brilliant cold light**! He named the fuel **`luciferin`** and the enzyme **`luciferase`** (`which William McElroy proved in nineteen forty-seven also uses ATP in fireflies`).

For seventy-five years, every textbook taught that **all** glowing creatures use **luciferin and luciferase** — until **Summer nineteen sixty-one**, when **Osamu Shimomura** netted **ten thousand crystal jellyfish** and every enzyme test **failed**! How did **throwing his failed extracts into a laboratory sink** solve the mystery?

Case closed — every glowing animal on Earth makes light by mixing a luciferin fuel with a luciferase enzyme! Wrong! In **Summer nineteen sixty-one** at Friday Harbor in Washington State, thirty-two-year-old Japanese chemist **Osamu Shimomura** and Princeton biologist **Frank Harris Johnson** netted **ten thousand glowing crystal jellyfish**, tried every luciferin and luciferase test in the book, and failed completely — until Shimomura **dumped his ruined extracts into a dirty laboratory sink**!

Having survived the Nagasaki atomic flash at sixteen, Shimomura was unstoppable in the lab. Every morning and evening at Friday Harbor, he scooped up thousands of saucer-sized **crystal jellyfish** (*Aequorea victoria*) and trimmed the glowing rings around their rims. Yet when he tested Dubois's hot-and-cold water recipe or added `ATP`, why did the extracts stay stubbornly dark? Late one evening in **Summer nineteen sixty-one**, frustrated after hours of dead tests, Shimomura poured his leftover jellyfish extract into the **laboratory sink**, switched off the lights to walk home — and **the sink drain exploded with a dazzling blue flash**!

Why did the sink glow? Because an aquarium overflow pipe was draining **seawater** into that sink — and seawater is full of **calcium ions**! Unlike fireflies, the jellyfish stores a pre-charged protein Shimomura named **`aequorin`** that flashes **blue** the instant calcium touches it! Wait: why did pure `aequorin` flash **blue** in the sink when a living jellyfish in the sea glows **emerald green**? In **June nineteen sixty-two**, Shimomura reported a **second protein** from those ten thousand jellyfish (`solving its glowing core in nineteen seventy-nine`): a tiny companion barrel that absorbs `aequorin`'s blue flash and shines it back out as **neon green** — **`Green Fluorescent Protein` (`GFP`)**!

Yet for thirty years, everyone assumed `GFP` needed special jellyfish enzymes to work. How did **Douglas Prasher in nineteen ninety-two**, **Martin Chalfie in February nineteen ninety-four**, and **Roger Tsien** prove that the **`GFP` gene glows all by itself inside any living cell**?

How did a scientist who lost his research funding and ended up driving a courtesy shuttle van in Alabama spark a revolution that won the **two thousand and eight Nobel Prize**?

At Woods Hole, molecular biologist **Douglas Prasher** asked: what if you cloned the jellyfish's **`GFP` gene** and stitched it onto the gene for any invisible protein inside a cell, so that protein carried its own **built-in glowing lantern**? In **May nineteen ninety-two**, Prasher cloned the **`GFP` gene** just as his grant money ran out — and mailed frozen tubes of the DNA to **Martin Chalfie** and **Roger Tsien**.

Everyone warned Chalfie that `GFP` would stay dark inside other creatures because they lacked jellyfish enzymes. Yet on **eleven February nineteen ninety-four** on the cover of *Science*, Chalfie inserted **only the `GFP` gene** into bacteria and the transparent roundworm *C. elegans*, shone blue light into the microscope — and **six touch-sensing neurons inside the living worm lit up brilliant jellyfish green all by themselves**! Why didn't `GFP` need any jellyfish enzymes? As **Roger Tsien** proved in **nineteen ninety-four and nineteen ninety-five**, the `GFP` chain folds itself into an **eleven-stranded soda-can barrel**, squeezes three of its own amino acids together inside the core, and uses **ordinary oxygen from the air** to snap its glowing ring into place automatically! Mutating that core, Tsien created a **neon rainbow of blue, cyan, green, and yellow tags** (`and when Shimomura, Chalfie, and Tsien won the two thousand and eight Nobel Prize, they flew Douglas Prasher to Stockholm as their guest of honour`)!

Wait — `GFP` lets us see inside transparent cells with light, but what when a doctor needs to see **deep inside a mother's womb** where light cannot reach? How did **Lazzaro Spallanzani's blinded bats in seventeen ninety-three**, **Pierre Curie's vibrating quartz in eighteen eighty**, **Donald Griffin's bat microphone in nineteen thirty-eight**, and **Ian Donald's shipyard metal-flaw detector in nineteen fifty-eight** invent **medical ultrasound**?

Today, in tens of thousands of laboratories around the world, a gene borrowed from a Pacific Northwest jellyfish lights up the hidden machinery of life every single second!

And what when scientists and doctors need to see **deep inside a living human body** where even the brightest light cannot penetrate — without using dangerous radiation? Nature solved that problem in pitch-black caves and murky oceans: not with light, but with **invisible high-frequency sound**! How did Italian biologist **Lazzaro Spallanzani in seventeen ninety-three** and Swiss surgeon **Charles Jurine in seventeen ninety-four** prove that **blindfolded bats** fly effortlessly through dark rooms hung with silk threads, yet crash the instant you plug their **ears** with wax?
