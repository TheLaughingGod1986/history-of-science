### PART 02: November 1880 & 1900 — Charles & Francis Darwin's Blindfolded Grass Tips (`Phototropism`) & Falling-Starch Gravity Sensors (`Gravitropism`) (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (for two thousand years, everyone assumed a plant bent toward a window because warmth or light acted directly on the bending stem — until a 71-year-old Charles Darwin and his son Francis put tiny tin-foil blindfolds on the top millimetre of canary-grass shoots in November 1880!):** **Down House, Kent, November 1880 & Graz / Prague, 1900**.
- **November 1880 (*The Power of Movement in Plants*) — Charles & Francis Darwin prove the 1-mm tip is the plant's "eye":**
  - In **November 1880** (`two years before Charles Darwin died`), **Charles Darwin** (1809–1882) and his son **Francis Darwin** (1848–1925) published *The Power of Movement in Plants*, studying the hollow protective shoot tip (**`coleoptile`**) of canary grass (*Phalaris canariensis*) and oats (*Avena sativa*).
  - Notice the paradox: when a seedling is lit from one side, the **very top 1 to 2 millimetres (`the tip`)** barely bends at all — the actual bending happens **several millimetres lower down** in the growing stem!
  - Yet when the Darwins slipped a tiny light-proof **tin-foil cap** (or a miniature glass tube blackened with India ink) over just the top **2 millimetres** of the tip (`leaving the lower bending zone completely exposed to the lamp`), the seedling **went completely blind and grew straight up**! When they replaced the blackened tube with a **clear glass tube** of the exact same weight, the seedling bent right toward the light!
  - And when they buried the entire lower stem in black sand so *only* the top 1-mm tip poked into the light, the buried stem below still bent toward the lamp — proving that *"when seedlings are freely exposed to a lateral light, some influence is transmitted from the upper to the lower part, causing the latter to bend"*!
- **1806 & 1900 (*Ber. Dtsch. Bot. Ges.*) — Thomas Andrew Knight's waterwheel & Haberlandt and Němec's falling-starch `Statoliths` (`Gravitropism`):**
  - How do plants know which way is **down** in total darkness (`after British horticulturist Thomas Andrew Knight in 1806 strapped bean seedlings to a spinning waterwheel to prove roots grow along centrifugal force / gravity`)?
  - In **1900**, Austrian botanist **Gottlieb Haberlandt** (1854–1945) and Czech botanist **Bohumil Němec** (1873–1966) independently discovered that specialized cells in the **root cap** contain heavy, microscopic pebbles of starch (**`statoliths` / `amyloplasts`**) that tumble to the lowest floor of the cell whenever a plant is tilted — acting like microscopic spirit levels!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Thomas Andrew Knight (1759–1838) — British horticulturist and botanist · WHEN: 1806 · WHEN: November 1880 · WHEN: 1900 · FACT: 2mm foil cap blinded grass · DATA: inked Charles & Francis Darwin (November 1880 The Power of Movement in Plants) 5-condition coleoptile tip experiment (1: intact bends; 2: tip cut off = no bend; 3: opaque tin-foil cap on tip = no bend; 4: clear glass cap on tip = bends; 5: opaque collar on lower stem = bends) alongside Haberlandt & Němec's (1900) root-cap statolith sedimentation diagram on period paper · SCALE: covering just the top one-and-a-half millimetres of a seedling with tin foil while leaving the rest of the stem in bright sunlight is like putting a blindfold over a runner's eyes while leaving their legs free — the muscles work, but they have no idea which way to turn`
- **Ends on:** When **Charles and Francis Darwin** wrote in **1880** that the tip of a grass shoot acts like a miniature brain sending a signal down the stem, the leading German botanist **Julius von Sachs** mocked them (`insisting plants had no nerves, so the signal had to be a physical vibration`). How did a Danish scientist with a **cube of gelatin** and a 23-year-old Dutch student at **3:00 a.m. in 1926** prove what Darwin's invisible messenger really was?

### PART 03: 1913 & 17 April 1926, 3:00 a.m. — Boysen-Jensen's Gelatin Bridge & Frits Went's Agar-Jelly Isolation of `Auxin` — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — if a plant tip sends a message down the stem, it must be an electrical nerve impulse travelling through living tissue! Wrong! In 1913 in Copenhagen and at 3:00 a.m. on 17 April 1926 in Utrecht, scientists sliced the tip completely off, replaced it with a lifeless cube of jelly, and watched the headless plant bend in the dark!"):** **University of Copenhagen, 1910–1913 & University of Utrecht, 17 April 1926**.
- **1910–1913 & 1919 — The Midpoint Twist (`Boysen-Jensen's gelatin vs mica test & Árpád Paál's dark-offset test`):**
  - Between **1910 and 1913** in Copenhagen, Danish plant physiologist **Peter Boysen-Jensen** (1883–1959) sliced the 1-mm tip off an oat seedling, painted a drop of **wet gelatin** onto the stump, and stuck the severed tip back on top: when lit from one side, the signal **passed right through the non-living gelatin** and bent the stem!
  - Even better, when Boysen-Jensen slid a tiny waterproof flake of **mineral mica** halfway into the **sunny side** of the stem just below the tip, the seedling still bent normally — **but when he slid the mica flake into the SHADED side, bending completely stopped**! Darwin's messenger was a **water-soluble chemical travelling down the shaded side**!
  - And in **1919**, Hungarian botanist **Árpád Paál** (1889–1943) placed a severed tip onto **one side** of a decapitated stump in **complete darkness** — and the shoot bent *away* from the side holding the tip, proving the tip constantly secretes a **growth-promoting chemical**!
- **17 April 1926, 3:00 a.m. (*Proc. K. Akad. Wet. Amsterdam*) — Frits Warmolt Went traps `Auxin` in agar jelly (`The Cholodny–Went Model`):**
  - At the University of Utrecht (`while doing military service by day and sneaking into his father's botany lab at night!`), 23-year-old **Frits Warmolt Went** (1903–1990) realized how to catch the chemical without a living tip: he sliced off oat coleoptile tips, set them cut-side down on a tiny **`2 × 2 millimetre` block of agar jelly** for an hour so the hormone diffused into the jelly, **threw the plant tips away**, and placed the lifeless agar cube onto one side of a decapitated oat stump in the dark!
  - At **3:00 a.m. on 17 April 1926**, Went checked his darkroom rack: **the headless oat shoots had bent sharply away from the agar cubes** (`in exact proportion to how many tips had sat on the jelly — the famous Avena curvature bioassay`)! He ran into his parents' bedroom at 3:00 a.m. shouting, *"Father, come and see, I have got it!"*
  - Named **`auxin`** (`from the Greek auxein = "to grow"`, chemically identified in **1934** by **Kenneth Thimann** and **Kögl** as **`indole-3-acetic acid / IAA`**), auxin explained both mysteries at once (**`the Cholodny–Went model`, 1927**):
    - In **stems (`phototropism`)**, blue light (`sensed by phototropin receptors`) drives auxin to the **shaded side**, where auxin tells cells to pump protons (`H⁺`) into their cell walls (`acid growth`), loosening the cellulose fibres so water rushes in (`071`, `073`) and the **shaded cells stretch longer**, bending the shoot toward the light!
    - In **roots (`gravitropism`)**, falling **`statoliths`** (`1900`) direct auxin to the **bottom side** of a horizontal root — and because root cells are 1,000 times more sensitive to auxin than stem cells, that high auxin **slows down** elongation on the bottom while the top cells keep stretching, curving the root **downward into the Earth**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Árpád Paál (1889–1943) — Hungarian botanist · WHO: Kenneth Vivian Thimann (1904–1997) — British-American plant physiologist · WHEN: 1910 · WHEN: 1913 · WHEN: 1919 · WHEN: 17 April 1926 · WHEN: 1927 · FACT: Shady cells stretch longer · DATA: inked Boysen-Jensen (1913), Árpád Paál (1919) & Frits Went (17 April 1926) Cholodny–Went Auxin Redistribution diagram (gelatin passes signal; mica on shaded side blocks bending; asymmetric auxin agar block bends shoot in dark; opposite auxin dose-response curves in stems vs roots) on period paper · SCALE: the exact same auxin hormone molecule makes a shoot grow up toward the sun and a root grow down into the soil — because in stems extra auxin acts like an accelerator on the shady side, while in roots extra auxin acts like a brake on the bottom side`
- **Ends on:** **`Auxin`** explained how plants bend toward light and gravity through liquid sap inside their stems — but how can **one ripe apple or banana inside a closed paper bag** command every green fruit around it to ripen **through thin air**, without touching them at all?

### PART 04: 1901 & 1934 — Leaking Streetlamp Gas in St Petersburg & Richard Gane's `Ethylene` Fruit-Ripening Proof (4:55–6:45)
- **Cold open (in the 1800s, German cities noticed that whenever an underground coal-gas streetlamp pipe leaked beneath a boulevard, every shade tree along the street dropped its leaves — and in 1901 in St Petersburg, a 21-year-old botany student discovered that the gas leaking from his laboratory lamps was actually a secret plant hormone!):** **St Petersburg University, 1901 & Low Temperature Research Station, Cambridge, 1934**.
- **1901 (*Beih. Bot. Centralbl.*) — Dmitry Neljubow & the leaking illuminating-gas pipes:**
  - In **1901** at the Botanical Institute of St Petersburg University, 21-year-old student **Dmitry Nikolayevich Neljubow** (1876–1926) noticed a bizarre mystery: pea seedlings (*Pisum sativum*) grown in the dark inside the laboratory air grew short, fat, and **bent sideways horizontally** (`the "triple response"`), whereas seedlings grown under a glass bell jar fed with fresh outside air grew tall and straight!
  - Testing every component of the laboratory's coal illuminating gas (`used to light the room's gas lamps`), Neljubow discovered the active culprit: **`ethylene` (`C₂H₄`)** — a tiny two-carbon hydrocarbon gas that bent pea shoots at concentrations as low as **1 part in 10 million (`0.1 ppm`)**!
- **1910–1934 (*Nature* 29 December 1934) — From kerosene heaters in Florida lemon sheds to Richard Gane's 56 kg of apples:**
  - For decades, California and Florida citrus growers had kept **kerosene stoves** burning inside lemon sheds because they thought *warmth* turned green lemons bright yellow — until **Frank E. Denny** (**1924**) proved it wasn't the heat at all, it was trace **`ethylene` gas** in the kerosene smoke!
  - Then in **1934** (*Nature*), **Richard Gane** (1904–1981) at the Low Temperature Research Station in **Cambridge** passed the air streaming over **56 kilograms (`2 bushels`) of ripening apples** through `-65 °C` cold traps and bromine, isolating pure **`ethylene` gas (`C₂H₄`)** and proving that **ripening fruits manufacture their own `ethylene` gas**!
  - Because `ethylene` triggers a **positive feedback loop** (`a tiny whiff of ethylene makes neighbouring fruit switch on their own ethylene genes and enzymes that turn sour starch into sweet sugar and soften pectin cell walls`), **one ripe apple or banana literally broadcasts an airborne hormone that ripens the entire barrel** (`and today allows cargo ships to transport hard green bananas across oceans in ethylene-free cooled holds before gassing them with ethylene for 24 hours so they turn yellow right on schedule`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Frank Earl Denny (1883–1964) — American plant physiologist · WHEN: 1901 · WHEN: 1924 · WHEN: 29 December 1934 · FACT: Apples breathe ripening gas · DATA: inked Dmitry Neljubow (1901 Beih. Centralbl. 10: 128–139) pea-seedling ethylene triple-response threshold (0.1 ppm C2H4) & Richard Gane (29 December 1934 Nature 134: 1008) climacteric apple ethylene autocatalytic burst chart on period paper · SCALE: ethylene gas is so potent that a single ripe banana sealed in a paper bag with hard green avocados acts like a chemical radio transmitter, broadcasting a two-carbon gas that tells every fruit around it to turn starch into sugar`
- **Ends on:** A plant heals a cut stem with sap and hormones (`auxin` and `ethylene`) — but what about **your own bloodstream (`005`)**? When you nick your finger, how does your liquid blood **turn into a solid plug in seconds (`platelets & fibrin`)** without clotting the rest of your veins — and how did **a milk can of unclotted cow's blood in a 1933 Wisconsin blizzard** turn **rat poison (`Warfarin`)** into a life-saving human medicine?
