For two thousand years, botanists faced a deceptive puzzle: why does a plant stem bend toward a sunny window? Everyone assumed light acted directly on the bending stem — until **November eighteen eighty**, when seventy-one-year-old **Charles Darwin** and his son **Francis Darwin** tested canary-grass and oat shoots (**`coleoptiles`**) at Down House!

The Darwins noticed a strange mystery: when lit from one side, the **top two millimetres (`the tip`)** barely bent at all — the bending happened **several millimetres lower down**! Yet when they slipped a tiny light-proof **tin-foil cap** over just the top **two millimetres** (`leaving the lower bending zone in bright light`), the seedling **went completely blind and stood straight up**! When they swapped the foil cap for a **clear glass tube**, or buried the lower stem in black sand so *only* the top millimetre poked into the light, the stem bent right toward the lamp — proving that *"some influence is transmitted from the upper to the lower part, causing the latter to bend"*!

And how does a buried root know which way is **down** (`after Thomas Andrew Knight in eighteen hundred and six strapped seedlings to a spinning waterwheel to prove roots follow gravity`)? In **nineteen hundred**, **Gottlieb Haberlandt** and **Bohumil Němec** discovered that root-cap cells hold heavy microscopic pebbles of starch (**`statoliths`**) that tumble to the lowest floor of the cell whenever a plant tilts!

When the Darwins wrote in **eighteen eighty** that the tip of a grass shoot sends a signal down the stem, critics mocked them: how could a plant send a message without nerves? How did a Danish scientist with a **cube of gelatin** and a twenty-three-year-old Dutch student at **three o'clock in the morning in nineteen twenty-six** prove what Darwin's messenger really was?

Case closed — if a plant tip sends a message down the stem, it must be an electrical nerve impulse travelling through living tissue! Wrong! In **nineteen thirteen** in Copenhagen and at **three o'clock in the morning on seventeen April nineteen twenty-six** in Utrecht, scientists sliced the tip completely off, replaced it with a lifeless cube of jelly, and watched the headless plant bend in the dark!

Between **nineteen ten and nineteen thirteen** in his Copenhagen laboratory, **Peter Boysen-Jensen** sliced the tip off an oat shoot, painted a drop of **wet gelatin** onto the stump, and set the tip back on top: when lit from one side, the signal **passed right through the non-living gelatin** and bent the stem! Even better, when Boysen-Jensen slid a waterproof flake of **mica** halfway into the **sunny side**, the shoot still bent — **but when he slid the mica into the shaded side, bending stopped cold**! Darwin's signal was a **water-soluble chemical travelling down the shaded side**! And in **nineteen nineteen**, **Árpád Paál** placed a cut tip on **one side** of a stump in **total darkness**, bending the shoot!

At the University of Utrecht, twenty-three-year-old **Frits Warmolt Went** set severed oat tips onto a **`two-millimetre` block of agar jelly** for an hour, **threw the living plant tips away**, and placed the lifeless jelly cube on one side of a headless oat stump in the dark! At **three o'clock in the morning on seventeen April nineteen twenty-six**, Went stared at his darkroom rack in disbelief: **the headless shoots had bent sharply away from the jelly**! He ran into his parents' bedroom shouting, *"Father, come and see, I have got it!"* Named **`auxin`** (`identified with Kenneth Thimann in nineteen thirty-four`), **`the Cholodny–Went model` (nineteen twenty-seven)** solved both puzzles: in stems, light drives `auxin` to the **shaded side** to stretch shaded cells longer; in horizontal roots, falling `statoliths` pool `auxin` on the **bottom side**, where high `auxin` *brakes* the bottom cells so the root curves down into the soil!

**`Auxin`** explained how plants bend through liquid sap — but how can **one ripe apple or banana inside a closed bag** command every green fruit around it to ripen **through thin air**, without touching them at all?

In the eighteen hundreds, European cities noticed a bizarre mystery: whenever an underground coal-gas streetlamp pipe leaked beneath a boulevard, every shade tree along the street dropped its leaves! Then in **nineteen hundred and one** at St Petersburg University, twenty-one-year-old student **Dmitry Neljubow** noticed that pea seedlings grown inside his laboratory air grew short, thick, and **bent sideways horizontally** — whereas seedlings under a glass jar fed with outside air grew tall and straight!

Testing the laboratory's coal illuminating gas, Neljubow found the culprit: **`ethylene` (`C₂H₄`)** — a tiny two-carbon gas that bent pea shoots at just **one part in ten million (`0.1 ppm`)**! In **nineteen twenty-four**, American scientist **Frank Denny** proved that the kerosene stoves citrus growers burned in Florida lemon sheds didn't yellow lemons by warmth — it was trace **`ethylene` gas** in the smoke!

Then on **twenty-nine December nineteen thirty-four** in *Nature*, **Richard Gane** in **Cambridge** passed the air streaming over **fifty-six kilograms of ripening apples** through minus-sixty-five-degree cold traps and isolated pure **`ethylene` gas** — proving that **ripening fruits manufacture their own airborne hormone**! Because one whiff of `ethylene` triggers neighbouring fruit to brew *more* `ethylene` and turn sour starch into sweet sugar, **one ripe apple or banana literally broadcasts a gas through the air that ripens the whole bowl**!
