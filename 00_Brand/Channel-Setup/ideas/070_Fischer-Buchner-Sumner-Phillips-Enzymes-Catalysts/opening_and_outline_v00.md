### PART 02: 1857, 1894 & 11 January 1897 — Pasteur's "Vital Force", Emil Fischer's Lock-and-Key & Eduard Buchner's 300-Atmosphere Yeast Press (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why for forty years the greatest chemist in Europe — Louis Pasteur — insisted that turning sugar into alcohol inside yeast required a mysterious "living vital force" that died the instant you broke the cell open):** **University of Berlin, 1894 & Hygienic Institute, Munich, Autumn 1896 – 11 January 1897**.
- **1857–1877 & 1894 (*Ber. Dtsch. Chem. Ges.*) — Pasteur's vitalism, Wilhelm Kühne's word `Enzyme` & Emil Fischer's Lock-and-Key (`Schloss und Schlüssel`):**
  - Even though French chemists **Anselme Payen and Jean-François Persoz (1833)** had extracted malt **`diastase` (`amylase`)** and **Theodor Schwann (1836)** had isolated stomach **`pepsin`**, **Louis Pasteur (`001`)** proved in **1857–1860** that alcoholic fermentation was carried out by living yeast cells — and insisted that intracellular reactions required a living **`vital force`** (`to avoid confusion, Wilhelm Kühne coined the word "enzyme" — Greek for "in yeast" — in 1877`).
  - In **1894** (*Ber. Dtsch. Chem. Ges.*), Berlin organic chemist **Hermann Emil Fischer** (1852–1919, **1902 Nobel Prize**) synthesised mirror-image (`right-handed D-` and `left-handed L-`) sugars and glucosides (`alpha-` vs `beta-`). Yeast ferments digested one mirror-image form effortlessly, yet couldn't touch its twin!
  - Fischer wrote the most famous sentence in biochemistry: *"To use a picture, I would say that enzyme and glucoside must fit each other **like lock and key** (*wie Schloss und Schlüssel*), in order to exert a chemical effect on each other."*
- **Autumn 1896 – 11 January 1897 (*Ber. Dtsch. Chem. Ges.*) — Eduard Buchner's quartz-sand yeast press (`Zymase`):**
  - Two years later in Munich, **Eduard Buchner** (1860–1917) and his assistant **Martin Hahn** ground **1 kilogram of brewer's yeast** with **1 kilogram of sharp quartz sand** and **200 grams of diatomaceous earth (`kieselguhr`)**, then squeezed the paste in a hydraulic press at **300 atmospheres** to extract cell-free liquid (`for immunology experiments with his brother Hans`).
  - To keep bacteria from spoiling the dead juice while Buchner was away, they added **40% cane sugar (`sucrose`)** — the same way fruit is preserved in jam.
  - When Buchner looked at the flask of dead, filtered, cell-free juice, it was **fizzing with streams of carbon dioxide bubbles and producing ethyl alcohol**! On **11 January 1897** (*Ber. Dtsch. Chem. Ges.*, winning Buchner the **1907 Nobel Prize**), Buchner named the soluble ferment **`zymase`**, proving once and for all that the chemistry of life needs **zero "vital force"** — it is driven by chemical molecules (`enzymes`) that work inside a glass beaker just as they do inside a cell!

Dtsch. Chem. Ges. 27: 2985–2993) alpha- vs beta-methyl glucoside stereospecificity chart & Buchner (11 January 1897 Ber. Dtsch. Chem. Ges. 30: 117–124) 300-atmosphere cell-free yeast press-juice CO2/ethanol yield chart on period paper · SCALE: Emil Fischer's lock-and-key discovery showed that a yeast enzyme is as picky as a front-door Yale lock — a right-handed sugar key slides straight in and turns, while its left-handed mirror image can't even enter the keyhole`
- **Ends on:** Buchner proved that invisible chemical locks inside cells drive the chemistry of life. But for the next thirty years, nobody knew **what an enzyme was actually made of**! Why did Germany's most powerful Nobel Prize-winning chemist mock a **one-armed American professor at Cornell in August 1926** when he claimed he had crystallised an enzyme — and found it was **100% pure protein**?

### PART 03: August 1926 & 1930 — One-Armed James Sumner vs Nobel Laureate Richard Willstätter — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — proteins are just passive structural jelly like egg white, so enzymes must be mysterious, unknown non-protein chemicals! Wrong! In August 1926, a one-armed chemist who had been told he could never work in a lab crystallised the first enzyme in history — and proved it was pure protein!"):** **Cornell University Medical College, Ithaca, New York, August 1926 & Rockefeller Institute, Princeton, 1930**.
- **1917–August 1926 (*Journal of Biological Chemistry*) — James Batcheller Sumner crystallises `Urease` with one arm:**
  - At age 17, **James Batcheller Sumner** (1887–1955) accidentally lost his left arm above the elbow in a grouse-hunting accident; his chemistry professor at Harvard told him a one-armed man could never succeed in experimental chemistry.
  - Undeterred, Sumner taught himself to pipette, filter, and blow glass with one hand, and in **1917** at Cornell set out to do what every great European laboratory had failed to do: **isolate a pure enzyme** — picking **`urease`** from the **jack bean (*Canavalia ensiformis*)** (`an enzyme that speeds up the breakdown of urea into ammonia and carbon dioxide by 100 trillion times, 10¹⁴×!`).
  - For nine years Sumner got only syrupy failures — until one morning in **August 1926** (*J. Biol. Chem.*), he mixed ground jack-bean meal with **31.6% aqueous acetone**, let it drip into a beaker in an ice chest overnight, and looked at a drop under his microscope: the field was glittering with **tiny octahedral crystals**!
  - Every chemical test showed those crystals had enormous `urease` activity — and were **100% pure globulin protein**!
- **1926–1935 — The 9-year rivalry with Nobel laureate Richard Willstätter & John Howard Northrop's `Pepsin` proof:**
  - Instead of applause, Sumner was attacked by German Nobel laureate **Richard Willstätter** (1872–1942, `1915 Nobel Prize`), who had purified peroxidase until no protein test showed up (`because enzymes are so potent that catalytic activity remained even when protein concentration fell below his chemical detection threshold!`) and insisted Sumner's crystals were just inert protein contaminated with a "true" non-protein enzyme!
  - Sumner fought back for nine years — until **John Howard Northrop** (1891–1987) at the Rockefeller Institute crystallised stomach **`pepsin` (1930)**, pancreatic **`trypsin` (1932)**, and **`chymotrypsin` (1935)**, proving every single one was a **pure protein** and winning **Sumner and Northrop the 1946 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** (shared with **Wendell Stanley** `064`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Richard Willstätter (1872–1942) — German organic chemist · WHO: John Howard Northrop (1891–1987) — American biochemist · WHEN: 1917 · WHEN: August 1926 · WHEN: 1930 · WHEN: 1932 · WHEN: 1946 · FACT: Enzymes are pure folded proteins · DATA: inked Sumner (August 1926 J. Biol. Chem. 69: 435–441) octahedral jack-bean urease crystal activity table & Northrop (1930 J. Gen. Physiol. 13: 739–766) crystalline pepsin solubility/activity proof on period paper showing constant specific enzymatic activity across repeated protein recrystallisations and urease's 10^14-fold catalytic rate acceleration · SCALE: Willstätter missed the protein in his diluted enzyme solutions because enzymes are so powerful that a pinch of enzyme too small for a 1920s protein test to spot could still chew through millions of molecules a second`
- **Ends on:** Sumner and Northrop proved that enzymes are **folded protein chains**. Biologists now faced a deeper mechanical puzzle: if an enzyme is just a rigid protein keyhole (`Fischer 1894`), why doesn't the target molecule just sit snugly inside the keyhole forever? How does an enzyme actually **break a chemical bond**?

### PART 04: February 1958 & 22 May 1965 — Daniel Koshland's `Induced-Fit` Glove & David Phillips's 3D X-Ray Movie of `Lysozyme` (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open (why an enzyme that fits a peaceful, unstrained molecule too perfectly is actually a terrible catalyst — and how an enzyme really works by grabbing a molecule and physically bending it toward its breaking point!):** **Brookhaven National Laboratory, New York, February 1958 & Royal Institution, London, 22 May 1965**.
- **1946 & February 1958 (*PNAS*) — Linus Pauling's Transition State & Daniel Koshland's `Induced-Fit` Theory:**
  - In **1930** (**J. B. S. Haldane**) and **1946** (**Linus Pauling**), chemists realized a crucial secret: to speed up a reaction, an enzyme's **`active site`** must not be shaped to fit the relaxed starting molecule — it must be shaped to fit the strained, halfway-broken **`transition state`**, pulling the molecule up and over the **activation-energy barrier**!
  - Then in **February 1958** (*PNAS*), American biochemist **Daniel E. Koshland Jr.** (1920–2007) solved how the enzyme closes around its target with the **`Induced-Fit Theory`**: an enzyme isn't a rigid metal lock (`Fischer 1894`), it is a **flexible hand or glove** that changes its 3D shape only when the right molecule binds, swinging its catalytic amino-acid claws into exact position!
- **22 May 1965 (*Nature*) — David Phillips & Louise Johnson solve the first 3D X-ray atomic structure of an enzyme (`Lysozyme`):**
  - Nobody had ever *seen* an enzyme's active site at atomic resolution — until **22 May 1965** (*Nature*), when **David C. Phillips** (1924–1999), **Colin Blake**, **Louise Johnson** (1940–2012), and colleagues at the Royal Institution in London (using Lawrence Bragg's X-ray crystallography `003`, `016` and heavy-atom isomorphous replacement) mapped all **129 amino acids (~1,950 atoms)** of **hen egg-white `lysozyme`** (`the natural antibacterial enzyme in human tears discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1922`) at **`2-angstrom` (`0.2 nm`) resolution**!
  - Right across the middle of the protein sat a deep cleft (`subsites A–F`) that grabs a 6-sugar chain of a bacterium's cell wall. As the cleft closes slightly (**`induced fit`**), it physically **forces the fourth sugar ring (`ring D`) to twist out of its comfortable armchair shape into a strained half-chair sofa shape**, while two amino acids — **glutamic acid 35 (`Glu35`)** and **aspartic acid 52 (`Asp52`)** — act like a pair of chemical wire-cutters to donate a proton, stabilise the charged intermediate, and **snap the bacterial cell wall in half**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Linus Pauling (1901–1994) — American chemist · WHO: Louise N. Johnson (1940–2012) — British structural biochemist · WHEN: 1922 · WHEN: 1946 · WHEN: February 1958 · WHEN: 22 May 1965 · FACT: Bends sugar rings until they snap · DATA: inked Koshland (February 1958 PNAS 44: 98–104) Induced-Fit conformational alignment diagram & Blake, Koenig, Mair, North, Phillips & Sarma (22 May 1965 Nature 206: 757–761) 2-angstrom hen egg-white lysozyme 129-amino-acid active-site cleft chart on period paper showing subsites A–F distorting bacterial peptidoglycan sugar ring D into a half-chair transition state between Glu35 and Asp52 · SCALE: breaking a sugar chain with lysozyme is like snapping a green stick over your knee — the enzyme's cleft grabs both ends and bends the middle ring into a tight curve until the two chemical claws clip the strained bond with effortless ease`
- **Ends on:** Enzymes explain how hundreds of chemical reactions race inside your cells every millisecond. But what keeps all those enzymes, sugars, and ions **inside** the cell instead of washing away — and how does a **liquid soap-bubble skin just two molecules (`5 nanometres`) thick (`the cell membrane`)** decide what gets in and what stays out?

### PART 05: Why Cyanide, Penicillin & Aspirin All Work by Jamming Enzymes — & How a Two-Molecule Soap Bubble Keeps Your Cells Alive (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (why nearly every medicine in a pharmacy — and nearly every poison in a murder mystery — is actually an artificial key designed to jam a specific enzyme's active site: aspirin `041` plugs the `COX` pain enzyme, penicillin `020` jams the bacterial cell-wall transpeptidase enzyme, statins block the cholesterol enzyme `HMG-CoA reductase`, and deadly cyanide locks the mitochondrial cytochrome oxidase enzyme `063`!):** Once **David Phillips** showed the world what an enzyme's **active site** looks like in 3D in **1965**, he unlocked modern **structure-based drug design**: nearly every medicine in your cabinet works as a custom-shaped key (**`an enzyme inhibitor`**) that slips into a target enzyme's active site and jams it shut — from **aspirin (`041`)** blocking the pain enzyme `cyclooxygenase`, to **penicillin (`020`)** jamming the enzyme bacteria use to weld their cell walls!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Inside every cell (`015`), thousands of enzymes (`070`), mitochondria (`063`), and ribosomes work together in a bustling chemical city.
  - Yet the wall that separates that living city from the outside world — the **cell membrane** — is a liquid fat film **just two molecules (`5 nanometres`) thick**, twenty thousand times thinner than a sheet of paper!
  - How did **Ernest Overton in 1895** and Dutch scientists **Evert Gorter and François Grendel in April 1925** spread the fats from red blood cells onto a tray of water (`with a silk thread!`) and discover the **`2:1` phospholipid bilayer**?
  - How did **Jens Christian Skou in 1957** boil **25,000 shore crabs** to find the **`sodium-potassium pump`** that burns a third of your body's energy, and how did **Jonathan Singer and Garth Nicolson in February 1972** prove the **`Fluid Mosaic Model`**?
