### PART 02: Torn Newspaper, Freshwater Reptiles, and the Greenland Ice (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (the torn-newspaper analogy):** **Marburg & Frankfurt, Germany, December 1910 – 6 January 1912**. At Christmas **1910**, 30-year-old German meteorologist and record-setting hot-air balloonist **Alfred Wegener** (1880–1930) was paging through his roommate's new world atlas when he noticed that the **submerged 200-metre continental shelves** of South America and Africa fit together even more tightly than their beaches!
- Wegener compared it to **two pieces of torn newspaper**: if you press two torn edges together and **every single printed line of type reads straight across the tear**, you cannot claim it is a coincidence!
- On **6 January 1912** in Frankfurt (and in his **1915** book *Die Entstehung der Kontinente und Ozeane*, where he coined **Pangaea**, "All-Earth"), Wegener laid out four lines of type that ran straight across the ocean:
  1. **Continental shelves:** Brazil and West Africa fit across **4,500 km** with less than 1 degree of gap;
  2. **Torn mountain chains:** The **Appalachian Mountains** of North America end abruptly at the coast of Newfoundland — and pick up again with rocks of the **exact same age and folding** in the Caledonian mountains of Ireland, Scotland, and Norway;
  3. **Impossible fossils:** Skeletons of ***Mesosaurus*** — a 1-metre freshwater reptile with webbed feet that could never swim across 5,000 km of salt ocean — appear *only* in black Permian shales in **southern Brazil and South Africa**, while fossil leaves of the seed fern ***Glossopteris*** span South America, Africa, India, Antarctica, and Australia;
  4. **Upside-down climates:** Wegener himself had seen **tropical coal beds (ancient equatorial swamps) frozen under Arctic ice in Spitsbergen**, while **300-million-year-old glacier scratches (`glacial striations`)** scored the hot deserts of South Africa, India, and Australia!
- **Why geologists savaged him — and his tragic death in Greenland (1924–November 1930):** Yet Wegener made one fatal mechanical error: he proposed that lighter granite continents **ploughed directly through the solid basalt floor of the ocean like icebreakers through sea ice**, pushed by Earth's centrifugal spin (*Polflucht*) and gravitational tides!
- Cambridge physicist **Harold Jeffreys** (1891–1989) calculated in **1924** that tidal and spin forces were **ten billion times (`10¹⁰×`) too weak** to push a continent through solid basalt rock without the continent crumbling to pieces! At the **15 November 1926 New York symposium**, American geologists tore Wegener's theory to shreds.
- In **November 1930** — days after his 50th birthday on **1 November 1930** — after leading a desperate ski rescue across the **Greenland ice sheet in `-54 °C` cold** to resupply his stranded weather station at *Eismitte*, Wegener collapsed and died on the ice on the return journey, buried under a pair of skis as his theory was written off as a delusion.
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: December 1910 · WHEN: 6 January 1912 · WHEN: 1915 · WHO: Harold Jeffreys (1891–1989) — British mathematician and geophysicist · WHEN: 15 November 1926 · WHEN: November 1930 · FACT: Died rescuing Arctic team · DATA: inked four-line "torn newspaper" diagram on period paper showing (1) 200 m continental shelf fit, (2) Appalachian–Caledonian mountain chain alignment, (3) Mesosaurus & Glossopteris fossil bands across 5,000 km, and (4) Harold Jeffreys's 1924 calculation that tidal force is 10¹⁰× too weak to plough granite through solid basalt · SCALE: the tidal push Wegener relied on was ten billion times too weak — like trying to push a brick wall through solid concrete by blowing on it`
- **Ends on:** Wegener's critics were right about one thing: granite continents *cannot* plough through the solid ocean floor. So how *do* continents move — and why did the answer stay hidden three miles underwater until a woman who wasn't allowed on a ship drew the bottom of the Atlantic?

### PART 03: "Girl Talk" and the 16,000-Kilometre Scar Under the Sea (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open:** **Palisades, New York, Autumn 1952 – 1953**. Until World War II and the Cold War, scientists knew more about the face of the Moon than the bottom of Earth's oceans, which most geologists assumed was a flat, featureless abyss of mud.
- Starting in **1947–1952**, Columbia University oceanographer **Bruce Heezen** (1924–1977) sailed back and forth across the Atlantic Ocean sending continuous **sonar (echo-sounder) pings** to the seabed (`depth = ½ × speed of sound in water ~1,500 m/s × echo time`).
- Because **superstitious maritime rules barred women from working aboard oceanographic research ships**, geologist and mathematician **Marie Tharp** (1920–2006) stayed behind in a basement drafting room at Columbia's **Lamont Geological Observatory**, painstakingly translating miles of paper sonar rolls into **six high-precision east–west cross-sections of the North Atlantic floor**.
- **Autumn 1952 — The V-shaped rift valley:** As Tharp inked all six profiles side by side, she noticed something extraordinary: running right down the crest of the **16,000-kilometre Mid-Atlantic Ridge** was a continuous, steep-walled **V-shaped cleft (`1.5 to 3 km` deep and `15 to 48 km` wide)** — a giant tension crack where the ocean crust was being **pulled apart from below**!
- When she showed the rift valley to **Bruce Heezen**, he groaned and dismissed it for a year as **"girl talk"**, saying: *"It cannot be. It looks too much like continental drift!"* He made her erase and redraw the map!
- **The earthquake overlay epiphany (1953):** At the exact same desk, Heezen had hired young draftsman **Howard Foster** to plot the epicentres of thousands of underwater earthquakes on a transparent sheet of plastic (for a Navy submarine-cable contract). When they laid Foster's transparent earthquake map directly over Marie Tharp's redrawn rift valley, **the string of earthquake dots ran straight down the middle of Tharp's V-shaped rift** — and extended for **65,000 kilometres** around the entire globe like the seams on a baseball!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Bruce Heezen (1924–1977) — American marine geologist · WHEN: Autumn 1952 · WHEN: 1953 · WHEN: 1957 · FACT: Barred from research ships · DATA: inked cross-section and overlay on period paper showing sonar echo depth (d = ½ v t) revealing the Mid-Atlantic Ridge with Marie Tharp's 1.5–3 km deep V-shaped rift valley at its crest, matched 1-to-1 by Howard Foster's 1953 earthquake epicentre dots along a 65,000 km global seam · SCALE: a continuous underwater mountain chain and rift valley stretching 65,000 kilometres around the planet — long enough to wrap around Earth's equator more than one and a half times`
- **Ends on:** Marie Tharp had found the giant seam where the ocean floor was splitting open. Case closed? **Wrong.** If brand-new rock is pouring up along a 65,000-kilometre crack and Earth isn't getting any bigger, why doesn't the planet blow up like a balloon — and what proved the ocean floor is actually a moving conveyor belt?

### PART 04: The Magnetic Barcode on the Ocean Conveyor Belt — Midpoint Twist (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Princeton, New Jersey, 1960–1962 & Cambridge, England, 7 September 1963**. Even after Tharp and Heezen published their famous **1957–1959** physiographic maps of the ocean floor (painted with Austrian artist **Heinrich Berann**), a few scientists (including Heezen himself for a time!) wondered if Earth itself was *expanding*!
- **Harry Hess's seafloor conveyor belt (1960–1962):** In **1960** (published **November 1962**, *"History of Ocean Basins"*), Princeton geologist and former WWII naval transport captain **Harry Hess** (1906–1969) revived British geologist **Arthur Holmes's 1929** theory of **mantle convection** to solve Wegener's mechanical riddle:
  - Continents do **not** plough *through* the ocean floor!
  - Instead, slow churning currents of hot rock deep in Earth's mantle rise beneath **Marie Tharp's mid-ocean rift**, erupt as fresh basalt lava to form new ocean floor, and move outward to both sides like **two giant conveyor belts** — carrying the continents embedded in the plates as passive passengers!
  - And why doesn't Earth expand? Because at the far edges of the ocean (like the **11-kilometre-deep Mariana Trench** around the Pacific), the cold, dense old ocean floor **dives back down ("subducts") into the mantle** to be melted and recycled!
- **The smoking gun — Vine & Matthews's magnetic zebra stripes (7 September 1963):** How could anyone prove the solid rock of the seabed was really moving outward from the rift? During the 1950s, ships towing **magnetometers** (originally built to hunt steel submarines!) found baffling **alternating stripes of normal and reversed magnetism** across the ocean floor.
- On **7 September 1963** in *Nature* (alongside Canadian geophysicist **Lawrence Morley**, whose paper had been rejected months earlier with the referee snark that *"such speculation makes interesting talk at cocktail parties, but it is not the sort of thing that ought to be published"*!), 24-year-old Cambridge PhD student **Fred Vine** (1939–1988) and his supervisor **Drummond Matthews** (1931–1997) connected two facts:
  1. Earth's magnetic field **flips north and south** every few hundred thousand years;
  2. When molten basalt lava erupts along Marie Tharp's rift and cools below its **Curie point (`~580 °C` for magnetite)**, its tiny iron crystals lock in the direction of Earth's magnetic field like a **frozen compass needle**!
  - As the seafloor splits and spreads to both sides, each magnetic flip prints a **mirror-image pair of magnetic zebra stripes** on the left and right of the ridge — an **exact tape-recorder barcode of time** proving the oceans are spreading and that **no ocean crust on Earth is older than ~200 million years** (while the continents are up to **4 billion years old**)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Harry Hess (1906–1969) — American geologist and Navy officer · WHO: Arthur Holmes (1890–1965) — British geologist · WHO: Fred Vine (1939–1988) — British marine geophysicist · WHO: Drummond Matthews (1931–1997) — British marine geologist · WHEN: November 1962 · WHEN: 7 September 1963 · FACT: Magnetic field flips north-south · DATA: inked seafloor conveyor-belt and magnetic-barcode diagram on period paper showing mantle upwelling at the mid-ocean rift, magnetite locking in Earth's magnetic field below ~580 °C, symmetrical normal/reversed magnetic stripes on both sides of the ridge, and oceanic crust age (≤ 200 million years vs 4,000 million years for continents) · SCALE: while continental rocks are up to 4 billion years old, the entire ocean floor recycles itself like a conveyor belt every 200 million years`
- **Ends on:** Vine and Matthews's magnetic barcode vindicated Alfred Wegener thirty-three years after he froze to death in Greenland. So does that mean the continents float on a liquid ocean of red-hot magma beneath our feet?

### PART 05: Solid Rock That Flows & How Continents Float (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** Many people picture Earth's tectonic plates floating on an underground sea of liquid red-hot lava. Is that true?
- **No:** Seismic waves from earthquakes (`030`) prove that **Earth's mantle is nearly 99% solid rock**! Under immense heat and pressure deep underground, solid mantle rock creeps and flows slowly over millions of years — the way glacier ice or hot wax deforms under weight — while rigid **lithospheric plates (`~100 km` thick, carrying both continents and ocean floor)** ride on top, measured directly to the millimetre today by **GPS satellites (`1.5 to 10 cm/year`)**!
- And why do the thick granite continents (`density ~2.7 g/cm³`) ride high above the sea for four billion years without sinking, while denser basalt ocean plates (`density ~3.0 g/cm³`) sink back into the mantle (`~3.3 g/cm³`)?
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Because continents literally **float in denser rock** by the exact same physical law that keeps an iceberg or a 100,000-tonne steel ship afloat in water: **buoyancy**!
  - How did we discover the law of buoyancy in the first place — and why did 22-year-old **Galileo Galilei** prove in **1586** that the famous story of **Archimedes** measuring spilled water from a bathtub to catch a crooked goldsmith was physically impossible (`031` candidate *Archimedes' Bathtub, the Golden Crown & Buoyancy*)?
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1586 · DATA: animated timeline building 6 Jan 1912 & 1915 (Alfred Wegener presents continental drift & Pangaea in Frankfurt) · 15 Nov 1926 & Nov 1930 (geologists reject Wegener in New York; Wegener dies at age 50 rescuing his team in Greenland) · Autumn 1952 & 1953 (Marie Tharp discovers the Mid-Atlantic Ridge V-shaped rift valley; earthquake epicentres match her rift) · Nov 1962 (Harry Hess publishes seafloor spreading) · 7 Sep 1963 (Fred Vine & Drummond Matthews prove seafloor spreading with symmetrical magnetic zebra stripes) · Today (GPS satellites measure plates moving at 1.5–10 cm/year) · SCALE: granite continents (2.7 g/cm³) float high above basalt ocean crust (3.0 g/cm³) on the denser mantle (3.3 g/cm³) like tall wooden blocks floating beside thin iron plates · FACT: Earth's mantle is solid rock`
