### PART 02: The Sideways Compass and the Cup of Mercury (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open:** **Copenhagen, Denmark, 21 April 1820**. During an evening lecture to advanced students at the University of Copenhagen, physics professor **Hans Christian Ørsted** (1777–1851) connects a thin platinum wire to a large Voltaic battery (`019`) directly over a magnetic compass needle pointing north.
- The instant the circuit closes, the compass needle **twitches sideways**!
- **Why everyone was baffled:** Since **Isaac Newton** (`009`), every known force in physics — gravity, static electricity, ordinary magnetism — pulled or pushed in a **straight line** directly toward or away from an object. Yet Ørsted's compass needle doesn't point *toward* the wire or *away* from it: it turns to stand at a **90-degree right angle** to the wire! Move the compass *under* the wire, and it swings the opposite way (`21 July 1820` Latin pamphlet *Experimenta circa effectum conflictus electrici in acum magneticam*).
- **Enter the bookbinder's apprentice:** In London, **Sir Humphry Davy** (1778–1829) and **William Hyde Wollaston** (1766–1828) at the Royal Institution try and fail to make a wire spin on its own axis. Watching quietly is Davy's 29-year-old laboratory assistant **Michael Faraday** (1791–1867) — a blacksmith's son who left school at thirteen, worked seven years binding books, and taught himself science by reading the encyclopaedias he bound!
- Because Faraday knows almost no formal algebra, he doesn't think in equations; he thinks in **pictures**. He realises the magnetic force around Ørsted's wire isn't pushing or pulling in straight lines at all — it wraps around the wire in **concentric circles**!
- **Monday & Tuesday, 3–4 September 1821 — the first electric motor:** In the basement lab of the Royal Institution, Faraday stands a bar magnet upright in wax at the bottom of a glass cup filled with conductive liquid **mercury**, hangs a stiff copper wire from a hook so its lower tip dips into the mercury, and connects a battery.
- Instantly, the copper wire **whirls continuously in circles** around the magnet pole for as long as the battery stays connected — converting electrical energy into continuous mechanical rotation for the first time in human history! Faraday dances around the lab table with his young brother-in-law George Barnard, shouting: *"There they go! There they go! We have succeeded at last!"*
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Copenhagen, 21 April 1820 · WHEN: 21 July 1820 · WHO: Humphry Davy (1778–1829) — Royal Society president · WHO: William Hyde Wollaston (1766–1828) — English physicist & chemist · FACT: Left school at age 13 · WHEN: London, 3–4 September 1821 · DATA: inked force-geometry diagram on period paper contrasting Newton's straight-line central forces (push/pull along the line joining two bodies) vs Ørsted and Faraday's 90-degree circular magnetic lines of force wrapping around a current-carrying wire · SCALE: a 90-degree sideways push — instead of pulling the wire in and stopping, the circular magnetic field chases the wire around in an endless circle`
- **Ends on:** Faraday had just invented the electric motor at age twenty-nine. Why did his greatest triumph almost ruin his career — and why did his own mentor turn against him?

### PART 03: The Blackball Vote and the Ten-Year Silence (3:05–4:55)
- **Cold open:** **London, October 1821 – 8 January 1824**. In October 1821, Faraday rushes his discovery into print in the *Quarterly Journal of Science* — and forgets to cite a passing conversation between **Humphry Davy** and **William Hyde Wollaston**.
- **Rivalry & jealousy beat:** Nine years earlier, when Davy had been temporarily blinded by a chemical explosion (`nitrogen trichloride`), he had hired young Faraday as his amanuensis, and later joked that his greatest discovery was *"Michael Faraday."* Now, watching his former servant eclipse him across Europe, Davy burns with resentment.
- Rumours spread that Faraday plagiarised Wollaston. Even after Faraday writes directly to Wollaston to clear his name and Wollaston graciously accepts, **Davy publicly opposes Faraday's election as a Fellow of the Royal Society**!
- When the ballot is finally held on **8 January 1824**, Faraday is elected — with **one solitary blackball vote** cast against him!
- For the next several years, Davy assigns Faraday tedious government committee work (making optical glass and analysing steel) that keeps him away from electromagnetism.
- Yet Faraday never forgets the reverse question he wrote in his notebook in **1822**: **"Convert magnetism into electricity."**
- Every physicist in Europe tries clamping powerful horseshoe magnets next to copper coils and watching galvanometers: **nothing happens**. Why won't a steady magnet make a steady electric current?
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: October 1821 · WHEN: 1822 · WHEN: 8 January 1824 · FACT: One blackball vote against him`
- **Ends on:** After Humphry Davy died in 1829, Faraday was finally free to return to his basement. What happened on 29 August 1831 when he wrapped four hundred feet of wire around an iron ring — and saw the needle move for just half a second?

### PART 04: The Iron Ring and the Half-Second Twitch — Midpoint Twist (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Royal Institution Basement, London, 29 August 1831**. Faraday takes a solid ring of soft wrought iron **6 inches (`15 cm`) in diameter** and **7/8ths of an inch thick**.
- Around Side A of the iron ring, he winds **72 feet** (in 3 coils totalling **203 feet**) of copper wire insulated with calico cloth and twine, connected to a 10-pair Voltaic battery. Around Side B — separated by air and cloth so no electricity can cross directly — he winds **about 60 feet** (2 coils, widened to ~203 feet in his notes) leading to a magnetic needle three feet away.
- He expects that while the battery current flows steadily through Side A, turning the iron ring into a strong electromagnet, a steady current will flow in Side B.
- He connects the battery. **The needle jerks violently — and then settles straight back to zero!**
- While the full battery current pours through the iron ring, the needle on the second coil sits **completely dead**!
- Baffled, Faraday disconnects the battery wire. **The needle jerks again — in the exact opposite direction!**
- **The eureka twist (17 October & 28 October 1831):** In a flash, Faraday sees why every physicist in Europe had failed for eleven years: **a stationary magnet never generates electricity!** Electricity is induced *only* while the magnetic field is **changing or moving**!
- On **17 October 1831**, Faraday winds **220 feet of copper wire** around a hollow paper cylinder and thrusts a cylindrical bar magnet (`8.5 inches` long) briskly in: the galvanometer needle swings one way; pull the magnet out, and it swings the other way!
- Eleven days later (**28 October 1831**), at the Royal Society and Royal Institution, Faraday mounts a **12-inch (`30 cm`) copper disc** on an axle between the poles of a giant compound magnet and turns the handle: as the copper cuts continuously through the invisible magnetic lines, a **steady, continuous electric current** flows out — **the world's first electrical generator (dynamo)**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: London, 29 August 1831 · WHEN: 17 October 1831 · WHEN: 28 October 1831 · FACT: Only moving magnets make current · DATA: inked galvanometer trace on period paper showing Faraday's 29 August 1831 & 17 October 1831 induction experiment (spike +I at battery connect / magnet thrust in → 0 while steady → spike -I at disconnect / magnet pull out) · SCALE: zero volts from a stationary magnet, even if it weighs a ton — but move the magnet with your hand and the electrons jump immediately`
- **Ends on:** How could a magnet push electrons inside a copper wire without ever touching it — and why did mathematicians laugh at Faraday's invisible "lines of force" until a young Scottish physicist turned them into the discovery of radio waves and light itself?

### PART 05: Invisible Lines of Force & the Speed of Light (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** People often think empty space between a magnet and a nail is completely empty, with the magnet reaching across "nothingness" at a distance. Is that true?
- **No:** in **1851–1852**, sprinkling fine **iron filings** onto a sheet of paper over a bar magnet and tracing the curved arcs, Faraday proposed a revolutionary idea that formal mathematicians ridiculed: space itself is filled with physical **magnetic and electric fields ("lines of force")**, and energy lives in the *space around* the wires and magnets!
- When a politician or visitor allegedly asked Faraday what practical use his tiny spinning disc and twitching needle could possibly have, every modern city on Earth became his answer: spin a Faraday motor with electricity and you get motion; spin a Faraday generator with wind, water or steam and you get electricity!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - One young Scottish mathematician didn't laugh at the self-taught bookbinder's invisible lines of force. In **1861–1865**, **James Clerk Maxwell** (1831–1879) translated Faraday's curved lines into four mathematical equations — and discovered that a rippling wave of electric and magnetic fields travels through empty space at **300,000 kilometres per second**: the exact speed of **light** (`018` Herschel), proving that light itself is an electromagnetic wave!
  - And what happens when you fire those high-voltage electric currents through a glass tube and accidentally fog a photographic plate inside a dark desk drawer? In **February 1896**, French physicist **Henri Becquerel** opened a cloudy-day drawer in Paris and discovered a strange energy coming from inside the atom itself (`022` candidate *Radioactivity: Becquerel's Drawer & Marie Curie's Shed*, or `020` *Penicillin*).
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1852 · FACT: Iron filings mapped the field · WHEN: 1865 · WHO: James Clerk Maxwell (1831–1879) — Scottish mathematical physicist · DATA: animated timeline building 21 Apr 1820 (Ørsted's sideways compass in Copenhagen) · 4 Sep 1821 (Faraday's mercury-cup electric motor) · 8 Jan 1824 (Royal Society election with 1 blackball) · 29 Aug 1831 (Faraday's iron-ring electromagnetic induction) · 17 & 28 Oct 1831 (bar-magnet coil & 12-inch copper-disc dynamo) · 1852 (lines of force / magnetic field) · 1865 (Maxwell's electromagnetic wave equations at 300,000 km/s) · SCALE: 300,000 kilometres per second — Maxwell proved Faraday's electric and magnetic lines ripple through space at the exact speed of light, seven and a half times around Earth every second`
