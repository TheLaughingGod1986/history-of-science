### PART 02: The Race to Absolute Zero & the Teacup of Liquid Helium (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (the ultimate floor of temperature):** **Paris, 1702, Glasgow, 1848 & Leiden, 10 July 1908**. You can heat matter as hot as you like — millions of degrees inside a star — so why is there a hard floor to how **cold** something can get?
- Because **heat is simply the jiggling motion of atoms** (`024` Joule & Rumford)! In **1702**, French physicist **Guillaume Amontons** noticed that air pressure in a sealed glass bulb drops steadily as it cools, implying a temperature where pressure hits zero; and in **1848**, **William Thomson (Lord Kelvin)** calculated the exact bottom of the thermodynamic scale: **Absolute Zero (`0 Kelvin` / `-273.15 °C`)**, where classical thermal motion stops completely!
- **The race to liquefy the "permanent" gases (1877–1908):** One by one, scientists cooled and compressed gases until they turned to liquid: oxygen and nitrogen (`77 K` / `-196 °C`), and in **1898** at the Royal Institution in London, Scottish chemist **Sir James Dewar** (1842–1923, inventor of the silvered vacuum **Dewar flask** / Thermos bottle!) liquefied **hydrogen at `20.4 Kelvin` (`-252.8 °C`)**.
- Only one gas on Earth refused to turn into a liquid: **helium** (discovered in the Sun's spectrum in 1868 and isolated on Earth in 1895).
- **10 July 1908 — Heike Kamerlingh Onnes wins the race in Leiden:** At Leiden University, **Heike Kamerlingh Onnes** (1853–1926) built a factory-scale cryogenic laboratory with brass cascade compressors, glassblowers, and the motto *"Door meten tot weten"* (*"Through measurement to knowledge"*).
  - Starting at **5:45 a.m. on 10 July 1908**, using 75 litres of liquid air and 20 litres of liquid hydrogen to pre-cool 200 litres of pure helium gas through a Joule-Thomson expansion valve, Onnes worked for nearly fourteen hours until **7:30 p.m.** — when a shimmering surface of **liquid helium (`60 millilitres`, a teacupful)** stood in his central glass flask at **`4.22 Kelvin` (`-268.93 °C`)**!
  - By pumping the vapour off the liquid helium, Onnes reached **`1.04 Kelvin` (`-272.11 °C`)** — making a room in Holland colder than the `2.7 Kelvin` cosmic background of deep space (`1913 Nobel Prize in Physics`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: James Dewar (1842–1923) — Scottish chemist and physicist · WHEN: 1702 · WHEN: 1848 · WHEN: 1898 · WHEN: 10 July 1908 · FACT: Colder than deep space · DATA: inked cascade cooling diagram on period paper showing Kamerlingh Onnes's 4-stage Leiden liquefier on 10 July 1908: methyl chloride (183 K) → ethylene (169 K) → oxygen/air (77 K) → liquid hydrogen (20.4 K) → 60 mL of liquid helium at 4.22 K (−268.93 °C), pumped down to 1.04 K · SCALE: sixty millilitres of liquid helium — barely enough to fill an espresso cup — made a room in Leiden colder than any natural place in the known universe`
- **Ends on:** Now that Kamerlingh Onnes controlled the coldest liquid in the universe, he decided to settle the biggest debate in physics: what happens to **electricity inside a metal wire** when the atoms stop jiggling?

### PART 03: "Kwik Nagenoeg Nul" — 8 April 1911 & the Current That Never Died (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open:** **Leiden, 8 April 1911, 4:00 p.m.** Why do ordinary wires resist electricity? Because as electrons flow through copper or gold, they collide with the thermal vibrations of the metal's atoms AND with tiny impurity flaws.
- **Three rival predictions in 1911:**
  1. **Paul Drude & Hendrik Lorentz:** resistance should fall smoothly as thermal vibrations quiet down;
  2. **heinrich Matthiessen / Kamerlingh Onnes (at first):** resistance should level off at a flat floor set by impurities;
  3. **Lord Kelvin (`1902`):** electrons themselves would **freeze onto their atoms**, turning metals into insulators with **infinite resistance**!
- **Why mercury?** When Onnes tested gold and platinum wires in liquid helium, trace impurities left a stubborn residual resistance. What was the only metal on Earth that could be purified to **99.9999% perfection** by distilling it again and again as a liquid at room temperature? **Mercury (`Hg`)**!
- **8 April 1911 — Gilles Holst watches the needle drop to zero:** Onnes, assistant **Cornelis Dorsman**, and technician **Gilles Holst** (1886–1968, sitting in a quiet basement room with the mirror galvanometer and Wheatstone bridge) froze a thin glass U-tube capillary of quadruple-distilled mercury in liquid helium:
  - At `4.3 Kelvin`, the mercury's resistance was a tiny fraction of an ohm.
  - Then, at **4:00 p.m. on 8 April 1911**, as the helium bath cooled to **`4.20 Kelvin` (`-268.95 °C`)**, the resistance **dropped to zero in a single step** (`less than 10⁻⁷ ohms`)!
  - At first they thought a wire had touched and **short-circuited** the tube! They warmed it up above `4.2 K`: the resistance popped right back. They cooled it down again: at `4.20 K`, it vanished! Onnes wrote in his blue notebook: ***"Kwik nagenoeg nul"*** (*"Mercury practically zero"*) — naming the phenomenon **"supraconductivity" (superconductivity)** in **1913**, and finding that **tin (`3.7 K`)** and **lead (`7.2 K`)** turned into superconductors too!
- **1914 — The year-long current with no battery:** In **1914**, Onnes induced a current in a closed ring of superconducting lead wire inside liquid helium, **unplugged the battery**, and watched a magnetic needle beside the flask: the current ran undiminished at a decay rate of **less than 1% per hour** — and modern tests prove a superconducting ring current lasts **over 100,000 years**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 8 April 1911 · WHEN: 1913 · WHEN: 1914 · FACT: Current flows 100,000+ years · DATA: inked resistance cliff chart on period paper from Kamerlingh Onnes's 1911 Leiden Communication No. 122b showing solid mercury resistance falling from 0.115 Ω at 4.3 K to <10⁻⁵ Ω at 4.20 K, alongside lead (Tc = 7.2 K) and tin (Tc = 3.7 K) · SCALE: a current started inside a superconducting ring with the battery unplugged will keep circling longer than the entire history of human civilisation`
- **Ends on:** For twenty-two years, every physicist on Earth thought a superconductor was simply a wire with **zero resistance**. Case closed? **Wrong.** What happened in Berlin in **October 1933** when two physicists placed a magnet next to a superconductor — and why did it **defy gravity**?

### PART 04: The Meissner Levitation Shock & the 46-Year Quantum Mystery — Midpoint Twist (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Berlin, October 1933 & Urbana, Illinois, February 1957**. If a superconductor were just an ordinary "perfect conductor" (`R = 0`), then by **Michael Faraday's law of induction (`021`)**, if you placed a warm metal cylinder inside a stationary magnetic field and cooled it down, the magnetic field lines should stay **frozen right inside the metal**.
- **October 1933 — Walther Meissner & Robert Ochsenfeld's shock:** At the *Physikalisch-Technische Reichsanstalt* in Berlin in **October 1933**, German physicists **Walther Meissner** (1882–1974) and **Robert Ochsenfeld** (1901–1993) cooled single crystals of tin and lead inside a weak magnetic field while measuring the field outside with a search coil.
  - The instant the metal crossed its critical temperature, the magnetic field outside **jumped up**: the superconductor had **actively kicked the entire magnetic field out of its interior (`B = 0` inside)**!
  - This active magnetic expulsion — **the Meissner Effect** — proved superconductivity is a brand-new **thermodynamic state of matter**, not just zero resistance! Because a superconductor refuses to let magnetic field lines enter (or unpin in Type II superconductors), a magnet placed above a superconductor sits on an **invisible cushion of repelled magnetic field lines, floating and locked in mid-air** (`first photographed by Vladislav Arkadiev in 1945`)!
- **Why Einstein, Bohr, Heisenberg, and Feynman all failed for 46 years (1911 → 1957):**
  - Why couldn't two negative electrons repel each other and scatter off atoms?
  - Finally, in **February 1957** at the University of Illinois, **John Bardeen** (`029` — who had already won the **1956 Nobel Prize** for co-inventing the silicon transistor!), postdoc **Leon Cooper** (1930–2024), and 25-year-old graduate student **John Robert Schrieffer** (1931–2019) cracked it (**BCS Theory**, winning the **1972 Nobel Prize in Physics** — making **John Bardeen the only person in history to win two Nobel Prizes in Physics**!):
  - As one negative electron zips through the crystal lattice of positive metal ions, it pulls the positive ions slightly toward its path — creating a fleeting ripple of concentrated positive charge (`a phonon`, like a bowling ball denting a mattress) that pulls a *second* trailing electron in behind it!
  - Bound together as a **Cooper pair**, millions of electron pairs lock step into a **single macroscopic quantum wave** (`Bose-Einstein-like condensate`): you can't slow down one pair by bumping a single atom unless you have enough thermal energy to break the entire quantum dance at once!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Leon Cooper (1930–2024) — American physicist · WHO: Robert Schrieffer (1931–2019) — American physicist · WHEN: October 1933 · WHEN: 1945 · WHEN: February 1957 · WHEN: 1972 · FACT: Bardeen won 2 Physics Nobels · DATA: inked two-panel diagram on period paper comparing a classical metal above Tc (magnetic field lines pass straight through; single electrons scatter off vibrating lattice ions, wasting heat) vs a superconductor below Tc (Meissner Effect expels magnetic field B = 0 to levitate a magnet; phonon lattice attraction binds electrons into frictionless BCS Cooper pairs) · SCALE: forty-six years elapsed between Kamerlingh Onnes seeing zero resistance in 1911 and Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer explaining how two negative electrons team up through crystal vibrations in 1957`
- **Ends on:** Until 1986, every known superconductor needed expensive liquid helium below **`23 Kelvin` (`-250 °C`)**. How did two researchers in Zurich break through the **liquid-nitrogen barrier (`77 K`)**, and how do superconductors power every hospital **MRI** and `603 km/h` maglev train today?

### PART 05: The 1986 Liquid-Nitrogen Breakthrough, MRI Scanners & the Steam Train Full of Trumpets (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (breaking the 77 K wall):** **IBM Zurich, January 1986 & Houston/Huntsville, January 1987**. Why would anybody think a brittle **ceramic oxide** — normally an electrical *insulator* like a coffee mug! — could become a superconductor?
- In **January 1986** (published April 1986, **1987 Nobel Prize**), Swiss physicist **K. Alex Müller** and German physicist **J. Georg Bednorz** at IBM Zurich tested a lanthanum-barium-copper-oxide ceramic (`LBCO`) and saw its resistance vanish at **`35 Kelvin`**!
- Within twelve months, in **January–February 1987**, **Maw-Kuen Wu** and **Paul Chu** substituted yttrium (`YBCO`, `YBa₂Cu₃O₇`) and pushed the critical temperature to **`93 Kelvin` (`-180 °C`)** — soaring past the **`77 Kelvin` (`-196 °C`) boiling point of liquid nitrogen**, a coolant cheaper than milk or beer!
- Today, superconducting niobium-titanium coils bathed in liquid helium generate the **1.5-to-3-Tesla magnetic fields inside 40,000 hospital MRI scanners** (imaging your body without X-rays!), guide proton beams at CERN's Large Hadron Collider, and levitate Japan's **Chuo Shinkansen SCMaglev train at `603 km/h` (`375 mph`)**!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Superconductivity happens when waves of vibration (`phonons`) inside a crystal lock electrons into step.
  - What happens when waves travel through the **air all around you** — and why does the pitch of an ambulance siren or a racing train drop in a sudden **`"NEEEE-owww"`** the instant it passes you?
  - How did Austrian physicist **Christian Doppler** predict that wave shift in Prague on **25 May 1842**, why did a skeptical Dutch scientist named **Christophorus Buys Ballot** hire a **steam locomotive and a flatbed car full of brass trumpet players** on **3 June 1845** to prove Doppler wrong — and how did **Ernst Mach** photograph a bullet breaking the **sound barrier** in **1887** (`038/039` candidate *Sound Waves, the Doppler Effect & the Sound Barrier*)?
