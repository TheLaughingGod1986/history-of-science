During the Age of Sail, scurvy killed an estimated two million sailors — yet the ship's dogs, cats, and rats eating the exact same scraps never got sick! Why?

Because almost all mammals manufacture their own **vitamin C** out of blood sugar inside their livers! Only humans, apes, guinea pigs, and fruit bats carry a broken gene from an ancient ancestor that leaves us unable to make a single drop of our own.

Inside your body, vitamin C is the essential spark plug for the enzymes that lock together **collagen** — the triple-stranded protein cable that makes up thirty percent of all your protein and holds your blood vessels, skin, gums, and bone scars together. Without just ten milligrams of vitamin C a day — the weight of a single grain of rice — your collagen unravels, capillaries burst, gums bleed, and old healed fractures dissolve!

On the twentieth of May seventeen forty-seven, eight weeks into patrol in the English Channel aboard the warship HMS *Salisbury*, thirty-year-old surgeon James Lind faced eighty sailors crippled by scurvy. Instead of giving one remedy to everyone, Lind invented the **controlled clinical trial**: he chose twelve sailors at the exact same stage of disease, laid them in the same room on the exact same diet, and divided them into six pairs.

Pair one drank a quart of cider a day; pair two took twenty-five drops of dilute sulphuric acid; pair three drank vinegar; pair four swallowed half a pint of cold seawater; pair five ate a paste of garlic, mustard, and horseradish; and pair six received two oranges and one lemon every day!

In just six days, before the ship's fruit ran out, one orange-eating sailor returned to duty at the helm and the other became nurse to the remaining ten!

Lind published his proof in seventeen fifty-three. Case closed? Not yet. Why did the British Navy wait forty-two more years to issue lemon juice — and how did boiling the juice and swapping lemons for cheap limes bring scurvy roaring back to doom explorers at the South Pole?

If James Lind proved oranges and lemons cured scurvy in six days in seventeen forty-seven, why did thousands of sailors keep dying for another half-century?

Because fresh fruit rotted on long voyages, Lind himself made a fatal mistake: he recommended boiling lemon juice for hours into a thick syrup called **rob**. He had no idea that heat and oxygen destroy vitamin C — so whenever captains tested Lind's boiled syrup at sea, it failed!

Only in seventeen ninety-five, after physician Gilbert Blane persuaded the Admiralty to issue unboiled Mediterranean lemon juice preserved with a splash of rum, did scurvy vanish from the British fleet: admissions at Haslar Naval Hospital plunged from one thousand four hundred and fifty-seven in seventeen eighty down to just two cases in eighteen-oh-six, allowing Nelson's fleet to blockade Napoleon for months on end!

Yet in the eighteen sixties, the Admiralty lost the cure all over again! To save money, officials replaced Spanish and Sicilian lemons — which hold fifty-three milligrams of vitamin C per hundred grams — with cheaper colonial West Indian **limes**, which held barely twenty-nine milligrams, and pumped the juice through copper pipes that destroyed most of what remained! That swap gave British sailors the nickname **"Limeys"** — and caused scurvy to return on polar expeditions, helping doom Captain Scott's nineteen twelve team in Antarctica!

Worse still, by the eighteen eighties, Louis Pasteur's Germ Theory was so triumphant that doctors assumed every mysterious illness was caused by a microbe or toxin — refusing to believe a disease could be caused by an absence in the diet!

So while doctors hunted in vain for a germ, how did a stingy military cook and a flock of paralysed chickens in Indonesia in eighteen ninety finally reveal vitamins?

In eighteen eighty-six, Dutch military doctor Christiaan Eijkman was sent to Java in Indonesia to find the germ behind **beriberi** — a terrifying nerve paralysis sweeping across Asia after steam-powered mills began polishing the brown skin off rice.

Eijkman injected laboratory chickens with blood from beriberi patients. In July eighteen ninety, his chickens suddenly developed paralysed legs and drooping necks! Eijkman thought he had found the bacterium — except every single uninjected control chicken across the yard got paralysed too! Then, at the end of November eighteen ninety, all the surviving chickens suddenly stood up and ran around completely cured!

Stunned, Eijkman investigated the kitchen. From the seventeenth of June to the twenty-seventh of November eighteen ninety, a lab servant had fed the chickens leftover cooked **white polished rice** from the military hospital officers' mess — until a strict new head cook took over and snapped that he refused to *"give military rice to civilian chickens,"* switching the birds back to cheap **unpolished brown rice**!

When Eijkman and Adolphe Vorderman surveyed two hundred and eighty thousand prisoners across a hundred and one Javanese prisons, the numbers were breathtaking: prisons serving polished white rice suffered beriberi at three hundred times the rate of prisons serving unpolished brown rice! The paper-thin silver bran skin of brown rice held a life-saving molecule — **Vitamin B1** — that steam milling stripped away!

After Eijkman's colleague Gerrit Grijns in nineteen-oh-one and Cambridge biochemist Frederick Gowland Hopkins proved that bodies need trace *"accessory food factors"* to live, Polish biochemist Casimir Funk in nineteen twelve isolated the anti-beriberi compound and coined a word that changed medicine forever: **"vitamine"** — from *"vital amine"*!

Yet nobody had ever held pure Vitamin C in their hand. How did a Hungarian scientist who hated the red paprika peppers on his dinner plate finally isolate it by the pound — and why do giant mega-dose vitamin pills mostly wash straight out of your body?

In nineteen thirty-two, Hungarian biochemist Albert Szent-Györgyi — who would win the nineteen thirty-seven Nobel Prize — and American chemist Charles Glen King proved that a tiny six-carbon acid was the long-sought anti-scurvy factor, naming it **ascorbic acid**: literally *"anti-scurvy acid,"* or **Vitamin C**!

And one evening in October nineteen thirty-two in Szeged, Hungary, Szent-Györgyi's wife served him fresh red paprika peppers for supper. Not wanting to eat them, he slipped them into his coat pocket and tested them in his lab — discovering that Hungarian paprika holds two hundred milligrams of Vitamin C per hundred grams, four times more than lemons, allowing him to crystallise over a pound of pure Vitamin C in a single week!

That brings us to a popular misconception: if ten milligrams of Vitamin C prevents scurvy, does swallowing two-thousand-milligram mega-dose pills make you two hundred times healthier? Not at all! Because Vitamin C dissolves in water rather than fat, once your blood and tissues saturate around two hundred milligrams a day, your kidneys simply flush the extra pills straight out into your urine!

Vitamins proved that removing a single trace molecule can bring the warm chemistry of the human body to a halt. What happens when you remove almost every last trace of heat itself from matter — cooling a metal wire down to minus two hundred and sixty-nine degrees Celsius, just four degrees above Absolute Zero? On the tenth of July nineteen-oh-eight and the eighth of April nineteen eleven in Leiden, Dutch physicist Heike Kamerlingh Onnes liquefied helium for the first time in history and watched the electrical resistance of frozen mercury vanish to absolute zero — unlocking **superconductivity** and magnets that float in mid-air!

Twelve sailors on a pitching warship, a flock of paralysed chickens in Java, and a plate of red peppers in Hungary. Together, they revealed the invisible molecules that keep us alive.
