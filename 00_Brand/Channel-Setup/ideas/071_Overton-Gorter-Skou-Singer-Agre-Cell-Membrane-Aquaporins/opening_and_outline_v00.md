### PART 02: 1895 & 1 April 1925 — Ernest Overton's Oil Barrier & Gorter and Grendel's `2:1` Soap-Trough Miracle (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why for two hundred and thirty years after Robert Hooke `015` saw thick wooden cork walls in 1665, biologists argued over whether soft animal cells even *had* an outer skin — because a 5-nanometre cell membrane is forty times thinner than the wavelength of visible light!):** **University of Zurich, 1895 & University of Leiden, Netherlands, 1 April 1925**.
- **1895–1899 (*Vierteljahrsschrift Naturforsch. Ges. Zürich*) — Charles Ernest Overton's olive-oil discovery:**
  - In **1895** in Zurich, British-born physiologist **Charles Ernest Overton** (1865–1933 — a distant cousin of Charles Darwin!) tested over 500 chemical dyes and compounds on plant root hairs and tadpoles.
  - Water-soluble salts and sugars bounced off the invisible cell boundary, whereas **fat-soluble (`lipophilic`) molecules** slipped straight inside (`and the more soluble a chemical was in olive oil, the faster it entered the cell — which Overton showed in 1899 also explained how ether and chloroform anaesthetics 011 dissolve into nerve membranes!`). Overton concluded that every living cell is wrapped in a microscopic skin of **fat (`lipids`)**, particularly **`lecithin` (`phospholipids`)** and **`cholesterol`**.
- **1 April 1925 (*Journal of Experimental Medicine*) — Evert Gorter & François Grendel's `2:1` Langmuir trough experiment (and their two lucky errors!):**
  - How many molecules thick was Overton's fat skin? Inspired by **Irving Langmuir's 1917** water trough (which spreads oil molecules on water with their water-loving **`hydrophilic` heads** in the water and their water-fearing **`hydrophobic` tails** sticking up in the air in a layer **1 molecule thick**), Dutch paediatrician **Evert Gorter** (1881–1954) and chemist **François Grendel** (1897–1969) in Leiden picked **mammalian red blood cells** (`because mammalian red blood cells have no nucleus and no internal organelle membranes — so 100% of their fat comes from the outer cell membrane!`).
  - On **1 April 1925** (*J. Exp. Med.*), Gorter and Grendel extracted the lipids from counted red blood cells of humans, dogs, sheep, rabbits, guinea pigs, and goats using acetone, spread the lipids into a 1-molecule-thick film on water using a silk thread, and compared the sheet's area to the surface area of the cells: the ratio was **exactly `2.0 to 1`**!
  - That meant the cell membrane is a **`bimolecular lipid layer` (`a phospholipid bilayer`)** two molecules thick, with the oily tails pointing inward and the water-loving heads facing the water inside and outside! *(Quirky historical twist proved by Bar, Deamer & Cornwell in 1966: Gorter and Grendel's acetone extraction missed ~1/3 of the lipids, AND their dried microscope slides underestimated the red blood cells' surface area by ~1/3 — two mistakes in opposite directions that miraculously cancelled out to give the exact right `2:1` answer!)*
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1895 · WHEN: 1917 · WHEN: 1 April 1925 · WHEN: 1966 · FACT: Two mistakes cancelled out · DATA: inked Overton (1895) olive-oil/water partition permeability curve & Gorter & Grendel (1 April 1925 J. Exp. Med. 41: 439–443) 2:1 erythrocyte lipid-monolayer ratio chart (with Bar, Deamer & Cornwell 1966 Science 153: 1010–1012 double-error correction) on period paper · SCALE: Gorter and Grendel getting the right two-to-one bilayer answer from an incomplete fat extraction and a shrunken cell measurement is like miscounting your change by ten pence when the shopkeeper also undercharged you by ten pence — both errors vanished and left the exact truth`
- **Ends on:** A pure oil bilayer (`Gorter & Grendel 1925`) is a brilliant waterproof wall — **too** good! If your cells are wrapped in a layer of oil that blocks charged ions (`sodium Na⁺ and potassium K⁺`), how do your nerves fire electrical sparks (`033`), and why did a Danish scientist in **February 1957** boil **twenty-five thousand shore crabs**?

### PART 03: February 1957 & 18 February 1972 — 25,000 Boiled Crabs (`The Sodium-Potassium Pump`) & the `Fluid Mosaic Model` — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — the cell membrane is a solid, motionless sandwich with proteins smeared flat on the outside like butter on toast! Wrong! In 1957 and 1972, scientists proved that the membrane is a two-dimensional liquid sea — and that giant protein machines float right through it, burning a third of your body's energy to pump ions against the tide!"):** **Aarhus University, Denmark, February 1957 & University of California, San Diego, 18 February 1972**.
- **February 1957 (*Biochimica et Biophysica Acta*) — Jens Christian Skou boils 25,000 crabs to find the `Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase` pump:**
  - For decades (`Hugh Davson & James Danielli 1935`), textbooks drew cell proteins plastered flat on the outside of the lipid bilayer like a rigid sandwich.
  - Meanwhile, physiologists knew a baffling fact: inside every living cell, **potassium (`K⁺`)** is kept **20 to 40 times higher** than outside, while **sodium (`Na⁺`)** is kept **10 times lower** (`Richard Keynes 1951–1955`). Something in the membrane had to be actively pumping sodium out and potassium in!
  - At Aarhus University in Denmark, **Jens Christian Skou** (1918–2018) ground up the leg nerves of **25,000 green shore crabs (*Carcinus maenas*)** (`making the entire university physiology building smell like a fish market!`). In **February 1957** (*Biochim. Biophys. Acta*, winning Skou the **1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry**), Skou discovered a membrane-spanning enzyme — **`Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase` (`the sodium-potassium pump`)** — that burns one molecule of **`ATP` (`062`, `063`)** to pump **3 sodium ions OUT** and **2 potassium ions IN**, keeping every nerve and muscle cell in your body electrically charged!
- **March 1970 & 18 February 1972 (*Science*) — Larry Frye & Michael Edidin's glowing hybrid cell & Singer and Nicolson's `Fluid Mosaic Model`:**
  - Were membrane proteins really locked in place? In **March 1970** (*J. Cell Sci.*), **Larry Frye** and **Michael Edidin** at Johns Hopkins tagged a mouse cell's membrane proteins with **green fluorescent dye** and a human cell's proteins with **red fluorescent dye**, then fused the two cells together into one ball.
  - At first, one half of the hybrid cell glowed green and the other half red — **but within 40 minutes at body temperature (`37 °C`), the red and green proteins were completely mixed like marbles rolling on a liquid pond**!
  - On **18 February 1972** (*Science*), **S. Jonathan Singer** (1924–2017) and **Garth L. Nicolson** (born 1943) at UC San Diego synthesized these breakthroughs into the **`Fluid Mosaic Model`**: the cell membrane is a **two-dimensional liquid phospholipid sea** where protein pumps, receptors, and channels float like **icebergs**, reaching right through the bilayer!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Michael Edidin (born 1939) — American cell biologist · WHEN: 1935 · WHEN: February 1957 · WHEN: March 1970 · WHEN: 18 February 1972 · WHEN: 1997 · FACT: Proteins float in liquid fat · DATA: inked Skou (February 1957 Biochim. Biophys. Acta 23: 394–401) crab-nerve Na+/K+-ATPase activation curve (requiring both Na+ and K+ ions to hydrolyse ATP) & Frye & Edidin (March 1970 J. Cell Sci. 7: 319–335) / Singer & Nicolson (18 February 1972 Science 175: 720–731) Fluid Mosaic lateral diffusion chart (100% intermixing of mouse-human membrane antigens in 40 minutes at 37 °C) on period paper · SCALE: the Fluid Mosaic Model proved that your cell membrane isn't a brick wall — it is a crowded Venice canal two molecules deep, where protein gondolas and pumping stations drift and spin through a liquid oil sea`
- **Ends on:** Biologists now had the lipid sea (`Gorter & Grendel`) and the ion pumps (`Skou, Singer & Nicolson`). Yet one giant mystery remained: if the middle of the cell membrane is **water-repelling oil**, how does **water (`H₂O`)** gush into red blood cells, salivary glands, and kidney tubules **a hundred times faster** than simple diffusion through oil allows? Why did **six frog eggs in Baltimore in October 1991** suddenly **explode like popcorn**?

### PART 04: 9 October 1991 & 17 April 1992 — Peter Agre's Exploding Frog Eggs & the `Aquaporin` Water Channel (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open (for seventy years biophysicists argued over whether cells had dedicated water pipes — until an American blood doctor hunting for Rhesus antigens injected a mystery protein into six frog eggs, dropped them into distilled water, and watched them blow up in three minutes!):** **Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, Baltimore, 9 October 1991 – 17 April 1992**.
- **1988–9 October 1991 (*Science*, 17 April 1992) — Peter Agre & Gregory Preston's `CHIP28` (`Aquaporin-1`) frog-egg experiment:**
  - While purifying Rhesus (`Rh`) blood-group proteins (`028`) from human red blood cells in **1988**,Johns Hopkins haematologist **Peter Agre** (born 1949) and **Bradley Smith** kept finding a mysterious, abundant `28-kilodalton` protein (`which they called CHIP28`, for *channel-forming integral protein*).
  - Every red blood cell carried **200,000 copies** of `CHIP28`, and kidney tubules were packed with even more! When Agre discussed the mystery protein with his former professor **John Parker** at UNC Chapel Hill, Parker suggested: *"Could it be the long-sought water channel?"*
  - On **9 October 1991** (published **17 April 1992** in *Science*, winning Agre the **2003 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** shared with **Roderick MacKinnon**), Agre's postdoc **Gregory M. Preston** tested the idea using **frog eggs (*Xenopus laevis* oocytes)**, which normally have very low water permeability. Preston injected `CHIP28` RNA into six frog eggs, left six control eggs uninjected, and transferred both groups into **distilled water** (`hypotonic medium`).
  - The six control eggs sat there calmly. The six **`CHIP28` (`Aquaporin-1`)** eggs **swelled up like water balloons and burst open within three minutes**!
- **2000–2001 (*Nature*) — How `Aquaporin` lets 3 billion water molecules a second race through while blocking every single proton (`H⁺`):**
  - Why doesn't a hole big enough for water (`H₂O`) also let protons (`H⁺`, acid) leak through and short-circuit the cell's electrical battery (`063`)?
  - When **Yoshinori Fujiyoshi**, **Peter Agre**, **bing Jap**, and **Robert Stroud** solved the `3D` atomic structure of `Aquaporin-1` in **October 2000–2001** (*Nature*), they saw a breathtaking hourglass tunnel just **`2.8 angstroms` wide** — wide enough for **one single water molecule at a time**!
  - Halfway down the hourglass sit two conserved **`NPA` (`asparagine-proline-alanine`)** loops whose positive electric dipole grabs each passing water molecule by its oxygen atom and **flips it upside down mid-flight** — breaking the bucket-brigade proton wire (`Grotthuss mechanism`) so **3 billion water molecules per second** stream through in single file while **zero protons (`H⁺`)** can cross!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Gregory M. Preston (born 1958) — American molecular biologist · WHO: Roderick MacKinnon (born 1956) — American biophysicist · WHEN: 1988 · WHEN: 9 October 1991 · WHEN: 17 April 1992 · WHEN: October 2000 · WHEN: 2003 · FACT: 3 billion waters per second · DATA: inked Preston, Carroll, Guggino & Agre (17 April 1992 Science 256: 385–387) Xenopus oocyte osmotic swelling & explosion curve (CHIP28/Aquaporin-1 vs water-injected control in hypotonic 70 mosM buffer over 3 minutes, reversibly blocked by HgCl2) alongside Murata, Fujiyoshi & Agre (October 2000 Nature 407: 599–605) 2.8-angstrom NPA hourglass dipole water-flip diagram on period paper · SCALE: an aquaporin channel is like a stadium turnstile exactly one water molecule wide — three billion water molecules sprint through in single file every second, but at the midpoint two electric magnets spin each water molecule halfway round so acid protons trying to hop along the line are stopped cold`
- **Ends on:** Where in your body do **aquaporins** and **sodium-potassium pumps** perform their biggest everyday miracle? Right inside your two fist-sized **kidneys** — which filter **180 litres of water out of your blood every single day** and use aquaporins to rescue **99% of it**! How did a microscopic quartz needle in **1924** and **twenty metres of sausage casing in Nazi-occupied Holland in 1943** unlock the kidney and build the first artificial dialysis machine?
