### PART 02: Starving in a Sea of Sugar & the 2:00 a.m. Notebook (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (starving in the midst of plenty):** **Strasbourg, 1889 & London, Ontario, 31 October 1920**. For over three thousand years — from the **1550 BC Egyptian Ebers Papyrus** to English physician **Thomas Willis** in **1674**, who noted that diabetic urine tasted *"wonderfully sweet as if imbued with honey"* (*diabetes mellitus*, meaning "honey-sweet siphon") — Type 1 diabetes was a swift death sentence.
- Why? Because even if a patient ate huge meals, their body couldn't move **glucose sugar** out of the bloodstream and into their muscle and fat cells! Their blood turned thick with sugar that spilled out in the urine, while their cells literally starved to death — forcing doctors like **Frederick Madison Allen** (`1914–1919`) to put children on brutal **400-calorie-a-day "starvation diets"** of thrice-boiled cabbage just to buy them a few extra months of life!
- **1869 & 1889 — The pancreas clue:** In **1869**, 21-year-old German medical student **Paul Langerhans** (1847–1888) spotted tiny, mysterious clusters of cells scattered like islands (~1% to 2% of the organ) inside the pancreas — later named the **Islets of Langerhans**. Twenty years later, in **1889**, **Oskar Minkowski** (1858–1931) and **Joseph von Mering** removed the pancreas from a dog and noticed flies swarming around the dog's sugar-filled urine!
- Yet for thirty years, whenever scientists ground up pancreas glands to extract the mystery islet hormone, the extract failed! Why?
- **31 October 1920, 2:00 a.m. — Banting's 25-word epiphany:** In **London, Ontario**, 29-year-old wounded World War I surgeon **Frederick Banting** (1891–1941) had such a struggling practice that in his first month he earned just **$4** (for one prescription!). Preparing a lecture for medical students on the night of **30 October 1920**, he read an article by Moses Barron about a gallstone blocking the pancreatic duct:
  - When the main duct of the pancreas is tied off (`ligated`), the **98% of the pancreas that makes harsh digestive enzymes (`trypsin`)** shrivels up and dies within weeks — **leaving the 1% to 2% Islets of Langerhans intact**!
  - At **2:00 a.m. on 31 October 1920**, Banting woke up and scribbled 25 famous words (misspelling both *diabetes* and *glycosuria*!): *"Diabetus. Ligate pancreatic ducts of dog. Keep dogs alive till acini degenerate leaving Islets. Try to isolate the internal secretion of these to relieve glycosurea."*
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Oskar Minkowski (1858–1931) — German-Russian physician and physiologist · WHEN: 1869 · WHEN: 1889 · WHEN: 31 October 1920 · FACT: Earned $4 his first month · DATA: inked comparison diagram on period paper showing a whole ground pancreas (98% acinar trypsin enzymes digesting and destroying the 1%–2% islet insulin) vs Banting's 31 October 1920 duct-ligation method (acinar enzyme cells degenerate over 6–8 weeks, leaving pure insulin-producing Islets of Langerhans intact) · SCALE: ninety-eight percent of the pancreas is a factory of meat-dissolving digestive enzymes — so grinding up a whole pancreas was like throwing a delicate hormone key straight into a vat of acid`
- **Ends on:** Banting had the idea on paper. But he had no laboratory, no money, and no experience in diabetes research! How did a skeptical Scottish professor in Toronto, a **coin toss between two students**, and a stray dog named **Marjorie** turn those 25 misspelled words into a living cure in the blistering summer of **1921**?

### PART 03: The Coin Flip, the Rooftop Lab & Dog #408 (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open:** **University of Toronto, 17 May – November 1921**. In **November 1920**, Banting took his 2:00 a.m. note to **John J. R. Macleod** (1876–1935), the world-famous professor of physiology at the University of Toronto.
- Macleod knew dozens of better-trained scientists had failed, but agreed to lend Banting a tiny, sweltering **rooftop operating room**, ten dogs, and one student assistant for the summer while Macleod sailed home to Scotland on holiday!
- **The coin toss (May 1921):** Two 22-year-old physiology students — **Charles Best** (1899–1978, whose beloved aunt Anna had just died of diabetes) and **Clark Noble** — **flipped a coin** to see who would work with Banting for the first half of the summer! Best won the toss; when Banting and Best clicked, Noble generously let Best stay on for the whole summer — meaning **a single coin flip put Charles Best into the history books**!
- **30 July 1921 — The breakthrough on Dog #408 and Marjorie:** Beginning on **17 May 1921**, Banting and Best worked in stifling summer heat (frying eggs and sausages over a Bunsen burner because Banting was so broke he sold his surgical instruments and slept in the lab!).
  - On **30 July 1921**, they mashed up the shrivelled, duct-tied pancreas of one dog in cold salt water (`Ringer's solution`) and injected `4 mL` into the vein of a comatose diabetic terrier (**Dog #408**): within **one hour, the dog's blood sugar dropped by 40% (`from 200 mg/dL to 120 mg/dL`)**, and the dying dog lifted its head and stood up!
  - In **August 1921**, they kept a severely diabetic dog named **Marjorie (Dog #33)** alive and playful for **70 days** on daily injections of their extract (which they called *"Isletin"*, renamed **"insulin"** by Macleod from the Latin *insula*, "island")!
  - Even better, in **November 1921**, Banting remembered that unborn calves in the womb already have working Islets of Langerhans **before their digestive enzymes develop** — allowing Banting and Best to make litres of insulin from slaughterhouse calf pancreases using **acidified alcohol**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 17 May 1921 · WHEN: 30 July 1921 · WHEN: November 1921 · FACT: Best won a coin toss · DATA: inked blood-glucose curve on period paper from Banting & Best's 30 July 1921 Toronto lab notebook (Dog #408) showing venous blood glucose falling from 0.20% (200 mg/dL) to 0.12% (120 mg/dL) in 1 hour after 4 mL of degenerated pancreas extract, and rising again only when injections stopped · SCALE: four millilitres of chilled saltwater extract — less than a single teaspoon — dropped a dying dog's blood sugar by forty percent in sixty minutes`
- **Ends on:** They had saved diabetic dogs all autumn. Case closed? **Wrong.** When doctors injected Banting and Best's brownish extract into 14-year-old **Leonard Thompson** on **11 January 1922**, **it failed**! Why did the first human shot nearly end in disaster — and how did a visiting biochemist unlock the **90% alcohol secret** twelve days later?

### PART 04: The Failed First Shot, the Alcohol Secret & the $1 Patent — Midpoint Twist (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Toronto General Hospital, 11–23 January 1922**. On **11 January 1922**, 14-year-old **Leonard Thompson** — weighing just **65 pounds (`29.5 kg`)** and smelling of acetone near death — received `15 mL` of Banting and Best's calf-pancreas extract.
- **The failure:** The boy's blood sugar dipped by only **25%**, the ketones in his urine didn't budge, and the impure, muddy brown liquid caused a painful **sterile abscess** on his hip! Doctors halted the trial at once.
- **James Collip's 90% alcohol breakthrough (12–23 January 1922):** Fortunately, Macleod had invited a brilliant 29-year-old visiting biochemist from Alberta, **James Collip** (1892–1965), to join the team in December 1921.
  - Working late at night in January 1922, Collip discovered the chemical secret to purifying insulin (**fractional alcohol precipitation**):
  - At **80% alcohol**, almost all the toxic beef protein contaminants precipitate out while insulin stays dissolved in the clear liquid; then, when you raise the alcohol concentration past **90% to 95%**, **pure insulin suddenly precipitates out at the bottom of the flask like white snow**!
  - (When Collip initially hesitated to tell Banting his exact purification recipe, the hot-tempered Banting pinned Collip to the floor by his coat lapels until Best pulled him off!)
- **23 January 1922 — Leonard Thompson's awakening & the Toronto ward miracle:**
  - On **23 January 1922**, Leonard Thompson was injected with Collip's crystal-clear purified insulin: within **24 hours**, his blood sugar plunged from **`520 mg/dL` to `120 mg/dL`**, his urine sugar dropped from `71 grams` to almost zero, and the dying boy brightened, gained weight, and lived another 13 years!
  - Weeks later came one of the most moving moments in the history of medicine: Banting, Best, and Collip walked down a Toronto ward where **up to fifty diabetic children lay in comas**, their grieving parents waiting for death. They moved from bed to bed injecting the purified insulin — and **before they had injected the last dying child at the end of the room, the first children were waking up from their comas**!
- **The 1923 Nobel feud & the $1 patent:**
  - On **23 January 1923**, Banting, Best, and Collip assigned the **US patent for insulin (`US Patent 1,469,994`) to the University of Toronto for just $1.00 each (`$3 total`)**, with Banting declaring: ***"Insulin does not belong to me, it belongs to the world."***
  - And when the **October 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** was awarded to **Banting and Macleod** — omitting Best and Collip — Banting immediately announced he was **splitting his Nobel medal honours and half the prize money 50/50 with Charles Best**, prompting Macleod to split his half 50/50 with **James Collip**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 11 January 1922 · WHEN: 23 January 1922 · WHEN: 23 January 1923 · WHEN: October 1923 · FACT: Sold patent for $1 each · DATA: inked purification & clinical chart on period paper comparing Banting & Best's 11 January 1922 crude extract (blood glucose 440 → 320 mg/dL, abscess, ketones unchanged) vs James Collip's 90%–95% alcohol-purified insulin on 23 January 1922 (Leonard Thompson's blood glucose 520 → 120 mg/dL in 24h; 24-hour urine glucose 71.1 g → 8.7 g) · SCALE: raising alcohol from 80% to 95% turned a muddy extract that caused abscesses into a crystal-clear cure that dropped a dying teenager's blood sugar from 520 to 120 in a single day`
- **Ends on:** So if eating sugar raises blood sugar in diabetes, does eating too much candy actually *cause* Type 1 diabetes — and how do Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes differ inside your cells?

### PART 05: Why Sugar Doesn't Cause Type 1 Diabetes & the Two Oranges That Cured Scurvy (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** Many people still confuse **Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes** or think eating sweets causes Type 1 diabetes in children. What is the real difference?
- **Missing key vs rusted lock (`TED-Ed What does the pancreas do?`):**
  - In **Type 1 diabetes** (what Leonard Thompson had), the body's own **immune system mistakenly attacks and destroys the beta cells in the Islets of Langerhans** — so the body makes **zero insulin keys** (`nothing to do with eating sweets!`);
  - In **Type 2 diabetes** (~90% of adult cases), the pancreas still makes insulin keys, but the body's cell doors have become **insulin-resistant ("rusted locks")**!
  - And thanks to **Frederick Sanger** (who sequenced insulin's **51 amino acids in 1951–1955**, winning his first Nobel Prize) and **Arthur Riggs, Keiichi Itakura, and Herbert Boyer** (who inserted the human insulin gene into harmless ***E. coli* bacteria in 1978**), today's patients inject **exact human insulin** brewed by microbes rather than extracted from pig or cow pancreases!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Insulin proved that a fatal disease can be caused not by an invading germ (`001`), but by **a single missing molecule** your body needs to survive.
  - How did a thirty-year-old Scottish Royal Navy surgeon named **James Lind** step into the dark, pitching sickbay of **HMS *Salisbury* on 20 May 1747**, divide twelve dying sailors into six pairs for the **world's first controlled clinical trial**, and cure **scurvy** in six days with **two oranges and a lemon** — and how did Dutch doctor **Christiaan Eijkman** watching paralysed chickens eat brown rice in Java in **1890–1896** discover **vitamins** (`036` candidate *Scurvy & the Discovery of Vitamins: James Lind & Christiaan Eijkman*)?
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1955 · WHEN: 1978 · WHEN: 20 May 1747 · WHEN: 1890 · DATA: animated timeline building 1869 & 1889 (Paul Langerhans spots pancreatic islets; Minkowski & von Mering link pancreas removal to diabetes) · 31 Oct 1920 (Frederick Banting's 2:00 a.m. 25-word note in London, Ontario) · 30 Jul 1921 (Banting & Charles Best's islet extract revives Dog #408 in Toronto) · 23 Jan 1922 (James Collip's alcohol-purified insulin saves 14-year-old Leonard Thompson) · 23 Jan & Oct 1923 (Banting, Best & Collip sell the US patent for $1 each; Nobel Prize shared four ways) alongside Type 1 (missing insulin key) vs Type 2 (resistant receptor lock) · SCALE: fifty-one amino acids linked into one tiny protein molecule — sold for a single dollar in 1923 so hundreds of millions of people could live · FACT: 51 amino acids save millions`
