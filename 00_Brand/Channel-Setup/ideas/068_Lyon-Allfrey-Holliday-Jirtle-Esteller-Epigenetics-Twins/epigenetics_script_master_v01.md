In **nineteen forty-two**, British embryologist **Conrad Hal Waddington** coined the word **`epigenetics`** — meaning *"above genetics"* — to ask how one fertilised egg can roll down different valleys to become a beating heart cell or a firing brain cell. Seven years later, on the **thirtieth of April nineteen forty-nine** in *Nature*, Canadian anatomist **Murray Barr** peered into the nerve cells of **female cats** and spotted a tiny, dark, tightly crumpled ball of chromatin (**`the Barr body`**) that male cat cells never had (`shown by Susumu Ohno in nineteen fifty-nine to be a single condensed X chromosome`)!

At Harwell in Oxfordshire, British geneticist **Mary Lyon** was breeding female mice whose fur grew in mottled light-and-dark patches. On the **twenty-second of April nineteen sixty-one** in *Nature* — in a landmark paper barely one page long — Lyon solved both mysteries at once!

Female mammals inherit **two X chromosomes (`XX`)**, while males inherit only **one (`XY`)**. Why don't females overdose on double the X-chromosome proteins? Because early in every female embryo, **each individual cell flips a molecular coin and randomly shuts down one of its two X chromosomes**, crumpling it into a silent **Barr body**! Once a cell picks which X chromosome to silence — mother's or father's — **every daughter cell it divides into for the rest of your life remembers and keeps that exact same X chromosome locked shut**! In cats, the orange-or-black fur gene sits on the X chromosome — so a female calico cat with one orange X and one black X grows into a living patchwork quilt of epigenetic switches!

Mary Lyon proved that a cell can switch genes off **without deleting a single letter of DNA** — and pass that memory down through **dozens of cell divisions**! But *how* does a cell physically lock or unlock a single gene? What did scientists in **New York in May nineteen sixty-four** and **London and California in January nineteen seventy-five** find clinging to the DNA strand itself?

Case closed — DNA sits naked in the cell nucleus, and every gene is always open to be read! Wrong! Your **two metres of DNA** are wound so tightly around millions of protein spools that most of your genes are physically locked shut — until chemical keys unspool them!

To pack two metres of DNA inside a microscopic nucleus (`shown by Roger Kornberg in nineteen seventy-four to wind around eight-part protein spools called histones`), cells wrap their DNA tightly like thread around cotton reels. In **May nineteen sixty-four** in *PNAS*, **Vincent Allfrey**, **R. Faulkner**, and **Alfred Mirsky** at the Rockefeller Institute discovered that cells clip tiny chemical **acetyl tags (`histone acetylation`)** onto the tails of those histone spools (`with C. David Allis isolating the first histone acetyltransferase enzyme in nineteen ninety-six`)! Because DNA is negatively charged and histone tails are positively charged, they grip each other like magnets (`keeping the gene OFF`) — until an **acetyl tag** neutralises the charge and **loosens the spool so the gene switches ON**!

And how does a cell lock a gene OFF **permanently** and copy that lock when the cell divides? On the **twenty-fourth of January nineteen seventy-five** in *Science*, British biologists **Robin Holliday** and **John Pugh** — alongside **Arthur Riggs** in California — discovered the copy mechanism of **`DNA methylation`**! Whenever a **`C` (`cytosine`)** letter sits next to a **`G` (`guanine`)** letter, an enzyme can snap a four-atom **methyl tag (`-CH₃`)** onto the `C` letter, silencing the gene. Because `C-G` on one strand always pairs with `G-C` on the opposite strand, when DNA unzips to divide, each new copy keeps **half of the methyl tags** — and a maintenance enzyme immediately stamps a **matching methyl lock onto the fresh DNA strand**!

Wait — biologists still assumed those epigenetic locks were sealed inside a genetic fortress, untouched by the outside world! What happened at **Duke University in August two thousand and three** when scientists fed pregnant **yellow obese mice** a simple bowl of **vitamins** — and in **Madrid in July two thousand and five** when they tested **eighty pairs of human identical twins**?

In **August two thousand and three** at Duke University, **Robert Waterland** and **Randy Jirtle** studied mice carrying the **`Agouti viable yellow` gene**. When a rogue genetic switch next to the `Agouti` gene is **unmethylated (`switched ON`)**, the mice grow up **bright canary-yellow, ravenously hungry, obese, and prone to diabetes and cancer**.

Waterland and Jirtle fed pregnant `Agouti` mothers a simple supplement of **methyl-donating nutrients — folic acid, vitamin B-twelve, choline, and betaine**. Without changing **a single letter of DNA**, those extra methyl tags clamped onto the rogue `Agouti` gene and **silenced it** — and the mothers gave birth to **sleek, brown, lean, healthy pups**! For the first time, you could see with the naked eye that **nutrition can flip epigenetic switches**!

Does life experience rewrite human epigenomes too? On the **twenty-sixth of July two thousand and five** in *PNAS*, **Mario Fraga** and **Manel Esteller** in Madrid studied **eighty pairs of identical twins aged three to seventy-four**! In **three-year-old twins**, their DNA methylation and histone acetylation marks were **almost one hundred percent identical**. But in **fifty-year-old twins** — especially twin pairs who had lived apart with different diets, habits, and medical histories — their chromosomes lit up with **four times as many differently expressed genes**! Their DNA `hardware` was still identical, but a lifetime of living had rewritten their epigenetic `software`!

If our epigenetic switches get rewritten as we age, **why aren't our children born with seventy-year-old epigenetic scars** — and how can a **misfolded protein (`a prion`)** spread a fatal brain disease **without a single strand of DNA, RNA, or epigenetic tags at all**?

People often hear about epigenetics and fear that every bad habit we have is permanently stamped onto our grandchildren's DNA. In reality, when a human egg and sperm unite, special enzymes **wipe almost the entire epigenetic slate clean (`epigenetic reprogramming`)** — which is why two thirty-five-year-old parents give birth to a **zero-year-old baby**, and why **Shinya Yamanaka's reprogramming genes** can erase the epigenetic marks of an old skin cell and rewind it into a young stem cell!

Across every story we have told — from DNA and viruses to antibodies, cancer genes, and epigenetics — one iron rule has held true: every biological instruction and every infectious germ relies on **nucleic acids, `DNA` or `RNA`**. So what happened when scientists encountered a terrifying brain disease that **survived boiling, hospital formalin, and DNA-shredding ultraviolet radiation** — because the infectious culprit contained **zero DNA and zero RNA**? How did **Carleton Gajdusek and Michael Alpers in February nineteen sixty-six** and radiobiologist **Tikvah Alper in London in May nineteen sixty-seven** uncover the radiation-proof mystery of **`kuru`** and sheep **`scrapie`**? And why did virologists laugh at American neurologist **Stanley Prusiner on the ninth of April nineteen eighty-two** when he announced **`prions`** — a normal brain protein folded into the wrong three-dimensional shape that snaps healthy proteins into its own shape like a **runaway chain of falling dominoes (`Mad Cow Disease`)**, until **Charles Weissmann's July nineteen ninety-three knockout mice** proved Prusiner right?

A patchwork calico cat in Oxfordshire, a bowl of mouse vitamins in North Carolina, and eighty pairs of twins in Madrid. Together, they proved that your DNA writes the hardware of life, but chemical switches write the story.
