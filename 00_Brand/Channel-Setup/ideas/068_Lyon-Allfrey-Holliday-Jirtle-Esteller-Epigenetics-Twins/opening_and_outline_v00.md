### PART 02: 1942, 1949 & 22 April 1961 — Mary Lyon's Calico Cat Puzzle & `X-Chromosome Inactivation` (`Lyonization`) (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (why female mammals carry two giant X chromosomes (`XX`, each packed with over 800 genes) while males carry only one (`XY`) — yet females don't overdose on double the X-chromosome proteins, and why almost every calico cat is female):** **Medical Research Council Radiobiological Research Unit, Harwell, Oxfordshire, 22 April 1961**.
- **1942 & 1949 — Conrad Waddington coins `Epigenetics` & Murray Barr spots the `Barr Body`:**
  - In **1942**, British embryologist **Conrad Hal Waddington** (1905–1975) coined the word **`epigenetics`** ("above genetics") to describe how one fertilised egg rolls down an "epigenetic landscape" of valleys to become totally different cell types (`heart, brain, or skin`).
  - Seven years later, in **1949** (*Nature*), Canadian anatomist **Murray Barr** (1908–1995) and graduate student **Ewart Bertram** peered into the nerve cells of **female cats** and spotted a tiny, dark, tightly crumpled ball of DNA sitting against the edge of the nucleus (**`the Barr body`**) that male cat cells never had (`shown by Susumu Ohno in 1959 to be a condensed X chromosome`)!
- **22 April 1961 (*Nature*) — Mary Lyon's 1-page masterpiece (`Lyonization`):**
  - At Harwell in Oxfordshire, British geneticist **Mary Frances Lyon** (1925–2014) studied female mice with mottled, patchy fur caused by X-linked coat-colour mutations.
  - On **22 April 1961** (*Nature*), in a landmark paper just over one page long, Lyon connected Barr's crumpled chromosome to mottled mice and **tortoiseshell/calico cats**: early in every female mammal embryo (`when the embryo is just a tiny ball of ~100–1,000 cells`), **each individual cell randomly switches off one of its two X chromosomes**, crumpling it into a silent **Barr body** (`wrapped in a non-coding RNA blanket called XIST, discovered in 1991`)!
  - Once a cell picks which X chromosome to silence — mother's or father's — **every daughter cell it divides into for the rest of your life remembers and keeps that exact same X chromosome switched off**! Because the orange/black fur gene sits on the X chromosome, a male cat (`XY`) can be solid orange or solid black, whereas a female cat (`XX`) with one orange X and one black X grows into a living patchwork quilt of orange and black clones!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Conrad Hal Waddington (1905–1975) — British embryologist and geneticist · WHO: Murray L. Barr (1908–1995) — Canadian physician and neuroanatomist · WHEN: 1942 · WHEN: 1949 · WHEN: 1959 · WHEN: 22 April 1961 · FACT: One X chromosome crumpled silent · DATA: inked Waddington (1942 epigenetic landscape), Barr & Bertram (30 April 1949 Nature 163: 676–677 female nucleolar satellite / Barr body), and Mary Lyon (22 April 1961 Nature 190: 372–373) X-inactivation inheritance chart on period paper showing random embryonic X silencing followed by 100% clonal mitotic memory across all descendant somatic cell divisions · SCALE: silencing an entire X chromosome inside a female cell is like vacuum-packing one of two identical instruction manuals into a locked vault — and every time that cell divides, the photocopy machine faithfully locks the exact same manual away in the new cell`
- **Ends on:** Mary Lyon proved that a cell can switch genes off **without deleting a single letter of DNA** — and pass that memory down through **dozens of cell divisions**! But *how* does a cell physically lock or unlock a single gene? What did scientists in **New York in May 1964** and **London and California in January 1975** find clinging to the DNA strand itself?

### PART 03: May 1964 & 24 January 1975 — Histone Cotton Reels & `DNA Methylation` Sticky Notes — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — DNA sits naked in the cell nucleus, and every gene is always open to be read! Wrong! Your two metres of DNA are wound so tightly around millions of protein spools that most of your genes are physically locked shut — until chemical keys unspool them!"):** **Rockefeller Institute, New York, May 1964 & National Institute for Medical Research, London / City of Hope, California, January 1975**.
- **May 1964 (*PNAS*) & 1974 — Vincent Allfrey's `Histone Acetylation` & Roger Kornberg's `Nucleosome` spools:**
  - If you stretched the DNA from just **one** of your cells end to end, it would measure **two metres long** — yet it packs inside a nucleus only **six micrometres across**! How? In **1974** (*Science*), **Roger Kornberg** and **Ada & Donald Olins** showed that DNA is wound like thread around millions of 8-part protein spools called **`histones` (`forming nucleosomes`, "beads on a string")**.
  - Ten years earlier — in **May 1964** (*PNAS*) — American cell biologists **Vincent Allfrey**, **R. Faulkner**, and **Alfred Mirsky** at the Rockefeller Institute discovered that cells clip tiny chemical **acetyl tags (`histone acetylation`)** onto the tails of those histone spools (`with C. David Allis isolating the first histone acetyltransferase enzyme in 1996`)!
  - Because DNA is negatively charged and histone tails are positively charged, they cling together tightly like magnets (`keeping genes OFF`). Clipping an **acetyl tag** neutralises the positive charge — **loosening the spool so the gene can be read (`turning the gene ON`)**!
- **24 January 1975 (*Science* & *Cytogenet. Cell Genet.*) — Robin Holliday, John Pugh & Arthur Riggs discover how `DNA Methylation` remembers across cell divisions:**
  - Histone spools loosen and tighten by the minute. How does a cell lock a gene OFF **permanently** — and copy that lock when the cell divides?
  - Independently in **January 1975**, British biologists **Robin Holliday** and **John Pugh** (*Science*, **24 January 1975**) and American geneticist **Arthur Riggs** (*Cytogenetics and Cell Genetics*, **1975**) solved the puzzle: **`DNA methylation`**!
  - Whenever a **`C` (`cytosine`)** letter sits next to a **`G` (`guanine`)** letter (`a CpG site`), an enzyme can snap a tiny four-atom **methyl tag (`-CH₃`)** directly onto the `C` letter, silencing the gene!
  - And here is the genius of the copy machine: because `C-G` on one DNA strand always pairs with `G-C` on the opposite strand, when a cell divides into two, each daughter strand keeps **half of the methyl tags (`hemi-methylated DNA`)** — and a **maintenance methyltransferase enzyme (`DNMT1`)** immediately spots every half-tagged site and **stamps a matching methyl tag onto the new DNA strand**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: C. David Allis (1951–2023) — American molecular biologist · WHEN: May 1964 · WHEN: 1974 · WHEN: 24 January 1975 · WHEN: 1996 · FACT: Chemical locks copied at division · DATA: inked Allfrey, Faulkner & Mirsky (May 1964, PNAS 51: 786–794) histone acetylation transcription chart & Holliday & Pugh (24 January 1975, Science 187: 226–232) / Riggs (1975) semi-conservative CpG DNA methylation inheritance diagram on period paper showing DNMT1 copying 5-methylcytosine (5mC) tags from the parental CpG strand onto the newly replicated daughter strand · SCALE: copying methyl tags during cell division works like tracing carbon paper — when the two DNA strands unzip to replicate, the old strand still wears its methyl sticky notes, and a maintenance enzyme immediately stamps matching sticky notes onto the fresh copy`
- **Ends on:** Biologists now understood how cells copy their chemical locks from division to division inside the body. But everyone still assumed those epigenetic switches were sealed inside a fortress — untouched by the outside world. What happened at **Duke University in August 2003** when scientists fed pregnant **yellow obese mice** a simple bowl of **vitamins** — and in **Madrid in July 2005** when they tested **eighty pairs of human identical twins**?

### PART 04: August 2003 & 26 July 2005 — The Yellow-to-Brown Agouti Mouse & Why Identical Twins Drift Apart (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (how two mice with 100% identical DNA can look like completely different species — one bright yellow and obese, the other sleek and brown — simply because of the vitamins their mother ate while pregnant):** **Duke University Medical Center, Durham, North Carolina, August 2003 & Spanish National Cancer Centre (CNIO), Madrid, 26 July 2005**.
- **August 2003 (*Molecular and Cellular Biology*) — Robert Waterland & Randy Jirtle's Agouti Mouse vitamin experiment:**
  - Mice carrying the **`Agouti viable yellow` (`A^vy`)** gene have a rogue jumping-gene sequence (`an IAP retrotransposon`) sitting just upstream of their coat-colour and appetite control gene. When that rogue sequence is **unmethylated (`switched ON`)**, the mice grow up **bright canary-yellow, ravenously hungry, obese, and prone to diabetes and cancer**.
  - In **August 2003** (*Molecular and Cellular Biology*), **Robert Waterland** and **Randy Jirtle** at Duke University took pregnant female `Agouti` mice and fed half of them normal mouse chow, while giving the other half a supplement of **methyl-donating nutrients (`folic acid, vitamin B12, choline, and betaine`)**!
  - Without changing **a single letter of DNA**, the extra methyl groups clamped onto the rogue `Agouti` gene and **silenced it** — and the vitamin-fed mothers gave birth to **sleek, brown, lean, healthy pups**! For the first time, scientists had proof you could see with the naked eye that **environment and nutrition can flip epigenetic switches**!
- **26 July 2005 (*PNAS*) — Mario Fraga & Manel Esteller solve the Identical-Twin riddle:**
  - Does life experience rewrite human epigenomes too? On **26 July 2005** (*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*), **Mario Fraga** and **Manel Esteller** in Madrid examined white blood cells, muscle, and cheek cells from **80 pairs of identical (`monozygotic`) twins aged 3 to 74**!
  - In **3-year-old twins**, their DNA methylation and histone acetylation patterns were **almost 100% identical**.
  - But in **50-year-old twins** — especially twin pairs who had lived apart with different diets, smoking habits, and lifestyles — their chromosomes lit up like red-and-green traffic lights with **four times as many differentially expressed genes**! Their DNA letters (`hardware`) were still 100% identical, but a lifetime of living had rewritten their epigenetic `software`!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: August 2003 · WHEN: 26 July 2005 · FACT: Diet silenced the yellow mouse gene · DATA: inked Waterland & Jirtle (August 2003, Mol. Cell. Biol. 23: 5293–5300) Agouti viable yellow (A^vy) CpG methylation & coat-phenotype chart and Fraga & Esteller (26 July 2005, PNAS 102: 10604–10609) 80-pair monozygotic twin epigenetic divergence plot on period paper · SCALE: the yellow obese Agouti mouse and the lean brown mouse are genetically as identical as two prints from the same photographic negative — the only difference is a handful of microscopic methyl tags that switched the runaway gene off`
- **Ends on:** If epigenetic tags are rewritten by diet, age, and disease, why aren't your children born with **seventy-year-old epigenetic scars** — and how can a **misfolded protein (`a prion`)** transmit a fatal brain disease **without a single strand of DNA, RNA, or epigenetic tags at all**?

In fact, as a human sperm and egg form and unite, enzymes **wipe almost the entire epigenetic slate clean (`epigenetic reprogramming`)** — which is why a thirty-five-year-old parent gives birth to a **zero-year-old baby**, and why **Shinya Yamanaka's four reprogramming genes (`058`)** can erase the epigenetic marks of an old skin cell and rewind it back into a young stem cell!
