### PART 02: 1820 & Easter 1856 — Andean Fever Bark, Quinine & How William Perkin's Failed Malaria Cure Dyed the World Purple (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (from "bad air" to Peruvian bark and a London attic):** **Paris, 1820 & Cable Street, East London, Easter 1856**. For centuries, Romans and Europeans died in thousands around marshlands every summer from a shivering, burning fever they called ***mal'aria*** (`Italian for "bad air"`, blaming toxic vapours rising from swamps).
- **1820 — Pelletier & Caventou isolate quinine (`and give it away free`):** In the **1600s**, Indigenous healers in the Andes and Jesuit missionaries brought Europe the bitter bark of the **cinchona tree** (`"Jesuits' bark"`). In **1820** in Paris, pharmacists **Pierre Joseph Pelletier** (1788–1842) and **Joseph Bienaimé Caventou** (1795–1877) extracted the active molecule from the bark — **quinine** (`which glows bright blue under ultraviolet light, 018!`) — and **refused to patent their process**, publishing it openly so anyone could make malaria medicine!
- **Easter 1856 — 18-year-old William Perkin tries to make quinine from coal tar (`and invents Mauveine`):** By the **1850s**, the British Empire was spending fortunes importing cinchona bark. At the Royal College of Chemistry in London, professor **August Wilhelm von Hofmann** (1818–1892) wondered whether quinine (`formula C20H24N2O2`) could be built synthetically out of **allyl toluidine** (`C10H13N`) from waste **coal tar**.
  - During his **Easter vacation in March 1856**, 18-year-old student **William Henry Perkin** (1838–1907) climbed up to his makeshift attic bedroom lab in **Cable Street, East London**, and oxidised coal-tar **aniline** with potassium dichromate.
  - Because he didn't know molecules have 3D architecture (`quinine wouldn't be fully synthesized until Woodward and Doering in 1944!`), his experiment failed completely, leaving a **black, sticky sludge** at the bottom of the flask.
  - Yet when Perkin washed the dirty flask out with alcohol, the liquid flared **brilliant, glowing purple** — and dyed a strip of silk cloth a rich violet that neither soap nor sunlight could fade: **Mauveine**, the world's **first synthetic dye** (`sparking the synthetic chemical industry in Germany and Britain that produced Paul Ehrlich's magic-bullet stains and Felix Hoffmann's Bayer aspirin in 041!`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: August Wilhelm von Hofmann (1818–1892) — German chemistry professor · WHEN: 1820 · WHEN: March 1856 · WHEN: 1944 · FACT: Refused to patent quinine · DATA: inked chemical-formula vs 3D-structure comparison diagram on period paper contrasting William Perkin's 1856 additive formula calculation (2 C10H13N + 3 O → C20H24N2O2 + H2O, which produced purple Mauveine C26H23N4+/C27H25N4+ instead of quinine) against Pelletier and Caventou's 1820 natural quinine alkaloid extracted at ~2–8% yield from Andean cinchona bark · SCALE: in eighteen fifty-six, purple dye had to be crushed out of ten thousand Mediterranean sea snails just to make a single gram — until eighteen-year-old William Perkin brewed vibrant purple out of black coal-tar waste in his bedroom`
- **Ends on:** Perkin's happy accident launched synthetic chemistry, and natural quinine kept patients alive — yet in **1880**, nobody still knew what actually caused malaria, or how it got into the human body! What did a French army doctor in Algeria and a British surgeon in India see when they looked through a microscope?

### PART 03: 6 November 1880 & 20 August 1897 — Laveran's Wriggling Parasite & Ronald Ross's "Mosquito Day" — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong — malaria isn't bad air or a bacterium; it's a shapeshifting animal parasite riding inside a female mosquito!"):** **Constantine, Algeria, 6 November 1880 & Secunderabad, India, 20 August 1897**. After **Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch (`001`, `043`)** proved bacteria cause anthrax and cholera, doctors hunted swamps for a "malaria bacterium."
- **6 November 1880 — Charles Laveran sees a parasite wriggling inside a red blood cell:** On **6 November 1880** at the military hospital in **Constantine, Algeria**, French army doctor **Charles Louis Alphonse Laveran** (1845–1922) looked at fresh, unstained blood from a feverish soldier under a `400×` microscope — and saw crescent-shaped bodies and **whip-like filaments thrashing violently** inside the red blood cells! Malaria wasn't bad air or a bacterium — it was a single-celled **protozoan parasite (`Plasmodium`)** eating red blood cells from the inside!
- **20 August 1897 ("Mosquito Day") — Ronald Ross's 1,000th mosquito:** How did *Plasmodium* get from one person's blood into another's? Scottish tropical doctor **Patrick Manson** (1844–1922) suspected **mosquitoes**.
  - In **Secunderabad, India**, surgeon **Ronald Ross** (1857–1932) spent **two and a half gruelling years (`1895–1897`)** dissecting nearly a thousand common grey (`Culex`) mosquitoes fed on malaria patients — and found **nothing**!
  - On **16 August 1897**, Ross fed a rare batch of ten brown, **dappled-winged mosquitoes (`Anopheles`)** on a malaria patient named **Husein Khan** (`paying Khan one anna per mosquito bite!`).
  - By the scorching afternoon of **20 August 1897**, only two mosquitoes remained alive. Exhausted and ready to quit for the day at **1:00 p.m.**, Ross sacrificed the seventh mosquito (`38th in the series`), sliced open its stomach wall, and adjusted his cracked microscope micrometre screw — and saw a **cluster of circular cysts packed with jet-black malaria pigment granules** growing right inside the mosquito's stomach wall (`confirmed by Ross in bird malaria in 1898 and by Giovanni Battista Grassi in human Anopheles mosquitoes in Rome in November 1898!`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Patrick Manson (1844–1922) — Scottish tropical physician · WHO: Husein Khan (fl. 1897) — Secunderabad hospital patient · WHO: Giovanni Battista Grassi (1854–1925) — Italian zoologist · WHEN: 6 November 1880 · WHEN: 16 August 1897 · WHEN: 20 August 1897 · WHEN: November 1898 · FACT: Dissected 1,000 mosquitoes · DATA: inked Plasmodium lifecycle & liver/blood amplification chart on period paper from Laveran (1880) and Ross (18 December 1897 British Medical Journal 2: 1786–1788) showing 10–100 mosquito-injected sporozoites multiplying ~10,000–30,000× inside a single liver cell before invading red blood cells and bursting 10¹¹–10¹² cells every 48 hours · SCALE: a single malaria parasite injected by one mosquito bite multiplies up to thirty thousand times inside one human liver cell before flooding the bloodstream with hundreds of billions of parasites`
- **Ends on:** Knowing mosquitoes carried the parasite let doctors drain swamps, hang bed nets, and invent synthetic **chloroquine** in **1934**. ### PART 04: 340 AD & 4 October 1971 — Tu Youyou's Sample #191, Ge Hong's Cold-Water Clue & Artemisinin (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open (when every modern synthetic pill stopped working):** **Beijing, China, January 1969 – 4 October 1971**. During the Vietnam War, chloroquine-resistant *Plasmodium falciparum* malaria was killing more soldiers than bullets, and American military labs screened over **`250,000` synthetic compounds** without finding a fast replacement.
- **1969–1971 — Project 523 & 2,000 ancient recipes:** On **23 May 1967**, China launched secret **Project 523**, and in **January 1969** appointed 38-year-old pharmaceutical chemist **Tu Youyou** (born 1930) to head the herbal research group at the Academy of Traditional Chinese Medicine in Beijing.
  - Tu Youyou and her team combed through **`2,000` traditional Chinese medical recipes**, narrowed them to `640` compiled in a notebook, and tested **`380` herbal extracts** on malaria-infected mice.
  - Extract after extract failed — except **sweet wormwood (`Qinghao`, *Artemisia annua*)**, which once showed a promising `68%` drop in parasites, then dropped back to **`12%` and `40%`** in later batches!
- **The 340 AD sentence that changed medicine (`4 October 1971`):**
  - Why were the wormwood extracts failing? Tu Youyou went back to the original **340 AD** scroll by Jin Dynasty physician **Ge Hong** (*Zhouhou Beiji Fang*, *A Handbook of Prescriptions for Emergencies*) and read the exact wording: ***"A handful of Qinghao immersed in two litres of water, wring out the juice and drink it all."***
  - In traditional medicine, almost every herb was **boiled in hot water** — yet Ge Hong specified **cold water**, wrung out by hand!
  - Tu Youyou realised that **heat was destroying the active molecule** (`a fragile endoperoxide bridge inside the crystal`)! Switching from boiling water (`100 °C`) and hot ethanol (`78 °C`) to **low-boiling diethyl ether (`35 °C`)** and removing an inactive acidic fraction, Tu Youyou on **4 October 1971** produced **Sample #191** — which cured **100% (`10 out of 10`) of malaria-infected mice and 100% of monkeys**!
  - In **July 1972**, when hospital trials were delayed by safety worries, Tu Youyou and two colleagues **swallowed the extract themselves** to prove it was safe for humans, then isolated pure crystalline **artemisinin (`Qinghaosu`, $C_{15}H_{22}O_5$)** in **November 1972** — saving **millions of lives worldwide (`2015 Nobel Prize`)**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 23 May 1967 · WHEN: January 1969 · WHEN: 4 October 1971 · WHEN: July 1972 · WHEN: November 1972 · WHEN: 2015 · FACT: Swallowed it herself first · DATA: inked screening-funnel & artemisinin endoperoxide mechanism diagram on period paper from Tu Youyou (2011 Nature Medicine 17: 1217–1220) showing 2,000 traditional recipes narrowed to 380 mouse-tested extracts and Sample #191 (4 October 1971, 35 °C ether extraction = 100% clearance), where the iron-rich heme inside malaria-infected red blood cells cleaves artemisinin's O–O endoperoxide bridge into parasite-killing free radicals · SCALE: out of two thousand ancient recipes and three hundred and eighty lab extracts, it took batch number one hundred and ninety-one — brewed in cool ether at thirty-five degrees — to hit one hundred per cent cure`
- **Ends on:** Why does artemisinin kill the malaria parasite without harming healthy human cells? Because the parasite gorging on red blood cells is packed with **iron (`heme`)**, which snaps artemisinin's delicate oxygen-oxygen bridge like a spring-loaded mousetrap! And how are scientists today combining Tu Youyou's artemisinin with the world's first **malaria vaccines (`RTS,S` and `R21/Matrix-M`)**?
