### PART 02: The Musket Blast and 238 Silk-String Experiments (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (millstone vs chemical vat):** **Mackinac Island, Michigan, 6 June 1822 – December 1833**. Before the nineteenth century, physicians argued fiercely over how the stomach worked: was it a **muscular millstone** that ground food by mechanical squeezing, a **warm stewpot** that cooked food by body heat, or a **fermenting vat** where food rotted?
- On **6 June 1822**, inside the American Fur Company store on Mackinac Island, a duck gun loaded with buckshot accidentally fired from less than three feet away into 20-year-old French-Canadian fur trapper **Alexis St. Martin** (~1802–1880), blowing open a hole the size of a man's palm in his left ribs and stomach.
- Post surgeon **William Beaumont** (1785–1853) expected him to die within thirty minutes. Instead, over the next year, St. Martin miraculously survived — but the edges of his torn stomach fused to the skin of his chest, leaving a permanent **one-inch valve-like keyhole (`gastric fistula`)** that had to be plugged with a lint compress to keep his dinner from pouring out!
- **May 1825 – 1833 — The silk-string experiments:** Hiring St. Martin as a woodchopper and helper (and chasing him down whenever the homesick voyageur bolted back to Canada!), Beaumont conducted **238 painstaking experiments** between **May 1825 and 1833** (published in **December 1833**, *Experiments and Observations on the Gastric Juice, and the Physiology of Digestion*):
  - Beaumont tied small morsels of **boiled beef, raw salt pork, stale bread, and cabbage** to a **silk string**, pushed them through the keyhole into St. Martin's stomach, and pulled the string out after **1, 2, 3, and 4 hours** to watch digestion in real time!
  - Even more decisively, using a rubber tube to syphon clear **gastric juice** (which British chemist **William Prout** had proved on **23 December 1823** contained free **hydrochloric acid, $HCl$**, along with the enzyme **pepsin** discovered by **Theodor Schwann** in **1836**) into a glass vial kept at body temperature (`37.8 °C` / `100 °F`) on a water bath, Beaumont proved that **stomach juice digested beef inside a glass bottle on a table** just as thoroughly as inside the body! Digestion was not mechanical grinding or mystical "vital force" — it was **pure chemistry**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: William Prout (1785–1850) — English chemist and physician · WHEN: 23 December 1823 · WHEN: May 1825 · WHEN: August 1825 · WHEN: December 1833 · FACT: 238 silk-string tests · DATA: inked digestion-time chart on period paper from Beaumont's 1833 book comparing boiled tripe & whipped eggs (~1 hour), boiled beef (~2 hours 45 minutes), and roast pork (~5 hours 15 minutes) alongside in-vitro hydrochloric acid + pepsin digestion at 37.8 °C · SCALE: by pulling a silk thread in and out of a living stomach 238 times, Beaumont timed digestion to the minute — from one hour for eggs to over five hours for roast pork`
- **Ends on:** Beaumont's 1833 book made him the father of gastric physiology. Yet how did his proof that the stomach is filled with burning hydrochloric acid create a **150-year medical blind spot** that condemned millions of ulcer patients to misery and surgery?

### PART 03: The "No Acid, No Ulcer" Dogma and the Forgotten Easter Petri Dish (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open:** **Royal Perth Hospital, Western Australia, 11 June 1979 – 14 April 1982**. For a century and a half after Beaumont and Prout proved the stomach is a bath of hydrochloric acid (`pH 1.5 to 2.0`), every medical textbook taught one unbreakable rule (famously stated by Karl Schwarz in **1910**: ***"No acid, no ulcer"***):
  - Because stomach acid is harsh enough to kill swallowed bacteria within minutes, doctors insisted **the human stomach is completely sterile**! Any bacteria seen on a stomach slide were dismissed as dead contaminants from the mouth.
  - Therefore, when patients suffered agonising, bleeding **stomach and duodenal ulcers** (which hit **1 in 10 people** over a lifetime), doctors blamed **job stress, neurotic personality, smoking, and spicy food** — prescribing bland milk diets, tranquillisers, lifelong acid-blocking pills, or even **cutting out large sections of the patient's stomach**!
- **11 June 1979 – 1981 — Robin Warren's silver-stained biopsies:** On his 42nd birthday (**11 June 1979**) at Royal Perth Hospital, Australian pathologist **J. Robin Warren** (1937–2024) examined a silver-stained stomach biopsy under his microscope and saw a dense layer of **curved, spiral bacteria** burrowed beneath the mucus layer of an inflamed stomach!
- Colleagues laughed when Warren said the bacteria were causing the inflammation. In **July 1981**, energetic 29-year-old internal-medicine registrar **Barry Marshall** (born 1951) joined Warren to study **100 ulcer and gastritis patients** and try to grow the mystery microbe in a Petri dish.
- **The 5-day Easter-weekend miracle (8–13 April 1982):** For eight months, **thirty-four consecutive patient cultures failed completely**! Why? Because hospital microbiology labs threw out routine throat-and-swab plates after **48 hours** if nothing grew.
- Then, on **Maundy Thursday, 8 April 1982**, biopsy #35 went into the warm incubator just before the **five-day Australian Easter holiday weekend** (`Good Friday, Saturday, Easter Sunday, Easter Monday`). Nobody checked the incubator until **Tuesday, 13 April 1982** — and after **five full days**, gleaming **1-millimetre transparent colonies** of the slow-growing bacterium ***Helicobacter pylori*** were covering the agar plate!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 11 June 1979 · WHEN: July 1981 · WHEN: 13 April 1982 · FACT: Left over 5-day Easter · DATA: inked biopsy & incubation chart on period paper from Warren & Marshall's 100-patient Perth study (1982–1984) showing 0 colonies at 48 hours vs 1 mm H. pylori colonies at 5 days (120 hours), present in 100% of duodenal ulcer patients (13/13) and 82% of gastric ulcer patients (18/22) · SCALE: throwing Petri dishes away after two days blinded doctors for decades — three extra days over the Easter weekend revealed the germ in nearly every duodenal ulcer patient`
- **Ends on:** Warren and Marshall had grown the culprit in a dish and found it in nearly every duodenal ulcer patient (`Lancet`, June 1983 & June 1984). Case closed? **Wrong.** Why did gastroenterologists still mock them and reject their paper — and what drove Barry Marshall to walk into his lab in **July 1984** and **drink a beaker of bacteria himself**?

### PART 04: The Doctor Who Drank a Billion Bacteria — Midpoint Twist (4:50–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Fremantle Hospital, Western Australia, June–July 1984**. Even after Warren and Marshall published their letters in *The Lancet* in **June 1983** and **16 June 1984**, the medical establishment refused to believe them!
- Why? Because doctors argued **"correlation is not causation"**: maybe stress caused the ulcer first, and the bacteria simply moved into the damaged tissue afterwards! And when Marshall tried to infect piglets in the lab to satisfy **Robert Koch's postulates (`001`)**, the human bacteria wouldn't infect pigs!
- **July 1984 — The ultimate human guinea pig:** Forbidden from infecting human volunteers and watching ulcer patients have their stomachs surgically removed while doctors ignored simple antibiotics, 32-year-old **Barry Marshall** decided there was only one experimental subject left: **himself**!
- First, in **June 1984**, Marshall swallowed an endoscopy tube so his colleague could biopsy his own stomach and prove his stomach lining was **100% normal and bacteria-free**.
- Then, one morning in **July 1984**, Marshall took a Petri dish of *H. pylori* cultured from a 66-year-old gastritis patient, scraped the bacteria into a glass beaker of beef broth (**about $10^9$ — one billion — live spiral bacteria**), and **gulped the whole beaker down**!
- **What happened next (Days 3 to 14):**
  - Marshall assumed it might take years to develop symptoms. Instead, after just **three days**, he developed foul-smelling "putrid" breath (because his stomach was no longer making acid!);
  - By **Day 5 to 8**, he was waking at Dawn **vomiting clear, acid-free mucus**, exhausted and aching with severe acute **gastritis** (the direct precursor to peptic ulcers)!
  - On **Day 10**, he swallowed the endoscope a second time: his stomach lining was **ragingly inflamed**, and the biopsy swarmed with billions of ***Helicobacter pylori*** bacteria!
  - On **Day 14**, before his terrified wife Adrienne found out what he had done, Marshall took **tinidazole (an antibiotic) and bismuth** — wiping out the infection in days and proving beyond a shadow of a doubt that ***H. pylori* causes gastritis and peptic ulcers, and that a simple course of antibiotics and bismuth cures them permanently**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 16 June 1984 · WHEN: July 1984 · WHEN: 2005 · FACT: Drank a billion bacteria · DATA: inked timeline and mechanism diagram on period paper of Barry Marshall's July 1984 self-experiment (Day 0: baseline clean biopsy + drinks 10⁹ H. pylori; Day 5–8: achlorhydric vomiting & gastritis; Day 10: biopsy proves heavy H. pylori colonisation; Day 14: cured by antibiotic + bismuth) alongside H. pylori's urease enzyme splitting urea into alkaline ammonia (NH₃) to neutralise pH 1.5 acid · SCALE: one billion spiral bacteria in a single glass of broth — using an enzyme shield to turn lethal stomach acid into a cosy neutral home in just five days`
- **Ends on:** Marshall and Warren won the **2005 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** for turning a chronic surgical disease into a two-week antibiotic cure. So if stomach acid is strong enough to dissolve a zinc nail, why doesn't your own stomach digest *itself* every afternoon?

### PART 05: Why Your Stomach Doesn't Digest Itself & the Sparks Inside Your Brain (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** Many people still believe that spicy food or a stressful week at work directly eats a hole in your stomach, or wonder why `pH 1.5` hydrochloric acid doesn't digest your own stomach wall during lunch. How does your stomach survive?
- **The bicarbonate-mucus shield (`TED-Ed Digestive system` & `Stomach ulcers`):** Your stomach lines itself with a constantly renewed coat of **thick mucus spiked with alkaline sodium bicarbonate (baking soda)**, keeping the stomach wall at a safe neutral **`pH 7.0`** just a millimetre away from the **`pH 1.5`** acid bath! Stress and spicy food don't *cause* ulcers on their own — instead, either ***H. pylori* bacteria** (using their corkscrew tails and ammonia cloud to burrow through the mucus coat) or heavy use of **painkillers like aspirin and ibuprofen (NSAIDs)** thins that protective mucus shield, allowing your own stomach acid to burn the wall beneath!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - And when William Beaumont peered through Alexis St. Martin's stomach window in **1825**, he noticed one more astonishing thing: whenever St. Martin became **furious or frightened**, his stomach lining blushed red and stopped secreting acid — proving that **nerves from the brain talk directly to the gut**!
  - How did we discover what the **brain and nervous system** are actually made of — and how did Spanish artist-turned-anatomist **Santiago Ramón y Cajal** working at his **kitchen table in Barcelona in 1887–1888** and pharmacologist **Otto Loewi** waking from an **Easter Sunday dream at 3:00 a.m. in March 1921** prove that your **86 billion brain cells** never touch each other, firing messages across microscopic gaps with tiny puffs of chemicals (`033` candidate *Sparks & Synapses: Santiago Ramón y Cajal & Otto Loewi*)?
