Before the eighteen twenties, doctors could not agree on what the stomach actually did. Was it a muscular millstone that ground meat to paste, a warm stewpot that cooked food by body heat, or a vat where meals simply rotted? Whenever you feel your stomach rumble after a meal, what is really happening inside? Nobody could look inside a living human stomach without killing the patient.

Then, on the sixth of June eighteen twenty-two, inside the American Fur Company store on Mackinac Island in Michigan, a shotgun loaded with duck shot accidentally fired from less than three feet away into twenty-year-old French-Canadian trapper Alexis St. Martin. Suddenly, the blast blew open a hole the size of a man's palm through his left ribs, diaphragm, and stomach wall.

Army surgeon William Beaumont arrived within minutes and expected the young man to die before sunset. However, St. Martin miraculously survived — but as the wound healed over the next year, the torn edges of his stomach fused directly to the skin of his chest. Worse still, that left a permanent one-inch keyhole that had to be plugged with a lint bandage just to keep his dinner from pouring out!

Hiring St. Martin as a woodchopper around his house — and tracking him down whenever the homesick trapper paddled a canoe back to Canada — Beaumont began a historic series of two hundred and thirty-eight experiments in May and August eighteen twenty-five.

What if you tied small pieces of boiled beef, raw salt pork, stale bread, and cabbage to a silk string, pushed them through the keyhole into St. Martin's stomach, and pulled the string out every hour? Whipped eggs vanished in an hour; boiled beef took two hours and forty-five minutes; fatty roast pork took over five hours!

Even better, after English chemist William Prout proved on the twenty-third of December eighteen twenty-three that stomach fluid contains free hydrochloric acid, Beaumont syphoned clear gastric juice into a glass bottle warmed to one hundred degrees Fahrenheit on his table. A chunk of beef inside the glass bottle dissolved at the exact same speed as inside St. Martin's body! Published in December eighteen thirty-three, Beaumont's book proved digestion is pure acid chemistry.

Yet how did Beaumont's triumph create a hundred-and-fifty-year blind spot that condemned millions of ulcer patients to major surgery?

Once Beaumont and Prout proved the human stomach is a churning bath of hydrochloric acid at pH one point five, every medical textbook taught one unbreakable rule: nothing alive can survive inside the human stomach!

So when one in ten adults developed agonising, bleeding stomach or duodenal ulcers, doctors swore the culprit was stress, worry, or spicy food. For decades, patients were told to drink bland milk, take tranquillisers, swallow lifelong acid-blockers, or have surgeons cut out large parts of their stomachs — only for the ulcers to come roaring back!

Then, on his forty-second birthday — the eleventh of June nineteen seventy-nine — pathologist Robin Warren at Royal Perth Hospital in Australia looked at a silver-stained stomach biopsy under his microscope. Huddled right beneath the protective mucus layer of the inflamed stomach wall was a thick carpet of curved, corkscrew-shaped bacteria!

Other doctors laughed and insisted the microbes were just dead mouth bacteria swallowed with lunch. Only one young doctor listened: in July nineteen eighty-one, twenty-nine-year-old internal-medicine trainee Barry Marshall teamed up with Warren to biopsy a hundred ulcer patients and grow the mystery germ in a Petri dish.

For eight months, thirty-four patient plates in a row failed completely! Why? Because hospital labs routinely threw out throat and swab dishes after forty-eight hours if nothing grew.

Then, on Thursday the eighth of April nineteen eighty-two, biopsy plate number thirty-five went into the incubator right before the five-day Australian Easter weekend. Nobody checked the incubator until Tuesday the thirteenth of April — and after five full days, shining one-millimetre colonies of a brand-new bacterium, *Helicobacter pylori*, covered the dish! Across their hundred-patient study, Warren and Marshall found the bacteria in eighty-two percent of stomach ulcers and one hundred percent of duodenal ulcers!

They had grown the culprit in a dish. Case closed? Wrong. Why did stomach specialists still laugh them off the stage — and what drove Barry Marshall to walk into his lab in July nineteen eighty-four and drink a glass of bacteria himself?

Even after Warren and Marshall published their findings in *The Lancet* in nineteen eighty-three and on the sixteenth of June nineteen eighty-four, leading gastroenterologists refused to believe that bacteria caused ulcers.

Critics argued that stress caused the ulcer first, and the bacteria merely moved into the damaged tissue afterwards! When Marshall tried to infect lab piglets to prove causation, the human microbe refused to take hold. Meanwhile, billion-dollar acid-blocker sales boomed and ulcer patients kept losing their stomachs on the operating table.

Forbidden from infecting human volunteers, thirty-two-year-old Barry Marshall decided there was only one guinea pig left: himself!

In June nineteen eighty-four at Fremantle Hospital, Marshall swallowed an endoscope tube so a colleague could biopsy his stomach and prove his lining was completely healthy and germ-free. Then, one morning in July nineteen eighty-four, Marshall scraped a Petri dish of *H. pylori* taken from an aching patient into a small beaker of beef broth — about one billion live spiral bacteria — and drank it down in one gulp!

How does *H. pylori* survive pH one point five acid before it reaches the stomach wall? By pumping out a powerful enzyme called **urease**, which splits natural urea in the stomach into a cloud of alkaline ammonia — wrapping the bacterium in its own personal acid-neutralising shield!

Marshall expected an ulcer to take years. Instead, within five days he was waking at dawn vomiting clear, acid-free mucus, exhausted and foul-breathed with raging stomach inflammation! On Day Ten, he swallowed the camera tube again: his stomach was fiery red, and the biopsy swarmed with *H. pylori*! On Day Fourteen — right after telling his horrified wife Adrienne what he had done — Marshall took a simple course of antibiotics and bismuth, wiping out the bacteria completely! In two thousand and five, Warren and Marshall stood in Stockholm to receive the Nobel Prize for turning a lifetime surgical illness into a two-week cure.

So if your stomach acid is strong enough to dissolve a metal nail, why doesn't your stomach digest *itself* every afternoon?

Many people still think a spicy curry or a stressful week at work burns an ulcer straight into your stomach — or wonder why pH one point five acid doesn't digest your own stomach wall along with your steak. How does your stomach survive?

Your stomach coats itself in a one-millimetre layer of thick mucus loaded with alkaline sodium bicarbonate — essentially baking soda! That thin blanket keeps the living cells of your stomach wall at a gentle, neutral pH seven, just one millimetre away from a pool of pH one point five acid! Spicy food doesn't eat through that shield; instead, either *H. pylori* corkscrews through the mucus or heavy use of painkillers thins the barrier, letting your own acid burn the wall underneath.

And when William Beaumont peered through Alexis St. Martin's stomach window in eighteen twenty-five, he noticed one more mystery: whenever St. Martin grew furious or frightened, his stomach lining changed colour at once — proving that nerves from the brain wire straight into the gut! Half a century later, in eighteen eighty-seven at his kitchen table in Barcelona, Spanish artist-turned-anatomist Santiago Ramón y Cajal — followed by Otto Loewi waking from an Easter dream at three in the morning in March nineteen twenty-one — discovered that your eighty-six billion brain cells never actually touch each other, firing messages across microscopic gaps with tiny chemical puffs!

A silk string lowered into a frontier musket wound, a Petri dish forgotten over Easter, and a doctor bold enough to drink a billion bacteria. Together, they proved how your body turns food into fuel and turned a dreaded disease into a simple cure.
