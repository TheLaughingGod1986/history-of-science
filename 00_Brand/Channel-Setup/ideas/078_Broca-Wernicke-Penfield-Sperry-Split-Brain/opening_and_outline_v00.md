### PART 02: 18 April 1861 & 1874 — Patient "Tan", Paul Broca's Left Frontal Lobe & Carl Wernicke's Two-Part Language Engine (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why for centuries anatomists believed the wrinkled outer bark of the brain was a uniform, undivided mush where both halves did the exact same thing — until a 51-year-old man who could only say a single syllable entered Paul Broca's Paris ward in April 1861!):** **Bicêtre Hospital, Paris, 12–18 April 1861 & Breslau, Germany, 1874**.
- **18 April 1861 (*Bulletins de la Société d'Anthropologie de Paris*) — Pierre Paul Broca & Louis Victor Leborgne ("Tan"):**
  - In the early 1800s, mainstream brain science (`led by Pierre Flourens, who demolished Franz Joseph Gall's bumpy-skull "phrenology"`) insisted that the cerebral cortex acted as a **single, holistic whole** (`equipotentiality`) — and because every organ in the body (`eyes, lungs, kidneys`) comes in symmetrical pairs, everyone assumed the **left and right halves of the brain were identical duplicates**.
  - On **12 April 1861** at Bicêtre Hospital in Paris, surgeon **Pierre Paul Broca** (1824–1880) examined 51-year-old former artisan **Louis Victor Leborgne**. For 21 years, Leborgne had understood everything said to him, could gesture and count with his fingers, and had healthy tongue and vocal muscles — yet whenever he tried to speak, the only sound his mouth could produce was **"Tan, tan"** (`plus a single French curse word, "Sacré nom de Dieu!", when angry`)!
  - When Leborgne died on **17 April 1861**, Broca performed an autopsy and presented the preserved brain to the Anthropological Society of Paris the very next day (**18 April 1861**): a walnut-sized lesion in the **posterior third frontal convolution of the LEFT hemisphere (`Broca's area`)** had destroyed Leborgne's ability to assemble spoken words!
  - After examining eight more aphasia patients in **1863–1865**, Broca dropped a bombshell: **every single speech lesion sat in the LEFT hemisphere**, leading to his famous **1865** dictum: *"Nous parlons avec l'hémisphère gauche"* (**"We speak with the left hemisphere"**)!
- **1874 (*Der aphasische Symptomencomplex*) — 26-year-old Carl Wernicke discovers the comprehension centre:**
  - Thirteen years later in **1874** in Breslau, 26-year-old German physician **Carl Wernicke** (1848–1905) discovered the mirror image of Broca's patients: patients with a lesion further back in the **left superior temporal gyrus (`Wernicke's area`)** could speak fast, effortless sentences with perfect rhythm — **that made zero sense**, while understanding nothing said to them! Language wasn't a single spot: `Wernicke's area` decodes the meaning of words and sends them forward along a nerve bundle (`arcuate fasciculus`) to `Broca's area` to move your lips and tongue!

- **Ends on:** If **Broca and Wernicke** proved that specific rooms in the **left brain** control speech, what did the rest of the wrinkled human cortex do — and how did neurosurgeon **Wilder Penfield in Montreal in December 1937** map the living brain while his patients were **wide awake and talking to him**?

### PART 03: 1870 & December 1937 — Fritsch & Hitzig's Crossed Wires and Wilder Penfield's Awake Cortical `Homunculus` (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open (how do you map a living human brain without hurting the patient? Because the brain itself has ZERO pain receptors — allowing neurosurgeon Wilder Penfield in Montreal in 1937 to chat with awake patients under local anaesthesia while touching a gentle electrode to their cortex and discovering a grotesque, giant-handed "little man" mapped across the brain!):** **Berlin, 1870 & Montreal Neurological Institute, December 1937**.
- **1870 (*Archiv für Anatomie, Physiologie und wissenschaftliche Medicin*) — Gustav Fritsch & Eduard Hitzig discover the motor cortex & crossed control:**
  - For decades, scientists believed the cerebral cortex was electrically unexcitable. In **1870** in Berlin, working on a bedroom dressing table (`because their university had no lab space!`), **Gustav Fritsch** (1838–1927) and **Eduard Hitzig** (1838–1907) touched weak galvanic currents (`from a battery 019`) to five spots on a dog's frontal cortex: touching the **left hemisphere** made the **right paw, face, or neck twitch**, and touching the **right hemisphere** moved the **left side of the body** (`contralateral motor control`)!
- **December 1937 (*Brain*) — Wilder Penfield & Edwin Boldrey map the human `Homunculus` on awake patients:**
  - At the **Montreal Neurological Institute** in the **1930s**, neurosurgeon **Wilder Graves Penfield** (1891–1976) operated on patients with severe focal epilepsy. Because brain tissue itself contains **no pain receptors (`nociceptors`)**, Penfield numbed only the scalp with local anaesthetic so his patients stayed **wide awake and talking** during surgery!
  - Gently touching a **`1–2 volt`** probe along the narrow strip of cortex running from ear to ear (`the precentral motor gyrus and postcentral sensory gyrus`) so he could spare vital areas while removing seizure scars, Penfield placed **tiny numbered paper tickets (`1, 2, 3...`)** on the brain surface and photographed each operation.
  - In **December 1937** (*Brain*, with **Edwin Boldrey**, illustrated by Mrs Hortense Cantlie), Penfield unveiled the **`cortical homunculus`** ("little man"): an upside-down body map draped across each hemisphere where body parts are sized **not** by how big they are on your body, but by how many nerve endings and precision muscles they use — giving you **enormous hands, giant thumbs, and balloon-sized lips and tongue**, while your entire back and legs shrink to a tiny corner!
  - Even more remarkably, when Penfield touched the **temporal lobe**, awake patients didn't twitch — they suddenly heard a childhood song playing as if on a gramophone or smelled baking bread (`flashback experiential responses`)!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Gustav Fritsch (1838–1927) — German physiologist · WHO: Eduard Hitzig (1838–1907) — German neuropsychiatrist · WHEN: 1870 · WHEN: December 1937 · FACT: Brain feels zero direct pain · DATA: inked Wilder Penfield & Edwin Boldrey (December 1937 Brain 60: 389–443) motor and sensory cortical homunculus chart on period paper comparing true anatomical body-surface percentage vs cortical representation area (giant thumb/hand, lips & tongue vs tiny trunk/legs) along the precentral and postcentral gyri · SCALE: on the sensory map inside your brain, your two thumbs and your lips take up more cortical real estate than your entire chest, back, and legs combined — because your brain allocates space by precision, not size`
- **Ends on:** By the 1940s, **Broca, Wernicke, and Penfield** had mapped both hemispheres — yet a massive mystery remained right in the middle of the skull: the **`corpus callosum`**, a thick bridge of **200 million nerve fibres** wiring the left and right hemispheres together! Surgeons noticed that when they cut the corpus callosum to stop epileptic seizures from crossing between hemispheres, patients walked out of the hospital talking and acting completely normal (`leading psychologist Karl Lashley to joke in 1951 that the corpus callosum only existed to keep the two halves of the brain from sagging!`). So how did **Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga at Caltech in 1962** prove that inside those patients' skulls, **two separate minds were now living side by side**?

### PART 04: 1962–1967 — Roger Sperry, Michael Gazzaniga & the `Split-Brain` Midpoint Twist (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Everyone thought cutting the 200-million-wire bridge between the left and right brain did nothing at all — until Roger Sperry and his 22-year-old graduate student Michael Gazzaniga built a 0.1-second flash screen at Caltech in 1962 and discovered that a split-brain patient's left hand had a mind of its own!"):** **California Institute of Technology (Caltech), Pasadena, 1962–1967**.
- **1953–1962 (*PNAS* October 1962) — Why nobody noticed the split until Sperry & Gazzaniga isolated each eye:**
  - Why did patients with a severed **`corpus callosum`** (`commissurotomy`, performed by Los Angeles neurosurgeons **Philip Vogel and Joseph Bogen** on WWII paratrooper **William J. Jenkins, "Patient W.J."**, in February 1962 to stop up to 20 grand mal seizures a day) seem normal at the breakfast table? Because in everyday life, **both eyes move together**, sending the same room to both halves of the brain at once!
  - After **Roger Wolcott Sperry** (1913–1994) and Ronald Myers (**1953**) proved in cats that cutting both the optic chiasm and the corpus callosum trapped visual learning inside one hemisphere, Sperry and his first-year Caltech graduate student **Michael S. Gazzaniga** (born 1939) tested **Patient W.J.** in **1962** (*PNAS*, October 1962) and a cohort of split-brain patients (**N.G., L.B., P.S.**) in **1965–1967** (*Brain*).
  - Their secret weapon was a **`tachistoscope`**: while the patient stared at a black dot in the centre of a screen, Gazzaniga flashed a word or picture for just **`0.1 seconds` (`100 milliseconds` — faster than the `200 ms` it takes human eyes to flick sideways!)** to either the **right half of the screen** (`which wires exclusively to the speaking LEFT hemisphere`) or the **left half of the screen** (`which wires exclusively to the mute RIGHT hemisphere`)!
- **The Midpoint Twist (`Two independent minds — and the "Left-Brain Interpreter"`):**
  - When Gazzaniga flashed a picture of a **spoon** (or the word **"HEART"** split across the centre as **"HE · ART"**) to the **right visual field (`left hemisphere`)**, the patient immediately said aloud: *"Spoon!"*
  - When Gazzaniga flashed the **spoon** to the **left visual field (`right hemisphere`)**, the patient's speaking left hemisphere said: *"I didn't see anything — just a flash of light!"*
  - Yet when Gazzaniga asked the patient to reach under the screen with their **left hand** (`controlled by the mute right hemisphere!`) and touch the object they had seen among a hidden row of items, **the left hand unerringly picked up the spoon** — while the patient's own mouth still insisted they had seen nothing!
  - Even more astonishingly: in **Patient W.J.**, when asked to arrange coloured blocks (`Kohs blocks`) to match a 3D pattern, his **left hand** (`spatial right hemisphere`) solved the puzzle in seconds while his **right hand** (`verbal left hemisphere`) couldn't do it — and sometimes his right hand reached over and **fought his left hand**, undoing the blocks his left hand had just placed!
  - And when Gazzaniga flashed a snow scene to the right brain (`which picked a snow shovel with the left hand`) and a chicken claw to the speaking left brain (`which picked a chicken with the right hand`) and asked *why* the left hand was holding a snow shovel, the speaking left brain (`which had never seen the snow!`) didn't say *"I don't know"* — it **instantly invented a story**: *"Oh, the chicken claw goes with the chicken, and you need a shovel to clean out the chicken shed!"* (**`The Left-Brain Interpreter`** — winning Roger Sperry the **1981 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine**)!
