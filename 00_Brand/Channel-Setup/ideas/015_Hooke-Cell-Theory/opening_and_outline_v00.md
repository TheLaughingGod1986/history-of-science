### PART 02: The Book That Kept London Awake (1:25–2:50) — `awe` → `explain`
- **Cold open (quirky anecdote before the hard idea):** **21 January 1665**, London. Naval administrator **Samuel Pepys** (1633–1703) buys a brand-new illustrated book by **Robert Hooke** called ***Micrographia***, sits up in bed by candlelight until **two o'clock in the morning**, and writes in his diary that it is *"the most ingenious book that ever I read in my life."*
  - Who was Hooke? Born on the Isle of Wight in **1635** with a curved spine, former choirboy and assistant to chemist **Robert Boyle** in Oxford, appointed in **1662** as the Royal Society's first **Curator of Experiments** — paid to bring three or four new experiments to every weekly meeting.
  - **The mechanism (Explainer diagram on period paper):** Seventeenth-century glass lenses were dim and blurry. Hooke solved the lighting problem by placing a small oil lamp behind a round **glass globe filled with salted water**, focusing a brilliant pinpoint of light onto his specimen under a six-inch tube of red leather and brass (~30× to 50× magnification).
  - In **Observation XVIII**, Hooke asks a deceptively simple question: *Why does a wine cork float so lightly on water, and spring back when you squeeze it?* He shaves off a paper-thin slice with a penknife, looks down the tube, and sees an endless honeycomb of tiny air-filled boxes separated by wafer-thin walls — naming them **"cells"** after the small bare rooms (*cella*) where monks lived in a monastery.
  - Because cork is dead outer bark, Hooke sees only empty walls with nothing inside — so he concludes they are merely tiny pipes or channels for plant juices!
  - *Markers:* `· WHEN: London, 21 January 1665 · WHO: Samuel Pepys (1633–1703) — Royal Navy administrator · WHO: Robert Boyle (1627–1691) — natural philosopher · WHEN: 1662 · DATA: inked optical diagram of Hooke's microscope (oil lamp → brine-filled glass globe condenser → cork slice → compound lenses at ~30×–50×) · SCALE: magnified 50 times — making a flea look as big as a cat · FACT: Pepys read until 2 a.m.`
- **Ends on:** Nine years later, a Dutch cloth merchant saw living single-celled creatures swimming in pond water — so why did Hooke's own face vanish from the Royal Society after he died?

### PART 03: The Missing Portrait & the Coffee-Table Epiphany (2:50–4:15) — `awe` → `explain`
- **Cold open:** **3 March 1703**, Gresham College, London. Robert Hooke dies blind and bedridden at age sixty-seven, leaving an iron chest stuffed with **£8,000** in gold and silver coins.
  - **Rivalry beat (back-and-forth cuts between Isaac Newton and Hooke's empty frame):** For thirty years Hooke had clashed bitterly with **Isaac Newton** (1642–1727), accusing Newton of taking his ideas on the colours of light (**1672**) and the inverse-square law of gravity (**1686**).
  - Nine months after Hooke's death, on **30 November 1703**, Newton is elected **President of the Royal Society**. When Newton moves the Society from Gresham College to Crane Court in **1710**, Hooke's instruments fall into disrepair and **the only known portrait of Robert Hooke disappears** (noticed missing by German visitor Zacharias Conrad von Uffenbach in 1710).
  - Meanwhile, microscopes suffer from severe colour fringes (**chromatic aberration**) for over a century until **Joseph Jackson Lister** (1786–1869, father of surgeon Joseph Lister) designs the **achromatic microscope lens in 1826–1830**.
  - With clearer lenses, Scottish botanist **Robert Brown** (1773–1858) looks at orchid leaves in **1831** and spots a dark, round kernel inside every plant cell — naming it the **nucleus** ("little nut").
  - Then, in **October 1838** in Berlin, volatile botanist **Matthias Jakob Schleiden** (1804–1881) — who had practiced law so unhappily in Hamburg that he shot himself in the forehead in **1831**, survived, and switched to botany — sits over coffee after dinner with quiet physiologist **Theodor Schwann** (1810–1882).
  - As Schleiden describes how every plant cell grows around a central nucleus, Schwann freezes: he has seen the exact same nucleated boxes inside frog spinal cords and pig embryos! That very night they walk to **Johannes Müller's** Berlin lab, peer through the microscope together, and publish in **1838–1839** that **every plant and every animal is built out of cells**.
  - *Markers:* `· WHEN: 3 March 1703 · WHO: Isaac Newton (1642–1727) — Royal Society president · WHEN: 30 November 1703 · WHEN: 1710 · WHO: Robert Brown (1773–1858) — Scottish botanist · WHEN: 1831 · WHEN: Berlin, October 1838 · WHO: Matthias Schleiden (1804–1881) — German botanist · WHO: Theodor Schwann (1810–1882) — German physiologist · WHEN: 1839 · FACT: Only portrait vanished in 1710`
- **Ends on:** Schleiden and Schwann proved *what* every living body is made of. So how could both men be completely wrong about where new cells come from?

### PART 04: The Sugar-Crystal Mistake — Midpoint Twist (4:15–5:45) — `explain` → `awe`
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **1839**, Berlin. If your body is made of trillions of cells, how does a growing child or a healing wound make *new* ones?
  - **The wrong theory:** Schleiden (and Schwann after him) claimed that new cells **crystallise spontaneously out of formless fluid** (*cytoblastema*), the exact way sugar crystals form on a string in sugar water! First a tiny granule appears in the fluid, then a nucleus precipitates around it, and finally a cell membrane balloons out like a soap bubble ("free cell formation").
  - Enter **Robert Remak** (1815–1865), a brilliant Polish-Jewish physician and embryologist working in Berlin. Because Prussia barred Jews from holding regular university professorships unless they converted to Christianity, Remak refused to convert and worked for years without a salary in a cramped attic laboratory at the Charité hospital.
  - **The proof (1841–1852):** Watching red blood cells in chick embryos (**1841**) and hardening fertilised frog eggs in a mixture of alcohol and chromic acid (**1851–1852**), Remak catches cells in the act: the nucleus pinches in the middle, a partition wall grows across the centre, and **one existing cell splits cleanly into two daughter cells**.
  - In **1852**, Remak publishes a categorical rebuttal of Schleiden and Schwann: free cell formation in fluid is a fantasy — **every animal cell comes from the division of a pre-existing cell**, starting from the single fertilised egg!
  - *Markers:* `· WHO: Robert Remak (1815–1865) — Berlin embryologist · WHEN: Berlin, 1841–1852 · WHEN: 1852 · DATA: inked two-panel explainer on period paper contrasting Schleiden & Schwann's false "crystal in fluid" model with Remak's observed binary cell division (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 cleavage stages in a frog egg) · SCALE: 1 cell doubling 45 times — enough to build all 30 trillion cells in an adult human · FACT: Barred from a professorship`
- **Ends on:** Remak had solved one of the greatest mysteries in biology. Why did another man walk away with the fame just three years later?

### PART 05: Three Latin Words (5:45–7:00) — `awe`
- **Cold open:** **1855**, Würzburg and Berlin. Pathologist **Rudolf Virchow** (1821–1902) — Remak's former colleague at the Charité — had himself believed in Schleiden and Schwann's fluid-crystal theory for years.
  - After seeing Remak's evidence, Virchow changes his mind and in **April 1855** publishes an essay in his own journal (*Archiv für pathologische Anatomie*) summing up the law in four memorable Latin words: ***"Omnis cellula e cellula"*** — *"All cells come from cells"* (echoing William Harvey's 1651 *"ex ovo omnia"* and François-Vincent Raspail's 1825 epigraph).
  - In his 1855 paper and his famous **1858** book *Die Cellularpathologie*, Virchow barely mentions Robert Remak — and textbooks for the next century credit the third pillar of cell theory to Virchow alone, leaving Remak to write outraged letters until his death in **1865**.
  - Yet together, that law revolutionised medicine: cancer was not a mysterious poison or fluid crystallising in the blood, nor was pus born from nothing — disease was **normal body cells dividing or malfunctioning**, and **Louis Pasteur** (in **1859–1861**) soon proved the exact same law for microbes in swan-neck flasks (`001` *Germs*).
  - *Markers:* `· WHO: Rudolf Virchow (1821–1902) — German pathologist · WHEN: April 1855 · WHEN: 1858 · WHEN: 1865 · DATA: animated timeline building 1665 (Hooke's cork) → 1703 (Hooke's portrait lost) → 1831 (Brown names nucleus) → 1838–1839 (Schleiden & Schwann) → 1852 (Remak proves division) → 1855 (Virchow's Omnis cellula e cellula) · SCALE: 190 years from Hooke's cork slice (1665) to Omnis cellula e cellula (1855) · FACT: Virchow left Remak's name out`
- **Ends on:** If every cell in your body comes from an earlier cell dividing in two, what does that mean if you trace the divisions all the way back?

### PART 06: The Unbroken Chain & After-Act (7:00–8:05) — `explain` → `awe`
- **Cold open (the big idea & misconception explained away):** People often say *"every cell in your body is replaced every seven years."* Is that true?
  - **No:** while the cells lining your gut divide and replace themselves every **3 to 5 days**, and red blood cells last about **120 days**, most of the neurons in your brain's cerebral cortex and the lens cells in your eyes have been with you since before you were born!
  - Even more extraordinary: because *"all cells come from cells"* (`Omnis cellula e cellula`) has never once been broken since life began, every one of your **30 trillion cells** is the end of an **unbroken four-billion-year chain of cell divisions** stretching back to the very first single-celled organism on Earth. Cells don't just build life — a dividing cell *is* life refusing to stop.
  - **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:05):**
    - When Robert Remak and Rudolf Virchow watched one cell split into two, they saw the dark central **nucleus** divide first — but neither man knew what was hidden inside that nucleus, or how a single microscopic photograph taken in a London basement a century later would reveal the twisted ladder of **DNA** (`016` candidate, or hand-off back to `012` *Mendel's 28,000 Pea Plants* / `013` *Darwin*).
    - Cream end card (`STUDIO_PLAYBOOK.md` §7). No goodbye, no subscribe ask.
