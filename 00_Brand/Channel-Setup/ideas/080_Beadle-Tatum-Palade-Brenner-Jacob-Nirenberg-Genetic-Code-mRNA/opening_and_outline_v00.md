### PART 02: 1941 & 1955 — Irradiated Bread Mould (`One Gene–One Enzyme`) & George Palade's `Ribosome` Factories (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (for forty years after Mendel's genes were rediscovered, scientists knew genes gave you brown eyes or tall pea plants — yet nobody knew WHAT a gene actually did inside a cell, until two scientists at Stanford in 1941 blasted pink bread mould with X-rays!):** **Stanford University, 1941 & Rockefeller Institute, New York, 1955**.
- **1902 & 15 November 1941 (*PNAS*) — Archibald Garrod's forgotten clue & George Beadle and Edward Tatum's bread-mould proof:**
  - Back in **1902**, British physician **Archibald Garrod** (1857–1936) noticed that patients with a rare inherited condition called **`alkaptonuria`** (`whose urine turned black on contact with air!`) lacked a single blood enzyme — calling it an **"inborn error of metabolism"**, a clue geneticists ignored for 39 years.
  - At Stanford University in **1941** (*PNAS*, 15 November 1941), geneticist **George Wells Beadle** (1903–1989) and biochemist **Edward Lawrie Tatum** (1909–1975) flipped traditional genetics upside down: instead of starting with a visible trait and hunting for its chemical cause, they took a simple pink bread mould (**`Neurospora crassa`**) that could grow on bare sugar, salt, and one vitamin (`biotin`), and **blasted its spores with X-rays (`003`)** to break individual genes!
  - On **tube number 299**, they found a mutant spore that died on bare medium — yet grew normally as soon as they added a single drop of **Vitamin B₆ (`pyridoxine`)**! Testing thousands more spores, each single-gene mutant lacked the ability to make **one specific vitamin or amino acid** (`such as arginine`), proving the **`One Gene–One Enzyme` (`One Gene–One Polypeptide`) law**: **every gene in your DNA is the recipe for building one specific protein** (`winning Beadle and Tatum the 1958 Nobel Prize`)!
- **January 1955 (*J. Biophys. Biochem. Cytol.*) — George Palade discovers `Ribosomes`:**
  - Where inside the cell are those proteins actually built? In **January 1955** at the Rockefeller Institute in New York, Romanian-American cell biologist **George Emil Palade** (1912–2008, `1974 Nobel Prize`) peered through an electron microscope and discovered millions of tiny, dense particles (`20–30 nanometres wide`, first called **"Palade granules"** and named **`ribosomes`** by Richard Roberts in **1958** because they were rich in ribonucleic acid, `RNA`) — the molecular assembly lines where amino acids are stitched into proteins!

- **Ends on:** Once **Franklin, Watson, and Crick (`016`)** solved the **`DNA` double helix in 1953** and **George Palade** found the **`ribosomes` in 1955**, biologists hit a brick wall: **`DNA` stays locked inside the cell's nucleus**, while **`ribosomes` build proteins far away out in the cytoplasm**! ### PART 03: Good Friday, 15 April 1960 — The Missing Messenger (`mRNA`) & the 3-Letter Codon Proof — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — since Palade's ribosomes are made of RNA, every ribosome must be custom-built in the nucleus as a permanent mould for one specific protein! Wrong! On Good Friday, 15 April 1960 in a Cambridge college room, François Jacob and Sydney Brenner realized that ribosomes are just neutral tape-players — and that cells run on a self-destructing photocopy tape called `messenger RNA`!"):** **King's College, Cambridge, 15 April 1960 & Caltech, Pasadena, Summer 1960 (published 13 May 1961)**.
- **1958–15 April 1960 (*Nature* 13 May 1961) — The Midpoint Twist (`Why ribosomes aren't the blueprint: the Good Friday eureka of mRNA`):**
  - After **Francis Crick** stated the **`Central Dogma`** (`DNA → RNA → Protein`, **1957–1958**) and predicted tiny **"adaptor" molecules** (`transfer RNA / tRNA`, discovered by **Mahlon Hoagland and Paul Zamecnik in 1957**) that carry each amino acid, everyone assumed the stable RNA inside Palade's **`ribosomes`** was the gene message itself (**"One Gene–One Ribosome"**).
  - Yet at the Pasteur Institute in Paris (**1959**, the famous **"PaJaMo" experiment** by **Arthur Pardee, François Jacob, and Jacques Monod**), the moment a new gene was slipped into a bacterium, it started churning out the new enzyme at full speed within **two minutes** — and stopped just as fast when the gene was removed, with no time to build brand-new ribosomes!
  - On **Good Friday, 15 April 1960**, inside **Sydney Brenner's** rooms at King's College, Cambridge (`with Francis Crick, Allan Garen, and Lesley Orgel listening`), **François Jacob** described the Paris puzzle — and **Sydney Brenner** and **Crick** leaped up at the exact same instant: the mysterious short-lived RNA that **Elliot Volkin and Lazarus Astrachan had spotted in phage-infected bacteria in 1956** WAS the missing message!
  - That summer (**1960**) in **Matthew Meselson's** laboratory at Caltech, **Brenner, Jacob, and Meselson** (*Nature*, **13 May 1961**, back-to-back with **François Gros and Jim Watson's** Harvard group!) used heavy isotopes (`¹⁵N` and `¹³C`) to prove it: **no new ribosomes are made** when a virus infects a cell; instead, the cell prints a short-lived, disposable RNA copy of the gene — named **`messenger RNA` (`mRNA`)** by Jacob and Monod — and feeds that tape through pre-existing **"for-hire" ribosomes**!
- **30 December 1961 (*Nature*) — Francis Crick & Sydney Brenner prove the 3-letter `Triplet` code:**
  - And in **1961** (*Nature*, 30 December 1961), **Francis Crick, Leslie Barnett, Sydney Brenner, and Richard Watts-Tobin** deleted or inserted single letters (`bases`) in a bacteriophage gene: deleting **1 or 2 letters** scrambled the whole protein, but deleting or adding **3 letters** restored the reading frame — proving the genetic code is read in non-overlapping **3-letter words (`codons`)**!
- *Markers:* `· WHO: Francis Crick (1916–2004) — British molecular biologist and biophysicist · WHO: Jacques Monod (1910–1976) — French biochemist and geneticist · WHEN: 1957 · WHEN: 15 April 1960 · WHEN: 13 May 1961 · WHEN: 30 December 1961 · FACT: mRNA is a disposable tape · DATA: inked Sydney Brenner, François Jacob & Matthew Meselson (13 May 1961 Nature 190: 576–581) heavy-isotope (15N/13C) ribosome density-gradient chart proving new phage mRNA binds pre-existing ribosomes alongside Crick, Barnett, Brenner & Watts-Tobin's (30 December 1961 Nature 192: 1227–1232) +1/+2 frameshift vs +3 triplet reading-frame restoration chart on period paper · SCALE: Sydney Brenner and François Jacob proved that DNA never leaves the nucleus for the same reason an architect never leaves the only master blueprint of a skyscraper out in the mud — instead, the cell prints disposable mRNA photocopies and sends them out to the ribosome construction crew`
- **Ends on:** Now scientists knew that **`messenger RNA`** (`written in A, U, C, G`) is read by **`ribosomes`** in **64 three-letter words (`codons`)** — but **WHICH 3-letter word spells WHICH of the 20 amino acids**? ### PART 04: 27 May 1961, 3:00 a.m. — Marshall Nirenberg, Heinrich Matthaei & the `Poly-U` (`UUU = Phenylalanine`) Breakthrough (4:55–6:45)
- **Cold open (how did a 34-year-old NIH researcher who had been rejected by Jacques Monod's Paris lab and wasn't even invited into Francis Crick's elite "RNA Tie Club" crack the greatest cipher in the history of biology at 3:00 a.m. on a Saturday morning?):** **National Institutes of Health (NIH), Bethesda, Maryland, 27 May 1961 & International Congress of Biochemistry, Moscow, August 1961**.
- **1954–1961 — The elite "RNA Tie Club" vs Nirenberg & Matthaei's cell-free mortar and pestle:**
  - Since **1954**, physicist **George Gamow** and **Francis Crick** had organized the **`RNA Tie Club`** — an exclusive 20-member circle of the world's top physicists and biologists (`one member named for each of the 20 amino acids, complete with custom woollen ties embroidered with a green-and-yellow RNA helix!`) — convinced it would take a decade of mathematical cryptography to decode the 64 codons.
  - Meanwhile, in a small lab at the **NIH in Bethesda, Maryland**, 34-year-old biochemist **Marshall Warren Nirenberg** (1927–2010) and German postdoctoral fellow **J. Heinrich Matthaei** (born 1929) took a direct biochemical shortcut: they ground up *E. coli* bacteria with alumina powder in a chilled mortar to liberate Palade's **`ribosomes`** and tRNAs into test tubes (**`"Matthaei's cell-free soup"`**), added the enzyme **`DNase`** to destroy all the bacteria's own DNA so no native `mRNA` could be made, and fed in **synthetic RNA** (`made using Marianne Grunberg-Manago and Severo Ochoa's polynucleotide phosphorylase enzyme`)!
- **27 May 1961, 3:00 a.m. (*PNAS* October 1961) & August 1961 (Moscow) — `UUU = Phenylalanine` & Har Gobind Khorana's full 64-codon dictionary:**
  - Starting at midnight on **Saturday, 27 May 1961**, Heinrich Matthaei prepared **20 test tubes** of ribosome soup — each doped with a different radioactive (`¹⁴C`) amino acid — and added a synthetic RNA chain made purely of repeated **Uracil letters (`U-U-U-U-U-U...`, `poly-U`)**.
  - At **3:00 a.m. on 27 May 1961**, Matthaei fed the filter papers into the radiation counter: 19 tubes stayed flat at **`70 counts per minute`** — **while the tube containing radioactive `phenylalanine` roared to `39,800 counts per minute`**!
  - The very first word of the genetic code had been read: **`UUU` = `Phenylalanine`**!
  - When unknown Nirenberg flew to the International Congress of Biochemistry in **Moscow in August 1961** (*PNAS* published **October 1961**), his 15-minute talk was put in a tiny side room with barely **30 people** in the audience — until **Matthew Meselson** heard him, ran up to hug him, and told **Francis Crick**, who immediately put Nirenberg onto the main stage before **over 1,000 cheering scientists**! Over the next five years (**1961–1966**), Nirenberg (with **Philip Leder's** 1964 triplet-binding filter assay) and chemist **Har Gobind Khorana** (1922–2011, synthesizing defined repeating RNA polymers) bersama **Robert Holley** (who sequenced the first `tRNA` cloverleaf in 1965) decoded **all 64 codons** (`winning Nirenberg, Khorana, and Holley the 1968 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine`)!
