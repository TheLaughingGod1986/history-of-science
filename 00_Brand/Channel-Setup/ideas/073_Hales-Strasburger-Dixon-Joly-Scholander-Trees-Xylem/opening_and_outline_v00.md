### PART 02: 1727 & 1891 — Stephen Hales's Bleeding Grapevine & Eduard Strasburger's 22-Metre Poisoned Oak (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open (why Galileo and Torricelli's `039` 10.3-metre suction limit made tall trees look like a violation of physics — and why an English vicar in 1727 discovered that a tree loses more water through its leaves every day than a human sweats in a desert!):** **Teddington Vicarage, Middlesex, 1727 & University of Bonn, Germany, 1891**.
- **1727 (*Vegetable Staticks*) — Stephen Hales measures `transpiration` and `root pressure` (and spots the summer paradox!):**
  - After **Van Helmont and Ingenhousz (`006`)** showed that plants build wood from air and sunlight, English clergyman **Stephen Hales** (1677–1761) in **1727** (*Vegetable Staticks*, dedicated to the Prince of Wales and praised by Isaac Newton) weighed a potted sunflower sealed in lead and found that its leaves evaporated **`20 ounces` (`~0.6 litres`) of water in a single 12-hour day** (`transpiration`) — seventeen times more water per unit surface area than a human body loses!
  - Where did the force to lift all that water come from? In April, before leaves opened, Hales cut a grapevine stump near the ground and tied a vertical glass tube of **mercury (`Hg`)** to it: rising sap pushed the mercury up **`32.5 inches` (`82.5 cm`, over `1.1 atmospheres` — enough to push water `11.6 metres` high via `root pressure`)**!
  - **But Hales noticed a fatal catch:** as soon as the grapevine's leaves opened in warm summer weather — right when the plant drank the *most* water — positive `root pressure` vanished completely! Cut a summer tree trunk, and instead of sap spurting out, **the wood sucks air and water IN**!
- **1891 (*Über den Bau und die Verrichtungen der Leitungsbahnen in den Pflanzen*) — Eduard Strasburger poisons a 22-metre oak tree:**
  - For 160 years after Hales, "vitalist" botanists (`like Julius von Sachs and Emil Godlewski in 1884`) insisted that living cells lining the wooden tubes must act like microscopic relay hearts, actively pumping water upward step by step.
  - In **1891** in Bonn, German botanist **Eduard Strasburger** (1844–1912) tested the relay-pump theory with a lumberjack experiment: he sawed down a **22-metre-tall (`72-foot`) oak tree**, slung the giant trunk upright from a wooden scaffold, and plunged its cut base into a tub of **`5% picric acid` poison** (and in other trials, `copper sulphate` or `90 °C boiling water`).
  - As the yellow poison climbed all **22 metres** to the top crown, it **killed every single living cell in the trunk**. Then Strasburger added red **`fuchsin` dye** to the vat — and the **completely dead wooden pipes (`xylem`) kept hauling dyed water all 22 metres to the top leaves**, more than **twice as high as any vacuum suction pump (`10.3 m`)**!

### PART 03: 15 November 1894 — Henry Dixon & John Joly's `Cohesion-Tension Theory` in Dublin — Midpoint Twist (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open ("Everyone knows you cannot pull on a liquid — try grabbing a fistful of water and pulling it up a hill! Wrong! In November 1894, an Irish botanist and an Irish physicist proved that if you trap pure water inside microscopic air-free tubes, water has the tensile strength of solid steel wire!"):** **Trinity College Dublin, Ireland, 15 November 1894**.
- **Why suction fails at 10.3 metres — and why `negative pressure` in water is possible (`Marcellin Berthelot 1850`):**
  - Why does a suction pump fail at `10.3 metres` (`34 feet`)? Because "suction" isn't really pulling — when you lift a piston at the top of a well pipe, you only drop the pressure above the water toward **`0 atmospheres` (`a vacuum`)**, and it is the **`+1 atmosphere` of outside air** pushing on the open well at the bottom that shoves water up `10.3 metres` (`where water weight equals 1 atm and any air bubble expands into a vapour gap`).
  - Wait — can liquid pressure go **below zero** (`into negative atmospheres`, where the liquid itself is stretched under tension)? In **1850**, French chemist **Marcellin Berthelot** sealed completely bubble-free water inside a thick glass tube, heated it until it filled the tube, and cooled it down: the water clung to the glass walls and stretched to **`-50 atmospheres` of negative tension** before snapping with a metallic *clang*!
- **15 November 1894 (*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 1895) — Henry Horatio Dixon & John Joly unveil the `Cohesion-Tension Theory`:**
  - At Trinity College Dublin, botanist **Henry Horatio Dixon** (1869–1953) and physicist **John Joly** (1857–1933) (alongside German physicist ** Eugen Askenasy** in **1895**) connected Berthelot's stretched water to Strasburger's dead oak!
  - In their paper read before the Royal Society on **15 November 1894** (*"On the ascent of sap"*), Dixon and Joly proved the **`Cohesion-Tension Theory`**:
    1. **Transpiration pull at the top (`Tension`):** Inside a leaf, water evaporates from wet cell walls honeycombed with microscopic pores just **`5 nanometres` wide**. As water molecules evaporate, the microscopic curved water surface (**`meniscus`**) retreats into those nanopores, creating a capillary surface-tension pull capable of generating up to **`-100 to -300 atmospheres`** of suction!
    2. **Unbroken hydrogen-bond chains (`Cohesion` & `Adhesion`):** Because water molecules are polar (`oxygen slightly negative, hydrogens slightly positive`), they lock together with **hydrogen bonds (`cohesion`)** and cling to the hydrophilic cellulose walls of narrow **`xylem` vessels (`20 to 200 micrometres wide`)**. Because the microscopic pores in xylem walls (`pit membranes`) block air bubbles from entering, the unbroken column of water hangs from the leaves **like a continuous steel cable stretched under `-15 to -20 atmospheres` of tension**!

### PART 04: 16 April 1965 — Per Scholander's Hunting Rifle & the `Pressure Bomb` in the California Redwoods (4:55–6:40)

- **The measurement trap — and Per Scholander's `Pressure Bomb` idea:**
  - If water inside a tree is stretched like a rubber band under `-15 atmospheres` of negative tension, the instant you cut a twig off a tree, the stretched water inside the xylem tubes **snaps back away from the cut surface** into the twig (`just like a cut elastic band pulling back into a sleeve`).

  - Then slowly pump **compressed nitrogen gas** into the steel chamber squeezing the leaves until the sap **just beads back out to the cut tip of the stem**! If it takes **`+15 atmospheres` of positive gas pressure** to push the retracted sap back to the cut line, then the sap was under **`-15 atmospheres` of negative tension** one second before the twig was cut!
- **16 April 1965 (*Science*) — Marksmanship in the redwoods proves `Cohesion-Tension` down to the atmosphere:**
  - To test the tallest trees on Earth (**`90-metre` California coast redwoods, *Sequoia sempervirens*, and Douglas firs**), Scholander's team brought a **hunting rifle** into the forest, **shot down high twigs from the `80-to-90-metre` upper crown** and lower branches, and raced each twig into the steel pressure bomb within minutes!
