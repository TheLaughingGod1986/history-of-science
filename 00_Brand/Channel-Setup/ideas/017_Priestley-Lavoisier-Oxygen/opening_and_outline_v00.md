### PART 02: The Brewery Next Door and the Mouse in the Jar (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (quirky anecdote before the gas chemistry):** **Leeds, England, 1767**. Before discovering oxygen, minister **Joseph Priestley** lived next door to the Jopson public brewery in Leeds. Fascinated by the heavy blanket of gas ("fixed air", carbon dioxide) hovering over the fermenting beer vats, Priestley poured water back and forth between two glasses inside the brewery cloud — inventing **soda water** (for which the Royal Society awarded him the Copley Medal in **1773**!).
- To trap gases without letting them escape into the room, Priestley perfected the **pneumatic trough** — bubbling gases up into upside-down jars filled with water or liquid **mercury**.
- On **1 August 1774** at **Bowood House** in Wiltshire (where he worked as librarian to Lord Shelburne), Priestley used a **12-inch burning lens** to focus sunlight onto a red stone chemisted called *mercurius calcinatus per se* (mercuric oxide, $\text{HgO}$) floating on mercury inside a glass vial.
- Silver beads of liquid mercury appeared, and a colourless gas filled the jar:
  1. It didn't dissolve in water.
  2. A glowing candle flame plunged into it blazed with blinding brightness.
  3. On **8 March 1775**, Priestley placed a live **mouse** under a glass jar of the gas. Where a mouse in the same volume of ordinary air would suffocate in **15 minutes**, Priestley's mouse was still alive and lively after **half an hour** (and survived again when warmed by the fire)!
  4. Priestley breathed the gas himself through a glass siphon, writing that his chest felt *"peculiarly light and easy for some time afterwards. Who can tell but that, in time, this pure air may become a fashionable article in luxury? Hitherto only two mice and myself have had the privilege of breathing it."*
- Yet Priestley was so wedded to the old **phlogiston theory** (proposed by **Georg Ernst Stahl** in **1703**) that he believed his gas simply had all its phlogiston sucked out — so he named it **"dephlogisticated air"**!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Leeds, 1767 · FACT: Invented fizzy soda water · WHEN: 1 August 1774 · WHEN: 8 March 1775 · WHO: Georg Ernst Stahl (1659–1734) — German phlogiston chemist · DATA: inked pneumatic-trough diagram on period paper showing sunlight focused by a 12-inch burning lens onto red mercuric oxide (HgO) over liquid mercury, releasing 4–5× its volume in pure oxygen gas · SCALE: 12-inch lens focusing sunlight to over 600°C — hot enough to split solid red oxide into liquid metal and gas`
- **Ends on:** Why did the old phlogiston theory collapse the moment someone put a rusting piece of metal on a pair of scales — and what happened when Priestley travelled to Paris in October 1774?

### PART 03: The Dinner Table in Paris and the 12-Day Retort (3:05–4:55)
- **Cold open:** **Paris, October 1774**. Wealthy tax-farmer and Academy chemist **Antoine-Laurent Lavoisier** (1743–1794) and his sixteen-year-old wife and laboratory collaborator **Marie-Anne Paulze Lavoisier** (1758–1836) — who learned English and Latin to translate foreign chemistry papers and trained with painter Jacques-Louis David to draw every apparatus plate — host the visiting Joseph Priestley for dinner.
- **The puzzle that broke phlogiston:** For years, chemists knew a baffling fact. When you burn wood, the ash left behind is lighter. But when you heat or rust a metal like tin, lead or mercury into a flaky powder (a *calx*), the metal gets **heavier**! If burning releases phlogiston, how could losing something make metal weigh more? Some phlogiston defenders even claimed phlogiston had "negative weight"!
- **Rivalry beat:** Over dinner in **October 1774**, Priestley tells Lavoisier about the strange gas he released from red mercury calx that makes flames blaze.
- Lavoisier immediately sees what Priestley missed — and sets up his famous **12-day sealed glass retort experiment** (presented at Easter **April 1775**, perfected **1777**):
  1. Lavoisier seals **4 ounces** of pure liquid mercury inside a swan-necked glass retort connected to a bell jar containing **50 cubic inches** of air over mercury, and heats it just below boiling for **12 days**.
  2. Red flakes of calx form on the mercury, and when the apparatus cools, the air in the bell jar has shrunk by **8 to 9 cubic inches** (**about one-sixth to one-fifth of its volume**). The remaining four-fifths of the air (nitrogen, *azote*) extinguishes a candle and suffocates animals!
  3. Next, Lavoisier collects the **45 grains** of red mercury flakes, heats them strongly, and recovers **41.5 grains** of liquid mercury plus **8 to 9 cubic inches** of Priestley's pure gas — whose weight (**3.5 grains**) equals the exact weight lost by the red powder!
- In **1777**, Lavoisier names the gas **oxygen** (from Greek for "acid-maker"), proving air is a mixture of two gases and that burning and rusting are simply substances combining with oxygen!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Paris, October 1774 · WHO: Marie-Anne Paulze Lavoisier (1758–1836) — French chemistry translator & illustrator · WHEN: April 1775 · WHEN: 1777 · DATA: inked two-part mass-balance diagram of Lavoisier's 12-day swan-neck retort experiment: 50 cubic inches of air loses 8–9 cubic inches (~1/5th) as 45 grains of red mercury oxide gain 3.5 grains of mass, then heating releases the exact 8–9 cubic inches (3.5 grains) of oxygen back · SCALE: 1/5th of the air (21%) is oxygen — the exact fraction missing from Lavoisier's bell jar · FACT: Tried to claim sole credit`
- **Ends on:** Wait — if Lavoisier solved the puzzle, why did a Swedish apothecary named Carl Wilhelm Scheele have reason to be furious with both of them?

### PART 04: The Unopened Letter and the Sealed Flask — Midpoint Twist (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Uppsala & Stockholm, Sweden, 1771–1772**. Two full years *before* Priestley's 1 August 1774 bell-jar experiment, poor Swedish pharmaceutical chemist **Carl Wilhelm Scheele** (1742–1786) had already isolated oxygen ("fire air", *Feuerluft*) by heating silver carbonate, mercuric oxide and saltpetre!
- Why didn't the world know? First, Scheele's publisher sat on his book manuscript (*Chemische Abhandlung von der Luft und dem Feuer*) until **1777**. Second, on **30 September 1774**, Scheele wrote a letter in French directly to **Antoine Lavoisier** in Paris explaining exactly how to make "fire air" — and Lavoisier never replied, later claiming he hadn't noticed it, until that original letter was found among Lavoisier's papers in **1890**!
- **The Law of Conservation of Mass (1789):** Aided by Marie-Anne's thirteen copperplate engravings in their landmark **1789** textbook *Traité Élémentaire de Chimie*, Lavoisier seals fruit must, sugar, yeast and water inside airtight glass flasks on his precision balance. Even as fermentation foams violently into alcohol and carbon dioxide gas, the total mass before and after does not change by a single grain: **matter is neither created nor destroyed in any chemical reaction**.
- **The guinea pig ice calorimeter (1780–1783):** With mathematician **Pierre-Simon Laplace** (1749–1827), Lavoisier places a live **guinea pig** inside a double-walled chamber packed with ice, measuring the melted water against the carbon dioxide breathed out — proving that **animal respiration is slow combustion**, burning carbon and hydrogen with oxygen inside the body to make warmth!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Sweden, 1771–1772 · WHO: Carl Wilhelm Scheele (1742–1786) — Swedish pharmaceutical chemist · WHEN: 30 September 1774 · FACT: Scheele made oxygen first (1772) · WHEN: 1780–1783 · WHO: Pierre-Simon Laplace (1749–1827) — French mathematician & physicist · WHEN: Paris, 1789`
- **Ends on:** When you diet and lose ten kilograms of weight, where does that lost mass actually go?

### PART 05: Where Lost Weight Goes & the Guillotine (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** If Lavoisier proved that mass can never vanish, when you exercise and lose **10 kilograms** of body fat, where does it go? Most people — and even many doctors — think it turns into heat or energy. Is that true?
- **No:** turning 10 kilograms of mass into pure energy would be a nuclear blast! Using Lavoisier's conservation of mass (`2014` *BMJ* study by Meerman & Brown), when your cells burn **10 kilograms of fat** using inhaled oxygen, **8.4 kilograms (84%) is breathed out of your lungs as invisible carbon dioxide gas ($\text{CO}_2$)**, and the remaining **1.6 kilograms (16%) leaves as water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**! You literally breathe your lost weight into the air.
- **The tragic ending of both rivals (1791–1804):**
  - In **July 1791**, a Birmingham mob angry at Priestley's support for the French Revolution burned down his house, chapel and laboratory; he fled to Pennsylvania in **1794** and died on **6 February 1804**, publishing pamphlets to the very end defending phlogiston!
  - Three months after Priestley sailed for America, on **8 May 1794**, during the Reign of Terror in Paris, revolutionary tribunal judge **Jean-Baptiste Coffinhal** condemned tax-farmer **Antoine Lavoisier** to the **guillotine** at age 50; mathematician **Joseph-Louis Lagrange** (1736–1813) lamented the next day: *"It took them only an instant to cut off that head, and one hundred years might not suffice to produce another like it."*
  - Yet **Marie-Anne Paulze Lavoisier** survived imprisonment, recovered his notebooks, and published his final memoirs in **1805**, securing the chemical revolution forever.
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - Priestley used a glass lens to focus visible sunlight onto red powder. Twenty-six years later, in **February 1800**, astronomer **William Herschel** placed a thermometer in the dark shadow *outside* a sunlit rainbow — and discovered that the hottest light from the Sun is completely invisible to your eyes (`018` candidate *Invisible Light: Herschel's Infrared & Ritter's Ultraviolet*).
