### PART 02: 1669 & 1864 — Jan Swammerdam's Public Caterpillar Dissection, Maria Sibylla Merian & August Weismann's `Imaginal Discs` (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open ("In 1651, William Harvey — the great doctor who discovered how blood circulates (`005`) — declared that when a caterpillar seals itself inside a chrysalis, the caterpillar dies completely, rots into formless matter, and is reborn as a brand-new animal! Then in 1668 in Amsterdam, a 31-year-old microscopist named Jan Swammerdam invited the future ruler of Tuscany into his study to prove Harvey wrong with a needle and a drop of vinegar."):** **Amsterdam (1668–1669), Nuremberg/Suriname (1679–1705) & Freiburg, Germany (1864)**.
- **1668–1669 (*Historia Insectorum Generalis*) — Jan Swammerdam Peels Open a Caterpillar:**
  - Before **Grand Duke Cosimo III of Tuscany** and his court physician Francesco Redi's circle (`090`), Dutch anatomist **Jan Swammerdam** (1637–1680) took a full-grown caterpillar just before pupation, dipped it briefly into warm water and vinegar (`to firm up its delicate tissues`), and peeled back its outer caterpillar skin with hair-fine needles:
  - Right there inside the crawling caterpillar were **miniature butterfly wings, six long adult legs, antennae, and a coiled proboscis already formed and folded up like a bud**! Swammerdam proved that the caterpillar, chrysalis (`pupa`), and butterfly (`imago`) are **one continuous individual**, while German naturalist-artist **Maria Sibylla Merian** (1647–1717) raised hundreds of caterpillars in Nuremberg and Suriname (*Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium*, **1705**) to map their exact host plants and life cycles.
- **1864 (*Zeitschrift für wissenschaftliche Zoologie*) — August Weismann Discovers `Histolysis` & `Imaginal Discs`:**
  - Wait — if the caterpillar already holds the butterfly's wings, why does the inside of a chrysalis turn into **creamy liquid soup** when you cut it open a few days later?
  - In **1864** at Freiburg, German zoologist **August Weismann** (1834–1914) solved the paradox under his microscope: during metamorphosis, the caterpillar's larval muscles, gut, and fat body undergo programmed self-digestion (**`histolysis`** — breaking down into nutrient-rich soup, `fuelled by enzymes and phagocytic cells, 065, 070`), while **dozens of tiny, flat, hollow packets of embryonic cells** that had waited quietly inside the caterpillar since it was an egg (**`imaginal discs`**, from *imago*, the adult insect!) drink up that nutrient soup and telescope outward (**`histogenesis`**) to build the adult wings, legs, compound eyes (`083`), and flight muscles (`062`)!

### PART 03: 1922, 1934 & 1954 — Stefan Kopeć's Tied Caterpillars, Vincent Wigglesworth's Headless Bugs & 500 Kilograms of Silkworms (`Juvenile Hormone & Ecdysone`) — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — an insect's genes automatically count the days until it turns into a chrysalis, with no hormones involved! Wrong! Until 1922, every textbook insisted insects had zero hormones (`060`) — until a Polish biologist tied a silk thread around the middle of a caterpillar, and a British entomologist joined two headless bugs together with a glass tube!"):** **Kraków, Poland (1917–1922), London (1934–1936) & Tübingen, Germany (1954)**.
- **1917–1922 (*Biological Bulletin*) & 1934–1936 (*Q. J. Microsc. Sci.*) — The Brain Hormone & `Juvenile Hormone`:**
  - In **1917–1922** in Kraków, Polish biologist **Stefan Kopeć** (1888–1941) tied a fine **hair-tight silk thread** around the middle of gypsy-moth caterpillars (*Lymantria dispar*) or removed their tiny brains: if he tied the thread *before* a critical day, **only the front half (with the brain) pupated, while the back half stayed a caterpillar forever** — proving the **insect brain secretes a chemical hormone (`PTTH`) into the blood (`haemolymph`)** (`the first neurohormone ever discovered in any animal!`)!
  - Then in **1934–1936** at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, **Vincent Brian Wigglesworth** (1899–1994) decapitated blood-feeding South American kissing bugs (*Rhodnius prolixus*) at different moments and connected young and old nymphs in pairs (`parabiosis`) with paraffin-sealed capillary tubes!
  - **The Midpoint Twist:** Wigglesworth discovered that a tiny gland just behind the insect's brain (`the corpus allatum`) secretes a second chemical he named **`Juvenile Hormone`** (`"the status quo hormone"`):
    - As long as **`Juvenile Hormone` is HIGH** when the molting signal arrives, the caterpillar **stays a caterpillar** (moulting into a bigger larva)!
    - If you remove that tiny gland from a young larva, it skips childhood and turns into a **miniature midget adult** right away; and if you graft an active gland into a final-stage larva, it moults into a **giant super-caterpillar** instead of pupating!
- **1954 (*Zeitschrift für Naturforschung*) — Adolf Butenandt & Peter Karlson's 500-Kilogram Silkworm Hunt for `Ecdysone`:**
  - What was the molting trigger hormone itself (released by the prothoracic gland when stimulated by Kopeć's brain hormone, as **Soichi Fukuda** showed in **1940–1944**)?
  - In **1954** in Tübingen, Nobel chemist **Adolf Butenandt** (1903–1995) and **Peter Karlson** (1918–2001) processed **500 kilograms (`half a metric ton!`) of male silkworm pupae (*Bombyx mori*)** down to **`25 milligrams` of pure white crystals** — isolating the first insect steroid hormone, **`ecdysone`** (`and in 1960, Ulrich Clever and Peter Karlson saw ecdysone make giant salivary chromosomes puff up under a microscope within 15 minutes — proving for the first time how steroid hormones switch genes on, 060, 075!`)!

### PART 04: 5 March 2008 — Douglas Blackiston, Elena Silva Casey & Martha Weiss's Caterpillar-Memory Experiment (4:50–6:45)
- **Cold open (how do you ask a moth whether it remembers something that happened before its body dissolved into liquid soup inside a chrysalis?):** **Georgetown University, Washington, D.C. (5 March 2008)**.
- **5 March 2008 (*PLOS ONE*) — Training a Caterpillar With Nail-Polish Odour (`Ethyl Acetate`):**
  - Since the 1920s, biologists had debated whether any learned memory (`050`) could survive complete metamorphosis, because earlier tests forgot a fatal flaw: what if leftover traces of the chemical smell simply stuck to the outside of the pupa shell and trained the emerging butterfly *after* it hatched?
  - At Georgetown University, **Douglas Blackiston**, **Elena Silva Casey**, and **Martha R. Weiss** (born 1959) designed a rigorous test using **tobacco hornworm caterpillars (*Manduca sexta*)** and a two-arm Y-tube wind tunnel:
    - They blew a puff of **ethyl acetate** (`the fruity smell of nail-polish remover, 056`) paired with a mild **`7-volt` electric tickle** (`which makes the caterpillar squirm, without harming it`).
    - Untrained caterpillars didn't mind the smell (`choosing the ethyl-acetate arm ~50% of the time`), whereas trained fifth-instar caterpillars learned to **avoid the ethyl-acetate tunnel `78%` of the time**!
  - Next, the trained caterpillars sealed themselves into pupae, **washed clean of any chemical trace**, and spent weeks undergoing complete metamorphosis into winged **Carolina sphinx moths**!
  - When Blackiston and Weiss placed the **adult moths** into the Y-tube with no shocks at all, **`77%` of the adult moths still fled from the ethyl-acetate smell** (`compared to ~46%–50% in untrained control moths and moths whose pupae were washed in ethyl acetate`)!
- **Why Only Older Caterpillars Remember — The Surviving `Mushroom Body` (`Corpora Pedunculata`):**
  - Even more telling: caterpillars trained when they were **too young (`third instar`)** forgot the smell as moths, whereas caterpillars trained in their **final fifth instar** remembered it for life!
  - Why? Because as neuroanatomists traced under the microscope, the insect brain's learning and smell-memory centre — the **`mushroom bodies` (`corpora pedunculata`)** — forms its mature core neurons in the late caterpillar: while their outer branches (`dendrites and axons`, `033`) prune back during pupal soup and sprout fresh connections to the adult butterfly's antennae, **those core memory neurons survive the chrysalis alive and keep their synaptic memory intact**!
