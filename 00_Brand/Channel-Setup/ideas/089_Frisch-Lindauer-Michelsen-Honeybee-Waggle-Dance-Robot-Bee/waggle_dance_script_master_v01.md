Why would wild meadows burst with blue, yellow, and violet blossoms if the insects visiting them could only see in black and white?

In **nineteen ten**, the powerful Munich eye doctor **Carl von Hess** declared that all insects and fish are completely colour-blind. Twenty-seven-year-old Austrian biologist **Karl von Frisch** didn't believe him! In **nineteen fourteen**, von Frisch trained honeybees to sip sugar water from a **blue card**, then slipped a clean, unscented blue card into a checkerboard of **thirty different shades of grey paper**. If bees only saw brightness, they would land on grey cards too — instead, every bee flew straight to the **blue card** (`proving bees see colour, including ultraviolet bullseyes invisible to human eyes`)!

To see how scouts alert the hive, von Frisch built a flat **glass-walled hive** and invented a five-colour paint code that numbered bees from **one to five hundred and ninety-nine**. In **nineteen twenty-three**, with feeders just ten metres away, he saw nectar bees dance in a circle (`the round dance`) and assumed the figure-eight **`waggle dance`** only meant "pollen". Then in **Summer nineteen forty-four and nineteen forty-five** at **Brunnwinkl**, he moved his sugar dish **hundreds of metres away** — and cracked the code (`published in nineteen forty-six`): beyond fifty to a hundred metres, the **duration of the central waggle run tells the distance** (`about one second per kilometre`), while on the dark vertical comb **straight up stands for the Sun** (`so waggling sixty degrees right of vertical means "fly sixty degrees right of the Sun"`)!
