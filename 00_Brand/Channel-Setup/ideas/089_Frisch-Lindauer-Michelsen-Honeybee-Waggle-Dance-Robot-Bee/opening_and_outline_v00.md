### PART 02: 1914–1923 & Summer 1944–1946 — Karl von Frisch's Painted Bees, the Glass Hive & How Gravity Becomes the Sun — Midpoint Twist (1:25–3:10)
- **Cold open ("Case closed — insects are tiny, colour-blind robots that find flowers purely by stumbling across their scent! Wrong! In 1914 in Munich, a 27-year-old Austrian biologist proved honeybees see colours with a checkerboard of grey paper — and thirty years later, by moving a dish of sugar water across an Austrian valley in Summer 1944, he discovered the only symbolic language known in any insect on Earth!"):** **Munich & Brunnwinkl, Austria, 1914–1946**.
- **1914 (*Zoologische Jahrbücher*) — How Karl von Frisch proved honeybees see colour (`including ultraviolet!`):**
  - Around 1910, Munich ophthalmologist **Carl von Hess** declared that fish and all invertebrates — including honeybees — are **completely colour-blind** (`which made no sense to young Karl von Frisch, 1886–1982: why would flowers evolve brilliant blue, yellow, and violet petals if pollinators only saw shades of grey?`).
  - In **1914**, von Frisch trained foraging bees to sip sugar water from a **blue card**, then placed a clean, unscented blue card randomly inside a checkerboard of **30 different shades of grey paper** (`from pure white to jet black`). If bees were colour-blind, they would confuse the blue card with a matching shade of grey — instead, every bee flew **straight to the blue card**! (He also proved bees cannot see pure red, which looks black to them, but can see **ultraviolet light**, `018`, `049`, revealing hidden UV bullseye nectar guides on flower petals!)
- **1920–1923 vs Summer 1944–1946 (*Experientia* 1946) — The 20-year mistake & the Midpoint Twist (`The Waggle Dance Code`):**
  - To watch what a returning scout bee does inside the hive, von Frisch built a flat **glass-walled observation hive** and devised a five-colour paint code (`dots on the front and back of the thorax and abdomen that let him number individual bees from 1 to 599!`).
  - In **1920–1923**, placing sugar dishes just **10 metres** from the hive, he saw returning bees perform a circular **`round dance`** (`which meant "search nearby!"`), while pollen-carrying bees from distant meadows performed a figure-eight **`tail-wagging dance` (`waggle dance`)**. For twenty years, von Frisch assumed the round dance meant "nectar" and the waggle dance meant "pollen"!
  - Then in **Summer 1944 and 1945** at his country home in **Brunnwinkl** on Lake Wolfgang, von Frisch moved his sugar-water dishes **hundreds of metres (`up to 1,000+ metres`) away** — and discovered his 20-year mistake:
    1. **Distance:** For any food more than **50 to 100 metres** away, nectar bees switch from the round dance to the **figure-eight `waggle dance`**, and the **slower and longer the central waggle run lasts, the farther away the flowers are** (`roughly 1 second of waggling per kilometre of flight`)!
    2. **Direction in pitch darkness (`Sun-to-Gravity transposition`):** Inside a dark hive on a **vertical honeycomb**, bees cannot see the Sun — so the dancing bee **replaces the Sun with gravity**!
       - Waggling **straight up (`0°`)** means: *"Fly straight toward the Sun!"*
       - Waggling **straight down (`180°`)** means: *"Fly straight away from the Sun!"*
       - Waggling **`60°` to the right of straight up** means: *"Fly `60°` to the right of the Sun!"*

### PART 03: 1949–1955 — The Polarized-Sky Compass, the 15-Degree-per-Hour Clock & Martin Lindauer's Swarm Democracy (3:10–4:55)
- **Cold open (how can a honeybee know the exact position of the Sun when the Sun is completely hidden behind a mountain or a cloud — and how do ten thousand homeless bees hanging from a tree branch hold a democratic election to pick their new home?):** **Munich & Graz, 1948–1955**.
- **1948–1949 (*Experientia*) — The Ultraviolet Polarized-Sky Compass & the Internal Clock (`061`):**
  - Von Frisch noticed that even when the Sun was blocked by a mountain peak (`the Schafberg at Brunnwinkl`) or clouds, bees still danced the **exact angle to the hidden Sun** as long as a `10°` patch of blue sky was visible!
  - In **1948–1949**, using a sheet of **Polaroid filter**, von Frisch proved that the ultraviolet photoreceptors in a honeybee's compound eye (`8 ommatidia arranged like a pinwheel`) read the **polarization pattern of scattered sunlight** across the sky (`when he rotated the Polaroid sheet above a horizontal comb, the bee instantly rotated her dance by the exact same angle!`).
  - Even more astonishing, in **1950–1954**, **Martin Lindauer** (1918–2008) watched "marathon dancers" that danced inside a windowless dark hive for hours without going outside: as the hours passed, the bee **steadily shifted her dance angle by `15 degrees per hour` (`matching Earth's rotation, 027`)**, guided by her brain's **24-hour circadian clock (`061`)**!
- **1951–1955 (*Zeitschrift für vergleichende Physiologie*) — Martin Lindauer's House-Hunting Swarm Democracy:**
  - In **Spring 1951–1955** in Munich, Lindauer paint-numbered hundreds of scout bees on a **homeless honeybee swarm** hanging from a bush and realized their waggle dances were advertising **not flowers, but hollow trees and wall cavities**!
  - Over 3 to 5 days, Lindauer tracked scouts advertising **up to 20 different candidate nest sites**:
    - A scout who found a mediocre, drafty cavity performed a brief, sluggish waggle dance (`~15 circuits`); a scout who found a dry, spacious hollow tree performed a vigorous dance (`hundreds of circuits`)!
    - Crucially, scouts **visited their rivals' proposed sites**, compared them honestly, and **switched their vote** to dance for the superior site — until the entire swarm reached a unanimous **quorum (`democracy`)** and lifted off together to fly to their new home (`later studied with miniature video cameras by Thomas Seeley`)!

### PART 04: 1989 — The 20-Year Odor-Plume Controversy & How Axel Michelsen's Mechanical Robot Bee Proved the Dance Language (4:55–6:45)

  - In **1989** (*Naturwissenschaften*), Danish biophysicist **Axel Michelsen** (born 1940), **Wolfgang Kirchner**, and **Martin Lindauer** built the world's first working **mechanical robot honeybee**:

    - A **single piece of razor blade** mounted on the back as a wing, vibrated by an electromagnet in **`280-Hz` pulses (`30 pulses per second`)**;
    - A **computer-controlled stepper motor** that swung the brass bee in a figure-eight at any chosen **angle to gravity** and any chosen **waggle duration**, while a tiny plastic tube dispensed a drop of unscented sugar water!
