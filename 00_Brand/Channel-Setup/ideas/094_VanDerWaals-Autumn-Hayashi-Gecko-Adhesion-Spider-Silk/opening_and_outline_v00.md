### PART 02: 14 June 1873 & 1930 — Johannes Diderik van der Waals's Equation & Fritz London's Quantum Electron Flicker (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open ("In 1873, physics textbooks taught that neutral gas molecules were like tiny billiard balls that never pulled on one another unless they carried an electric charge! Then on 14 June 1873 at Leiden University, a 35-year-old former schoolteacher named Johannes Diderik van der Waals defended a thesis that made James Clerk Maxwell learn Dutch just to read it!"):** **Leiden, Netherlands (14 June 1873) & Berlin (1930)**.
- **14 June 1873 (*Over de Continuiteit van den Gas- en Vloeistoftoestand*) — Van der Waals's `a/V²` Attraction:**
  - Why can gases like oxygen (`017`) or helium (`037`) be cooled and compressed into liquids? If neutral molecules never attracted each other, no gas could ever condense into a drop of liquid!
  - On **14 June 1873**, **Johannes Diderik van der Waals** (1837–1923, `1910 Nobel Prize in Physics`) modified the ideal gas law into `(P + a/V²)(V - b) = RT`, proving that every molecule has a real volume (`b`) and experiences a **universal short-range attractive force (`a`)** whenever two molecules come within a few billionths of a metre of each other!
- **1930 (*Zeitschrift für Physik*) — Fritz London's Quantum Dispersion Force:**
  - Why do two completely neutral, uncharged molecules attract each other?
  - In **1930**, German quantum physicist **Fritz London** (1900–1954, `037`) used the brand-new quantum mechanics of electrons (`004`) to solve the puzzle: even in a neutral atom, the electron cloud is constantly shimmering! When electrons momentarily slosh to one side of Atom A, they create a fleeting **instantaneous dipole** that induces a synchronized opposite dipole in neighbouring Atom B — pulling the two atoms together (**`London dispersion / van der Waals force`**)!
  - Individually, one van der Waals bond is **50 to 100 times weaker than a covalent chemical bond**, and it vanishes if the gap widens past **2 nanometres** (`falling off with the sixth power of distance, 1/r⁶`) — unless an animal finds a way to bring **billions of molecular tips** within one nanometre of a surface at the exact same time!

### PART 03: 1934, 1965 & 2000–2002 — Kellar Autumn's Single-Gecko-Hair Sensor & the 30-Degree Peel Trick — Midpoint Twist (3:05–4:50)
- **Cold open ("How does a gecko stick to a polished glass window? Glue? No — gecko toes are bone dry! Suction cups? - **1965 (*J. Morphol.*) & 8 June 2000 (*Nature*) — Measuring One Microscopic Hair (`200 Micronewtons`):**
  - In **1965**, **Rodolfo Ruibal** and **Valerie Ernst** placed a gecko toe pad under an **electron microscope** and discovered a breathtaking fractal forest: each toe ridge (`lamella`) carries thousands of keratin bristles (**`setae`**, `~100 micrometres` long), and each single seta branches at its tip into **100 to 1,000 flat, spoon-shaped nano-pads called `spatulae`** (`each just 200 nanometres wide`)!
  - On **8 June 2000** (*Nature* 405: 681–685), biomechanist **Kellar Autumn**, **Robert Full**, **Ronald Fearing**, and **Thomas Kenny** isolated a **single tokay gecko seta** and pressed it against a dual-axis silicon micro-electromechanical (`MEMS`) force sensor:
    - Simply pushing the seta straight into the sensor produced almost **no stickiness at all**;
    - Yet when they pressed the seta down **and pulled it parallel by just 5 micrometres** (`the exact tiny tug a gecko's foot makes as it steps!`), the single hair locked on with **`200 micronewtons` of force** — **ten times stronger** than whole-animal measurements had predicted!
    - And when they tilted the hair shaft past **`30 degrees`**, the spatulae popped off with **zero measurable detachment force** (`which is why a gecko curls its toes upward like a party blower before lifting its foot, peeling off in just 15 milliseconds without ever getting stuck!`).
- **The Midpoint Twist — 27 August 2002 (*PNAS*): Capillary Water vs Pure Van der Waals Attraction:**
  - Even after the **2000** *Nature* paper, one rival theory remained: what if gecko toes stuck using **microscopic water bridges (`capillary adhesion`, `087`)** from humidity in the air, which would fail on water-hating (`hydrophobic`) surfaces?
  - On **27 August 2002** (*PNAS* 99: 12252–12256), **Kellar Autumn** and colleagues tested living tokay geckos and single setae on **water-hating (`hydrophobic`) gallium arsenide (`GaAs`) semiconductor wafers** vs **water-loving (`hydrophilic`) silicon dioxide glass** (`and forged synthetic polymer nano-tips of two different materials`): **both stuck with the exact same force (`~0.4 newtons per square millimetre`)**, proving that gecko adhesion depends on **200-nanometre geometry** and **100% van der Waals intermolecular forces**!

### PART 04: 1990–29 March 2001 — Randall Lewis, Cheryl Hayashi, Fritz Vollrath & the Nanocrystal Architecture of Spider Silk (4:50–6:45)

    2. **Stretchy `glycine-rich` spirals (`GGX / GPGXX`)** that uncoil and absorb shock (`giving rubber-like elasticity`).
