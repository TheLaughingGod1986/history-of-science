### PART 02: The Pinhole Camera & the Two Shapes of DNA (1:25–2:55) — `awe` → `explain`
- **Cold open (quirky anecdote before the hard crystallography):** **Paris, 1947–1950**, then **London, January 1951**. Before coming to King's College London, **Rosalind Franklin** spent four happy years in Paris learning X-ray diffraction on the dirtiest substance imaginable — **coal and charred graphite** — discovering why some coals turn into porous molecular sieves while others turn into crystal graphite (work that even helped classify wartime gas-mask filters!).
  - When she arrives at **King's College London** in **January 1951**, lab director **John Randall** assigns her to X-ray DNA fibres originally prepared by **Maurice Wilkins** (1916–2004), who is away on holiday — and a fateful misunderstanding leaves Wilkins thinking Franklin was hired as his assistant, while Franklin has been told the X-ray DNA project is hers and PhD student **Raymond Gosling's** alone.
  - **The mechanism (Explainer diagram on period paper):** DNA is far too small to see under any light microscope. Instead, you shine a needle-thin beam of X-rays (wavelength ~1.54 ångströms) through a bundle of DNA fibres pulled from calf thymus (using a bent paperclip!) and catch the scattered rays on film.
  - Why had every previous X-ray of DNA looked blurry? Franklin discovers that **DNA changes its shape when it drinks water!** At **75% relative humidity**, DNA shrinks into a dry, boxy crystalline form she names the **A-form**. But when she bubbles hydrogen gas through a salt solution to raise the chamber to **92% relative humidity**, water molecules latch onto the outer sugar-phosphate chains, stretching the fibre by **about 25%** into a long, wet, regular spiral she names the **B-form**!
  - Separating A and B for the first time, Franklin and Gosling expose a single B-form fibre for **100 hours** on **2–6 May 1952** to produce **Photograph 51** — where the stark black `X` is the unmistakable fingerprint of a helix, and the missing fourth rung (`n = 4` layer line) shows the two grooves of the spiral are unevenly spaced!
  - *Markers:* `· WHEN: Paris, 1947–1950 · WHEN: London, January 1951 · WHO: Maurice Wilkins (1916–2004) — King's College biophysicist · DATA: inked two-state humidity diagram on period paper showing dry A-DNA (75% relative humidity, shorter crystalline form) stretching by ~25% into wet B-DNA (92% relative humidity, paracrystalline helix) as water molecules bind the outer phosphate groups · SCALE: 1.54-ångström X-ray wavelength — 3,500 times shorter than green visible light · FACT: Studied coal before DNA`
- **Ends on:** If Franklin already had the sharpest X-ray of DNA on Earth in May 1952, why did she lock *Photograph 51* in a drawer — while two rivals in Cambridge tried to guess the structure with tin plates and cardboard?

### PART 03: The Inside-Out Blunder (2:55–4:20) — `awe` → `explain`
- **Cold open:** **November 1951**, the Cavendish Laboratory, Cambridge. Twenty-three-year-old American biologist **James Watson** (born 1928) and thirty-five-year-old British physicist **Francis Crick** (1916–2004) hate waiting for slow, hundred-hour X-ray calculations; they want to crack DNA the way California chemist **Linus Pauling** (1901–1994) had just cracked the alpha-helix of proteins in **spring 1951** — by building scale models out of brass rods and tin plates!
  - **Rivalry beat (back-and-forth cuts between King's College London and Cavendish Cambridge):** After Watson attends Franklin's King's College colloquium on **21 November 1951** without taking a single written note, he misremembers her water-content numbers by nearly a factor of ten!
  - Within days, Watson and Crick proudly invite Wilkins and Franklin up from London on **28 November 1951** to admire their first model: a **three-stranded helix with the sugar-phosphate backbones jammed together on the inside** and the nitrogen bases pointing outward.
  - Franklin demolishes it in minutes: First, the negatively charged phosphate groups repel each other unless neutralised by water. Second, her humidity measurements show wet DNA holds **ten times more water** than Watson remembered — meaning the water-loving (**hydrophilic**) sugar-phosphate backbones *must* sit on the **outside** of the molecule, with the water-shunning bases tucked on the inside!
  - Humiliated, Cavendish chief **Sir Lawrence Bragg** (1890–1971) bans Watson and Crick from working on DNA and orders them to hand their metal model moulds over to King's College.
  - Then, in **January 1953**, Linus Pauling sends his son Peter in Cambridge an advance manuscript claiming *he* has solved DNA at Caltech — and when Watson and Crick open it with trembling hands, they discover Pauling has made the **exact same three-stranded inside-out mistake**, forgetting that phosphate groups in water are ionised! They have only weeks before Pauling realises his error.
  - *Markers:* `· WHEN: Cambridge, November 1951 · WHO: James Watson (born 1928) — American postdoctoral biologist · WHO: Francis Crick (1916–2004) — Cavendish doctoral physicist · WHO: Linus Pauling (1901–1994) — Caltech chemistry professor · WHEN: 21 November 1951 · WHEN: 28 November 1951 · WHO: Lawrence Bragg (1890–1971) — Cavendish laboratory director · WHEN: January 1953 · FACT: Bragg banned their model-building`
- **Ends on:** Watson and Crick were still barred from doing X-ray experiments of their own. So what happened on the afternoon of 30 January 1953 when James Watson took the train down to London?

### PART 04: The Drawer Opens — Midpoint Twist (4:20–5:50) — `explain` → `awe`
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **30 January 1953**, King's College London. Preparing to leave King's for Birkbeck College to escape the tense atmosphere, Franklin is methodically calculating the mathematical Patterson function of the dry A-form of DNA before turning back to her B-form plate.
  - **The unapproved disclosure:** On **30 January 1953**, Watson visits King's to show Pauling's flawed paper. In the corridor after a tense encounter with Franklin, **Maurice Wilkins** leads Watson into the next room, opens a drawer, and hands him **Photograph 51** — which Raymond Gosling had given Wilkins as part of the handover — **without telling Rosalind Franklin**.
  - Years later, Watson wrote: *"The instant I saw the picture my mouth fell open and my pulse began to race."* On the train back to Cambridge, Watson sketches the stark black `X` from memory on the blank margin of his newspaper: a helix with a **34-ångström repeat** and **10 steps per turn** (`3.4 ångströms` apart).
  - **The second leak (mid-February 1953):** Still missing the exact symmetry of the chains, Crick asks his supervisor **Max Perutz** (1914–2002) for a copy of an internal **December 1952 Medical Research Council (MRC) committee report** written by the King's group. Inside, Franklin had recorded that DNA crystals belong to the **face-centred monoclinic space group $C2$** — a geometry that instantly tells Crick that DNA consists of **two chains running in opposite directions** (one up, one down — **antiparallel**)!
  - *Markers:* `· WHEN: London, 30 January 1953 · WHEN: February 1953 · WHO: Max Perutz (1914–2002) — Cambridge molecular biologist · DATA: inked diagram on period paper showing Franklin's C2 monoclinic crystal symmetry (two antiparallel sugar-phosphate backbones running in opposite directions on the outside, rotating 180° around a dyad axis) · SCALE: 3.4 ångströms between rungs — 3 million rungs stacked end-to-end in a single millimetre · FACT: Shown without Franklin's permission`
- **Ends on:** They now had the outside rails of the twisted ladder and knew one ran up while the other ran down. How did the four chemical letters lock together as the rungs in the middle?

### PART 05: Cardboard Cut-Outs & the Secret of Copying (5:50–7:05) — `awe`
- **Cold open:** **27–28 February 1953**, Cambridge. For a week, Watson tries to pair identical bases together (`adenine` with `adenine`, `cytosine` with `cytosine`) — but the rungs bulge unevenly and refuse to fit inside Franklin's uniform **20-ångström** cylinder!
  - **Two crucial clues:**
    1. First, Austrian-American biochemist **Erwin Chargaff** (1905–2002) had proved in **1950** that in every species on Earth, the amount of **adenine ($A$)** always equals **thymine ($T$)**, and **guanine ($G$)** always equals **cytosine ($C$)**.
    2. Second, on **27 February 1953**, American crystallographer **Jerry Donohue** (1920–1985), sharing Watson and Crick's office, glances at Watson's textbook drawings and laughs: the textbook has drawn guanine and thymine in the wrong hydrogen arrangement (*enol* instead of *keto* tautomers)!
  - On the morning of **Saturday, 28 February 1953**, Watson cuts out four flat **cardboard shapes** in the right *keto* form and slides them across his desk. Suddenly, two-ring **adenine ($A$)** locks with one-ring **thymine ($T$)**, and two-ring **guanine ($G$)** locks with one-ring **cytosine ($C$)** — forming two rungs of the **exact same width and angle**!
  - Better still: if you unzip the two strands down the middle, each strand is a direct mould for building its partner — explaining in a single stroke how cells copy their genetic code! That lunchtime at **The Eagle pub** in Cambridge, Crick announces they have *"found the secret of life."*
  - *Markers:* `· WHEN: 1950 · WHO: Erwin Chargaff (1905–2002) — Columbia University biochemist · WHEN: 27–28 February 1953 · WHO: Jerry Donohue (1920–1985) — American crystallographer · DATA: animated timeline building 1869 (Miescher isolates nuclein) → 1944 (Avery proves DNA carries genes) → May 1952 (Franklin & Gosling's Photograph 51) → 30 Jan 1953 (Wilkins shows Photo 51) → 28 Feb 1953 (Watson & Crick base-pairing) → 25 Apr 1953 (three Nature papers) · SCALE: 3 billion base-pair rungs in every copy of your genome · FACT: Solved with cardboard cut-outs`
- **Ends on:** When *Nature* published the breakthrough on 25 April 1953, why did the world think Rosalind Franklin had merely confirmed someone else's discovery?

### PART 06: The Draft Dated 17 March & After-Act (7:05–8:05) — `explain` → `awe`
- **Cold open (the big idea & misconception explained away):** For decades after **James Watson's** bestselling **1968** book *The Double Helix*, popular myth claimed that Rosalind Franklin was so stubbornly opposed to helices that she couldn't read her own photograph. Was that true?
  - **No:** decades later, historians found Franklin's own **unpublished draft manuscript dated 17 March 1953** — written *before* she ever learned of Watson and Crick's model! In it, she had already worked out from *Photograph 51* that B-DNA is a **two-chain coaxial helix** with its phosphate groups on the outside and **ten units per turn**. She was only one step (base pairing) behind them, working entirely from real experimental evidence.
  - When the three papers appeared together in *Nature* on **25 April 1953**, Watson and Crick's one-page model came first (acknowledging only that they had been *"stimulated by a knowledge of the general nature of the unpublished experimental results and ideas"* at King's), while Franklin and Gosling's paper featuring **Photograph 51** was printed third — making her foundational data look like an afterthought.
  - After moving to Birkbeck College and doing pioneering work on the polio and tobacco mosaic viruses, **Rosalind Franklin died of ovarian cancer on 16 April 1958 at just thirty-seven years old** — never knowing that Watson and Crick had seen *Photograph 51* and her MRC crystal numbers before building their model. Four years later, in **December 1962**, Watson, Crick, and Wilkins received the **Nobel Prize** (which forbade posthumous awards). Yet every time a cell in your body unzips its three billion rungs, it follows the geometry first caught on a four-inch square of film in a London basement.
  - **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:05):**
    - Every cell in your body burns fuel to copy those three billion rungs — and that fuel requires a gas you breathe in seventeen thousand times a day. Yet when an English minister first trapped pure **oxygen** under a glass bell jar with a burning lens and a mouse in **1774**, why did he go to his grave insisting that oxygen didn't exist (`017` candidate *Oxygen & the Phlogiston Myth: Priestley & Lavoisier*)?
