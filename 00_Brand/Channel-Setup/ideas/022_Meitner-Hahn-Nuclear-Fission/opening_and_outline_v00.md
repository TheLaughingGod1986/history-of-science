### PART 02: The Wooden Basement, the Escape and the Barium Mystery (1:25–3:05)
- **Cold open (quirky anecdote):** **Berlin, 1907–1938**. When Austrian physicist **Lise Meitner** (1878–1968) first arrived in Berlin in **1907** to work with chemist **Otto Hahn** (1879–1968), women were banned from Prussian university laboratories — so Meitner had to work in a converted **carpenter's basement workshop** with a separate street entrance and walk to a restaurant down the street just to use a toilet!
- Over the next thirty years, Meitner and Hahn became scientific equals at the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute. After **James Chadwick** discovered the uncharged **neutron** in **1932** and **Enrico Fermi** (1901–1954) bombarded uranium (element **92**) with neutrons in Rome in **1934**, Meitner, Hahn and chemist **Fritz Strassmann** (1902–1980) spent four years trying to identify the mysterious new radioactive substances left behind — assuming they were heavier "transuranic" elements beyond 92.
- **The flight from Nazi Germany (13 July 1938):** After Nazi Germany annexed Austria in **March 1938**, Meitner — of Jewish heritage — lost her Austrian protection and was forbidden to leave Germany. On **13 July 1938**, with tip-offs that her arrest was imminent, Meitner fled across the Dutch border in secret with two small suitcases, **ten marks** in her purse, and Otto Hahn's mother's **diamond ring** in case she had to bribe a border guard, settling in Stockholm!
- **The baffling Berlin experiment (17–19 December 1938):** Five months later, guided by Meitner's letters and a puzzling report from **Irène Joliot-Curie** in Paris, Hahn and Strassmann bombarded uranium with slow neutrons on **17 December 1938** and tried to separate the tiny radioactive product using **barium** (element **56**) as a chemical carrier.
- No matter what chemical test they ran, the mystery substance refused to separate from barium — because **it WAS barium**!
- On the night of **19 December 1938**, Hahn wrote a desperate letter to Meitner in Stockholm: *"Perhaps you can come up with some sort of fantastic explanation. We knew ourselves that it can't actually burst into barium."*
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Berlin, 1907 · WHO: Enrico Fermi (1901–1954) — Italian physicist · WHEN: 1934 · WHEN: 13 July 1938 · FACT: Fled with 10 marks · WHEN: 19 December 1938 · DATA: inked atomic-number scale comparing Uranium (Z = 92, mass 238/235) against Radium (Z = 88) and Barium (Z = 56, mass ~139 — 36 protons lighter than Uranium) · SCALE: chipping 36 protons off a uranium nucleus in one hit — like tossing a pebble at a boulder and watching the boulder split cleanly in half`
- **Ends on:** Every physicist's equations said a tiny slow neutron had nowhere near enough energy to crack a uranium nucleus into barium. What did Lise Meitner see in the snowy woods of Sweden on Christmas Eve that everyone else had missed?

### PART 03: The Liquid Drop in the Snow and $E = mc^2$ (3:05–4:55)
- **Cold open:** **Kungälv, Sweden, Christmas Eve, 24 December 1938**. Lonely in exile, sixty-year-old Lise Meitner travels to the small coastal town of **Kungälv** near Gothenburg for Christmas and is joined by her physicist nephew **Otto Robert Frisch** (1904–1979), visiting from **Niels Bohr's** (1885–1962) institute in Copenhagen.
- Over breakfast, Meitner shows Frisch Hahn's baffled letter about **barium**. At first Frisch says it must be a mistake — but Meitner knows Hahn and Strassmann are too good at chemistry to mistake barium for anything else.
- **The walk in the snow:** They step outside into the snowy woods — Frisch on cross-country skis, Meitner walking briskly beside him through the snow.
- Sitting on a **fallen tree trunk** in the woods with pencils and scraps of paper, Meitner recalls **Niels Bohr's** **liquid-drop model** of the nucleus: what if a heavy uranium nucleus isn't a brittle rock at all, but an **overcrowded, quivering drop of liquid**?
- Inside a uranium nucleus sit **92 positively charged protons**, all fiercely repelling one another electrically and barely held together by short-range nuclear surface tension. When just **one slow neutron** drops in, the liquid drop wobbles and stretches into a **waisted dumbbell** — until the electric repulsion pushes the two halves apart and the drop **snaps in two** (`56 protons of Barium + 36 protons of Krypton = 92 protons of Uranium`)!
- **Calculating the energy in her head ($E = mc^2$):** First, Meitner calculates the electric repulsion between a barium nucleus and a krypton nucleus touching each other: they will fly apart with **200 million electron-volts (`200 MeV`)** of energy!
- Where could such a huge energy come from? Meitner remembers the packing-fraction mass table in her head: the two smaller nuclei together weigh slightly **less** than the original uranium nucleus — by about **one-fifth of the mass of a single proton**. Plugging that missing mass into **Albert Einstein's** (1879–1955) famous **1905** formula **$E = mc^2$**, one-fifth of a proton's mass converts into **200 million electron-volts** — an exact match!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Kungälv, 24 December 1938 · WHO: Niels Bohr (1885–1962) — Danish theoretical physicist · WHO: Albert Einstein (1879–1955) — German-born theoretical physicist · WHEN: 1905 · DATA: inked liquid-drop & $E = mc^2$ calculation diagram on period paper showing 1 uranium nucleus + 1 neutron → dumbbell drop → Barium ($Z=56$) + Krypton ($Z=36$), where missing mass $\Delta m \approx 0.2$ proton mass $\times c^2 = 200\text{ MeV}$ · SCALE: one-fifth of a single proton's mass vanishes — and turns into enough energy to hurl the two atomic halves apart at a thirtieth of the speed of light · FACT: Solved on a snowy log`
- **Ends on:** Meitner and Frisch had solved how the atom splits on a tree trunk in Sweden. Yet why did Otto Hahn win the Nobel Prize completely alone — and how did Frisch prove the split in a laboratory in Copenhagen just days later?

### PART 04: The Pulse in Copenhagen and the Missing Nobel — Midpoint Twist (4:55–6:40)
- **Cold open ("Case closed? Wrong."):** **Copenhagen & Stockholm, January 1939 – 1945**. Returning to Copenhagen on **3 January 1939**, Otto Frisch tells **Niels Bohr**, who slaps his own forehead and cries: *"Oh, what idiots we all have been! Oh, but this is wonderful! This is just as it must be!"*
- **The experiment & the word "fission" (13 January & 11 February 1939):** On **13 January 1939**, Frisch lines an ionization chamber with uranium in Copenhagen and fires neutrons at it: giant electrical pulses shoot across the oscilloscope screen — direct physical proof of the **200 MeV** flying fragments! Asking an American biologist in the hallway (**William Arnold**) what biologists call a living cell dividing in two (`015`), Frisch coins the name **"nuclear fission"** in his and Meitner's landmark *Nature* paper published on **11 February 1939**!
- **The midpoint twist — erased by exile:** Yet back in Nazi Berlin, Otto Hahn had published the chemical discovery of barium in *Die Naturwissenschaften* on **6 January 1939** with only his and Strassmann's names — because putting a Jewish exile's name on a German paper was politically impossible.
- Worse still, to protect his institute under the Nazi regime, Hahn downplayed Meitner's thirty-year partnership and claimed the discovery belonged to chemistry alone. When the Royal Swedish Academy awarded the delayed **1944 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** (announced in **November 1945**), **Otto Hahn received it alone** — while Lise Meitner, the physicist who led the Berlin project and explained nuclear fission itself, was left out despite being nominated **49 times**!
- **History's verdict (1966 & 1997):** Meitner refused to work on the atomic bomb at Los Alamos, declaring, *"I will have nothing to do with a bomb!"* In **1966**, she, Hahn and Strassmann shared the US Enrico Fermi Award, and in **1997** element **109** on the periodic table (`002`) was officially named **Meitnerium (`Mt`)** in her honour — making her the first non-mythological woman to have an element named after her alone!
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: Copenhagen, 3 January 1939 · WHEN: 6 January 1939 · WHEN: 13 January 1939 · WHEN: 11 February 1939 · FACT: Coined "fission" from biology · WHEN: November 1945 · WHEN: 1997 · FACT: Nominated 49 times, 0 Nobels`
- **Ends on:** When people see $E = mc^2$, why do so many think nuclear fission destroys the protons and neutrons themselves into pure energy — and how did scientists tame that runaway chain reaction to power cities without an explosion?

### PART 05: What $E = mc^2$ Really Converts & the Chain Reaction (6:40–8:15)
- **Cold open (popular misconception debunked):** Many people think splitting an atom annihilates protons or neutrons into pure energy. Is that true?
- **No:** count the particles before and after nuclear fission, and **not a single proton or neutron disappears** (`92 protons in Uranium = 56 in Barium + 36 in Krypton`, and all 236 nucleons are still there!). Instead, the smaller nuclei bind their protons and neutrons slightly more tightly, releasing **0.1% of the atom's total mass** (binding energy) as kinetic heat according to $E = mc^2$.
- And because each splitting Uranium-235 atom also spits out **2 or 3 fresh neutrons**, **Enrico Fermi** was able to stack graphite blocks and cadmium control rods beneath a stadium in Chicago on **2 December 1942** (`Chicago Pile-1`) to absorb extra neutrons and hold a gentle, steady **chain reaction** — the exact principle inside every nuclear power plant generating carbon-free electricity today!
- **After-act hand-off (7:45–8:15):**
  - And how do nuclear power plants turn the heat of splitting uranium atoms into electricity for millions of homes? By boiling water into steam to spin a turbine — the exact mechanical-to-heat conversion that **Count Rumford** discovered while boring brass cannons with horses in Munich in **1798** and **James Prescott Joule** measured with a falling-weight paddle wheel in **1845** (`023` candidate *Thermodynamics: Rumford's Cannon & Joule's Paddle Wheel*, or `023` *Becquerel & Marie Curie's Radioactivity*).
- *Markers:* `· WHEN: 2 December 1942 · WHEN: 1798 · WHEN: 1845 · DATA: animated timeline building 1905 (Einstein's E = mc²) · 1934 (Fermi bombards uranium with neutrons) · 13 Jul 1938 (Meitner flees Nazi Germany) · 17 Dec 1938 (Hahn & Strassmann find barium in Berlin) · 24 Dec 1938 (Meitner & Frisch solve nuclear fission in the snow at Kungälv) · 11 Feb 1939 (Nature paper coins "fission") · 2 Dec 1942 (Fermi's first controlled reactor) · 1997 (Element 109 named Meitnerium) · SCALE: zero protons or neutrons destroyed — releasing just one-thousandth (0.1%) of the atom's binding mass powers an entire city`
