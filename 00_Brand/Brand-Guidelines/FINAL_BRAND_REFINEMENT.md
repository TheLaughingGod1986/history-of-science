- Tone: wonder, intelligence, warmth — not clickbait or conspiracy

# 1. Banner optimisation

## Live line review: “BIG QUESTIONS. BIGGER UNIVERSE.”

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Curiosity | ★★★★☆ | “Bigger” creates scale lift — but feels like a punchline, not a destination. |
| Explains channel | ★★☆☆☆ | Says *mood*, not *space / astronomy / science stories*. |
| Premium | ★★★☆☆ | Fine; slightly ad-copy vs editorial. |
| Mobile | ★★★☆☆ | Two short lines work IF inside YouTube safe band (centre ~1546×423). |

**Verdict:** Strong enough to ship early — but **not** the final brand slogan. It competes with the better philosophy line. Align them.

## Five improved alternatives

### A — `BIG QUESTIONS.` / `DEEP UNIVERSE.`

- **Why it works:** Exact philosophy line. One brand system across banner, About, trailer, end cards.
- **Emotional impact:** Calm awe. Editorial, not hype.
- **SEO impact:** Indirect (brand memory). Pair with About keywords.
- **Mobile:** Excellent — parallel structure, equal weight.

### B — `SMALL ROBOT.` / `INFINITE UNIVERSE.`

- **Why it works:** Instant character + scale contrast. IP-forward.
- **Emotional impact:** Charm + wonder (Pixar hook).
- **SEO impact:** Low topic clarity.
- **Mobile:** Excellent — short words.

### C — `EXPLORING THE BIGGEST QUESTIONS` / `IN THE UNIVERSE`

- **Why it works:** Clearest channel promise in plain language.
- **Emotional impact:** Inviting, adult, purposeful.
- **SEO impact:** Strong topical (“universe,” “questions”).
- **Mobile:** Weaker — longer; risk of crop if not carefully sized.

### D — `SPACE STORIES.` / `BIG QUESTIONS.`

- **Why it works:** Topic + promise. Helps first-time visitors understand category.
- **Emotional impact:** Friendly, clear, less poetic.
- **SEO impact:** Strongest of the five for “space.”
- **Mobile:** Excellent.

### E — `ONE LITTLE ROBOT.` / `THE BIGGEST QUESTIONS.`

- **Why it works:** Storybook contrast; memorable IP sentence.
- **Emotional impact:** Highest warmth / character attachment.
- **SEO impact:** Weak topic keywords.
- **Mobile:** Good if type is large enough.

## Final banner recommendation

**Ship alternative A:**

```
BIG QUESTIONS.
DEEP UNIVERSE.
```

**Layout (unchanged structure):**  
Left — warm galaxy · Centre — two-line cream slogan (TV-safe) · Right — Orbit · Small **ORBIT** wordmark · **No “with Ben”** on banner.

**Optional after 30 days if discovery is soft:** add one micro safe-zone line under slogan:  
`Space · Astronomy · Cosmic mysteries`

**Do not** keep both “Bigger” and “Deep” in active circulation — pick **Deep** as the eternal line.

# 2. Logo and brand hierarchy

## Hierarchy

| Layer | Form | Role |
|-------|------|------|
| **Primary IP** | **ORBIT** | Wordmark, character, colour, universe |
| **Channel / legal** | Orbit with Ben | YouTube display + @OrbitWithBen |
| **Philosophy** | Big questions. Deep universe. | About, trailer, end cards, merch tags |
| **Creator credit** | with Ben / Ben Oates | Secondary — never visual co-equal |

## How large ORBIT should appear

| Surface | ORBIT size | “with Ben” |
|---------|------------|------------|
| Banner | Small wordmark (planet-O), not competing with slogan | **Omit** |
| Avatar | None (face only) | Omit |
| Title / mission card | Dominant display | Omit or micro |
| Thumbnail | Rare as text; prefer character | Never |
| End card | Large ORBIT + Orbit figure | Micro or omit |
| Merch | ORBIT primary | Fine print only |
| Spoken | “Orbit” / “Orbit with Ben” interchangeable early | Credit Ben in About |

**Rule:** If you remove the nav/channel name and the first viewport still feels like ORBIT, hierarchy is correct.

## When to use “with Ben”

| Use | Don’t use |
|-----|-----------|
| YouTube display name | Banner hero type |
| About footer | Thumbnail wordmark |
| Contracts, press, About credit | Equal-weight lockup with ORBIT |
| Spoken “I’m Ben, and this is Orbit…” | Merch chest print |

## Thumbnail branding

- Default: **no logo text**
- Orbit appears when emotion/curiosity needs a face (≈40–60% of thumbs)
- Corner Orbit watermark in-video only (150×150), not on thumbs

## Goal state

Orbit becomes a **media property**. Ben is the author/creator. YouTube is one distribution surface.

# 6. Video identity system

Every long-form film follows this spine:

## 0–10s — Opening (hook)

1. Visual mystery (planet, void, signal, structure) — no logo wall  
2. One sharp spoken question or paradox line  
3. Optional Orbit eye-take (≤2s) — not full PiP wallpaper  

**Job:** Stop the scroll. Promise a *question*, not a lecture.

## Introduction — Meet the guide

- Orbit appears briefly (wave / hover)  
- Spoken or title: mission identity (`MISSION 001`) + episode question  
- Signature optional: soft “Another question awaits.”  

**Job:** Brand recognition without slowing the story.

## Story — Science explanation

- Picture-locked to VO (mystery plates > generic starfields)  
- Orbit used sparingly as reaction / transition guide  
- Wonder > conspiracy; claim only what science supports  

**Job:** Earn trust and awe.

## Ending — Reflection + curiosity

- Quiet visual hold (stars / Earth / void)  
- Reflective beat: what we know / what we don’t  
- CTA soft: like & subscribe as *join the next mission*  
- Lock line: **Another question awaits.** + ORBIT end card  

**Job:** Emotional residue + series continuity.

# 7. Episode branding — Mission format

## Naming

```
MISSION 001
Could We Ever Meet Aliens?
```

| Element | Spec |
|---------|------|
| Code | `MISSION` + zero-padded number (001, 002…) |
| Title | Question form preferred (curiosity + SEO) |
| On-screen | Cream type on deep space; orange underscore or ring accent |
| Duration | 2–4s title card after hook |

## Typography

- Mission code: wide tracking, small caps or letterspaced sans (system display — not Inter as “brand face”; prefer distinctive geometric or soft sci-fi display already in brand kit)  
- Episode title: larger, high contrast cream  
- Avoid dense newspaper layouts and purple-glow UI  

## Animation style

- Slow push or gentle star drift behind type  
- Orbit may enter from edge *after* title resolves — not competing with type  
- No frantic motion; no sticker explosions  

## Sound design

- Soft sub “whoom” into title  
- Single bright antenna chime optional on Orbit entrance  
- Music bed: low, cinematic, unresolved — resolve at outro  

## Transitions

- Cross-dissolves and motivated camera moves  
- Hard cuts only on idea turns  
- Never loop the same cutscene as filler

# 8. Thumbnail system

## Rules (mobile-first)

1. **One idea** — if you need a caption to explain two things, cut one.  
2. **Huge focal point** — face, planet, or object must read at ~150px wide.  
3. **3–5 words max** on thumb (or none). Question fragments beat statements.  
4. **High contrast** — cream/orange vs deep space; avoid muddy midtones.  
5. **No logo sticker** unless testing; Orbit = character, not badge.  
6. **Emotion first** — curiosity, awe, unease (gentle), never gore/fearbait.

## When Orbit appears

| Include Orbit | Omit Orbit |
|---------------|------------|
| Reaction / wonder needed | Object alone is iconic (black hole, Mars) |
| Soften a heavy topic | Thumb already crowded |
| Series recognition boost | A/B test object-only |

Orbit: large eyes toward subject or camera; never tiny corner mascot spam.

## Example frames

| Video | Composition | Text | Orbit? |
|-------|-------------|------|--------|
| **Aliens** | Nightside exoplanet city lights · tiny distant craft | `ARE WE ALONE?` | Optional curious Orbit left |
| **Black hole** | Event horizon curve + accretion glow | `NOTHING ESCAPES?` | Omit or tiny rim silhouette |
| **Mars** | Rust horizon + Earth as pale star | `HOME AWAY?` | Orbit looking to horizon |
| **AI future** | Human city lights → circuit constellation morph | `WHAT COMES NEXT?` | Orbit mid-frame, thoughtful |
