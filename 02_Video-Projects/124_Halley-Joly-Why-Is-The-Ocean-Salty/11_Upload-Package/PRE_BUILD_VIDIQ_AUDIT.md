## 1. The Discovery Story (Person, Date & Twist — UNVERIFIED)

- **Familiar phenomenon:** Boil one litre of seawater dry in a pan and about **`35 grams` of white salt** (`~7` level teaspoons) remain behind — yet the river water pouring into the ocean every day tastes completely fresh. Why is the ocean salty, and why hasn't it turned into solid brine over billions of years?
- **Person, place & date (UNVERIFIED — to be checked against primary sources in `SOURCES.md`):**
  1. **Edmond Halley (Royal Society of London, `1715`, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond.* `29(344): 296–300`, *"A Short Account of the Cause of the Saltness of the Ocean, and of the Several Lakes That Emit No Rivers; with a Proposal, by Help Thereof, to Discover the Age of the World"* — UNVERIFIED):** Notices that closed inland lakes with no river outlet (the Caspian Sea, the Dead Sea / *Lacus Asphaltites*, the Lake of Mexico) are intensely salty while lakes with an outlet stay fresh. He reasons that rivers continually wash tiny traces of dissolved mineral salt from weathered continental rock into the ocean while solar evaporation removes only pure water vapour — and proposes measuring how fast ocean salinity rises to calculate the **age of the world** (regretting that ancient Greek and Roman observers never left seawater salinity measurements to compare against).
  2. **John Joly (Trinity College Dublin, `17 May / September 1899`, *Sci. Trans. R. Dublin Soc.* Ser. 2, Vol. 7, pp. 23–66, *"An Estimate of the Geological Age of the Earth"* — UNVERIFIED):** Carries out Halley's calculation using global ocean-volume estimates and Sir John Murray's 19th-century river-discharge chemical tables. Dividing the total mass of **sodium ($\text{Na}$)** in the world ocean (`~1.41–1.56 × 10^16 tonnes`) by the annual mass of sodium delivered by all rivers (`~1.57 × 10^8 tonnes/yr`), Joly calculates that the ocean — and therefore Earth's crust and water cycle — is roughly **`80 to 100 million years old`** (`~89–99.4 Myr` uncorrected, `~80–90 Myr` after allowances).
  3. **The Midpoint Twist — The Ocean Is Not an Accumulating Tub (`1952–1958`, Thomas F. W. Barth `1952`, Clair C. Patterson `1953/1956`, Edward D. Goldberg & Gustaf Arrhenius `1958` *Geochim. Cosmochim. Acta* `13: 153–212` — UNVERIFIED):** Radiometric uranium-lead dating of meteorites proves Earth is **`~4.55 billion years old`** (`~50 times older` than Joly's salt clock!). Why did Joly's sum fail? Because the ocean is in **chemical steady state**: salt leaves the sea as fast as rivers bring it in (via evaporite salt basins left by dried-up seas, wind-blown sea spray, pore-water burial, and clay reverse weathering). What Joly actually measured was not the age of the Earth, but the **`~50–68 million-year` oceanic residence time of sodium**!
  4. **The Missing Half of the Salt Budget (`17 February 1977–1979`, Galápagos Rift DSV *Alvin* hydrothermal vents — John B. Corliss et al. `1979` *Science* `203: 1073–1083` & John M. Edmond et al. `1979` *Earth Planet. Sci. Lett.* `46: 1–18` — UNVERIFIED):** Geochemists still couldn't balance where the rivers' dissolved **magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$)** and **sulphate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$)** went — until the `1977` *Alvin* dive on the Galápagos Rift discovered deep-sea **hydrothermal vents**, proving that the entire volume of the world ocean circulates through hot mid-ocean ridge volcanic crust every few million years, stripping out magnesium and sulphate while releasing calcium and locking the ocean's salinity at a steady `~35 g/L`.
