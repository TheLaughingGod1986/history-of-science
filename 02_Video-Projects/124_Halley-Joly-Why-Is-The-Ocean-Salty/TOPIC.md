## 2. The film

### 2.1 Story (person, date, twist) — UNVERIFIED
- **Open (first 15 s):** one litre of seawater boiled dry in a pan leaves about 35 grams of salt. Rivers taste fresh. So where does the salt come from?
- **1715, Edmond Halley:** notices that lakes with no outlet (the Caspian, the Dead Sea) are salty, reasons that rivers bring dissolved salts and evaporation leaves them behind, and proposes measuring the sea's salt over time to find the age of the world.
- **1899, John Joly (Dublin):** does the sum. Sodium in the ocean divided by sodium delivered by rivers each year gives roughly 80 to 100 million years for the age of the Earth.
- **The twist:** the clock is wrong. The sea is not getting saltier; salt leaves as fast as it arrives (salt beds from dried-up seas, sea spray, reactions with the seabed). Radiometric dating later puts the Earth at about 4.5 billion years.
- **1977 onward:** hot vents on the sea floor (found from the submersible *Alvin*) turn out to add some elements and strip out others, the missing half of the salt budget.
- **Close:** the salt in the sea is rock, dissolved by rain over hundreds of millions of years, held at a steady level.
- **Candidate DATA moments:** 35 g per litre; Joly's sum as a running counter; 90 million against 4.5 billion years on one scale bar; a timeline 1715 → 1899 → 1977.
