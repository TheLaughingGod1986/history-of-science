## 2. The film

### 2.1 Story (person, date, twist) — UNVERIFIED
- **Open (first 15 s):** one litre of seawater boiled dry in a pan leaves about 35 grams of salt. Rivers taste fresh. So where does the salt come from?
- **1715, Edmond Halley:** notices that lakes with no outlet (the Caspian, the Dead Sea) are salty, reasons that rivers bring dissolved salts and evaporation leaves them behind, and proposes measuring the sea's salt over time to find the age of the world (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond.* 29(344): 296–300, UNVERIFIED).
- **1899, John Joly (Dublin):** does the sum. Sodium in the ocean divided by sodium delivered by rivers each year gives roughly 80 to 100 million years for the age of the Earth (*Sci. Trans. R. Dublin Soc.* Ser. 2, 7: 23–66, UNVERIFIED).
- **The twist:** the clock is wrong. The sea is not getting saltier; salt leaves as fast as it arrives (salt beds from dried-up seas, sea spray, reactions with the seabed — what Joly measured was the ~50–68 million-year residence time of sodium in a steady-state ocean, Barth 1952 / Goldberg & Arrhenius 1958, UNVERIFIED). Radiometric dating later puts the Earth at about 4.5 billion years (Patterson 1956, UNVERIFIED).
- **1977 onward:** hot vents on the sea floor (found from the submersible *Alvin*, Corliss et al. 1979 / Edmond et al. 1979, UNVERIFIED) turn out to add some elements and strip out others (magnesium and sulphate), the missing half of the salt budget.
- **Close:** the salt in the sea is rock, dissolved by rain over hundreds of millions of years, held at a steady level.
- **Candidate DATA moments (each paired with `SCALE:` in script stage, UNVERIFIED):** `35 g` salt per litre of seawater (`SCALE: about seven level teaspoons of salt in every one-litre bottle of seawater`); Joly's 1899 river-sodium into ocean-sodium sum as a running counter (`SCALE: rivers carry weak dissolved rock that tastes completely fresh, yet across the whole planet they dump hundreds of millions of tonnes of salt into the sea every year`); `80–100 million years` (Joly's salt clock / sodium residence time) against `4.55 billion years` (true Earth age) on one scale bar (`SCALE: Joly's salt clock stopped after just one-fiftieth of Earth's history because the ocean drains salt out the bottom as fast as rivers pour it in the top`); an animated timeline `1715 → 1899 → 1953–1958 → 1977`.
