What really happened in Benjamin Franklin's kite experiment — and why was the kite never struck by lightning?
In June 1752 a silk kite, an iron key and a glass Leyden jar proved that lightning is electricity, and gave the world the lightning rod.

For thousands of years, lightning set roofs on fire and church steeples were struck again and again. Benjamin Franklin noticed that the spark from a Leyden jar looked, sounded and smelled like a tiny thunderstorm. So he wrote a list of twelve likenesses, and five famous words: let the experiment be made.
