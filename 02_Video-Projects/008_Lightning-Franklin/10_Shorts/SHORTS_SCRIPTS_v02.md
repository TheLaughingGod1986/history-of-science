## Short A — the kite was never struck (lead, the Friday after the long airs, 11:30 UK)

**YouTube title (test B, search-worded, autocomplete-checked 9 Oct):** *Franklin's Kite Experiment: Was It Hit by Lightning?*


- **Frame 0:** the silk kite already climbing into a dark storm cloud, wet string glinting (long Part 03 plate `kite_climb`).
- **Hook caption:** NEVER **STRUCK**
- **Change by 1 s:** a tiny blue spark jumps from the key to a knuckle.

| 18–24 | calm teach card: charge down the string, no bolt; back to the kite (loop) | If lightning had struck the kite, he would almost certainly have died. |

**Script (60 words):**

Franklin's kite was never hit by lightning. In seventeen fifty-two he flew it into a storm cloud, holding a dry silk ribbon inside a shed. The loose threads of the twine stood up on end. He brought his knuckle to the key, and a spark jumped across. If lightning had struck the kite, he would almost certainly have died.

*Fact check:* Priestley (1767) for the shed, threads and knuckle spark; *Pennsylvania Gazette*, 19 Oct 1752 for the silk ribbon; "never struck" as in the long and FACT_NOTES. No death shown.
