## VO (spoken · British · upbeat curiosity)

Centuries before the word germ meant what it means to you, lenses cracked open a secret ocean.

A drop of pond water is not empty. It is a bustling kingdom — darting shapes, spinning cells, life with no face and no name on the hospital chart.

Look closer. Light bends through glass and paints a spectrum across the slide. Suddenly the world has a second population living under your nose — a civilisation of cells dancing in a bead of liquid.

But seeing is not the same as blaming.

People could watch microbes dance and still insist disease came from foul smells, angry gods, or imbalance. The microscope showed a tiny world. It did not yet rewrite the hospital.

What would you believe — your eyes in a lens, or the story your century repeats?

The story said: clean the air. Burn herbs. Open windows. Chase the stench. Why question a tale that feels sensible when the room smells wrong?

However, a sensible story can still be false. And a false story can still feel like medicine — until the data arrives like a slap.

The truth was waiting for a harder kind of proof: not that life exists in drops, but that these living passengers cause this sickness — and that we can stop them.
