## 1. The Discovery Story (Person, Date & Twist — UNVERIFIED)

- **Familiar phenomenon:** Why zebras have bold black-and-white stripes across the open African savannah.
- **Person, place & date (UNVERIFIED — verify exact quotes in `SOURCES.md`):**
  - **Charles Darwin** (`1809–1882`, *The Descent of Man*, **1871**) & **Alfred Russel Wallace** (`1823–1913`, *Tropical Nature* **1878** & *Darwinism* **1889**): debate why zebras evolved high-contrast stripes, launching 140 years of competing hypotheses (predator camouflage in tall grass, motion dazzle, social recognition, convective air cooling, and biting-fly deterrence).
  - **Ádám Egri, Miklós Blahó, Susanne Åkesson & Gábor Horváth** (Hungary / Lund, **February 2012**, *Journal of Experimental Biology* `215(5): 736–745`): demonstrate using sticky oil-coated trays and full-sized horse models that blood-seeking female tabanid horseflies are guided by horizontally polarised light and strongly avoid narrow black-and-white stripes (`attracting fewer flies than even pure white models`).
  - **Tim Caro, Amanda Melin & Martin How** (UC Davis / University of Calgary / University of Bristol, **2014–2020**, *Nature Communications* `5: 3535`, *PLOS ONE* `11(1): e0145679` & `14(2): e0210831`, *J. Exp. Biol.* `223: jeb222133`): map all **7 extant wild equid species and 20–21 subspecies** (`2014`, proving striping tracks biting-fly abundance rather than lions, woodland cover, temperature, or herd size), prove lions and spotted hyenas cannot resolve stripes beyond **`~50 m` in daylight, `~30 m` at twilight, or `~9 m` on a moonless night** (`2016`), and dress `7` domestic English horses in **black, white, and zebra-striped cloth coats** (`2019`, Dundry, North Somerset, UK) to film horseflies failing to decelerate within the final `~0.5 m` and bouncing right off the stripes (`reducing coat landing rates by >75%`, while landings on the horses' uncovered heads remained unchanged).
