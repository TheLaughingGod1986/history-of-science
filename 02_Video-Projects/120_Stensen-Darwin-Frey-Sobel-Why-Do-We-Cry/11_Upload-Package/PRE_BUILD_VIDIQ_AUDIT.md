## 1. The Discovery Story (Person, Date & Twist — UNVERIFIED)

- **Familiar phenomenon:** Every land mammal makes tears to keep its eyes wet and wash out dust, yet humans are the only species on Earth that sheds tears from emotion—and a December 2023 Weizmann Institute study revealed that human emotional tears carry an odourless chemical signal that switches off aggression in other people.
- **Person, place & date:**
  - **Niels Stensen (Nicolaus Steno)** (Leiden, **1662**, *Observationes anatomicae*) & **Charles Darwin** (Down House, **November 1872**, *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals*, Chapter VI): Stensen dissects the lacrimal gland ducts above the eye, disproving the ancient belief that tears well straight from the brain or heart; two centuries later, Darwin compares weeping infants and animals worldwide and declares human emotional tears an evolutionary mystery beyond basal lubrication and reflex irritation.
  - **William H. Frey II** (St. Paul-Ramsey Medical Center, Minnesota, **1981**, *American Journal of Ophthalmology* `92(4): 559–567`, `"Effect of stimulus on the chemical composition of human tears"`): collects tears in glass test vials from volunteers watching tragic films (*Brian's Song*, *The Champ*) vs volunteers slicing raw onions, discovering that emotional tears carry roughly **20% to 24% higher protein concentration** than irritant onion tears.
  - **Shani Gelstein & Noam Sobel** (Weizmann Institute of Science, Rehovot, **January 2011**, *Science* `331(6014): 226–230`, `"Human tears contain a chemosignal"`): run a double-blind smell experiment comparing women's sad-movie tears against saline trickled down the same women's cheeks, discovering that even though the liquids have zero detectable odour, sniffing emotional tears lowers male salivary testosterone and dampens hypothalamic brain activity.
  - **Shani Agron, Claire A. de March, Hiroaki Matsunami & Noam Sobel** (Weizmann Institute of Science & Duke University, **21 December 2023**, *PLOS Biology* `21(12): e3002442`, `"A chemical signal in human female tears lowers aggression in males"`): prove that sniffing human emotional tears cuts human male aggression by **43.7%** in a double-blind behavioural assay, activates **four specific human olfactory receptors (`OR2J2`, `OR2W1`, `OR5A1`, `OR11H6`)** out of `62` tested, and strengthens fMRI connectivity between the olfactory cortex and prefrontal aggression-control regions.
