## PART 01

In Hartford, Connecticut, a dentist called Horace Wells saw a laughing gas show in December eighteen forty-four. The next day, he breathed the gas himself, and had a colleague pull out one of his own teeth. He felt nothing.

Wells was thrilled. A few weeks later, he went to a famous hospital in Boston to show the doctors there.

It went wrong. The gas wasn't enough, and the patient said it hurt. The students in the room called it a humbug. A fake.

But Wells's old business partner was in that room. A young dentist called William Morton. And he didn't give up on the idea. He tried a different vapour instead. Ether.

On the sixteenth of October, eighteen forty-six, Morton walked into the same Boston hospital, carrying a glass globe. A young man called Edward Abbott had a lump on his neck. Morton held the globe to his mouth, and Abbott breathed the ether in, and slowly fell asleep.

The surgeon, John Collins Warren, began. Abbott didn't scream. He didn't move. When it was over, Warren is said to have turned to the packed room and told them: "Gentlemen, this is no humbug."

The room where it happened is still there. It's called the Ether Dome. And within weeks, the news was crossing the Atlantic.

A happy ending, then? Case closed? Wrong.
