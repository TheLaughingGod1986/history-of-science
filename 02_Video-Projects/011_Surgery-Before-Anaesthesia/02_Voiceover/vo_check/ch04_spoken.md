## PART 01

On the twenty-first of December, eighteen forty-six, in London, Robert Liston tried ether for himself. His patient slept through the whole operation. When he woke, he is said to have asked when it would begin. It was already over.

Liston was delighted. He is said to have told the room: "This Yankee dodge, gentlemen, beats mesmerism hollow." The fastest knife in London no longer needed to be fast.

The next year, in Edinburgh, a doctor called James Young Simpson went looking for something even better. One evening, he and two friends sniffed a liquid called chloroform at his dinner table, and moments later all three were out cold on the floor. When they woke, Simpson knew he'd found it.

Not everyone trusted it. Then, in eighteen fifty-three, a London doctor called John Snow gave chloroform to Queen Victoria, for the birth of her son, Prince Leopold. If it was good enough for the Queen, people decided, it was good enough for them.

But here's the frightening question. Is it safe to be put to sleep like that? Doesn't it sometimes go wrong?

Today, for a healthy adult, deaths caused by an anaesthetic are very rare. About one in every hundred thousand. Imagine a stadium full of ninety thousand people, all going to sleep for an operation. On average, fewer than one of them would die because of the anaesthetic.

That's because a specially trained doctor, an anaesthetist, stays beside you the whole time. They watch every heartbeat and every breath, and they keep adjusting the medicine, so you stay just asleep enough, and no more.

Why was this so groundbreaking? Because it changed what surgery could be. Once the patient slept, surgeons could slow down, take care, and try operations that nobody would ever have dared before.

But there was still one problem. Many patients slept peacefully through their operations, and then fell ill a few days later. The answer to that would need a microscope, a spray, and a surgeon called Joseph Lister.

A gas, a bag and a globe. Together, they turned surgery from a race into a sleep.
