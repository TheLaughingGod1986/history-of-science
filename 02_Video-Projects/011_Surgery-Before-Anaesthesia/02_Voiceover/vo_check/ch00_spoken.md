## PART 01

Before eighteen forty-six, the best surgeon in the world was simply the fastest one. Why? Because the patient was awake for every second of it, and in Europe and America, nobody knew how to stop the pain.

In London, one surgeon was famous for his speed. His students timed him with their pocket watches, and he could take off a leg in just a few minutes.

Then, in one week in the autumn of eighteen forty-six, all of that changed. In Boston, a dentist held a glass globe to a young man's face, and the young man felt nothing at all.

So how do you put a person safely to sleep, and bring them back again? Who worked it out first? And why did the men who found it spend the rest of their lives arguing about it?

By the end of this film, you'll know how a party trick, a country doctor and a room full of doubters turned surgery from a race into something you could sleep through.

Picture yourself in eighteen forty, about to have an operation. There's no injection, no mask, no sleep. Just a strong drink, perhaps, and a few people to hold you still. What would you want most from your surgeon? Not a gentle touch. Speed.

Surgeons had wished for something better for centuries. And the strange thing is, the answer had already been written down, nearly fifty years earlier.
