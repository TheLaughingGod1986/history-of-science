What was surgery like before anesthesia — and who invented anesthesia? Before 1846, patients in Europe and America were wide awake for every second of an operation, and the best surgeon in London, Robert Liston, was timed by his students with pocket watches.
Then, on 16 October 1846 in Boston's Ether Dome, dentist William Morton held a two-necked glass globe of ether to a patient's face, surgeon John Collins Warren operated without pain, and surgery changed forever.
