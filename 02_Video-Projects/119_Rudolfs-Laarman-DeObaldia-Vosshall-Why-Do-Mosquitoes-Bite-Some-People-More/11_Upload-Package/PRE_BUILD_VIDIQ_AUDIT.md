## 1. The Discovery Story (Person, Date & Twist — UNVERIFIED)

- **Familiar phenomenon:** Why two people can sit side by side on a summer evening while one is bitten dozens of times and the other is barely touched ("mosquito magnets").
- **Person, place & date:**
  - **Willem Rudolfs** (New Jersey Agricultural Experiment Stations, Rutgers University, **June 1922**, *Bulletin 367: 1–23*, *"Chemotropism of Mosquitoes"*) & **Jakob Jan Laarman** (University of Leiden, **1955**, *Acta Leidensia* `25: 1–144`; **1958**, *Trop. Geogr. Med.* `10: 293–305`): Rudolfs tests dozens of isolated chemicals, sweat, sebaceous secretions, and exhaled breath on *Aedes* mosquitoes inside glass assay tubes, identifying **carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$)** and body warmth as the long-range activators that wake mosquitoes up, while Laarman builds a precision **two-stream wind-tunnel olfactometer** proving that even when temperature, humidity, and $\text{CO}_2$ are equalised, an activated mosquito steers toward specific, invisible human skin odours that differ sharply from person to person (later traced in part to **L-lactic acid** by **Fred Acree Jr., R. B. Turner, H. K. Gouck, Morton Beroza & N. Smith**, **1968**, *Science* `161(3848): 1346–1347`).
  - **Matthew DeGennaro, Carolyn S. McBride et al. & Leslie B. Vosshall** (Rockefeller University, **May 2013**, *Nature* `498(7455): 487–491`): use zinc-finger nucleases to mutate the master odorant co-receptor gene **`Orco`** in *Aedes aegypti* mosquitoes — expecting them to become odour-blind to humans — only to discover that in the presence of $\text{CO}_2$, `Orco`-mutant mosquitoes still home in on human skin via a backup ionotropic acid-sensing pathway (**`Ir8a`**, confirmed by **Joshua I. Raji & Matthew DeGennaro**, **2019**, *Current Biology* `29(8): 1253–1262.e7`, alongside **`Ir25a`** and **`Ir76b`**).
  - **Maria Elena De Obaldia, Takeshi Morita, Laura C. Dedmon, Daniel J. Boehmler, Caroline S. Jiang, Emely V. Zeledon, Justin R. Cross & Leslie B. Vosshall** (Rockefeller University, **18 October 2022**, *Cell* `185(22): 4099–4116.e13`, alongside **Margaret Herre, Olivia V. Goldman et al. & Leslie B. Vosshall**, **18 August 2022**, *Cell* `185(17): 3104–3123.e28`): have **64 human volunteers** wear **nylon stockings** over their forearms for **6 hours** a day across a **3-year longitudinal tournament**, cutting the stockings into **5 cm (2-inch) pieces** and pitting them head-to-head in a two-choice olfactometer trap. They discover that **"Subject 33"** was **more than 100 times more attractive** than the two least-attracting volunteers (`Subject 19` and `Subject 28`, and `4×` more attractive than the next highest volunteer) and stayed an unbeatable **"forever mosquito magnet"** for three years because mosquito-magnet skin sebum produces dramatically higher levels of long-chain saturated **carboxylic acids** (`C10–C20`, led by **pentadecanoic (`C15:0`)**, **heptadecanoic (`C17:0`)**, and **nonadecanoic (`C19:0`) acid** across `50` enriched GC×GC-MS molecular features) — and even when scientists knocked out entire families of smell receptors, Herre et al. (August 2022) proved mosquitoes broke the textbook "one-receptor-per-neuron" rule by co-expressing both `Orco` odorant and `Ir` ionotropic acid receptors inside the **very same olfactory sensory neuron** so they can never lose our scent!
