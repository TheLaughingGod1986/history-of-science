## Short A — the sum (lead, Fri 30 Oct 11:30 UK)

- **Frame 0:** close on a wrist, two fingertips on the pulse, a warm red stream already racing up the arm (long Part 01 plate `01_pulse_wrist`, trimmed to the peak).
- **Hook caption:** MORE THAN YOU **HAVE**
- **Change by 1 s:** the stream reaches the glowing heart.

| s | Picture | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | wrist pulse, stream racing to the heart | You pump more blood than you have. |
| 2–5 | Harvey writing at his desk by candlelight | One London doctor proved it with a sum. |
| 5–12 | the page: *2 oz*, *1/8*, *1000* written in turn (Part 03 plates 08–10; numbers checked by hand) | Say the heart holds two ounces. Each beat, an eighth of that goes out. In half an hour, it beats more than a thousand times. |
| 9–14 | **title on screen:** *The Tied Arm That Proved Your Blood Circulates* | |
| 12–17 | jug tower beside the body outline (Part 03 plate 12) | That's more blood than your whole body holds. Food could never make that much. |
| 17–24 | the glowing loop, then back to the wrist pulse (loop) | So it's the same blood, going round and round. |

**Script (63 words):**

You pump more blood than you have. One London doctor proved it with a sum. Say the heart holds two ounces. Each beat, an eighth of that goes out. In half an hour, it beats more than a thousand times. That's more blood than your whole body holds. Food could never make that much. So it's the same blood, going round and round.

*Fact check:* 2 oz × 1/8 × 1,000 = 250 oz, about 7 litres; an adult holds about 5 litres. Harvey's own deliberately small numbers (*De Motu Cordis*, 1628, ch. 9). The film says the same.

## Short B — the tied arm

- **Frame 0:** two hands pulling a linen band tight round a bare upper arm, arm only (long Part 04 plate `03_tie_band`).
- **Hook caption:** ONE TIGHT **BAND**
- **Change by 1 s:** the band snaps tight round the arm.

| s | Picture | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | band pulled tight on the arm | One band proved your blood goes round. |
| 2–8 | Harvey ties it; below the band the hand keeps its natural colour, no red gets past (Part 04 plates 03–04, graded v04) | William Harvey tied it tight round a man's arm. Below the band, the pulse stopped. No blood was getting in. |
| 8–14 | fingers loosen it; the veins swell into blue cords (plates 06, 09) | He loosened it, just a little. The veins below the band swelled up like ropes. |
| 9–14 | **title on screen:** *The Tied Arm That Proved Your Blood Circulates* | |
| 14–20 | cutaway: red in deep, blue home near the skin (plate 10) | So blood goes in through the arteries, and comes home through the veins. |
| 20–24 | the back of a hand, glow moving home; then back to the band tightening (loop) | Look at your hand. That blood is heading home now. |

**Script (65 words, v02 3 Oct 2026: "pale" line fixed per PR #180 desk 5952762841):**

One band proved your blood goes round. William Harvey tied it tight round a man's arm. Below the band, the pulse stopped. No blood was getting in. He loosened it, just a little. The veins below the band swelled up like ropes. So blood goes in through the arteries, and comes home through the veins. Look at your hand. That blood is heading home now.

*Fact check:* Harvey's ligature experiment, *De Motu Cordis* ch. 11; arm only, no wound, no blood on skin.

## Short C — the vessels Harvey never saw

- **Frame 0:** through the microscope eyepiece, a mesh of vessels finer than hairs with blood creeping through, already moving (long Part 05 plate `07_eyepiece_mesh`).
- **Hook caption:** HE NEVER **SAW** THIS
- **Change by 1 s:** the camera pushes into the mesh as the blood moves.

| s | Picture | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | capillary mesh, moving | Harvey never saw the last link. |
| 2–8 | the glowing loop with a dark gap where arteries meet veins (Part 05 plate 01) | He proved your blood goes round, but not how it crosses from arteries to veins. |

| 9–14 | **title on screen:** *The Tied Arm That Proved Your Blood Circulates* | |
| 11–16 | Malpighi at the brass microscope by the Bologna window (plate 04); no animal on screen | Four years later, Marcello Malpighi put a frog's lung under a microscope. |
| 16–21 | the mesh beside a single hair (plate 08) | There it was: a net of vessels finer than hairs. Capillaries. |
| 21–24 | back to the opening mesh (loop) | The last link Harvey never saw. |

**Script (57 words):**

Harvey never saw the last link. He proved your blood goes round, but not how it crosses from arteries to veins. He died in sixteen fifty-seven, still guessing. Four years later, Marcello Malpighi put a frog's lung under a microscope. There it was: a net of vessels finer than hairs. Capillaries. The last link Harvey never saw.

*Fact check:* Harvey died 3 June 1657; Malpighi, *De pulmonibus*, Bologna 1661, frog lung. The Explorer stays out of this Short (he is in the long's Part 05 microscope beat, never at frame 0).
