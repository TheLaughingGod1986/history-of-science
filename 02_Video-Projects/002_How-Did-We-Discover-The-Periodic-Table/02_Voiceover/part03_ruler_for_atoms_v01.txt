Karlsruhe, eighteen sixty. A packed hall. Chemists argue about the size of invisible things.

Stanislao Cannizzaro wins with a method, not poetry. Treat molecules honestly. Derive atomic weights you can repeat. Put the same number on the same element everywhere.

Suddenly the zoo has a ruler.

Cards stop floating and sit on a line of mass. Light metals here. Heavy strangers there. Properties pulse in waves — reactive, calm, reactive again.

If mass is a coordinate, families can become columns. A hole in a column is not failure. It is a prediction.

Could you leave a chair empty on purpose — and call that science?

Empty space can be a claim: something belongs here, with this mass, with this temper.

You are no longer collecting stamps. You are drawing a city plan for matter.
