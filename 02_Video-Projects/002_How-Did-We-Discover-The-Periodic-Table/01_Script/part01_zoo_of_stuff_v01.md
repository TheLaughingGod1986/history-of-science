## VO (spoken · British · upbeat curiosity)

What if the stuff of your world came with a secret seating plan — and most of the chairs were still empty?

Would you risk a century on a map with holes in it? What happens if the missing metal shows up years later, wearing the properties someone wrote down in advance?

We climb inside the moment chemistry stopped being a pile of names and became a pattern you could bet on. This is how we discovered the periodic table — and why the gaps were the cleverest part.

Look around. Iron in a nail. Salt on a table. A flame. The air you are breathing. For a long time the story said the world was built from four ingredients: earth, air, fire, water.

That story feels tidy. It is also a trap. Heat a rock and it does not become fire. Weigh a gas and it has mass. The atmosphere is not one element.

So how do you fight a pile that keeps growing? Alchemists hunted gold and found accidents. Later chemists found more names. The list got longer. The pattern did not.

A zoo with labels is still a zoo. What if the secret is not a new jar — it is a seating plan nobody has dared to draw?
