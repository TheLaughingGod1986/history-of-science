# The Guests Arrive

HOOK
The guests do not arrive as a dream. They arrive as work.

Eighteen seventy-five. Gallium. Soft. Strange. Almost liquid in a warm room. It sits where eka-aluminium was told to sit. The density is close. The chemistry rhymes.

Then scandium. Then germanium. Each one is a verdict: the empty chair was not a bluff.

ESCALATION
What happens to a civilisation that can predict a metal before it is found?

It stops treating discovery as luck. It starts treating discovery as navigation. You do not wander the dark. You walk toward a vacant lot with an address.

But the table is not finished. It never was, in eighteen sixty-nine.

Noble gases crash the party — colourless, almost aloof, a whole family nobody had a column for. The map stretches. A new street appears.

DISCOVERY
Then a sharper ruler. Atomic number. X-ray fingerprints. Wavelengths of light become a barcode. Suddenly the leftover swaps stop looking like cheating. They look like the difference between weight and identity. Mass was the first coordinate. The deeper coordinate is how many protons sit in the nucleus. A photon can read the difference.

PAYOFF
You — right now — live inside that victory every time a battery, a phone, a pill, a steel beam trusts the right element in the right seat.

Think of the salt you ate. The iodine in a wound. The silicon under a chip. The helium that makes a voice go high and silly. Each one sits on a chair someone once left empty on purpose.

The zoo became a city. Once it was a city, you could find the missing houses.

BIGGER QUESTION
If a whole civilisation can hold the pieces of the world and still miss the pattern that seats them… what other table are we staring at — full of names, empty of order — waiting for the next stubborn gap?

Could the next breakthrough be another obvious list we have not dared to leave a chair empty in?

Hold on the last glowing vacancy. Soft return. The next element is already late for dinner.
