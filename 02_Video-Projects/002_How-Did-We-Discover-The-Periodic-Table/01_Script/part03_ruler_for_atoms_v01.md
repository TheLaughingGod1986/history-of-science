## VO (spoken · British · upbeat curiosity)

Karlsruhe, eighteen sixty. Chemists pack a hall and argue about the size of invisible things.

Stanislao Cannizzaro does not win with poetry. He wins with a method. Treat molecules honestly. Derive atomic weights you can repeat. Put the same number on the same element in every country.

Suddenly the zoo has a ruler.

Watch the cards. They stop floating. They sit on a line of mass. Light metals at one end. Heavy strangers further along. Properties begin to pulse in waves — reactive, calm, reactive again — as if matter had a wavelength you could walk.

What happens next is a worldview leaning.

If mass is a coordinate, families might become columns. If families are columns, a hole in a column is not a failure. It is a prediction.

Could you leave a chair empty on purpose — and call that science?

Most people fill gaps because empty space looks like ignorance. The clever move is the opposite. Empty space can be a claim: something belongs here, with this mass, with this temper, and we have not met it yet.

Now the lab changes. You are not collecting stamps. You are drawing a city plan for matter — streets, houses, vacant lots.

Yes — a pamphlet can change a civilisation, because it turns a private hunch into a number two rivals can check.

The table is still unborn. But chaos has an address: bad weights. Fix the weights, and the pattern can finally risk being wrong in public.
