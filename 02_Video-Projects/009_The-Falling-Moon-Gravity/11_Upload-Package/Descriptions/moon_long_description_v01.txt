Why doesn't the Moon fall to Earth? Newton showed that gravity makes the Moon fall — and miss — every second.
An apple, an imaginary cannon on a mountain, and one careful sum: the same pull that drops an apple holds the Moon in the sky.

In sixteen sixty-five a plague closed Cambridge and a young Isaac Newton went home to Woolsthorpe. There he asked how far the Earth's pull reaches — and whether it could reach the Moon.
