Why doesn't the Moon fall to Earth? Isaac Newton's theory of gravity showed that the Moon is falling — and missing — every second.
From an apple and a cannon on a mountain to how Newton proved gravity across sixty Earth-radii (long before the Cavendish torsion balance weighed the Earth in a lab), one pull holds the Moon in the sky.

In sixteen sixty-five a plague closed Cambridge and a young Isaac Newton went home to Woolsthorpe. There he asked how far the Earth's pull reaches — and whether it could reach the Moon.
