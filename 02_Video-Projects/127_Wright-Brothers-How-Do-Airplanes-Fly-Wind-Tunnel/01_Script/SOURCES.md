## Primary & Institutional Sources (Verified Live 24–25 Nov 2026)

1. **NASA Glenn Research Center — *Smeaton Coefficient***  
   URL: `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/smeaton-coefficient/`  
   Backs: Wilbur's 1899 Smithsonian inquiry; John Smeaton's earliest published air-pressure factor (`0.005`, drag in pounds on a 1 sq ft flat plate at 1 mph); Lilienthal's lift table using `0.005`; 1901 glider designed to lift `100 lb` airframe + `150 lb` pilot in a `15 mph` wind at `5°` angle of attack yet barely lifting itself; Wrights' experimental correction to `0.0033` and modern drag-equation derivation `k = 0.00327`.

2. **NASA Glenn Research Center — *Bernoulli and Newton***  
   URL: `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/bernoulli-and-newton/`  
   Backs: Accurate aerodynamic lift physics — both pressure variation across the wing (Bernoulli) and downward turning of airflow (Newton's third law) describe the same aerodynamic force; explicit refutation of the "equal transit time" / "longer path" fallacy (`"In reality, the velocity on the upper surface of a lifting wing is much higher than the velocity on the upper surface that produces an equal transit time"`).

3. **NASA Glenn Research Center — *1901 Wind Tunnel Tests*, *Wright Brothers Aircraft*, *Wind Tunnel*, *Wind Tunnel Models*, and *Lift Balance – Design***  
   URLs:  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/1901-wind-tunnel-tests/`  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/wright-brothers-aircraft/`  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/wind-tunnel/`  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/wind-tunnel-models/`  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-balance-design/`  
   Backs: 1901 glider producing `1/3` of predicted lift due to Smeaton's `0.005` together with flawed Lilienthal lift/drag curves; typical 1901 glides `"frequently up to 300 feet in a single glide"`; open-return wooden-box wind tunnel belt-driven by the Dayton bicycle shop's small gas engine; nearly a month spent perfecting flow-straightening vanes; lift and drag balances built from **bicycle spokes and hacksaw blades**; miniature wings cut, hammered, filed, and soldered from **20-gauge steel (`1/32` inch thick)**; between `100` and `200` preliminary wing models tested in Oct 1901 and `~30` selected for detailed parametric studies.

4. **Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum — *Researching the Wright Way***  
   URL: `https://airandspace.si.edu/explore/stories/researching-wright-way`  
   Backs: May 30, 1899 Smithsonian letter; 1899 `5-foot` (`1.5 m`) biplane wing-warping kite; Otto Lilienthal's 1896 fatal stall and published lift/drag table; 1900 & 1901 gliders; fall 1901 horizontal bicycle-wheel test (`>3×` greater angle than Lilienthal predicted); October–December 1901 Dayton wind tunnel (`as many as 200` preliminary model wing shapes, `nearly 50` formally tested and graphed, `0.005` since the 1700s vs Wrights' `0.0033 for Smeaton's coefficient`); 1902 glider thinner, longer, narrower wings, fixed vertical tail, `"well digging"` turn reversals, and movable single rudder linked to the wing-warping hip cradle; 1903 propulsion (`Ten responded`, none able to meet spec; Charlie Taylor building the `12-horsepower`, 4-cylinder cast-aluminium-crankcase engine in `six weeks`, first tested `February 12, 1903`; `8.5-foot` laminated-spruce rotary-wing propellers driven via figure-eight bicycle chains); 1904 Huffman Prairie first circle (`20 Oct 1904`); 1905 Flyer (`5 Oct 1905`, `30` circles, `39 minutes`, `24.5 miles`).

5. **Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum — *1903 Wright Flyer* (`A19610048000`) & *The Wright Brothers Made History at Kitty Hawk***  
   URLs:  
   - `https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/1903-wright-flyer/nasm_A19610048000`  
   - `https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/wright-brothers-made-history-kitty-hawk`  
   Backs: 1902 glider `700 to 1,000` flights (best `622.5 ft` / `191.5 m` in `26 seconds`); 1903 Flyer specs (`40 ft 4 in` wingspan, `605 lb` empty / `750 lb` gross, `12 hp`, `60-foot` wooden launching rail); December 14, 1903 coin toss and `3.5-second` stall; **December 17, 1903 at 10:35 a.m.** first flight (`12 seconds`, `120 feet` / `36 m`, Orville piloting) and fourth flight (`59 seconds`, `852 feet` / `255.6 m`, Wilbur piloting); wind gust overturning the Flyer after the fourth flight.

6. **National Park Service (Wright Brothers National Memorial) — *1903 — The First Flight***  
   URL: `https://www.nps.gov/wrbr/learn/historyculture/thefirstflight.htm`  
   Backs: Kill Devil Hills / Big Kill Devil Hill dunes on North Carolina's Outer Banks (near Kitty Hawk); Wilbur's quote `"It is possible to fly without motors, but not without knowledge and skill"`; December 14 coin toss & steep climb stall; `27-mph` freezing headwind on December 17, 1903 (`6.8 mph` groundspeed, `34 mph` airspeed); Kill Devil Hills life-saving station volunteers; `10:35` launch (`120 feet`) and fourth flight (`852 feet`, `59 seconds`).

7. **NASA Glenn Research Center — *Aircraft Control – 1902 Glider* & *Propellers***  
   URLs:  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/aircraft-control-1902-glider/`  
   - `https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/propellers/`  
   Backs: Three-axis control definitions (pitch, roll, yaw) achieved first on the 1902 glider; `12-horsepower` engine compared to a typical `6-horsepower` lawn-mower engine (`2×` lawn mower); high-speed (`~350 rpm`) thin counter-rotating pusher propellers eliminating gyroscopic twist.

8. **Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London — John Smeaton (1759)**  
   Citation: John Smeaton (1759), *"XVIII. An experimental enquiry concerning the natural powers of water and wind to turn mils, and other machines, depending on a circular motion."*, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London*, pp. 100–174.  
   DOI URL: `https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1759.0019`  
   Backs: Exact `1759` publication year of Smeaton's wind-pressure table (`142` years before the Wrights' `1901` wind-tunnel correction, supporting `"seventeen fifty-nine"` in script `v02` and `"142-year-old"` in `LAUNCH_PLAN.md` / `PRE_BUILD_VIDIQ_AUDIT.md`, and the exact year John Harrison completed `H4`).

9. **Royal Museums Greenwich — *Marine timekeeper, H4* (`ZAA0037`) & *Longitude found***  
   URLs:  
   - `https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-79142`  
   - `https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/topics/harrisons-clocks-longitude-problem`  
   Backs: Part 05 final handoff — John Harrison's prize-winning brass marine longitude watch (`H4`), begun in `1755` and completed in **`1759`** (`142–146` years — `"a century and a half earlier"` and `"in the very same year Smeaton published his table"` — before `1901–1905`), trialled at sea in `1761` and `1764`.
