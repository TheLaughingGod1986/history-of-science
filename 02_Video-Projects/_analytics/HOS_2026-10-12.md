## 2. What's working and what isn't

**Working:**
1. **Search finds subject-named content.** Half of all views come from search, and they come from the subject words people type: "history of the periodic table", "mendeleev", "gallium", "how x rays work". The periodic table long is the only long with traction, and it has a clear subject phrase plus a TED-Ed neighbour feeding it suggested views. That matches the neighbour lesson (orbit-with-ben lesson 1).
2. **Concrete, visual Shorts hold viewers.**
   - *A flask that proved germs come from outside*: 102% average viewed.
   - *Microbes in a drop of pond water*: 89% viewed, 110 views (the top Short).
   - *How X-rays Were Discovered by Accident*: 67% viewed, with 50% of day-1 views from the Shorts feed.
   - *The periodic table's empty chairs*: 59% viewed.
3. **Last week's few viewers watched longer** (2:01 average, against 1:02 the week before). On 16 views, though, that's noise and not a trend.

**Not working:**
1. **Nothing has gone public since 27 Sep, and views collapsed as a result.** Weekly views went 147 → 65 → 12 (tracker week-on-week). The fix is already scheduled: a Short on Tue 13 Oct, the atom long on Thu 15 Oct, then Shorts on 16, 18 and 20 Oct. Next week's audit should show whether it recovers.
2. **The long open loses viewers.** Losing 45% of viewers by 0:30 is below YouTube's benchmark for similar videos. Shorts reach 52% viewed and longs 22%.
3. **Riddle-titled Shorts don't get watched or found.**
   - *What other table has empty chairs?*: 4 views, 23% viewed.
   - *The first X-ray showed a wedding ring*: 8 views, 32% viewed.
   - *Gallium sat where the table said*: 33% viewed.

   The Shorts that did best by views have search-style titles (*How X-rays Were Discovered by Accident*, *He predicted a metal before it was found*). This backs Test B (search-titled Shorts from 007).
4. **Shorts don't convert to the longs.** Shorts brought 187 views and the longs got 11 suggested views. The Germs and X-rays longs are almost unseen (6 and 2 views).

**Patterns:**
- **Story titles with a person or a contradiction beat topic labels.** "The Con Man Who…", "…stumped scientists for so long", "Scientists Can't Agree…". HOS 005–010 already lean this way (*The Tied Arm That Proved…*, *Why Vaccines Are Named After a Cow*).
- **History of science at TED-Ed length (5–7 min) still pulls about 0.5M on a big channel.** Our 7–9 min window is fine; the open is the problem, not the length.
- **The debate framing is the strongest this month** (SciShow's ancient city, 2.4M). That fits 010, the Alvarez dinosaur story, which was a decade-long fight.
- **Veritasium's Enigma film is the month's outlier** and shows huge demand for history-of-technology stories with a famous object at the centre.
