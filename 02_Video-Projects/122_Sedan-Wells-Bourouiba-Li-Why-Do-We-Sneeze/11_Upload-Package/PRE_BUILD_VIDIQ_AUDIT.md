## 1. The Discovery Story (Person, Date & Twist — UNVERIFIED)

- **Familiar phenomenon:** Everyone sneezes when dust or pepper tickles their nose, and 1 in 4 people sneeze when stepping into bright sunlight—yet it took a 1964 Johns Hopkins sun-sneeze survey, a 2,000-frame-per-second MIT high-speed camera rig, and a June 2021 brainstem knockout study to uncover the single peptide switch that detonates a sneeze and how a turbulent gas cloud carries sneeze droplets 8 metres across a room.
- **Person, place & date:**
  - **Jean Sédan** (Marseille, **1954**, *Revue d'Oto-Neuro-Ophtalmologie* `26: 123–126`) & **Henry C. Everett** (Johns Hopkins Hospital, Baltimore, **May 1964**, *Neurology* `14(5): 483–490`, `"Sneezing in response to light"`): Sédan notices patients sneezing when his bright ophthalmoscope beam strikes their retinas; Everett surveys medical students and patients and proves the **photic sneeze reflex** (`ACHOO` syndrome, **1978**) is an autosomal-dominant trait in **18% to 35% of people**, triggered when an intense optic-nerve pupillary light signal cross-activates the neighbouring trigeminal nerve.
  - **Carl Flügge** (Breslau, **1897**), **William Firth Wells** (Harvard, **1934**, *American Journal of Hygiene* `20: 611–618`), & **J. P. Duguid** (Edinburgh, **1946**, *Journal of Hygiene* `44(6): 471–479`): map airborne respiratory droplets on nutrient agar plates and celluloid slides, showing a single sneeze atomises up to **40,000 to 100,000 droplets**, while establishing the textbook "1-to-2-metre (3-to-6-foot) large-droplet fall rule."
  - **Lydia Bourouiba, Eline Dehandschoewercker & John W. M. Bush** (MIT, Cambridge, Massachusetts, **April 2014**, *Journal of Fluid Mechanics* `745: 537–563`; **2016** *NEJM* `375: e15`; **March 2020** *JAMA* `323(18): 1837–1838`): film human sneezes at **1,000 to 2,000 frames per second** and prove a sneeze is not a 1-metre spray of ballistic drops, but a warm, moist **multiphase turbulent buoyant gas cloud** that traps droplets and carries them **7 to 8 metres (23 to 27 feet)** across a room.
  - **Faping Li, Haowu Jiang, Xiaoying Shen, et al. & Qin Liu** (Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis, **15 June 2021**, *Cell* `184(13): 3504–3518.e15`, `"Sneezing reflex is mediated by a peptidergic pathway from nose to brainstem"`): discover that nasal `MrgprC11+`/`TRPV1+` sensory neurons trigger sneezing exclusively by releasing a single signalling peptide—**Neuromedin B (`NMB`)**—onto **`NMBR+` neurons** in the brainstem's sneeze-evoking region.
