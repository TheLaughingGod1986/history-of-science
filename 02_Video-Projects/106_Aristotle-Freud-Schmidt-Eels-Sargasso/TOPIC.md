## 2. The film

- **People and dates (all to be sourced in SOURCES.md before any script lock; written from memory here, unverified):** Aristotle (4th century BC) concluded eels grow from mud because none had eggs. In 1876 Sigmund Freud, a student in Trieste, dissected about 400 eels looking for male organs and found nothing certain. In 1896 Giovanni Battista Grassi showed that a transparent leaf-shaped sea fish was the eel's larva. From 1904 Johannes Schmidt towed nets across the Atlantic for close to twenty years, following ever smaller larvae, and in 1922 named the Sargasso Sea as the breeding place.
- **Twist:** Schmidt never saw an adult eel there. The first adult European eels were tracked by satellite tag to the Sargasso only in 2022, and still nobody has seen eels spawn in the wild or found their eggs.

### Why it should work

- Resembles our best performers: a question title about a named everyday thing, like the gold Short (1,227 views, 42.5/day) and *How Did John Snow Stop Cholera?* (231 views, 12.3/day); a famous name in an unexpected place (Freud), like *Are Marie Curie's Notebooks Still Radioactive?* (274 views, 16.3/day, 76.9% viewed).
- Search-led title with a bracketed tail, the shape of our fastest-starting long, 007 Vaccine (1.95 views/day on day 2), and of 002 Periodic Table (161 views, 3.4% CTR).
- LEARNINGS rules followed: 1 (positive question naming the subject), 15 and 23 (stakes in a bracketed tail), 26 (search phrase first), 28 (Shorts as curriculum-style questions), 10 (concrete physical facts first: the opening is an eel with no eggs).
