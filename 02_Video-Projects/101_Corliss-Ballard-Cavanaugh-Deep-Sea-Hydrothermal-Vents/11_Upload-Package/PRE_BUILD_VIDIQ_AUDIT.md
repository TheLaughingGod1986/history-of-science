1. **Part 01 (Cold Open, no chapter card): The Lifeless Abyss** — *Alvin*'s headlight cutting through `2,500 m` abyssal darkness onto `2-metre` crimson tube worms (`0:05` answer image, `0:08` title question) → British naturalist **Edward Forbes** (`1843`, Aegean Sea) dredging from HMS *Beacon* and declaring the ocean below `300 fathoms` (`~550 metres`) a dead "Azoic" desert because sunlight dies out by `200 metres` (`WHO: Edward Forbes (1815–1854) — Marine naturalist`, `WHEN: Aegean Sea, 1843`, `DATA #1 + SCALE` at `0:25–0:40`: `Depth chart — Sunlight zone 200 m vs Forbes 550 m limit vs Galápagos Rift 2,500 m · SCALE: Deeper than six Empire State Buildings stacked`).
2. **Part 02: The Microbe That Ate Rotten-Egg Gas** — In `1887` in Strasbourg (and `1890` in Zurich), **Sergei Winogradsky** starves *Beggiatoa* bacteria of light and proves they build sugars in total darkness by oxidising toxic **hydrogen sulphide (`H2S`)** into yellow elemental sulphur droplets—discovering **chemosynthesis** (`WHO: Sergei Winogradsky (1856–1953) — Microbiologist`, `WHEN: Strasbourg, 1887`, `FACT: Bacteria eat rotten-egg gas`). Yet for `90` years, everyone assumes chemosynthesis can feed only microscopic scum.
3. **Part 03: Dive 713 and the Vodka Jars** — Hunting missing volcanic heat on the **Galápagos Rift** (`15–17 February 1977`), **Robert Ballard**'s camera sled *ANGUS* flashes over hundreds of clams at `2,500 m`, and geologists **Jack Corliss** and **Tjeerd van Andel** dive in DSV *Alvin* (`Dive 713`) to find warm shimmering vents and the *"Garden of Eden"* forest of `2-metre` crimson tube worms (`WHO: Jack Corliss (1936–2015) — Marine geologist`, `WHO: Tjeerd van Andel (1923–2010) — Marine geologist`, `WHO: Robert Ballard (born 1942) — Oceanographer`, `WHEN: Galápagos Rift, 1977`, `FACT: Preserved in crew's vodka`, `EXPLORER BEAT #1`).
4. **Part 04: The Chimney That Melted the Thermometer (`380 °C`) and the Worm With No Mouth** — In **April 1979** at `21°N` on the East Pacific Rise, *Alvin* finds black-smoker chimneys whose water melts its plastic thermometer probe before a rebuilt thermocouple records **`380 °C`** (`DATA #2 + SCALE`: `2,500 m / 250 bar pressure / 380 °C vent water vs 327.5 °C molten lead · SCALE: Hot enough to melt lead, yet pressure stops it boiling`, `WHEN: East Pacific Rise, 1979`). Back at the Smithsonian (`1980`), zoologist **Meredith Jones** slices open the giant tube worm (*Riftia pachyptila*) and discovers it has **no mouth, no gut, and no anus** (`WHO: Meredith Jones (1926–1996) — Smithsonian zoologist`, `WHEN: Washington, 1980`, `FACT: No mouth, gut, or anus`).
5. **Part 05: The 23-Year-Old Grad Student Who Saw the Sulphur** — In a `1980` Harvard seminar, `23-year-old` first-year graduate student **Colleen Cavanaugh** spots yellow crystals inside *Riftia*'s trophosome and proves in *Nature* (**July 1981**) that the worm houses **`3.7 billion` chemosynthetic sulphur bacteria per gram of tissue**, delivered hydrogen sulphide and oxygen by its crimson haemoglobin plume (`WHO: Colleen Cavanaugh (born 1953) — Graduate biologist`, `WHEN: Harvard, 1981`, `EXPLORER BEAT #2`, `DATA #3 + SCALE`: animated timeline `1843 -> 1887 -> 1977 -> 1979 -> 1981` + bacterial density counter `3.7 billion bacteria per gram — half the worm's weight`).
