### 2.2 Person, Date & Twist (Historical Discovery Chain + Teach-on-Screen Plan, Refined in `J2473`)
1. **Robert Norman (`1576`/`1581`) & William Gilbert (`1600`, London — *De Magnete*):** After London instrument maker **Robert Norman** (`1576`, published `1581` *The Newe Attractive*) notices that a freshly magnetised compass needle dips downward toward the floor (`~71°` in London), English physician **William Gilbert** (then President of the Royal College of Physicians; appointed Queen Elizabeth I's personal physician in February `1601`) carves a spherical lodestone (`terrella`, "little Earth") in **1600** and moves a tiny pivoted iron needle across its surface—proving that Earth itself is a giant magnet whose field lines run flat at the equator and plunge steeply into the ground toward the poles (`WHO: William Gilbert (1544–1603) — English physician & physicist`, `WHEN: London, 1600`).
2. **Alexander von Middendorff (`1855`) & Stephen Emlen (`1966`, Michigan & Cornell):** In `1855`, Russian zoologist **Alexander von Middendorff** (`1815–1894`) proposes migrating birds might sense Earth's magnetic field (`WHO: Alexander von Middendorff (1815–1894) — Russian zoologist`, `WHEN: St Petersburg, 1855`). Physicists dismiss the idea for over a century because Earth's surface field (`~50 microteslas`, **`100×` weaker than a `5,000 µT` fridge magnet**) imparts ten million times less energy to a molecule than room-temperature thermal jostling (`DATA: Earth's field 50 µT vs fridge magnet 5,000 µT · SCALE: 100 times weaker than a fridge magnet`). 3. **Wolfgang & Roswitha Wiltschko (`1972`, Frankfurt — *Science*):** In a windowless room at Goethe University Frankfurt wrapped in **Helmholtz copper coils**, **Wolfgang and Roswitha Wiltschko** test caged European robins (*Erithacus rubecula*) (`WHO: Wolfgang Wiltschko (born 1938) — Frankfurt zoologist`, `WHO: Roswitha Wiltschko (born 1947) — Behavioral zoologist`, `WHEN: Frankfurt, 1972`). **Twist #1 (*Science*, 7 April 1972):** Reversing horizontal north and south polarity alone does *not* fool the robins—unless the vertical field is also flipped! The birds do not read north vs south polarity like a pocket compass; they read the **inclination dip angle** where magnetic lines meet gravity (`poleward` = steeper dip into the ground, `equatorward` = flatter lines), and in a completely flat horizontal field (`0°` dip) they are lost (`DATA: Dip-angle diagram — horizontal flip vs vertical flip inside Helmholtz coils · SCALE: Tilted at 66° in Frankfurt vs 0° flat at the equator`, `FACT: Birds read angle, not north`).
