On paper, that sounds promising.

So promising that in the nineteen-sixties, astronomer Frank Drake tried to estimate how many communicative civilisations might exist in our galaxy. The Drake Equation isn’t a final answer. It’s a way of lining up our ignorance in a useful order: star formation… planets… life… intelligence… technology… and how long those civilisations last.

Plug in optimistic numbers, and the galaxy should be buzzing. Plug in pessimistic ones, and we might be alone — for practical purposes.

But even the Drake Equation hides a deeper uncertainty: time.

A civilisation that lasts ten thousand years is a spark. One that lasts a million years is a beacon. If technological species are brief candles, then at any given moment the galaxy could be mostly dark — not empty of life… just empty of overlapping radio eras.

That idea alone can turn a crowded galaxy into a lonely sky — without contradicting the maths.
