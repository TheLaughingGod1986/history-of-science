The wood of a tree is built mostly out of thin air.

So where does a tree's weight really come from?

By the end of this film, you'll know how a willow in a pot, a sprig of mint and a sunny window cracked one of nature's oldest secrets, and why it took more than a hundred and fifty years.

For two thousand years, people believed plants ate the soil. The Greek thinker Aristotle taught that roots take a plant's food from the earth, like a mouth. It seemed obvious. But if it was wrong, nobody really knew where wood, or food, or even the air we breathe came from.

Near Brussels, in the first half of the sixteen-hundreds, a doctor called Jan Baptist van Helmont decided to test it. He didn't argue. He weighed.

He would spend five years watching one tree in one pot. What he found broke the old idea, and then led him straight to the wrong answer.
