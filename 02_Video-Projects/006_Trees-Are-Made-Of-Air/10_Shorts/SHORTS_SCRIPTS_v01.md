**Script (53 words, same as test T1):**

A tree is built from thin air. One man spent five years proving the soil wasn't feeding it. He planted a willow in two hundred pounds of dried soil and gave it only water. Five years later the tree weighed a hundred and sixty-nine pounds, and the soil had lost just two ounces.

*Fact check:* van Helmont, *Ortus Medicinae* (1648): 200 lb dried soil, 5 lb willow, rain or distilled water only, five years, 169 lb 3 oz, soil short by about 2 oz. Same as the long.

## Short C — bubbles in the sunlight

- **Frame 0:** a green leaf in a water jar on a sunny sill, silver bubbles already streaming off it (long Part 04 plate).

**Script (59 words):**

Leaves only make oxygen in the light. In 1779, a Dutch doctor called Jan Ingenhousz put fresh leaves in jars of water by a sunny window. Tiny silver bubbles rose from the green leaves. Move the jar into the shade, and the bubbles stopped. Back into the sun, and they started again. Plants run on sunlight.

*Fact check:* Ingenhousz, *Experiments upon Vegetables* (1779); the long, Part 04. The gas was later called oxygen; the opening line uses today's word, as the long does.
