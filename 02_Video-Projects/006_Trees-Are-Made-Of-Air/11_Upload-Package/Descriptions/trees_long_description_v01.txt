A fully grown tree is built almost entirely out of thin air. So where does a tree's weight really come from?
For 2,000 years people believed plants ate the soil, until a willow in a pot, a sprig of mint and a sunny window revealed photosynthesis.

This film is how we found out: van Helmont's five-year willow (169 pounds of tree, two ounces of soil gone), the gas he named and never connected, Priestley's mint that repaired the air (1771), Ingenhousz's bubbles in sunlight (1779), and the carbon Senebier and de Saussure traced to the air.
