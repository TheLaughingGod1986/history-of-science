- **Familiar phenomenon:** How a woodpecker slams its beak into solid wood 20 times a second at `1,200 g` without suffering a concussion or brain damage.
- **Person, place & date:**
  - **Philip R. A. May & Jaroslav Hrdlicka** (UCLA, **1976** *The Lancet* & **1979** *Archives of Neurology*): film an acorn woodpecker at `2,000 frames per second`, measuring `1,200 g` impact decelerations (`~17×` the human concussion threshold) and straight-line trajectories, launching 45 years of textbook and bio-engineering claims (**Lorna Gibson**, 2006; **Yubo Fan & Ming Zhang**, 2011) that spongy cranial bone and the wrap-around hyoid tongue bone act as mechanical **shock absorbers**.
