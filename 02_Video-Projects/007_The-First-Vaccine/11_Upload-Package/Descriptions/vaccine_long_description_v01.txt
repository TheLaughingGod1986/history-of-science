Every vaccine is named after a cow. How was the first vaccine made?
In 1796 a country doctor took cowpox from a milkmaid's hand and put it into a boy's arm, then tested whether smallpox could still touch him.

This film is how we found out: the dairy rumour that milkmaids never caught smallpox, Benjamin Jesty's family in 1774, Edward Jenner's test on James Phipps (14 May and 1 July 1796), the book that spread it (1798), and the word vaccine, from the Latin vacca, cow.
