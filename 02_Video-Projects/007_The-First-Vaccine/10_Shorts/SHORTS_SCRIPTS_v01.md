## Short A — named after a cow (lead, Fri 13 Nov 11:30 UK)

- **Frame 0:** the cow in the meadow already turning to camera, the gold word VACCA writing itself above her (long Part 01 plate `01_cow_vacca`).
- **Hook caption:** NAMED AFTER A **COW**
- **Change by 1 s:** VACCA slides into VACCINE.

| s | Picture | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | cow, VACCA → VACCINE | Every vaccine is named after a cow. |
| 2–6 | Sarah Nelmes shows Jenner her hand (Part 03) | In 1796, a milkmaid caught a mild disease from a cow, called cowpox. |
| 6–12 | Jenner's case-book, 14 MAY 1796; the boy well in the garden | A country doctor gave it to a healthy boy, then showed that smallpox could no longer make him ill. |
| 9–14 | **title on screen:** *Why Vaccines Are Named After a Cow* | |
| 12–18 | the book's title page: VARIOLAE VACCINAE | He named it after the Latin word for cow. Vacca. |

**Script (59 words):**

Every vaccine is named after a cow. In 1796, a milkmaid caught a mild disease from a cow, called cowpox. A country doctor gave it to a healthy boy, then showed that smallpox could no longer make him ill. He named it after the Latin word for cow. Vacca. So every jab you have ever had is named after her.

*Fact check:* Nelmes, Phipps, 14 May and 1 July 1796 (*Inquiry*, Cases XVI–XVII); *variolae vaccinae*, *vacca* = cow; Pasteur extended "vaccine" to all such treatments (1881). "Named after her": the cow, later called Blossom, is not named on screen.

**Script (62 words):**

Milkmaids never caught smallpox. They caught cowpox from the cows instead, a few sore spots and nothing more. And farmers swore that anyone who'd had it was safe from smallpox for life. One country doctor took the rumour seriously, and tested it. He was right, and it became the first vaccine.

*Fact check:* the dairy belief and cowpox's mildness are in FACT_NOTES and the long (Part 02). "Never" is the rumour as stated, not a medical claim; the next line says it had to be tested.

- **Frame 0:** a turning globe with small lights going out one by one (long Part 05 plate `02_globe_lights`).

**Script (61 words):**

Smallpox is gone. Forever. For centuries it killed about three in every ten people it caught. Then, in 1796, one test showed that cowpox could protect you from it. Health workers spread that idea to every country, until smallpox had nowhere left to hide. In 1980, it was declared gone. The first disease humans ever wiped out.

*Fact check:* about 30% case fatality (variola major; WHO); *Inquiry* Case XVII (1796); WHO declaration 8 May 1980. Both lines must be in FACT_NOTES before VO (they're on the long's new-claims list).
