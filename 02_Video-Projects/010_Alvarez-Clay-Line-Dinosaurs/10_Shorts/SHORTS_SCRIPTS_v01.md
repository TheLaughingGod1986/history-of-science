## Short A — the line of clay (lead, Fri 4 Dec 11:30 UK)

- **Frame 0:** the camera already pushing in on the thin dark clay band in the Gubbio cliff, a fingertip resting on it (long Part 01 plate `clay_line`).
- **Hook caption:** THE **LINE**
- **Change by 1 s:** the lens reveals the shells below the line.

| s | Picture | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | finger on the clay line | This thin line of clay marks the end of the dinosaurs. |
| 2–6 | the gorge at Gubbio, wide | It's in a cliff in Italy, about a centimetre thick. |
| 6–12 | lens: shells below, almost none above | Below it, the rock is full of tiny fossil shells. Above it, almost all of them are gone. |
| 9–14 | **title on screen:** *What Killed the Dinosaurs Was Hidden in a Line of Clay* |  |
| 12–19 | the iridium spike drawn on a notebook page | In nineteen eighty, scientists found the clay was packed with a rare metal from space. |
| 19–24 | calm dust veil over a forest, then back to the clay line (loop) | Something huge had hit the Earth. |

**Script (60 words):**

This thin line of clay marks the end of the dinosaurs. It's in a cliff in Italy, about a centimetre thick. Below it, the rock is full of tiny fossil shells. Above it, almost all of them are gone. In nineteen eighty, scientists found the clay was packed with a rare metal from space. Something huge had hit the Earth.

*Fact check:* Alvarez et al., *Science* 208 (1980); W. Alvarez (1997). "Packed with" is the 30× iridium enrichment. No impact fire shown.

## Short B — a clock made of space dust

- **Frame 0:** glittering space dust already falling softly onto the Earth (long Part 03 plate `space_dust`).
- **Hook caption:** A **SPACE** CLOCK
- **Change by 1 s:** the dust settles into a thin grey layer.

| s | Picture | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | dust falling on Earth | A father and son used dust from space as a clock. |
| 2–7 | close on silvery specks in the dust | That dust settles on the Earth all the time, carrying a rare metal called iridium. |
| 7–13 | Luis and Walter at the kitchen table with the sample; the lab | Luis and Walter Alvarez had the clay at the end of the dinosaurs tested, expecting almost none. |
| 9–14 | **title on screen:** *What Killed the Dinosaurs Was Hidden in a Line of Clay* |  |
| 13–18 | the spike on the graph | It held about thirty times too much. |
| 18–24 | a calm scale card of a 10 km asteroid beside a city; back to the dust (loop) | The answer was an asteroid, about ten kilometres wide. |

**Script (59 words):**

A father and son used dust from space as a clock. That dust settles on the Earth all the time, carrying a rare metal called iridium. Luis and Walter Alvarez had the clay at the end of the dinosaurs tested, expecting almost none. It held about thirty times too much. The answer was an asteroid, about ten kilometres wide.

*Fact check:* Alvarez et al. (1980): about 30× at Gubbio, 10 ± 4 km. "Had it tested" covers Asaro and Michel's measurement.

**Script (58 words):**

Not all the dinosaurs died. When the asteroid struck, sixty-six million years ago, a few small, feathered ones survived. Their descendants are all around you now. The pigeon on the pavement. The robin in the garden. The gull at the seaside. Every bird you have ever seen is a living dinosaur. Look up. They never really left.

*Fact check:* Natural History Museum (birds are avian dinosaurs); the long's FACT_NOTES rows (66 Ma; Larson 2016, Field 2018).
