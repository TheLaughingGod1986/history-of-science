What killed the dinosaurs? Follow the clue hidden in a pencil-thin line of clay in an Italian gorge.
In 1980 a geologist, his physicist father, and two chemists showed that the clay at the end of the Cretaceous held far too much iridium for ordinary Earth rocks.

For more than a hundred and sixty million years, dinosaurs ruled the land. Then, about sixty-six million years ago, they disappeared. The clue was a pencil-thin layer of clay in a gorge near Gubbio, Italy — later matched around the world, and finally to the Chicxulub crater in Mexico.
