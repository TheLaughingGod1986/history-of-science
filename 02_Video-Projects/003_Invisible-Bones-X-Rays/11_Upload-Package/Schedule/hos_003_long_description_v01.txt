How Did We Discover X-rays?

In a dark Würzburg lab in 1895, Wilhelm Röntgen covered a cathode tube so nothing should escape. Across the room, a cardboard screen painted to glow under light… glowed anyway.

Something invisible had left the tube. He called it X — for unknown.

This is the story of how we discovered X-rays: the cardboard that should have stayed dark, the living bones seen without a knife, and Bertha Röntgen’s hand — bones and wedding ring — that turned a haunted glow into proof the world could not argue with.
