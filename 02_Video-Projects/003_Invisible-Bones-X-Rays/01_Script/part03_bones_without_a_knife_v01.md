## VO

Then the leap that still feels like science fiction.

A hand in the path. Soft flesh fades in the glow. The bones hold. A denser shape — a ring — sits darker still.

You are looking at a living skeleton. No knife. No pain. Just a new kind of light writing density onto a screen.

Here is the teach, in plain words. X-rays pass soft tissue more than bone. Soft tissue is less dense, so more of the ray gets through and the screen stays brighter there. Bone is denser, so more of the ray is stopped and the screen goes darker. Metal is denser still — that is why a wedding ring draws a hard black line.

The picture is not magic. It is a map of how much invisible light each part of the hand absorbs. Bright means more got through. Dark means more was stopped.

What happens to medicine the day you can see a break before you cut? A fracture under swollen skin stops being only a guess. A bullet stops being only a hunt with a knife.

Wonder arrives first. Then caution. Invisible light that writes bones on cardboard can also burn skin and deeper tissue if you stay too long in the beam. Power and danger travel together — a lesson medicine will learn the hard way in the years after this night.
