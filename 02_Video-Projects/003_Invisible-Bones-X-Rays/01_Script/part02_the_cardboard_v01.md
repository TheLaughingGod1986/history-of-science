## VO (spoken · British · any-age · teach lock)

Röntgen covers the tube so cathode rays should stay trapped. He wraps it carefully — paper, shields, whatever should stop ordinary light and keep the known rays inside. Across the room sits a cardboard screen painted with a chemical that glows when light hits it — a fluorescent screen, waiting in the dark.

The cardboard should stay dark.

It does not.

A soft glow blooms where no ordinary light is shining. Something left the tube. Something that was never supposed to leave. Something that passes paper, wood, and soft things more easily than metal.

He has not named it yet. He refuses a romantic name. He calls it X — for unknown. An unknown ray that writes on cardboard.

Would you publish a discovery that looks like a haunted glow?

He does not rush the newspapers. He locks the door and tests. Block the path with a book — the glow weakens or shifts. With a hand — soft flesh and denser bone leave different marks. With metal — the shadow hardens. Move the object, move the shadow. The glow changes. Pattern, not ghost story.

Teach it simply: if covering the tube still lights the screen, the old story is wrong. Cathode rays were supposed to stop at the glass. This new light does not.

That glowing cardboard is the first detector. The unknown light is writing on it. Bit by bit, the map of the invisible has just begun.
