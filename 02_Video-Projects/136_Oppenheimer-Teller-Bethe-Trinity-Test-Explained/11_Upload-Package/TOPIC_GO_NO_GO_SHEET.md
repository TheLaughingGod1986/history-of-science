## 2. Script-Stage Physics, Historical & Policy Guardrails (Logged by `J3502` for `J3507`)

1. **`LA-602` date vs pre-Trinity calculation timeline:** Declassified Los Alamos report **`LA-602`** (*Ignition of the Atmosphere with Nuclear Bombs*, by E. J. Konopinski, C. Marvin, and E. Teller) is dated **August 1946** — a post-war write-up documenting the calculations started by Edward Teller in **July 1942** at the Berkeley summer conference and checked by Konopinski, Hans Bethe, and Robert Serber before the test. Never claim `LA-602` was written in 1942 or handed to General Groves before July 1945.
2. **Atmospheric-ignition physics & large safety margin (no Hollywood "near-zero" conflation):** The feared mechanism was **nitrogen–nitrogen (`¹⁴N + ¹⁴N`) nuclear fusion** in air (not chemical burning of oxygen). It proved impossible with a **large safety margin** because radiative heat losses (**Bremsstrahlung** and inverse Compton cooling) outpaced fusion energy generation across reachable temperatures. Do **not** present Arthur Compton's famous *"three chances in a million"* line (from a 1959 Pearl Buck interview, disputed by Bethe) as a 1945 Los Alamos probability calculation without explicit primary-source attribution and Bethe's rebuttal in `SOURCES.md`.
3. **Separate Fermi's pre-test side bet from his post-flash paper-scrap measurement:** Before detonation, Enrico Fermi made a dark-humour side bet at Base Camp on whether the test would ignite the atmosphere (New Mexico vs the whole world). Separately, roughly **40 seconds after the flash** (~10 miles / ~16 km from Ground Zero), he dropped small torn scraps of paper as the shockwave arrived, saw them displaced roughly **2.5 metres**, and estimated a yield around **10 kilotons** TNT equivalent (later official/radiochemical measurements put Trinity around **~20–21 kilotons** — verify exact Los Alamos / Bainbridge numbers in `SOURCES.md`). Keep the two anecdotes cleanly distinct.
