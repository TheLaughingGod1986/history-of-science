## 2. The film

### 2.1 Story (person, date, twist) — UNVERIFIED
- **Open (first 15 s):** a harbour where boats sit on mud, then float six hours later (the Bay of Fundy's range is over ten metres). Twice a day, everywhere. What is pulling the sea?
- **1609, Johannes Kepler:** suggests the Moon attracts the sea.
- **1616 and 1632, Galileo Galilei:** calls a pull from the Moon "occult" and argues instead that the sea sloshes because the Earth spins and orbits, like water in a moving barge. It is the closing argument of his *Dialogue*. It predicts one tide a day; sailors knew there were two.
- **1687, Isaac Newton (*Principia*):** the Moon's gravity is stronger on the near side of the Earth than the far side, which stretches the ocean into two bulges. The Sun adds its share: spring and neap tides.
- **The twist:** Newton's own picture is not the whole answer either. In 1775 to 1776 Pierre-Simon Laplace shows the sea cannot keep up with the Moon; the tide is a wave forced around ocean basins, which is why real tide times and heights differ from place to place.
- **Close:** tide tables, and the tide-predicting machines built from 1872 (Lord Kelvin), used into the Second World War.
- **Candidate DATA moments:** two tides in about 24 h 50 min; Fundy's range against a house; a timeline 1609 → 1632 → 1687 → 1776 → 1872; Sun's pull as a share of the Moon's.
