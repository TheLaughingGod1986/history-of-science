Animistry = history brought alive in **3D stylised cartoon**. We steal the *form*; the *subject* is **how science was discovered**.

## Steal for History of Science

1. **3D cartoon worlds** — discoveries, labs, cities, telescopes, people of the time (not photoreal space dread).
2. **Runtime 8–9 minutes** for new longs (Animistry’s tight end; matches our stage lock).
3. **Title grammar:** one-promise curiosity — *What / How / Why / Did / A day in…* — about **science discoveries**, not generic battles.
4. **Immersive story plates** — put the viewer inside the moment of discovery.
5. **Thumbs:** one big scene or character + bold hook text; sell the wonder of *finding out*.

### HOS title shape examples (illustrative)

- How Did We Learn the Earth Moves?
- What Was a Day in Faraday’s Lab Really Like?
- Did Alchemy Accidentally Invent Chemistry?
- Why Did Everyone Think the Plague Was Bad Air?

## Do not clone

- Pure political / military history as the default (Rome–China, medieval London) — we bend every film toward **science, method, evidence, discovery**.
- Zero guide character forever — HOS keeps **the Explorer** as a **sparse** side character (every few scenes), not Animistry’s total absence and not Orbit’s continuous presence.
- Shop reads, fearbait, conspiracy.
- Stretching to 15–16 min until an 8–9 min film earns impressions + hold.

## HOS twist (one line)

Animistry’s 3D cartoon immersion + question titles, pointed at **the history of science**, with British VO and a light Explorer garnish.
