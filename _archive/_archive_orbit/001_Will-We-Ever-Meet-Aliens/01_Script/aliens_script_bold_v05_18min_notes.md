# Will We Ever Meet Aliens? — Bold Explainer Rebuild

## Editorial target

- Audio-first science mystery that remains satisfying without the picture.
- Target duration: 18–20 minutes at the tuned Professional Voice Clone pace.
- Narration length: 2,359 words.
- Tone: intelligent, intimate, calm, curious, and quietly dramatic.
- Scientific claims must remain provisional where the evidence is provisional.
- The film must not show a brand card before the opening hook.

## Story structure

### Cold open — The silence

Open on the apparent contradiction: a galaxy full of worlds with no confirmed
answer. The first brand appearance follows the hook and holds for approximately
two seconds.

### Act I — The problem

Establish distance, time, the Fermi question, the exoplanet revolution, and the
Drake Equation. The audience should understand why the silence is genuinely
surprising without being buried in numbers.

### Act II — The suspects

Test the main explanations as competing possibilities: rare intelligence, the
Great Filter, short technological lifetimes, quiet civilisations, non-overlapping
radio eras, the zoo hypothesis, and the possibility that humanity is early.

Each explanation must answer one part of the mystery while opening a deeper
question. This creates forward pull without inventing certainty.

### Act III — A different kind of meeting

Reframe the solution. Humanity may be searching for a deliberate conversation
when the first evidence is more likely to be an unintended fingerprint:
technosignatures, atmospheric biosignatures, or microbial life in the Solar
System.

The closing reveal is that a populated galaxy may not look populated until our
instruments and questions become sufficiently sensitive.

### Cliffhanger

End on the possibility that the first credible sign is already present in
archived data and has not yet been recognised. This creates a natural bridge to
a future episode about candidate signals, false positives, and how scientific
confirmation works.

## Brand placement

1. Hook plays first for approximately 14–18 seconds.
2. Orbit turns towards the central mystery.
3. Two-second brand hold appears with a restrained audio sting.
4. Narration resumes immediately with “This is Orbit.”

No long logo animation and no repeated branding during the story.

## Visual rules

- Primary language: bold editorial explainer illustration.
- Palette: deep navy, petrol teal, cyan, warm cream, and Orbit orange.
- Texture: restrained paper grain and painterly detail.
- Each visual must illustrate the current spoken idea, not merely “space”.
- Never reuse the same source image or video clip in the episode.
- A recurring concept may return only as a newly designed composition.
- Do not reuse a crop, reverse a clip, or change speed to disguise duplication.
- Information cards are reserved for the few facts that materially benefit from
  text. They must not appear every few seconds.
- Orbit is animated independently from the illustrated backgrounds.
- Orbit occupies a consistent lower-right stage unless the composition clearly
  requires another planned position.
- Orbit appears in approximately 35–45% of the film, using expressions and
  gestures that respond to the narration.
- Do not embed a static Orbit image inside generated background art.
