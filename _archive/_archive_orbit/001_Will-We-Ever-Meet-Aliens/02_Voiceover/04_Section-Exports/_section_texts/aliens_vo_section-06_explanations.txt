Then there’s distance again — not as poetry, but as logistics.

Even if a civilisation only a thousand light-years away sent a radio message today, we wouldn’t hear it for a thousand years. If they sent a ship at a fraction of light speed, the journey could outlast empires, languages… and maybe even species.

So “meeting” might not mean a handshake. It might mean detecting a spectrum… a chemical fingerprint… a patterned signal — evidence that someone existed, even if we never share a room.

That’s why modern searches look different from flying-saucer stories.

SETI — the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence — listens for artificial radio or optical signals. Projects scan the sky for narrow-band transmissions that nature doesn’t usually make. So far, no confirmed “hello.” We’ve had intriguing candidates, like the famous Wow! signal in nineteen seventy-seven… but nothing that has repeated cleanly enough to settle the question.

It helps to remember what “listening” actually means. The sky is huge. Our surveys are still incomplete. We have not listened to every star, at every frequency, for every kind of message, for long enough to be sure.

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — especially when our microphone has only been on for a cosmic blink.
