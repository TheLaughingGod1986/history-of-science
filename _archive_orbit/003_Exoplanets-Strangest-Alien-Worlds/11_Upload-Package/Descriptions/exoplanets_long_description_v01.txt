Alien worlds — Orbit visits the strangest planets we've ever found: diamond crusts, glass rain sideways, three suns, hot Jupiters, and eyeball exoplanets. Exoplanets explained as a space documentary cosmic journey.

What are alien worlds actually like? Diamond crusts. Glass rain sideways. Three suns in the sky. Hot Jupiters with nights that still glow. Eyeball planets locked between fire and ice.

Join Orbit — a small orange exploration robot — on History of Science through the strangest planets in the universe. This is exoplanets explained the Orbit way: one world at a time, with the science we actually have… and the wonder we cannot help feeling.

In this space documentary we explore:
• What an exoplanet is (and why the catalogue is a museum of extremes)
• How we find alien planets — transit, stellar wobble, and atmospheric spectra
• Carbon-rich diamond worlds
• HD 189733 b–style glass rain and sideways weather
• Multi-star skies and triple shadows
• Hot Jupiters and the hottest nights in the data
• Tidally locked eyeball planets
• The habitable zone — and how we might one day smell life in a spectrum

Not fantasy. Measurements. Models. Honest uncertainty. Real wonder.
