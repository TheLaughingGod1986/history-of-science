So will we ever meet them — face to… faceplate?

Honest answer: we don’t know. Anyone claiming certainty is selling comfort, not science.

What we can say is more careful.

If “meet” means a physical encounter with interstellar travellers in our lifetime, the odds look long. The energy costs are enormous. The timescales are brutal. The universe does not schedule appointments around human careers.

If “meet” means detecting clear evidence of life beyond Earth — microbial or technological — then this century is one of the most interesting windows we’ve ever had. Instruments are getting sharper. Catalogues of planets are growing. The question is moving from philosophy… toward measurement.

There’s also a quieter lesson inside the Fermi Paradox.

The silence of the sky isn’t only about aliens. It’s about us. How long do technological civilisations last? How carefully do we listen? How honestly do we interpret ambiguous data — without turning every anomaly into a headline?

Wanting company in the cosmos is deeply human. So is the fear that we might be alone. Both feelings can coexist with careful science.
